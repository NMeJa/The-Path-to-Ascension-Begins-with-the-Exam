# Chapter 14: In the Midst of the Grand Sect Examination

[These questions are nothing special after all.]

At that very moment, Su Xin arrived at the exact same conclusion.

When he initially discovered that the Heavenly Spirit Sect's "Grand Sect Competition" was not an ordinary martial clash of magical arts, he had briefly contemplated [beating a retreat](# "Literal CN: 打退堂鼓 (dǎ tuì táng gǔ) — backing out or chickening out")—shouldn't cultivation be measured by combat prowess? Su Xin loathed pure theoretical discussions on scriptures and the Dao; he felt that was something only doddering old fogies bothered with.

But once he laid eyes on the deceptively simple question formats and the Qi Condensation-level problems, Su Xin felt confident again.

[Hmph, a third-rate sect like the Heavenly Spirit Sect only amounts to this much... right?]

Su Xin thought out of pure habit.

Yet when he lifted his head and reached for his writing brush, taking in the scene within the "examination hall," his certainty wavered:

[...A third-rate sect?]

Even with Su Xin's spoiled, pampered temperament, he couldn't bring himself to utter such words here.

After all, rather than calling this place an "examination hall," it was far more accurate to describe it as a palace.

It was precisely the very palace Su Xin had gazed upon from afar aboard the cloud carriage.

Now that he sat inside the palace, Su Xin discovered that its interior was even more exquisite than its exterior: not only were there palace walls sculpted from crystalline giant clamshells, but also lavish, ornate seating daises of chalcedony and pearls.

Most striking of all were the coral platforms positioned in the heart of the palace.

Due to the Sea Clan's unique aquatic environment, their palaces were multi-dimensional; this palace replicated that feature faithfully, allowing multicolored coral platforms to float like blossoming flowers suspended high in the hall's center.

When beautiful merfolk and clam maidens glided between them and alighted upon the coral daises to dance gracefully, it was a spectacle capable of eliciting marvel even from True Immortals.

The reason Su Xin knew this was that he had accompanied his parents to a Sea Clan banquet during his childhood.

That banquet had left such a profound impression on Su Xin that it lingered in his memory for years. Unfortunately, the Dragon Clan rarely invited human cultivators; moreover, given the Sea Clan's vast lifespans, hosting a banquet once every few centuries already counted as [partying every single night](# "Literal CN: 夜夜箫歌 (yè yè xiāo gē) — music and revelry every night"). For a human to have the fortune to attend even once was bragging rights for a lifetime.

Who could have imagined that this little Heavenly Spirit Sect had actually constructed such a breathtaking palace in imitation of the Sea Clan?

And their purpose wasn't even to entertain distinguished guests from the Heavenly Dao Alliance, but to host an internal sect examination!

There were no dancing merfolk upon the gorgeous coral daises; in their stead stood simple wooden desks. Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples sat before them, while a translucent bubble snugly enveloped each coral dais, blocking out external noise and isolating the examinees from one another.

Right now, Su Xin was seated upon just such a small coral dais encased in a bubble.

He glanced left and right, unable to communicate with a soul.

[What a complete waste of heavenly treasures!]

Indignant, Su Xin picked up his writing brush and began answering questions.

Yet after writing just three questions, he ground to a screeching halt.

> When paired with a Nine-Flames Explosive Talisman, which of the following talismans saves the most runes while producing the optimal explosive effect?

[Why would anyone need to pair them at all? If one Explosive Talisman isn't enough, can't you just hurl ten more?]

Su Xin thought dismissively, casually scribbling down an answer.

Next came a problem of the same type, which Su Xin also bluffed his way through with a random choice.

A third question he couldn't make heads or tails of...

A fourth question that was utter gibberish to him...

Having run into wall after wall, Su Xin's patience finally snapped.

[What kind of damn questions are these?]-Su Xin grumbled bitterly in his heart.

He glared venomously at the test paper for quite a while, looking as if he wanted to rip it to shreds, before a smirk curled the corners of his lips:

—"If you think this is enough to stump this young master, you are sorely mistaken,"-Su Xin muttered.

Muttering under his breath, he produced an exquisite bronze mirror from his robes.

He gave the mirror face a light wipe and angled it toward a certain coral dais. To his satisfaction, another person's exam paper materialized upon the polished bronze surface!

Indeed, this was a magical treasure known as the [Light-Stealing Mirror](# "Literal CN: 偷光镜 (tōu guāng jìng) — magical treasure enabling the user to peek through formations and view others' exam papers").

With it, one could effortlessly pierce through Golden Core-grade formations to spy on another cultivator's immortal cave; using it to peek at someone else's exam paper in an exam hall like this was child's play.

What, cheating?

No, Su Xin didn't believe for a single second that he was cheating.

Whether in the Heavenly Dao Alliance or during competitions at other sects, Su Xin had always used the magical treasures bestowed by his parents in broad daylight to crush his opponents—utterly heedless of how game-breaking a treasure personally forged by Tribulation Transcendence powerhouses was when deployed in a contest between ordinary low-level sect disciples.

After all, magical treasures were an intrinsic part of a cultivator's overall strength!

How could a cultivator using a magical treasure to obtain the results he desired possibly count as cheating?

Su Xin rotated the mirror, searching for a suitable target to spy on.

As a "distinguished guest" of the Heavenly Dao Alliance, Su Xin's seat was situated very close to Zhou Hui's direct disciples. For instance, on the coral daises immediately flanking his own sat a pair of identical 'young maidens'—clearly twin sisters (?).

Su Xin had originally intended to aim the mirror at them, but for some reason, he recalled Yuwen Xuan and the other two direct disciples who had welcomed him earlier.

—"Tsk, does this [Un-Grinding Immortal](# "Literal CN: 不卷仙尊 (Bùjuǎn Xiānzūn) — Su Xin's derisive pun on Zhou Hui's title 'Untiring Immortal Venerable' (不倦仙尊), replacing '倦' (untiring) with '卷' (juǎn, involution/grinding) to mock him as an uncompetitive backwater bumpkin") pick his disciples based purely on their faces?"-Su Xin scoffed.

He felt an inexplicable surge of irritation.

Especially since this pair of twins looked barely fourteen or fifteen years old, Su Xin was struck by an inexplicable sensation that copying off them would mean admitting defeat. Thus, he shifted his mirror, aiming it toward a farther vantage point.

Various faces of Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples flashed across the mirror's surface:

—"This girl looks well-behaved; she probably gets decent grades?"-Su Xin mused as he peered into the glass.

Looking into the mirror, he randomly picked a girl whose test paper was signed with the name "Gan Tang."

Primarily, Su Xin believed that only well-behaved girls would foolishly and obediently memorize these boring theoretical questions; besides, every stroke of her brush was meticulous, exuding the distinct aura of an honor student.

Her exam paper seemed to corroborate this deduction.

Although Gan Tang wrote with great caution, her pace was far from slow. Only an incense stick of time had elapsed since the exam began, yet she had already reached question thirty-something. The problems on her paper, however, left Su Xin thoroughly bewildered—let alone solving them, the raw difficulty level alone was more than a notch higher than his own exam paper!

—"Sh-She must be an elite inner sect disciple of the Heavenly Spirit Sect, right? Her cultivation might be quite high, which is why her test paper is different!"-Su Xin reassured himself.

He swiftly angled the mirror toward another pair:

—"Ah, another pair of twins... Heavenly Spirit Sect sure has a lot of twins. But this time it's a boy and a girl. Let's check the boy first—maybe his test paper is the same as mine?"-he wondered.

Heaven only knew how he had arrived at the absurd notion that exam papers were segregated by gender.

Yet this guess strangely appeared to hold some water: the difficulty of the boy's test paper was indeed comparable to Su Xin's. However, after squinting at it for half a day, Su Xin couldn't find a single question that matched his own.

At this point, a sinking premonition crept into Su Xin's chest. He shifted the mirror onto the twin sister's exam paper.

The girl's exam paper was significantly harder again, appearing similar in difficulty to the paper belonging to the girl named "Gan Tang" he had viewed earlier. Yet once again, not a single question matched.

[Could it be that every single exam paper is different?!]

[Have they lost their minds? There are hundreds of people here, and every single one has a different test paper?!]

[Besides, why do these exam papers feel like they have no end? I just saw someone on question one hundred...]

[And how on earth are all these people answering them so smoothly?!]

He scanned several people in succession, but truly failed to locate a single identical question. Refusing to admit defeat, Su Xin aimed at disciple after disciple, scouring them one by one, until his gaze finally halted upon the paper belonging to a disciple named "Qin Xian."

It wasn't that Su Xin had finally found someone to copy from, but rather that the other party had just gotten a question wrong.

And that question happened to be one Su Xin knew how to solve!

[The one that grows in the subterranean flames of the Southern Border's desolate valleys is Seven-Leaf Amber! Hah, even I know how to solve that question, yet he can't get it right. This guy's grades must be absolute trash.]

Su Xin gloated in malicious glee.

He failed to realize that by thinking this, he was implicitly conceding that his own academic performance was abysmal.

It couldn't be helped: while observing the others answering their exams just now, Su Xin's self-confidence had suffered a catastrophic blow.

But watching someone else suffer was evidently far more entertaining than suffering alone.

For the time being, Su Xin forgot why he had resorted to peeking in the first place, watching the other boy take his exam with relish:

[Wrong on the next one too. Truly useless.]

[Tsk, this question is probably... correct.]

[The flame for refining Purple Gold? This artifact refinement question is a bit too hard—it's at least Foundation Establishment level... Huh? He filled in the answer without the slightest hesitation! He didn't just guess randomly, did he?]

Su Xin subconsciously looked up, glancing in the direction the mirror was angled—toward Qin Xian.

* * *

Qin Xian's seat was actually very close to Su Xin, less than ten meters away.

However, all of Qin Xian's attention was fixed upon his test paper; he hadn't noticed that someone was actively spying on him.

Even if he had noticed, Qin Xian wouldn't have cared.

He felt like he was about to be a goner:

[[Clinging to Buddha's feet at the last minute](# "Literal CN: 临时抱佛脚 (línshí bào fójiǎo) — scrambling to cram or pray at the last second") really doesn't work after all!]

Even though he had struggled and crammed with all his might!

It wasn't that he hadn't improved at all; Qin Xian estimated his current performance was roughly getting two wrong for every two right, which should be slightly higher than his score last time. Unfortunately, he had squandered too much time in the past, and on top of that, all his fellow classmates were studying so relentlessly that his ranking might very well end up the same as before—no, with the outer sect included this time, his ranking might drop even further.

Just as Qin Xian was teetering on the brink of despair, the very next question made his eyes light up.

> Which flame is used to refine Purple Gold?

[Awesome! An artifact refinement question!]

Unlike a half-baked dabbler like Su Xin, Qin Xian was a genuine elite of the Heavenly Spirit Sect's inner sect! Although he had crammed the foundational theory at the last minute, when it came to artifact refinement, he was a true professional!

As if terrified the question might vanish into thin air, Qin Xian swiftly penned his answer.

Whether his luck had taken a turn for the better or not, the artifact refinement questions that followed visibly proliferated. (Incidentally, this examination paper was different from the first one: the paper had been enchanted with spellwork, operating on [dynamic difficulty adjustment](# "Zhou Hui's computerized standardized testing algorithm dynamically generating questions based on the examinee's performance and answers") such that only after finishing one question would a new question appear. What started as one artifact refinement question every seven or eight problems gradually became three or four out of seven or eight, and then five or six out of seven or eight.)

Confronted with his strongest discipline, Qin Xian naturally became [like a fish in water](# "Literal CN: 如鱼得水 (rú yú dé shuǐ) — in one's proper element").

He even began to feel that solving problems was becoming genuinely enjoyable.

Especially when, after effortlessly knocking out three or four questions, he suddenly encountered a challenging problem and managed to crack it through his own wits and ingenuity.

After answering who knew how many questions like this, Qin Xian finally hit another roadblock:

> How can the flame used for refining the Seven-Star Sword be made deeper purple?

This was also an artifact refinement question, but the Seven-Star Sword was a weapon used only by late-stage Foundation Establishment cultivators, which clearly exceeded the scope of Qin Xian's current studies.

[Guess I'll have to skip it for now.]

Qin Xian thought with a sigh. He lifted his brush to dip it in ink, only for his hand to freeze midway:

[Wait a minute, isn't the raw material for the Seven-Star Sword Purple Gold? A previous question was about the flame used to refine Purple Gold. Generally speaking, making a flame deeper purple means purifying the flame, and the best method is adding an associated substance—and the ideal associated substance is something that grows within that flame... Mmm, haven't I seen a similar question before?]

Qin Xian rustled through his previous questions, flipping back.

He knew full well that doing this for the sake of a single problem was dreadfully inefficient; he could easily wait until he finished the rest before coming back to consider it. Yet at this precise moment, Qin Xian wanted nothing more than to uncover the answer to this riddle.

All of a sudden, he had recaptured a flicker of the fiery drive he had felt when he first began studying the art of artifact refinement.
