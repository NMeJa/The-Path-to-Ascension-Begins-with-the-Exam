# Chapter 67: White Jade Capital in the Heavens (第67章 天上白玉京)

White Jade Capital.

The ancient capital of the cultivation world.

Before the God-Demon War, back when the Sky Ladder remained unbroken and the Central Domain was still lush and hospitable, the spiritual energy of this world had been vastly more abundant. In those days, anyone could cultivate, and the number of practitioners reached its golden zenith. In the skies above the center of the Central Domain, cultivators erected a colossal celestial metropolis, christened the "White Jade Capital."

Legends told that it was the most magnificent, breathtaking capital across the three thousand worlds, rivaling the True Immortal Realm... No, not even the True Immortal Realm possessed a city so grand. It had enticed True Immortals to descend to the mortal dust, and ascendants to linger and wander its boulevards, loath to ever depart.

Until the extraterrestrial fiends invaded.

Later generations whispered that White Jade Capital had been too ostentatious, too presumptuous, defying the boundaries between mortal and divine until it drew the wrath of the extraterrestrial fiends.

Whatever the truth, White Jade Capital fell during the God-Demon War. Plunging from high in the heavens, it smashed into the heart of the Central Domain, carving out a colossal crater so vast that the Abyssal Ocean below flooded upward to drown the entire region. The lingering demonic qi and spiritual energy mixed and churned, creating titanic vortices of magical power that spawned raging storms across the entire Central Domain. The land was rendered permanently uninhabitable for mankind—whether cultivators or mortals.

Yet people never forgot White Jade Capital.

Especially in a cultivation world where ["progress in science (cultivation) relies entirely on archaeology."](# "Literal CN: 科技（X）修仙靠考古 — parody of the internet meme 'technology relies on archaeology', applying it to cultivation civilization uncovering ancient relics")

Whether seekers of the Dao like the East Hedge Garden or gold-rush opportunists like the Abyssal Ocean Society, both had spent millennia scouring the ruins of the ancient battlefield deep within the Abyssal Ocean. Beyond excavating fragments to piece together the truth of antiquity, what they coveted most was this fabled capital said to rival the halls of True Immortals.

Regrettably, no matter how they searched, their efforts yielded nothing.

Forget White Jade Capital itself; not a single shred of evidence proving its existence or crash site could be found.

Some began to argue that White Jade Capital had never existed at all—that it was merely the boastful exaggeration of ancient cultivators, and that this very arrogance had invited disaster and war.

Even Zhou Hui had once shared that skepticism.

Granted, that excluded the part about "inviting the extraterrestrial fiends"—Zhou Hui maintained that whenever a catastrophic war was fought on a land where the primary combatants were not local inhabitants, the root cause had nothing to do with the natives. Even if an excuse were concocted, it was no different from [demanding whether someone wore a hat today or not](# "Reference to the classic fable/joke where a predator beats up a rabbit regardless of whether it wore a hat or didn't wear a hat — an arbitrary pretext for aggression"); it was entirely irrelevant. Still, absent concrete evidence, one could not prove White Jade Capital truly existed.

The most optimistic hypothesis was that White Jade Capital had been ground into dust by the warring gods and fiends, swallowed whole by the demonic vortex, and turned into the fuel powering the violent spiritual storms that swept the Central Domain.

Because it had been so thoroughly polluted and disintegrated, no one had ever found it.

Yet now, this ancient capital—fabled to have crashed and perished tens of thousands of years ago—lay pristine and complete right before Zhou Hui's eyes.

It was indeed magnificent.

It was indeed breathtaking.

Even Zhou Hui, with all his vast breadth of knowledge, could not help but marvel in awe, struggling to imagine how such an imposing, sublime metropolis had once floated serenely in the skies.

Yet what stunned Zhou Hui even more was its absolute completeness.

Looking down from the precipice of the fractured gallery, one could take in the panoramic vista of White Jade Capital's Twelve Pavilions and Five Cities. It appeared completely brand new, without a single flaw; even the pavilions and terraces skirting the outer perimeter showed not the slightest fracture, bearing zero trace of wartime devastation.

The entire city resembled a sleeping beauty, quietly awaiting the dawn.

Or perhaps a delicate bubble enclosing a sweet dream, liable to burst into nothingness at the gentlest tap of a finger.

Zhou Hui stood enthralled by the sight, but the creature beside him possessed not an ounce of romantic sensibility.

—"Master! Master! Let's go down and take a look!"-Yuwen Xuan urged impatiently.

Had he not devoured those bones and ended up in this wretched shape, Yuwen Xuan probably wouldn't have waited for Zhou Hui to arrive before charging straight down.

Even now, he was dying to see what treasures lay hidden inside this legendary capital.

—"No rush,"-Zhou Hui said, shaking his head.

Although Zhou Hui was impressed, he had not lost his reason; he could clearly see the deep anomalies of this city.

Its supernatural preservation was only the first.

The second, far more unsettling oddity was that this city contained not a single wisp of spiritual energy.

One had to understand that, true to its name, White Jade Capital had been constructed using massive, seamless blocks of jade as its foundational bedrock. Unlike the other world (probably?), where jade was merely an ornamental mineral, in the cultivation world, jade was the premier medium for spell preservation, employed to store cultivation methods and record mystic arts. For instance, Zhou Hui's damaged Ascending Immortal Platform had been carved from jade before he remodeled it into the Library Pavilion; the spells inside that ["turned words into stairs"](# "Reference to the Floating Text Escalator in Heavenly Spirit Sect's Library Pavilion") had originally been stored in jade, allowing even mortals devoid of spiritual power to trigger them as long as they weren't encrypted with divine sense.

Naturally, such jade theoretically held staggering reserves of spiritual energy.

Especially for a metropolis of this scale, Zhou Hui should have sensed its aura while standing in the hall... No, even ten thousand li away from Blood Demon Hall, he should have detected its presence! Yet he had walked step by step to the very precipice of the corridor before confirming its existence with his own eyes.

In fact, even standing right here, Zhou Hui still couldn't sense any spiritual energy whatsoever.

If his divine sense hadn't swept downward to confirm with absolute certainty that every brick and tile truly existed, he would have suspected this was an intricately crafted illusion.

Zhou Hui decided to handle things with greater prudence.

Fumbling through his sleeve, he drew out a talisman paper.

Catching sight of the talisman, Yuwen Xuan's face twisted into an expression of sheer panic:

—"Master! Aren't the two of us enough?! There isn't a speck of spiritual energy down there; it looks just like a mortal city. There can't possibly be any danger!"

As he spoke, his tiny hooves clicked and clattered as he sprang into the air like a bouncy ball, trying to bite the talisman from his master's hand.

Small as his body was, he bounced surprisingly high.

—"...Given its origins and this environment, you can actually say with a straight face that 'no spiritual energy means no danger'? It seems your punishment hasn't been thorough enough!"-Zhou Hui pinched him with one hand while igniting the talisman with the other.-"When we get back, you are going to copy my *Fundamental Theory of Spiritual Energy* twenty thousand times!"

Twisting all four hooves in frantic defiance, Yuwen Xuan whimpered:

—"Ying, ying, no! I can memorize that stuff after reading it just once—why do you always punish me with copying?! Besides, I have hooves now! How am I supposed to write?!"

However, a panicked gasp echoing from the burning talisman abruptly cut short Yuwen Xuan's flailing.

[—"...Master?"](# "Speaker: Lu Shulong")

It was Lu Shulong's voice.

Because the talisman operated on a one-way transmission—meaning Lu Shulong could only hear Zhou Hui—he hadn't heard his Eldest Senior Brother's whimpering. But he had caught that crisp order to "copy twenty thousand times" loud and clear, sending a shudder of existential dread down his spine.

Consequently, Lu Shulong's tone was trembling with utmost deference and terror:

—"M-Master, did I do something wrong?"

—"No, I wasn't speaking to you just now,"-Zhou Hui replied.

[Then who was he talking to? Who on earth was unfortunate enough to get assigned twenty thousand copies?!]

On the other end, Lu Shulong glanced blankly toward Ran Deng and Guan Ye, who were still diligently constructing the grand array. He didn't recall Master bringing along any other martial brothers or sisters. Regrettably, Master offered no explanation, merely instructing him:

—"Come down here for a moment... Aside from A-Ran and Ye-zi, don't let anyone know."

—"Yes, Master. Has something happened?"-Lu Shulong asked worriedly upon hearing this.

—"It's a bit complicated to explain. We'll talk when you arrive,"-Zhou Hui said.

Lu Shulong pressed no further, evidently departing to carry out the order.

Having given Lu Shulong his instructions, Zhou Hui looked down at the little lamb still thrashing in his grasp.

Watching the talisman burn to ash, it panicked even more:

—"Alright, Master, I'll be good, I promise! So please, I beg you, don't call Lu Shulong down here! I don't want him to see me like this! What happens... what happens to my dignity as Eldest Senior Brother?!"-saying this, tears began streaming down its face again.

Yuwen Xuan truly couldn't control it; this vessel seemed biologically predisposed to weeping.

—"You actually possess self-esteem?"-Zhou Hui gave it an astonished look.

Watching the little lamb weep *pitter-patter*, Zhou Hui reflected for a moment and decided it was best to indulge this troublesome brat's pride just a little.

Of course, Zhou Hui wasn't about to alter his plans to suit Yuwen Xuan's whims. Tucking the lamb into his arms, he patted its back:

—"If you don't want to be exposed, just pretend to be a mute spirit beast. I won't tell Shulong or the others about your current condition."

—"I don't want to be a spirit beast!"-[Isn't that just emergency reserve rations?!]-Yuwen Xuan complained petulantly.

Yet under Zhou Hui's soothing pats, his body relaxed obediently, even nudging Zhou Hui's palm to demand a few more strokes.

Still, his mouth refused to surrender the struggle for higher status:

—"At the very least, I should be Master's spiritual pet!"

[You'd settle for that?]

Zhou Hui parted his lips to retort, but footsteps echoed from the far end of the corridor.

[—"Master—"](# "Speaker: Lu Shulong")

It was Lu Shulong.

He walked down as instructed, only to freeze when he spotted the pancake-collared lamb cradled in Zhou Hui's arms:

—"Master, is this an ingredient for tonight's dinner? [Lamb Paomo with Pita Bread](# "Literal CN: 羊肉泡馕 (yángròu pào náng) — humorous spin on Yangrou Paomo (mutton soup with torn flatbread), here using Xinjiang nang/pita bread")?"

Yuwen Xuan snapped his jaws shut right on his fifth junior brother's hand.

* * *

Naturally, Zhou Hui hadn't summoned Lu Shulong here to eat Lamb Paomo with Pita Bread.

He instructed Lu Shulong to reveal his true form, mounted the dragon's back, and descended slowly toward the celestial city below.

—It was not that Zhou Hui couldn't fly on his own; rather, the blood of a True Dragon bypassed many unforeseen perils. For example, if the city harbored ancient suppression arrays designed to forbid the circulation of spiritual energy or restrict spellcasting, not even a True Immortal-grade restriction could instantly paralyze a True Dragon, thereby buying Zhou Hui ample time to cast his arts.

Yet White Jade Capital appeared devoid of traps. The White Dragon bore Zhou Hui downward without the slightest impedance.

Furthermore, the thoroughfares of this capital were exceptionally wide; even the side alleys between market shops and residences were spacious enough for a dragon's colossal frame to glide through with ease. Sitting atop the White Dragon's back, cradling the wide-eyed, inquisitive little black lamb, Zhou Hui closely inspected the architecture flanking both sides.

Just as his divine sense had observed from above, the city was perfectly intact. Its buildings stood flawless, and there was scarcely a speck of dust, as if diligent hands swept the avenues every single day.

Gliding through these neighborhoods, one felt as though ancient citizens might step out from the doorways at any moment.

—"Master, where should we head next?"-Lu Shulong asked.

By this point, Lu Shulong had reached the mouth of the neighborhood. Just ahead lay the capital's grand Main Avenue.

It was staggeringly vast—broad enough for twelve dragons to fly abreast down its center.

In truth, this avenue had been specifically engineered for such celestial processions; during the era of White Jade Capital, True Immortals would descend from the heavens riding chariots drawn by twelve dragons.

—"...Follow the Main Avenue forward,"-Zhou Hui directed.-"We are going to the Thirteenth Pavilion."

White Jade Capital was famous for its Twelve Pavilions.

Taller even than the skyscrapers of the other world, they towered along the perimeter of the metropolis, interconnected by lofty suspended aerial walkways that bound the Five Cities into a single unified bastion.

Yet according to forgotten legends—an irony in itself, for while cultivators enjoyed lifespans far exceeding mortals, virtually no historical records regarding the pre-war cultivation world, White Jade Capital, or the God-Demon War had survived, leaving Zhou Hui to gather most of these tales from the mouths of mortals during his travels across the world—mortals insisted that White Jade Capital actually housed a Thirteenth Pavilion. So long as one traveled straight along the Main Avenue, it would eventually come into view, marking the royal court of the immortals.

Cultivators had no kings, leading scholars to speculate it was the headquarters of the Asking Dao Alliance.

The Asking Dao Alliance was an organization akin to the modern Heavenly Dao Alliance—or rather, the reverse was true: the Heavenly Dao Alliance had inherited its mantle from the Asking Dao Alliance, though altering a single character had completely transformed its philosophical soul.

Zhou Hui had previously treated such folklore as mere fairy tales, but now that White Jade Capital itself had manifested, he saw no harm in following the legend.

It proved to be unequivocally the right choice.

After Lu Shulong flew ninety thousand li along the Main Avenue—a distance absurdly disproportionate to the city's visible scale, for no matter how colossal White Jade Capital might be, its avenue could not be that long; yet in this mystical realm, such impossibility was precisely what made it correct—Zhou Hui faintly discerned the shadowy silhouette of towering pavilions ahead.

Advancing another ninety thousand li, faint murmurs of voices and solemn celestial music drifted into his ears.

Another ninety thousand li forward, Zhou Hui spotted patrol soldiers standing guard along the roadside.

They were clearly not living beings, their forms semi-transparent. Yet Zhou Hui detected no aura of undead specters; they were merely phantoms of the distant past. Even so, they stood rod-straight with spears in hand, guarding the ancient capital of cultivators just as they had eons ago.

As these spectral soldiers appeared, Zhou Hui felt an invisible weight settling upon his frame.

His arms holding Yuwen Xuan grew especially heavy, as though pressed down by the weight of a thousand *jun*, making it feel as if he were cradling not a tiny lamb, but a towering mountain.

Of course, "merely" this degree of pressure posed no challenge to Zhou Hui, but Lu Shulong was clearly bearing the same crushing burden.

Persisting for another several thousand li, the dragon's speed noticeably flagged.

Yet Lu Shulong uttered not a word of complaint, gritting his teeth to carry his master forward. Zhou Hui held his peace at first, but upon crossing a certain threshold, he leaped down from Lu Shulong's back.

—"Mas... ter?"-Lu Shulong panted.

—"You have reached your limit here,"-Zhou Hui explained.

—"I can still fly,"-Lu Shulong insisted.

Zhou Hui gently stroked Lu Shulong's dragon horns:

—"Up to this point, it was good training; beyond this line, it becomes pointless torment. You are still young, and you have plenty of time. I will await the day you can carry me through the entire journey."

—"Nn..."-Lu Shulong murmured, looking somewhat dissatisfied with his own performance.

—"Bootlicker,"-Yuwen Xuan whispered, using a tone inaudible to Lu Shulong but clear to Zhou Hui.

Zhou Hui cast a half-smiling glance at the little lamb in his arms and dropped it onto the paving stones:

—"You walk on your own as well."

—"!"-Yuwen Xuan's eyes went wide.

Scrambling on his little hooves, he attempted to throw a tantrum:

—"I'm so tiny! I'm way younger than Old Five! Why can't I take my time too?!"

—"Master, this sheep is too small; its hooves haven't even fully grown,"-Lu Shulong chimed in.-"It looks like it can't walk very far."

—"If it can't walk far, then it doesn't have to,"-Zhou Hui said, not forcing the issue as realization dawned upon him.-"This appears to be the rule for reaching the Thirteenth Pavilion: one must traverse it entirely under one's own power."

At the same time, Zhou Hui finally understood why he hadn't sensed any spiritual energy.

The entire city's spells were likely channeled into sustaining this Grand Avenue, operating in an overdrawn state; hence, Zhou Hui could detect no ambient spiritual qi.

This was an ancient trial for anyone seeking to enter the core of the capital!

Though Zhou Hui was confident he could reach the end even while carrying the little lamb, having deduced the rules, it was wiser not to challenge them.

After all, he had not come here to destroy this city.

Thus, master and disciples walked slowly along the Main Avenue, pressing forward.

Yet before they had gone very far, the little lamb that was Yuwen Xuan suddenly buckled at all four hooves and collapsed onto the ground.

—"Ah, it really can't walk anymore?"-Lu Shulong looked at the little lamb with pity.

Even for a spirit beast, it seemed far too tiny.

—"Yuwen Xuan?"-Zhou Hui, however, didn't believe Yuwen Xuan would lose out to Lu Shulong, calling out sternly via divine sense.

—"It's not that I won't walk! I can't do it, Master!"-Yuwen Xuan answered via divine sense.

He tried thrusting his hornless head forward, but to no avail:

—"There seems to be an invisible force blocking me! I can't move forward at all!"

—"An invisible force?"-Zhou Hui reached out and touched Yuwen Xuan's head, indeed sensing a faint ripple.

It carried a vague impression of "rejection."

Evidently, this avenue was rejecting Yuwen Xuan.

The reason was simple enough: as a primal ferocious beast, Yuwen Xuan was lucky the surrounding soldiers hadn't attacked him on sight.

—"Wait for us here,"-Zhou Hui instructed.

Yuwen Xuan whimpered in frustration, sending through divine sense:

—"Master, you have to come back for me!"-[Don't you dare abandon me!]

[You make it sound as if I've ever abandoned you... Ah, wait, I actually did! Across six lifetimes.]

With a wry smile, Zhou Hui patted the lamb's head and promised:

—"Rest assured, I will definitely return."

—"Ying..."-only then did the little lamb lower its head in relief.

—"Do sheep really bleat like that?"-Lu Shulong muttered, puzzled.

Fortunately, Lu Shulong had always lacked intrusive curiosity. He didn't dwell on the peculiar lamb and continued walking alongside his master. After traversing several tens of thousands of li, he suddenly crashed to the ground—just like Yuwen Xuan, utterly unable to advance an inch further.

Lu Shulong thrashed several times, flapping like a beached fish on dry land.

—"I told you not to push yourself,"-Zhou Hui said with a wry smile, casting a spell to alleviate physical fatigue over Lu Shulong.-"You might as well wait for me here too."

Yet even a True Dragon was rejected?

What on earth was White Jade Capital testing?

Zhou Hui wondered in bewilderment, yet he comfortably left his disciple in place.

Sensing something profound, Lu Shulong nodded:

—"...Master, you will certainly succeed."

Zhou Hui nodded and strode forward alone.

The road ahead was mind-numbingly monotonous. The surrounding scenery remained identical, there was no one to speak to, and the trek seemed to stretch on into eternity.

If this truly was a trial, it was surely the most boring trial in existence.

Yet Zhou Hui did not mind.

He was accustomed to it.

One could even say his path of cultivation had closely mirrored this very avenue—stretching endlessly without a destination in sight, suffused with despair.

Yet whether it was having Five-Element Spirit Roots, witnessing the "Heavenly Scripture" on Broken Sky Cliff, traveling the world to decipher that text, or striving for ascension, Zhou Hui had always confronted that endless road all by himself, never giving up.

Ah, wait... had he given up after all?

He had indeed given up on ascension.

Though for reasons unknown, not a single person in the world believed he had surrendered, he truly had intended to abandon it.

Zhou Hui mused in idle boredom.

—He didn't stop to question why a man who had supposedly given up on ascension was currently pressing relentlessly forward along the Main Avenue of White Jade Capital.

Thus, devoid of fatigue and without a single pause, Zhou Hui marched on.

He was merely curious—curious as to when he would be barred from advancing. If both a primal ferocious beast and a True Dragon had been turned away, then for a cultivator who had been repeatedly rejected by the Heavenly Dao and now held zero hope of ascending, being stopped in his tracks was all but a foregone conclusion.

Zhou Hui was actually eager to experience that unyielding barrier firsthand.

He was equally curious to know what kind of entity could ever reach the end.

Until... he reached the end himself.

Indeed. After traversing an untraceable expanse of distance and losing all count of time, Zhou Hui heard the celestial music swelling louder and clearer, as though heralding an honored guest. The soldiers lining both sides grew denser, arraying themselves in orderly phalanxes as if awaiting Zhou Hui's arrival. The towering pavilions ahead drew closer and closer—

At last, Zhou Hui stood beneath the Thirteenth Pavilion.

No, this was not a "pavilion," but the Grand Gate of White Jade Capital! When Lu Shulong had descended from above, he had bypassed this gate entirely, yet it now loomed directly before Zhou Hui, its colossal doors sealed shut.

The regiments lining the avenue—for they were no longer mere guards, but a vast imperial army—stood gazing upon Zhou Hui. Their eyes seemed to appraise a potential enemy, yet simultaneously anticipate their master.

Zhou Hui was in no hurry to push the gate. Instead, he stared dumbfounded at the inscription carved into the battlements.

**白玉京** — *White Jade Capital*.

These three characters were not the common script of the cultivation world, but the "True Immortal Script." Zhou Hui had encountered it long ago on Broken Sky Cliff, where he had obtained rubbings of the Heavenly Scripture and used them to decipher reality and create his own cultivation technique.

—"...True Immortal Script?"-Zhou Hui murmured.

A sudden suspicion seized his mind.

Unlike the inscriptions on Broken Sky Cliff or other examples of True Immortal Script—which inflicted agonizing migraines and spiritual trauma if stared at for too long—these three characters hung there placidly, resembling ordinary seal script.

Could it be that the "Heavenly Scripture" he had discovered back then had never been a divine scripture from the heavens at all, but rather a legacy left behind by White Jade Capital?!

With that thought in mind, Zhou Hui reached forward and pushed against the gates.

On the first attempt, he channeled no spiritual power.

An ordinary counter-force rebounded against him; the gates did not budge.

On the second attempt, Zhou Hui deliberately invoked a standard spell common to the cultivation world.

Still the gates remained shut, rebounding with far greater force. Yet this time Zhou Hui did not yield; instead, he intentionally ramped up his strength, adopting a posture as though preparing to breach the gate by brute force.

Instantly, the surrounding army twisted into ferocious expressions, leveling their spears in unison at him as if preparing to annihilate an invader.

A faint smile touched Zhou Hui's lips.

On the third attempt, he channeled the self-created cultivation method derived from the True Immortal Script, placing his palms against the gate.

The doors swung open almost instantaneously.

The colossal metal gates, weighing thousands of tons, seemed entirely weightless—swinging wide at the gentlest touch of Zhou Hui's hand.

As the gates parted, the ferocious scowls vanished from every soldier's face. Snapping to attention, they bowed deeply in ceremonial homage to Zhou Hui, and a resounding cry rose above the swelling celestial music:

> —"Welcome—"
> 
> —"Welcome, Sacred Lord—"
> 
> —"Welcome, Sacred Lord, to the Capital—"

Amidst the drawn-out, echoing salutations, the phantom of an Azure Bird descended from the towers beyond the gate. Clasped in its delicate talons was a small jade signet.

Following an intuitive premonition, Zhou Hui raised his hand, and the jade signet settled softly into his palm.

At that very moment, Zhou Hui sensed it:

This entire city was his.

* * *

—"White Jade Capital?!"

—"This is the legendary White Jade Capital?!"-Shen Qingqian stammered in complete disorientation.

Anyone discovering that the passage beneath their back mountain (X)—or rather, beneath that titanic severed head—led directly to the fabled White Jade Capital would react the same way, especially after laying eyes on this sublime city firsthand.

It took Shen Qingqian a full half-day to regain her composure.

When she finally sought out Zhou Hui—who was serenely drafting and revising a proposal across a white light screen—an expression of dread crept across her features.

—"Immortal Venerable, why did you tell me about this?"-Shen Qingqian asked.

Thick-skinned as she might be, she was no fool.

Having made her home amidst the ancient battlefield ruins of the Abyssal Ocean, Shen Qingqian had seen more than enough bloodshed over cultivation resources. In fact, the very reason she had been swept away by that spiritual energy storm and dumped into a spatial rift was because finding a rare spirit herb had triggered infighting among her companions, making them forget to take shelter in time.

Moreover, Shen Qingqian was acutely aware of White Jade Capital's unfathomable worth.

She knew full well that if word of White Jade Capital's discovery ever leaked out, the Heavenly Dao Alliance could morph into a [Demonic Syndicate](# "Literal CN: 黑道盟 (Hēidàoméng) — Underworld / Demonic Dao Alliance; pun on 天道盟 (Tiāndàoméng / Heavenly Dao Alliance)") within the span of a breath.

Thus, her initial euphoria upon beholding White Jade Capital had swiftly cooled into paralyzing terror.

She realized that even if she had known about this treasure beneath her 'back mountain' beforehand, she would have evacuated every disciple from the sect long ago, buried all memory of White Jade Capital, and pretended she had never seen a thing.

A "mere" Body Integration cultivator could never hold onto such colossal wealth.

Furthermore, putting herself in Zhou Hui's shoes, she asked herself: if she were a Tribulation Transcendence titan who discovered such a treasure beneath an affiliated sect, how would she react?

She might not even resist the surge of greed, choosing to wipe out the entire sect to silence all witnesses!

Even at his most merciful, he would shatter the spirit roots of every cultivator in the sect, erase their memories of the past, and cast them out of the cultivation world forever.

Yet Zhou Hui had "temporarily" done neither, and had even disclosed the existence of White Jade Capital to her face.

Shen Qingqian was thoroughly petrified.

She was on the verge of crying out: *Mighty Senior, I didn't see anything! Please spare me!*

—"Spare you? I'm afraid I cannot,"-Zhou Hui replied with a smile.-"Your sect sits right here. No matter how one attempts to conceal it, there is no bypassing you."

Shen Qingqian blanched, realizing she had spoken her inner thoughts aloud.

A bitter taste filled her mouth. Taking several ragged breaths, she couldn't summon the slightest will to resist, managing only to grit her teeth and plead:

—"Th-Then, Immortal Venerable, I beg you to at least spare the children in our sect below the Qi Condensation stage! They haven't learned anything, they know nothing, and they can hardly even be considered cultivators. If you cripple their spirit roots, they can still live out their lives as ignorant mortals."

[Every time Blood Demon Hall faces catastrophe, this sect leader's choice is always the same!]

Zhou Hui let out a soft sigh.

He truly felt a measure of guilt toward Blood Demon Hall. While it hadn't been his doing, Yuwen Xuan had unquestionably been the catalyst behind their tragic fates in previous timelines.

Naturally, another factor was just as he had said: White Jade Capital was so close to Blood Demon Hall that keeping them completely in the dark was impossible.

Unless he truly slaughtered every last disciple in Blood Demon Hall.

—Such a measure was far too ruthless, and entirely unnecessary.

Considering White Jade Capital's current dormant state, Blood Demon Hall was an ideal labor force; wasting them would be a tragedy. Furthermore, given Blood Demon Hall's unique body-refinement arts, Zhou Hui found them to be exceedingly useful [tool-workers](# "Literal CN: 工具人 (gōngjù rén) — slang for people used as functional instruments / utility cogs").

—"What would I want your lives for?"-Zhou Hui said.-"I merely want you to sign this cooperation agreement."

—"...I would rather die!"-Shen Qingqian declared, shutting her eyes tight.

Thinking with her kneecaps, she assumed this was an unequal treaty designed to reduce them to indentured slaves.

Shen Qingqian truly would rather perish than forfeit her freedom.

—"Read it first before making up your mind,"-Zhou Hui said in amusement, observing her pose of unyielding martyrdom.-"Even if not for yourself, think of your disciples! Are you certain every single one of them would rather die?"

At those words, Shen Qingqian reluctantly picked up the contract.

She began reading with deep reluctance, as if every character scalded her eyes. But gradually, her entire focus was pulled into the text, until her eyes widened in utter disbelief.

This contract was exceptionally fair!

Shockingly, astonishingly fair.

It was a genuine "cooperation agreement"!

Most importantly, Zhou Hui had invoked Heaven and Earth as witnesses, meaning that even as a Tribulation Transcendence powerhouse, he could not easily breach its terms unless he chose to abandon his ascension entirely.

Only then did Shen Qingqian feel the sensation of a meat pie falling straight from heaven into her lap.

Dazed and lightheaded, she stared at Zhou Hui:

—"Immortal Venerable, why are you doing this?"

—"Because Blood Demon Hall is chosen by destiny, of course,"-Zhou Hui smiled gently.

[Just like him—an unfortunate soul chosen by the Heavenly Dao to serve as a sacrificial scapegoat.]
