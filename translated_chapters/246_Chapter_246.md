# Chapter 246: Final Boss

—"Untiring... are you all right?"-Peilan asked worriedly.

He felt as though he were speaking not to an old friend, but to the sculpted statue of a god.

Yet Zhou Hui at this moment did not quite resemble a deity.

Slumped upon the elevated dais with his head bowed, his fair skin and even his white robes took on a bizarre, near-dispersing translucence. The white mist shrouding him made it seem as if in the very next instant he would sublimate from a solid entity into an intangible, vaporous existence. Even the silver hair cascading across his body looked like moonlight shimmering upon snowmelt on the verge of vanishing, as though it would shatter at the slightest touch.

Peilan had known him back when his cultivation base was still modest, and had long served as his primary physician. For the stubbornly obstinate Zhou Hui to willingly seek medical treatment, describing his condition as [critically ill beyond cure](# "Idiom: 病入膏肓 (bìngrù gāohuāng) — so sick that disease has entered the vitals between the heart and diaphragm; terminally ill") was an understatement; every single time, he presented states that thoroughly broadened Peilan's horizons. In truth, a substantial portion of Peilan's medical advancement was owed entirely to Zhou Hui.

Yet even when confronted with so many perilous predicaments, not once had Peilan felt that Zhou Hui was about to give up.

The instance closest to the present would probably be that one injury back when Untiring was still in the Nascent Soul stage, wouldn't it?

Peilan reminisced.

He remembered with absolute clarity that on that occasion, because Zhou Hui had studied taboo knowledge, his body began undergoing a melting phenomenon. The man had looked like a lit white candle, dripping downward from skin to bone. For a time, Peilan couldn't find any medical cure, barely able to use spells to stabilize Untiring's physical form.

In such a horrifying state, Zhou Hui's silver-gray eyes had remained brilliantly bright throughout, devoid of the slightest hint of dread or surrender.

Keeping his eyes wide open, he had thoroughly studied that knowledge beyond mortal endurance, mastered and integrated it, and finally discovered a method to rescue himself right before his body disintegrated completely.

Back then, Peilan had already felt that Zhou Hui would become a legendary figure in the future—provided he didn't manage to get himself killed beforehand.

Looking at it now, Zhou Hui indeed hadn't gotten himself killed.

Not only had he avoided killing himself, but he had even skipped the procedural step of ascending to the Heavenly Court to serve as an immortal official—and was about to become a god!

Yes, becoming a god.

Although Zhou Hui had inexplicably acquired that power and immediately rejected the path of commanding it to achieve deification—dispersing the power outward instead—it was... no, rather, it was precisely his act of dispersing his power that had accelerated his pace of deification!

As for the specific reason, Peilan didn't know either.

All Peilan knew was that shortly after Zhou Hui dispersed that power, something akin to "karmic feedback" descended upon Untiring's body. Furthermore, the more power Zhou Hui gave out, the more pronounced this feedback became, causing the divinity clinging to his form to grow increasingly dense.

As an "ordinary fox" who had yet to ascend, Peilan was utterly incapable of withstanding such overwhelming divinity.

First, he found himself unable to conceal his fox ears and bushy tail. Next, he began feeling that standing so close to Zhou Hui was an act of sheer sacrilege, instinctively retreating several paces with his tail tucked tightly between his legs. Finally, he couldn't even stand upright, desperately fighting the urge to prostrate himself upon the floor.

Reaching this juncture, Peilan could no longer look directly at Zhou Hui.

He dared not raise his head, only daring to steal glances through the periphery of his vision.

Yet Zhou Hui's personal condition could hardly be considered good.

Accompanying the escalation of divinity, Zhou Hui's gaze grew increasingly vacuous. Slowly losing all interest in this mortal realm, he cast his gaze toward higher, farther reaches that mortals could never perceive. His body turned equally incorporeal, blossoming with dazzling brilliance as it sublimated, as if he would transform into a divine deity in the very next breath.

The problem was, Zhou Hui had no desire to become a god!

Moreover, looking at that vacant, unfeeling gaze, Peilan felt that this was by no means the expression the Untiring Immortal Venerable he knew would ever wear—it was not an expression any living human being ought to have!

Peilan shouted, attempting to drag back Zhou Hui's faculties:

—"Untiring! Untiring! Wake up! Do you still remember what your original will was?! Don't let divine power wash away your consciousness—"

"..."

Amidst Peilan's frantic shouts, Zhou Hui's frost-white eyelashes fluttered faintly.

Yet this did not mean he had registered Peilan's words.

Zhou Hui's current state was profoundly miraculous. He felt himself—no, it wasn't "he felt"; the current Zhou Hui could hardly be said to possess individual perceptions. His thoughts and divine soul were suspended in a state of primordial chaos. In particular, the moment he shared his power outward, faint surges of power came flooding back, and carried alongside them were the thoughts, memories, prayers, and even fragments of the souls of the recipients. These chaotic thoughts washed over Zhou Hui's mind like a raging deluge, rendering coherent thought impossible.

In truth, it was fortunate that this was Zhou Hui. Had anyone else—even a True Immortal—been subjected to such an impact, their core will and divine soul would have been washed away into nothingness in an instant.

Even Zhou Hui could only maintain a single thread of clarity.

And this thread of clarity was precarious, flickering like a candle in the wind.

This was because Zhou Hui discovered that in this state, he could touch higher dimensions and broader realms of space and time, effortlessly acquiring knowledge that his present self could never reach. A vastly more colossal universe unfolded before his eyes.

*Yes, yes, cast aside the last of your desires—your humanity!*

*You have already ascended to godhood!*

*Everything pertaining to mortals is merely an encumbrance. From now on, you may be as mighty as you wish, and obtain whatever you desire.*

The chaotic consciousnesses raised a unified chorus, attempting to coax Zhou Hui into surrendering that final sliver of clarity.

Yet it was precisely because of this chorus that Zhou Hui hesitated. Even if he could not think clearly, he felt that the grand vision described by the chorus was by no means what he himself desired.

Mighty strength was indeed something Zhou Hui required, but that was merely a means, never the objective.

Then what, ultimately, was most important?

The tiny candle-flame of consciousness trembled amidst the chorus, refusing to extinguish as it sought to reclaim the dispersed true self.

It was precisely at this moment that Peilan's voice drifted over, faint and indistinct.

Yet as a "mortal fox" who was far from achieving immortality, Peilan's voice was almost imperceptible within the overwhelming chorus, resembling the minuscule squeak of a tiny ant beneath the trampling feet of an 8:00 AM rush-hour commuter crowd.

(Hm? What kind of metaphor is that?)

Zhou Hui's divine sense stirred faintly.

Regardless of what that "chorus" was, one thing was entirely accurate: Zhou Hui was on the verge of deification. Before a supreme deity, the voices of all living beings were equal. Whether mortal or True Immortal, human or spirit beast, so long as the deity was willing to listen, they could always be heard.

Peilan's voice drifted in faintly, as though traveling from an unfathomable distance.

Peilan was desperately trying to pull him back using the matters Zhou Hui cared about most:

—"...Don't forget you still haven't finished your cultivation research topics!"

The chorus countered:

*No longer needed, no longer needed.*

*Upon ascending to godhood, all knowledge is at your fingertips. You will no longer require... You may freely explore realms you could never touch before.*

—"...Your sect still has a new pedagogical curriculum to finish!"

*No need for concern, no need for concern.*

*Upon ascending to godhood, the rules you draft shall become the Heavenly Dao. The engineering feats you wish to implement shall be accomplished in a single stroke. There will no longer be anything you cannot achieve, nor any wish you cannot fulfill.*

—"...Your disciples..."

*No need to worry, no need to worry.*

*Upon ascending to godhood, your disciples shall become your Chosen. You can have them grow precisely to your heart's desire. If you wish them to be mighty, they will be more formidable than primordial behemoths nurtured by heaven and earth; if you wish them to be docile, they will be tamer than long-domesticated lambs...*

Wait... wait!

What kind of thing is a behemoth-like lamb?!

My Eldest Disciple?!

Such an intensely concrete image struck like an outright scare, instantly snapping Zhou Hui's scattering divine sense back into sharp clarity:

—"...No!"-Zhou Hui blurted out.

Peilan was startled by Zhou Hui's sudden exclamation.

[Did he recover?]

[But what does 'No' mean?]

[Is becoming a god a 'no', or not becoming a god a 'no'?]

Peilan wondered in a mixture of pleasant surprise and dread. Yet under the crushing divine pressure, he remained unable to raise his head, still only daring to steal glances at the other's state through the corner of his eye.

Unfortunately, Zhou Hui hardly looked as though he had recovered.

His expression remained hazy, his gaze still lacking focus. He merely lifted his head slightly, staring vacantly in a certain direction—and fortunately so, for Peilan at this moment had zero spare strength to endure the direct gaze of a deity.

In truth, just from Zhou Hui shifting slightly, Peilan felt the pressure bearing down upon his back redouble.

Fortunately, Zhou Hui paid little heed to Peilan; he merely moved his fingers.

Accompanying Zhou Hui's gesture, the surrounding sea of clouds surged, coalescing into several colossal cloud mirrors. Images shone through the "mirrors"—and upon closer inspection, they displayed the figures of Zhou Hui's direct disciples.

Seeing this, Peilan breathed a sigh of relief.

—"So you really couldn't let them go after all,"-Peilan said.-"Since that's the case, what's the point of watching them like this? Why not hurry back..."

*BOOM!*

Peilan was about to say more when a deafening crash of thunder cut him off.

Startled, Peilan whipped his head around in panic, only to discover that the thunderclap had echoed from one of the cloud mirrors.

Within that reflection, two young men were locked in a vicious brawl amidst crackling golden lightning and roiling dark clouds. One possessed heavenly, ethereal beauty yet was suffused with overflowing demonic qi; the other looked upright and honest yet exuded sinister, chilling ghost qi. They were none other than the Untiring Immortal Venerable's most outstanding Second Disciple and most hardworking Fourth Disciple.

Peilan held the best impression of these two disciples of Zhou Hui, and watching them fight tooth and nail now made his vision turn black.

—"What on earth happened? Why are they fighting? And their cultivation bases..."

[These two kids' cultivations shouldn't be this high, right?]

[They must be using the power Untiring dispersed earlier?]

[...No! Your Master gave you power, and this is how you use it?!]

Peilan stared at the cloud mirror in disbelief, engulfed by a long-lost sense of powerlessness.

This powerlessness was not the inability of a physician to cure a patient, nor was it the despair of a patient abandoning their own treatment. Rather, it was the utter helplessness of knowing the patient could unmistakably be saved, yet the patient's unfilial descendants were brawling right outside the intensive care ward doors!

[What are Untiring's kids brawling over? Do you think your Master's ascension isn't thorough enough?!]

Peilan cursed inwardly.

However, no matter how many complaints Peilan harbored inside, he could not discuss this issue directly with the "patient." Not only could he not discuss it, but he also had to divert the "patient's" attention to prevent him from truly being enraged to death:

—"These two naughty brats... they must be so worried about you that they had a little disagreement,"-Peilan rambled, completely unaware that he had somehow stumbled onto a fraction of the truth.-"Never mind them for now, your other disciples must be deeply worried too..."

Saying so, Peilan pointed toward another cloud mirror, only for his finger to begin trembling violently.

Upon the adjacent mirror, a dashing, handsome young man was unreservedly gorging and guzzling like a glutton. Wine dribbled from the corners of his mouth, and like any drunken monster, his body was uncontrollably reverting to its true form.

Looking at this wretched spectacle, never mind worrying about his Master—he was lucky if he even remembered who his Master was!

And where on earth was he drinking?! Why did the scenery behind him look suspiciously similar to this sacrificial altar, and why did the drunken guests keeping him company, though looking quite battered, radiate unmistakable celestial air...?

While Peilan was swimming in bewilderment, a voice drifted from yet another cloud mirror:

[—"When did Eldest Senior Brother arrive in the Heavenly Court?"](# "Speaker: Mo Bei")

Within that cloud mirror, a young maiden and a dragon were darting haphazardly through what looked like an immortal celestial garden. Peilan could see clearly that not a single blade of grass or branch in that garden could ever grow in the mortal realm; it was undeniably the "Heavenly Court" the maiden spoke of.

[Why on earth are you two in the Heavenly Court?!]

[Seeing your Master about to ascend to godhood, are you preparing to storm the Heavenly Court?!]

Peilan screamed inwardly.

He dared not dwell on it, nor dared he look further.

Thank goodness these were Untiring's disciples. If they were his own, whether ascending to godhood or descending to hell, anywhere was fine so long as he didn't have to face the apocalyptic mess created by this pack of disciples!

However, since these were someone else's disciples, it was a different matter. A guardian had to step in and discipline these [troublemaking brats](# "Slang: 熊孩子 (xióngháizi) — unruly, mischievous kids"), didn't they?

Peilan secretly hardened his resolve.

If his prior attempts to halt Zhou Hui had stemmed from medical ethics and concern for an old friend, from this moment onward, Peilan was acting entirely out of consideration for himself and the future of the world he inhabited.

Pretending not to see the two cloud mirrors concerning the Heavenly Court, Peilan looked toward the remaining mirror. The scene within finally allowed him to breathe a sigh of relief.

—"Hey, this disciple of yours isn't bad,"-Peilan noted with relief.-"Managing the Immortal Arts Assembly, single-handedly maintaining the sect, and even helping that Jian clan kid contact Sky City..."

—"Ah-Ran,"-Zhou Hui finally spoke.

Evidently, the outrageous stunts pulled by this pack of disciples had delivered a sufficiently massive shock to Zhou Hui, dragging him forcibly back down into the human realm.

Unlike Peilan, who could only see the visuals within the cloud mirrors, these mirrors were merely media connecting Zhou Hui to his disciples. Thus, through the mirrors, Zhou Hui could "see" the entire sequence of events that had led these disciples to devolve into their current chaotic states.

Among them, the one that left him most [at a loss whether to laugh or cry](# "Literal CN: 哭笑不得 (kūxiào-bùdé) — finding something simultaneously hilarious and helpless") was actually Ran Deng.

After learning what Ran Deng had done, Zhou Hui finally understood where the sudden "noise" and faith power flooding his divine soul had originated. As it turned out, Ran Deng's action of gathering the power of the participants in the Immortal Arts Assembly had inadvertently generated a positive cycle of "bestowal, faith, and feedback," effortlessly satisfying the deification criteria that certain True Immortals could only dream of achieving.

Naturally, Ran Deng could not be blamed for this.

Setting aside the fact that Ran Deng knew nothing of Zhou Hui's condition, purely regarding the matter itself, the responsibility lay squarely with Zhou Hui.

Zhou Hui had grown overconfident.

Under circumstances where he completely failed to understand the mechanisms of "godhood," upon acquiring divine power, he had conducted zero analysis or research, established zero restrictions, and simply blasted it outward through the Mountain-Protecting Grand Array. While it had indeed aided his disciples, it was undeniably far too reckless. That it had not triggered a worse catastrophe was already a fortunate outcome.

Yet this overconfidence was not Zhou Hui's innate temperament.

It seemed that the moment he acquired divine power, he had been subjected to a peculiar influence, such that the aspect of confidence regarding his ability to master power had been deliberately magnified.

Zhou Hui reflected upon himself.

As his human consciousness overpowered the "chorus," he instantly perceived his own abnormality.

—In truth, in his "divine" state, He could also perceive it, but the "god" simply did not care.

In fact, even now, when Zhou Hui's human desires had begun to recover, that lingering divinity was still interfering with his thoughts.

From this perspective, the timing this time had merely been slightly unfortunate. Otherwise, Zhou Hui's decision to immediately transfer the divine power outward upon receiving it was actually the correct choice. Otherwise, under subtle, imperceptible influences that even Zhou Hui could not detect, "he" would have transformed into "He."

Once more, Ran Deng could not be blamed for this.

—"Ah-Ran is a good child,"-Zhou Hui said slowly, cleansing the extraneous clamor within his mind.-"She did wonderfully, whether dealing with the Heavenly Court or reassuring the people of the Immortal Arts Assembly. With her at the Heavenly Spirit Sect, I can rest easy."

Only, because Zhou Hui's consciousness had not fully recovered, the words he voiced were merely a form of muttering aloud while wrestling against his divinity, without giving much thought to the precise implications of his phrasing. As a result, hearing this from the side made Peilan's tail bristle in shock.

[What does 'with her around, I can rest easy' mean?!]

[You aren't really planning to ascend to godhood with peace of mind, are you?!]

Peilan warned vigilantly:

—"No matter how good she is, she still belongs to the Ran Clan! You can't just refuse to return her for the sake of the Heavenly Spirit Sect—the Ran Clan still needs her! Speaking of which, her father hasn't even been retrieved yet! Tsk, those old bastards like Jiuzhai take forever to find a person, who knows where they died!"

His final remarks were undeniably laced with intense personal grievance, clearly displeased that the others were taking so long, leaving him to face the deified Untiring all by his lonesome fox self.

This time, Zhou Hui paid little heed to Peilan's complaints.

He was not merely watching Ran Deng; he was observing his other disciples as well.

Yet Zhou Hui did not harbor any of the disappointment toward his disciples that Peilan imagined. On the contrary, he actually felt quite satisfied, and even a bit proud—with the sole exception of Yuwen Xuan!

After all, having weathered several cycles of reincarnation, he had seen these disciples at their absolute worst. Compared to previous lifetimes, the performance of all his disciples this time was commendable across the board: Qianxing tied down the celestial officials of the Heavenly Court, Ah-Ran presided over sect affairs, Guan Ye assisted his senior martial brother and sister while uprightly pointing out his Second Senior Brother's flaw of disregarding human life, Mo Bei noticed blind spots the other siblings had overlooked, Mo Xi handled all back-end data calculations from behind the scenes, and even Shulong served as a mode of transportation—again, with the sole exception of Yuwen Xuan!!

Everyone was a good child, achieving their utmost in their respective fields of expertise—for the third time, Yuwen Xuan excluded!!!

Considering how hard everyone had worked, as for how they had lured the celestial soldiers and celestial generals down, and what they had done behind his back... well, depending on the circumstances, those who needed to write self-criticisms would write self-criticisms, those who needed remedial lessons would take remedial lessons, and those who needed to be hung from trees would be hung from trees. This time, aside from Yuwen Xuan—who probably didn't need to ever come down from the tree in this lifetime—a few more would likely be hung alongside him...

Zhou Hui reasoned calmly.

As he contemplated these matters, he could clearly sense his divinity receding, and the parts belonging to him as a human were returning bit by bit. Indeed, no deity would ever spend their idle time pondering how to discipline disciples.

Noticing this, Zhou Hui felt a tinge of worry:

[Am I being too strict with the disciples?]

Just as Zhou Hui was considering whether to grant a moderate reduction in sentence, he abruptly sensed something deeply ominous. Frowning slightly, he waved his sleeve, and yet another cloud mirror rose into the air.

By this point, Peilan was no longer suppressed by Zhou Hui's divine might.

He had already returned to his usual state when facing Zhou Hui, yet before he could even register this fact, the newly manifested cloud mirror instantly made him tense up once more.

—"Wh-What now?!"-Peilan cried in terror.-"Weren't all your disciples already... huh? How did two more pop up?! And why these two?!"

He was on the verge of developing post-traumatic stress disorder over Untiring's disciples.

Especially the two biggest troublemakers!

That's right—Untiring's Eldest and Second Disciples! One had run off to the Heavenly Court to drink, while the other was battling celestial soldiers and generals. Why didn't they just join forces, storm the Heavenly Court, set up a banquet, and declare themselves Celestial Emperors while they were at it?!

Peilan had originally harbored such sarcastic thoughts in his heart, but now he felt as though this very scene was about to come true.

Within the newly risen cloud mirror was precisely the scene in the Immortal-Demon Realm where Yuwen Xuan and Qu Qianxing were hunting down the remnant souls of Tian Yan and Tian Shou!

Although Peilan didn't understand the full cause and effect, even an outsider could tell that Yuwen Xuan and Qu Qianxing here were not acting on an impromptu whim; they had clearly planned and prepared for this for a very long time.

But why were they there? How had they begun planning? How long had they been scheming? And how did they know Tian Yan and Tian Shou would flee to that location? What purpose did they have in scheming against celestial officials of the Heavenly Court like this? Was the previous incident where the Heavenly Court assaulted the demon race only to have the battlefield displaced into minor worlds also part of their plan? What on earth did they want to do?!

An endless cascade of questions flooded Peilan's brain.

Peilan refused to think. Peilan even wanted to reject reality altogether:

—"Wait, aren't those two disciples of yours drinking and fighting? How could another two pop up? Couldn't they be monsters or impostors in disguise?"

—"Unfortunately, they are not."

Even someone as protective as Zhou Hui felt a genuine headache coming on upon seeing the actions of these two disciples—or more accurately, upon "perceiving" what they had done.

—"They are the joint product of the previous reincarnation... of the preceding cycles,"-Zhou Hui sighed.

—"As you know, to undo unwanted outcomes, I rewound time several times. But the closer someone was to me, and the greater their destiny shifted, the more they remembered the developments of each cycle."

—"From the third cycle onward, because of my miscalculation, their memories were restored. And starting from that cycle, this pack of 'clever little rascals' each found ways to preserve their memories, merely putting on an act in front of me."

Speaking to this point, Zhou Hui couldn't help but let out a cold chuckle.

Watching him, Peilan felt a chill run down the back of his neck. He realized that Zhou Hui had fully returned to being human, but looking at him like this, he was genuinely angry—which was far more terrifying than him becoming a god.

Using his divine power to inspect his disciples' actions across past cycles, Zhou Hui continued:

—"By the previous cycle, because I forced ascension, it caused spatial tearing, creating a split reincarnation cycle. I myself was excluded from that cycle. Laughably, because my cultivation was insufficient, I failed to notice, leaving that cycle devoid of 'me'—to the point where a divine projection of myself manifested..."

—"W-Wait a moment!"-Peilan interrupted, his head spinning.-"What did you say you did just now? Spatial tearing? Divine projection?"

"..."

Zhou Hui guiltily averted his gaze.

Yes, there were no "previous two cycles"—there was only the "previous cycle."

The truth of the matter was that in his fifth lifetime, Zhou Hui had forcibly attempted ascension. However, because the rules governing both the upper and lower realms had rigidly locked the normal ascent path of Langhuan, unless Zhou Hui became a god—and specifically a god possessing authority over rules—he could not forcibly alter those laws. Yet at the time, he did not possess such authority. The colossal power required to breach space nonetheless existed, resulting in that power converting into cataclysmic heavenly lightning that plummeted downward. Instead of shattering the barrier, the genuine world was blasted away from its original spatial coordinates; only Zhou Hui and a few fragments remained at the original coordinates.

In any world where one's disciples were absent and familiar friends were gone, anyone would have realized that the world they occupied was merely a fragment, wouldn't they? But Zhou Hui did not think so; he trusted mathematics, and he trusted spatial coordinates. Miraculously, Lu Shulong, who possessed higher-dimensional vision, did not think so either.

Lu Shulong believed that only the world where Master resided was the true world. And what was truly terrifying about his Master was that Zhou Hui, firmly believing he was merely "patching the world," forcibly dragged the true world back to its original coordinates, artificially wedging an entire extra cycle of reincarnation into existence!

Yet while Zhou Hui was "patching the world," the Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples of the "true sixth cycle" naturally had no Master.

—"...Where there is an absence, something will fill it,"-Zhou Hui said slowly.

Skipping past the parts that were unimportant (and profoundly embarrassing to him), he continued his explanation:

—"Inopportunely, I nearly ascended to godhood moments ago. Although it was merely 'nearly,' the immense might of a deity is sufficient to cast a projection across a considerable span of time and space. And it was precisely that 'divine projection' which filled the vacancy of the cycle where I was absent."

In other words, the "Master" in that worst of all reincarnation cycles was, strictly speaking, not an outright counterfeit.

He was a projection of the Zhou Hui from just a few moments ago.

No wonder that after Zhou Hui realized the other party might be an avatar of himself, he had searched his memories over and over again, yet could never recall possessing an avatar so utterly devoid of humanity that it would emerge to torment his own disciples.

As it turned out, it resided not in the "past," but in the "future"!

Peilan, however, was oblivious to Zhou Hui's inner mortification. Having finally pieced together what Zhou Hui was explaining, he grew even more bewildered:

—"Since they're all you, what are your two disciples rebelling over?"

—"Since it was all me, why did you try to 'wake' me just now?"-Zhou Hui countered with a wry smile.

—"Wasn't that because you yourself..."-Peilan mumbled. Yet recalling that emotionless, dispassionate "deity," he couldn't suppress a shiver.-"True enough. You already look like an immortal detached from the mortal dust, and if you were to become a god on top of that... no, you hadn't even fully become a god, yet the few scraps of humanity you had left were gone. It's only natural that your disciples wanted to rebel."

—"It depends on who they want to rebel against,"-Zhou Hui said, frowning.

Looking at his Second Disciple within the cloud mirror, who had completely devolved into a Demon Sovereign, Zhou Hui felt a dull ache throbbing in his temples:

—"['If the child is uneducated, it is the father’s fault; if teaching is not strict, it is the teacher’s negligence,'](# "Allusion: 三字经 (Three Character Classic) — 'To feed without teaching is the father's fault; to teach without rigor is the teacher's laziness'")-I have indeed failed them. If they wanted to rebel against me, even killing and stewing me would be understandable."

[...No, that is NOT understandable at all! Is that how you raise your kids?!]

Peilan stared at Zhou Hui with eyes wide as saucers.

Zhou Hui continued:

—"The problem is, these children dumped all the blame for the repeated cycles and the 'counterfeit Master' onto the Heavenly Court. Especially Qianxing—he's inherently stubborn, and those grievances before he began cultivating were also intertwined with the Heavenly Court's covert manipulation. Now, with old and new grievances combined, he didn't even hesitate to cleave his own soul, resolved to fight the Heavenly Court to the bitter end."

—"...And so?"

Hearing this, Peilan did not join Zhou Hui in reflecting upon pedagogical failures—though Peilan felt Zhou Hui should have engaged in self-reflection long ago.

Instead, Peilan asked with a bizarre expression:

—"When all is said and done, whether it's the cycles, nearly ascending to godhood, or the divine projection causing past chaos, all of this seems to be your own problem. At worst, it's just bad luck. Is there really no trace of a conspiracy or someone else pulling the strings?"

Peilan asked this because he naturally felt something was amiss.

After all, listening to Zhou Hui's account, aside from Tian Yan coveting Langhuan and orchestrating the "Child of Destiny" plot, the Heavenly Court seemed to have done little else; everything that befell the Heavenly Spirit Sect appeared to be Zhou Hui's own doing.

However, so many "coincidences" and instances of "bringing it upon oneself" sounded in and of themselves like a cog in the wheel of Heavenly Destiny.

And resisting Heavenly Destiny and the Heavenly Dao was precisely the essence of cultivation. Even though Peilan had not recovered memories of past lifetimes, merely listening from the side, he felt something was distinctly off.

Peilan looked at Zhou Hui suspiciously:

—"Do you truly believe there was zero external interference?"

Zhou Hui let out a cold scoff.

—"How could there be no external interference?"-Zhou Hui said.-"Wouldn't you agree, Your Majesty the Celestial Emperor?"

The Celestial Emperor?!!

Hearing Zhou Hui say this—especially that final form of address—even the root of Peilan's tail shot straight up in terror!

He looked nervously in every direction, even glancing down beneath his paws, but found no third "person" present.

The other party was clearly not here. Yet in the very next instant, Zhou Hui extended his hand and made a "grasping" gesture. A figure clad in opulent imperial robes and a celestial imperial crown was abruptly dragged straight out of "thin air," tumbling clumsily onto the ground!

*This* was the "Celestial Emperor"?

Peilan instinctively shrank his body back, yet couldn't help scrutinizing the newcomer with excitement.

How should one describe him? Disappointing.

Although this "Celestial Emperor's" appearance could hardly be deemed ugly—in fact, he possessed the fairly handsome and dignified looks of a middle-aged man—and the attire adorning his figure was extraordinarily lavish and noble, had he been surrounded by celestial soldiers, heavenly generals, civil ministers, and military commanders, or seated upon a resplendent palace throne, he would undeniably have looked imposing and majestic, wouldn't he?

Unfortunately, having been abruptly plucked over by Zhou Hui and casually tossed to the floor, not only was his posture profoundly undignified, but his luxurious robes were rumpled in disarray. More importantly, never expecting to be captured so effortlessly, his face was plastered with sheer panic and terror, and he even instinctively scrambled backward several paces on all fours. Looking like this, he could hardly be considered dignified in the slightest.

Thus, never mind Peilan, who was merely a wild fox harboring zero expectations toward the Heavenly Court—even someone who genuinely held reverence and longing for the Heavenly Court would likely have felt their enthusiasm completely cooled upon witnessing such a Celestial Emperor!

Zhou Hui, however, harbored no particular sentiments regarding the Celestial Emperor.

Having reincarnated so many times, he had long ceased to care about ascension, let alone the Heavenly Court. But not caring did not equate to letting debts slide—especially when confronting the ultimate culprit behind this tangled knot of chaos.

—"You have been watching all along, and waiting all along, haven't you?"-Zhou Hui asked coldly.

It was rare for his throat to produce such a mocking and scathing tone. Combined with his perpetually expressionless countenance and icy gaze, facing him felt akin to standing completely naked upon a snowfield on a moonlit night. Beautiful though it was, one could safely assume no one would ever have the leisure to admire the moonlit snow, praying only that they would not dissolve directly beneath that chilling gaze in the very next heartbeat.

Scrutinizing the entity said to govern the three thousand worlds with such eyes, Zhou Hui asked bluntly:

—"For things to go so 'smoothly' for me this time, not only directly seeing the 'Genesis Machine' but nearly ascending to godhood—this was all Your Majesty the Celestial Emperor’s 'grace,' was it not?"

—"...N-No need to be so polite."

Pretending not to understand Zhou Hui's sarcasm, the "Celestial Emperor" forced a brazen, dry laugh.

This reaction was quite interesting; it hardly resembled someone who had reigned as an immortal emperor for countless millennia.

Yet while he could pretend not to understand, he could not truly fail to comprehend.

This "Celestial Emperor" understood with absolute clarity that for the other party to "invite" him from Xuanpu so effortlessly without alerting a single soul, squashing him to death would be no different from crushing a bug. It seemed the nightmare that his "Heavenly Destiny was at its end" was destined to be fulfilled through this very man.

Thinking of this, the "Celestial Emperor's" legs turned somewhat watery.

Small wonder he reacted like this. Peilan's intuition was spot on: this incumbent Celestial Emperor was truly nothing extraordinary. In terms of both seniority and innate aptitude, he was exceedingly "ordinary."

After all, the Heavenly Court had existed for tens of millions of years—calculated according to the temporal flow of the Celestial Realm, no less. The very first Celestial Emperor had long since ascended to godhood, and all subsequent emperors had ascended via hereditary succession. Aside from their bloodline, they possessed no remarkable qualities whatsoever. In fact, due to their supreme status, they didn't even need to descend to the mortal realm to undergo tribulations. Even if they did descend, they merely followed Tian Yan's pre-scripted "Child of Destiny" power-fantasy storylines, acquiring nothing except an even more conceited and frivolous temperament. From this perspective, they were inferior even to ancient veterans like Tian Yan and Tian Shou, possessing nothing more than an imperial title.

Thus, facing imminent death, the mighty "Celestial Emperor" could only feebly defend himself:

—"The Heavenly Court has always recruited worthy talents and broadly opened channels for ascension. If someone is fated to ascend to godhood, we would never obstruct them,"-the "Celestial Emperor" stammered.-"The three thousand worlds are too vast and numerous; the Heavenly Court would be only too eager to have a few more deities looking after our mortal subjects."

Setting aside his desire to deflect the blame back onto Zhou Hui, these words were technically the truth.

However, in Zhou Hui's eyes, from the perspective of the Heavenly Court, sending someone to ascend to godhood versus sending them to death was roughly the difference between being [sent to the Western Paradise](# "Literal CN: 上西天 (shàng xītiān) — passing away / ascending to the Western Heaven; used euphemistically for death or deification, contrasted with 下地狱 (descending to hell)") and being cast into hell. Among those, powerful cultivators cast into hell were far more troublesome for the Heavenly Court; there was no guaranteeing some wouldn't wreak havoc in the underworld, rewrite the life-and-death ledgers, and crown themselves kings. But "sending them to the Western Paradise" was far more thorough and convenient. Unlike ghosts, whose worldly obsessions only intensified in death, deification required relinquishing all earthly attachments—including the will to resist the Heavenly Court.

For a powerhouse like Zhou Hui, fast-tracking him into deification was the simplest, most trouble-free solution.

The Heavenly Court had clearly pulled this maneuver more than once.

Zhou Hui's sneer deepened:

—"For instance, having them guard the gate for the Heavenly Court once they become gods?"

He recalled the first "god" he had ever slain; back then, he had felt the entity was far too weak, and it had likely been fast-tracked in this exact manner.

—"They... they are not like you,"-the "Celestial Emperor" mumbled dryly, his evasive gaze unable to conceal the terror deep within his eyes.

This was also the truth.

What comparison could there possibly be between a minor gatekeeper god and an existence like Zhou Hui, who, before even completing deification, could cast a divine projection across time with mere shadows?

What was most terrifying was that at the very final threshold, Zhou Hui had actually pulled his foot back on his own!

At least in the "Celestial Emperor's" memory, no one had ever accomplished such a feat—not even the foundational First Celestial Emperor. What was more, through the catalyst of nearly achieving deification, Zhou Hui's mastery over divine power had visibly elevated. Even without ascending to godhood, he could now command that terrifying power!

Perceiving this, the "Celestial Emperor" felt profound regret.

He also realized he truly shouldn't have allowed Zhou Hui to make contact with the "Primordial God"—which was the title and attitude the higher echelons of the Heavenly Court held toward that colossal machine.

—However! Zhou Hui was simply too strong! Even his disciple could effortlessly slay Tian Yan! Even utilizing the divine authority of the Celestial Emperor, casually conferring a godhead upon Zhou Hui to make him guard a gate was utterly impossible; he could only be driven to do it himself!

Who could have anticipated that this Zhou Hui would be so unappreciative? Never mind treating the "Primordial God" merely as an academic research subject, he actively avoided deification like the plague!

Fortunately, the man had "consumed" the celestial feast offerings.

Although it was the Taotie he had raised committing high treason against the Heavenly Court, that still technically counted as indirectly partaking in the Heavenly Court's emoluments, falling within the purview of the Celestial Emperor's divine authority. Even if he could not forcibly confer godhood, giving him a gentle shove was entirely feasible.

For instance, the chaotic clamor that had yet to cease in Zhou Hui's mind was precisely the interference the "Celestial Emperor" had orchestrated.

Yet even he had no desire to see Zhou Hui achieve complete deification now. When the man was terrifyingly uncontrollable as a mere human, the "Celestial Emperor" could hardly fathom what kind of nightmare a deified Zhou Hui would become.

For the "Celestial Emperor," the optimal scenario was for Zhou Hui's deification to fail, leaving him severely wounded and stripped of a portion of his power—he didn't even dare fantasize about Zhou Hui outright dying—reducing him to an entity that could be restrained by imperial authority.

After all, when one's life was on the line, even [grasshoppers in late autumn](# "Idiom: 秋后的蚂蚱 (qiūhòu de màzha) — grasshoppers nearing winter; doomed entities making a futile last struggle") would struggle for survival, let alone a Celestial Emperor!

The "Celestial Emperor" reasoned that since this madman had plucked him here without killing him on the spot, he clearly still harbored reservations toward the Heavenly Court. That made sense; slaying the Celestial Emperor would offend not merely the Heavenly Court, but the three thousand worlds—especially the numerous upper-tier immortal realms intimately tied to the Heavenly Court. Even if Zhou Hui were willing to become a god, facing the hatred of so many entities would likely cause him to degenerate into an evil god in the very next instant. The "Celestial Emperor" felt there was still room for negotiation.

Thus, the "Celestial Emperor" probed cautiously:

—"Do you not wish to ascend to godhood? That was indeed presumptuous of me... however, if you wish, you can revoke the godhead..."

"Pfft."

This time, the unceremonious scoff came from Peilan.

He hadn't thought much of this "Celestial Emperor" from the very first glance, and now he was even more certain: this person was truly not the sharpest needle in the cushion. To still be playing petty tricks at a time like this—did he honestly believe that after Untiring had personally touched the core machinery, Untiring still required his assistance for anything?

Zhou Hui likewise felt that conversing with this entity was a complete waste of time.

In truth, he hadn't "invited" this person here to demand an explanation. His earlier words had merely been venting displaced anger. Now, witnessing this "Majesty's" spineless demeanor, Zhou Hui felt that venting anger upon a mere cooking ingredient was somewhat unbecoming.

—"You misunderstand,"-Zhou Hui stated plainly.-"I summoned you here because I was worried my foolish disciple would get an upset stomach from overeating."

"?"

The "Celestial Emperor's" eyes went wide.

Zhou Hui offered no direct explanation, merely enlarging the cloud mirror displaying the two figures within the palace of the Immortal-Demon Realm.

Over there, the true Demon Sovereign, Qu Qianxing, had already gathered the blood-jade amber sealing the remnant souls of Tian Yan and Tian Shou into his palm. Yuwen Xuan—or rather, "Tunhuo(?)"—merrily bounced over to him.

—"My, they were caught so easily. How utterly useless,"-he remarked, his eyes rolling greedily.-"Are they really the most formidable civil official and martial general of the Heavenly Court? Couldn't there be a mistake? Give me a lick to taste if they're real!"

Saying so, he opened his maw and swallowed the blood-jade amber whole!

Had Qu Qianxing moved even a fraction slower, it looked as though the Taotie would have chomped off his entire hand alongside it.

Seeing this scene, the "Celestial Emperor" froze for a moment, assuming he understood:

—"Are you referring to Tian Yan and Tian Shou? Their greed was excessive; this is their deserved retribution. I bear no grudge."

This was genuine sincerity. The "Celestial Emperor" had never held a high opinion of these two "founding elders," yet had never dared truly cross them. Seeing them perish so thoroughly brought a secret sense of relief to his heart.

At the same time, his dread toward Zhou Hui deepened.

After all, those two had fallen entirely to Zhou Hui's disciples—disciples whose cultivation bases were modest at best—while Zhou Hui himself had not moved a single finger.

However, the "Celestial Emperor" was completely overthinking it. Just as Peilan had previously concluded, when it came to sheer power, it was far from his turn to feel wary of Zhou Hui.

Zhou Hui said nothing more to him, continuing to watch the cloud mirror.

Within the mirror, Qu Qianxing frowned:

—"Don't eat it all! You haven't forgotten what we're going to do, have you?!"

—"Relax! For the grand feast I planned for so long, how could I abandon it over a mere appetizer!"-Yuwen Xuan replied, crunching down on the blood-jade amber with regretful reluctance.

Fortunately, just as he said, when it came to choosing between an "appetizer" and the "grand feast," he still knew how to prioritize.

After taking one last reluctant lick of the amber, a plume of gray mist billowed from "Tunhuo's" facial orifices, coalescing in midair into a chubby, round gray lamb that tumbled onto the floor.

Accompanying the departure of the gray mist lamb, the face and body of "Tunhuo"—which had looked identical to Yuwen Xuan—began to warp. If Tian Yan still possessed consciousness, he would have realized that the form "Tunhuo" was mutating into now was what the Sovereign of the Immortal-Demon Realm was originally supposed to look like...?

No, calling it his original appearance was a stretch.

Because that Tunhuo had originally been fairly presentable—otherwise, he could never have seduced so many young maidens. Yet at present, he had visibly been gnawed and licked by some ferocious beast. Chunks of flesh were missing across his face and body, and even his half-devoured internal organs hung exposed to the air.

With such a mangled, incomplete appearance, had he not been possessed by Taotie, he couldn't have even walked to this chamber on his own.

Yet what was most horrifying was that despite being chewed into such a wretched state, *he was still alive*!

Gazing upon the lamb formed of gray mist, he squeezed out a slurred, agonized plea:

—"...P-Please... kill me..."

—"How could I? Didn't you want to survive at the very beginning? I agreed! I never break my word,"-the gray lamb said with a beaming smile.-"Look, your lover is waiting right beside you! You swore to be together with her forever, didn't you? And... your child."

—"NO!"

The mutilated humanoid shrieked in terror as if hearing the most dreadful words imaginable, struggling with what little remained of his battered body to flee. Yet from his maw, the blood-jade amber that Taotie had swallowed—the one sealing the remnant souls of Tian Yan and Tian Shou—began to sprout outward.

Like defiled blood spilling from Tunhuo's mouth, it parted like a titanic, ravenous maw, swallowing Tunhuo whole—

Amidst the crimson luminescence, the Demon Sovereign asked:

—"Can you truly eat all of it? The Heavenly Court, the Celestial Emperor... everything."

—"Haha, Old Second, you can doubt anything, but you must never doubt my appetite~~"
