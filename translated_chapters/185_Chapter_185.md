# Chapter 185: If You're Going to Use Someone, at Least Tell Them First

Whether it was [graveyard grass](# "Literal CN: 坟头草 (féntóu cǎo) — grass growing on a grave; dark slang for the dead") or dry straw for decoration, what Snow Glow Grass was in essence did not matter.

What mattered was what lay buried inside the "grave."

While Mo Bei and "Mo Xi" engaged in their routine bickering, Zhou Hui released his divine sense to "gaze" upon this sea of grass.

To be frank, this grassy sea was not nearly as terrifying as "Mo Xi" made it out to be.

True, the sea of grass was indeed the resting place where immortal remains were buried, and Zhou Hui did not know what had transpired back then either. Without question, however, the departed souls laid to rest here had possessed immense resolve and determination regarding the "fall from the sky" and the "plugging of the demonic eye." Consequently, the resentment and hatred palpable here were exceedingly faint; under ordinary circumstances, they could not even coalesce into ghosts.

Yet there were simply too many immortal remains, and the residual power too overwhelming. Moreover, no matter how resolved one might be to make the ultimate sacrifice, facing death inevitably brought regret and fear. Beneath the influence of the overflowing spiritual energy, these tangled, negative emotions had ultimately coalesced into a colossal yin spirit.

Naturally, a yin spirit formed for such reasons was "fundamentally" harmless.

Due to the nature of a yin spirit, it was bound to take negative emotions as its foundation and drag nearby living beings into the same emotional mire. But taken as a whole, because there was no grudge or malice, as long as sentient beings did not dwell here, they would not be affected by those negative emotions.

This was also evident from the yin spirit's "outward appearance."

The yin spirits here did not take the usual form of malevolent specters or ominous clouds, but existed in the shape of clouds. Sensing the approach of living beings—namely Zhou Hui and his entourage—they had retreated far into the distance to avoid corroding them, condensing above the horizon of the grassy sea into towering forms resembling massive cumulonimbus clouds. And due to their frigid nature, they looked like vast expanses of soft rime frozen out of the cloud deck.

It was Zhou Hui's first time beholding such a magnificent and beautiful yin spirit.

Regrettably, Zhou Hui had not come merely for sightseeing, nor had he come to pay respects to the parents of his two youngest disciples. He had an inescapable reason for coming here, an object he absolutely had to find—

—"Strange,"-Zhou Hui muttered.-"It should be very conspicuous."

Having swept his divine sense across the sea of grass without finding what he sought, Zhou Hui had no choice but to close his eyes and unfold his entire divine sense for a carpet search.

At this moment, a celestial body resembling the moon hung in the sky. Chilly moonlight spilled downward evenly and impartially, settling upon every stalk of Snow Glow Grass like a layer of white frost coating their already translucent texture, making the entire surroundings resemble a tranquil winter night with softly falling fresh snow.

Under this moonlight, even "Mo Xi" could not help but cease (unilaterally) bickering with his sister, holding his breath in anticipation.

After quite a while, however, Zhou Hui's eyelashes, delicate as frost blossoms, fluttered slightly. His silver-gray pupils were filled with utter bewilderment:

—"...Nothing? Nowhere at all?"-Zhou Hui murmured.

—"This old man has not found it either,"-the Sacred Tree nodded. It had just searched the entire subterranean realm with its root network and was now withdrawing its roots from the soil layer.

Hearing the words of the two seniors who were both at or above the Tribulation Transcendence stage, "Mo Xi" wore an anxious expression:

—"Impossible! I investigated for so long! Second Senior Brother said it was most likely here as well. If it's not here, then it won't be anywhere—"-as he spoke, his face warped into sheer despair.

Mo Bei looked at this version of Mo Xi with bewilderment, asking hesitantly:

—"What thing? What is it you can't find?"-Mo Bei asked.

Hearing Mo Bei's voice, the despair on "Mo Xi's" face only deepened.

—"It's all! It's all your fault..."-he choked out suddenly, a bloody mist bursting from his body to engulf Mo Bei.-"I should have just killed you from the very start! Killed you—!!"

Seeing "Mo Xi" like this, Zhou Hui sighed inwardly.

This was "Mo Xi," yet ultimately not *the* Mo Xi.

Such was the nature of an illusory phantasm. Perhaps he possessed an appearance, personality, and memories similar to the original, but in the end he was merely an incomplete fragment. He had no past, nor would he have a future. If his obsessive fixation could not be fulfilled, he would easily lapse into a state of rampage.

In truth, "losing control" was relatively benign. What was far worse was Zhou Hui's gnawing concern: had the demonized Mo Xi of that cycle, pushed to despair, actually done something similar to Mo Bei?

At this thought, Zhou Hui pinched a hand seal, preparing to suppress "Mo Xi."

Yet Mo Bei was faster even than her master.

Not that her sword was swifter than Master's, but rather she harbored none of those complicated sentiments regarding "Mo Xi's" loss of control.

The instant "Mo Xi's" gaze turned wrong, her hand was already resting on her scabbard. By the time that bloody stench spread, Mo Bei had already struck "Mo Xi" ruthlessly in the stomach with her scabbard. Next came a swivel of her hips, a raised leg, and a sweeping side-kick—a series of motions executed with immaculate, textbook precision. By the time "Mo Xi" crashed to the ground, the blood mist swirling around him had not grazed a single strand of Mo Bei's hair.

Mo Bei gave "Mo Xi" no chance to recover either, planting her foot firmly onto "Mo Xi's" throat.

Her force was calibrated to absolute perfection: pinning "Mo Xi" in so much agony that he could not budge an inch, yet carefully stopping short of crushing him to death.

Maintaining her posture of pinning her younger brother beneath her boot, Mo Bei combed through the fresh batch of memories acquired from "Mo Xi," confirming she had no recollection whatsoever regarding secrets hidden within this sea of Snow Glow Grass.

[Perhaps that's a good thing? At least it's not another chapter of my dark history?]

Mo Bei mused uncertainly, asking Master as if nothing had happened at all:

—"Master, what is it that we can't find?"-Mo Bei asked.

["..."]-looking at this, at least Zhou Hui didn't need to worry about this child being bullied.

Zhou Hui glanced at "Mo Xi" and sighed. Having no intention of liberating the youth from beneath her heel, he merely replied:

—"A ritual vessel... The ritual vessel that suppresses the immortal remains cannot be found,"-Zhou Hui answered.

[—"Eh?"](# "Speaker: Mo Bei")

—"There was indeed a ritual vessel here,"-Zhou Hui explained in greater detail.-"Regardless of the intentions of the departed themselves, so many immortal remains would inevitably cause spiritual contamination. Those who buried them were clearly well-prepared, employing a series of ritual vessels to conduct meticulous rites of sacrifice—and suppression. That so many remains have exerted no ill effect upon this world, with no one even knowing of this place, is the best proof of all."

—"This old man does not understand the customs of you humans very well—and immortals, naturally, are still human,"-the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree interjected, watching Zhou Hui prepare to deliver a lecture.

It had already discerned this Immortal Venerable's habit of thoroughly investigating and expounding upon everything under the sun. If Untiring Immortal Venerable were allowed free rein to get to the root of the matter, they could probably talk here for several centuries. For an ancient tree, that was nothing, but for the children—whether the kids present here or those back upon its boughs—it was far too long.

The Fire Abyss Sacred Tree cut through the fluff as best it could, offering its testimony:

—"Though this old man does not grasp the details, this place falls within reach of my root network. I have indeed sensed the presence of a formidable artifact of suppression here in the past, which is precisely why my roots dared not actually take root in this soil. Yet coming here this time, that object has vanished."

—"...Was it stolen?"-Zhou Hui mused.

—"Immortal Venerable, you underestimate this old man far too much,"-the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree grumbled, slightly displeased.-"To carry off such a formidable artifact of suppression, one would inevitably pass through the domain of my roots. It would be impossible to evade my notice."

—"No, you misunderstand. I do not doubt your prowess, but am rather contemplating the thief's methods,"-Zhou Hui furrowed his brows.-"The fact that it could be hidden from your senses only makes it all the more alarming."

—"...Um, could we perhaps ask the 'residents' here?"-Mo Bei suggested in a small voice.

She gazed into the distance at the cloud-like yin spirits.

"Mo Xi" had said those were their parents. Mo Bei cared about those yin spirits, if only a little.

Mo Bei didn't even know what she wanted from them. Certainly not familial affection or some emotional validation. Perhaps she simply wanted to see how a person like herself had been created, and what these so-called "parents" felt toward children brought into the world without their own consent.

Yet regarding Mo Bei's suggestion, neither of the two supreme figures considered it a sound idea.

—"Yin spirits? Those are hardly suitable partners for conversation,"-the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree shook its head.

Zhou Hui glanced at Mo Bei. Discerning his disciple's tangled emotions, he indulgently humored her proposal:

—"We can give it a try. But do not harbor too much hope—in every sense of the word,"-Zhou Hui added, the final remark clearly aimed as a gentle caution to Mo Bei.

Mo Bei looked at her master in bewilderment.

She watched as Zhou Hui raised his hand, condensing the moonlight into a moon-white cane that dipped toward the gathering yin spirits, herding them gently. Under the guidance of this moonlight cane, several clouds—the yin spirits—separated from the main cloud deck, trotting over like fluffy, soft little lambs.

The cloudy posture of the yin spirits reminded Mo Bei of the little black lamb Master kept by his side lately, and the corners of her lips curved upward involuntarily.

However, the moment the yin spirits drew near, Mo Bei realized that yin spirits were nowhere near as adorable as little lambs (even if the true nature of that little black lamb back in the Heavenly Spirit Sect wasn't particularly cute either).

Unlike the little black lamb's warm fleece, the first sensation the yin spirits brought was:

Cold!

A frigid chill that even Mo Bei, with her Extreme Yin Constitution, could scarcely endure. It was a cold that manifested almost entirely as piercing agony.

Moreover, that agonizing arctic chill did not seem to come from outside, but welled up from the deepest recesses of her body—a frost capable of freezing even the soul solid.

Accompanying this bitter cold, the negative emotions suppressed deep within her heart surfaced without her noticing:

[How am I worthy of being alive?!]

[Having done such things to Master in my past life, why didn't I just die?]

[Better to just die...]

—"Bei'er,"-a sigh resonated beside Mo Bei's ear. She felt wrapped in a gentle warmth, instantly yanked back from the abyss of dark emotions.

Snapping back to reality, Mo Bei found Master's warm palm resting gently upon her head. Looking toward the yin spirits once more, she realized their approach had been a mere illusion; they were still hovering near the cloud deck as billowing vapor. Only now, several eye-like dark voids had formed on their surface, staring fixedly at her. That sight alone was enough to send a nightmare shiver down Mo Bei's spine.

—"Master, I..."-Mo Bei stammered in shame.

She hadn't imagined she would succumb so easily. She was already at Golden Core!

—"There is no need to be disheartened. That was never something you could handle,"-Zhou Hui said.-"If anything, I was careless as well! I did not anticipate that merely drawing their attention would inflict an instantaneous mental assault on anyone below the Void Refinement stage. Should we expect anything less from the departed souls of immortals? After marinating through untold millennia, their power has become a bit excessive."

So that was it...?

Hearing Master's words, Mo Bei subconsciously checked herself.

Her limbs still felt dreadfully stiff, as though frozen through. Fortunately, the yin spirits' influence had affected "Mo Xi" at the exact same moment—and far more severely. Thus, "Mo Xi" had not seized the opportunity to escape from under her boot, still frozen in an expression of rigid terror, his mind apparently paralyzed.

Mo Bei silently pressed her boot back down.

—"Hey!"-"Mo Xi" attempted to protest, only for his voice to be crushed back into his throat.

Mo Bei had no intention of letting "Mo Xi" regain his mobility. Seeing how terrifying those yin spirits were, if "Mo Xi" were assimilated into them, it would be a huge headache—though with Master present, such a possibility was virtually nonexistent.

Zhou Hui paid no heed to the petty squabbles between Mo Bei and "Mo Xi," engrossed in discussion with the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree regarding the yin spirits:

—"Fortunately, those yin spirits possess no conscious desire to harm others. One could say this is another proof of the ritual vessel's existence! Otherwise, regardless of the departed's original wishes, after accumulating resentment over such vast ages, it would be sufficient to escalate into a cataclysmic disaster.—Come to think of it, we ought to thank Your Excellency Sacred Tree. Given the nature of yin spirits, they would inevitably slaughter living beings, yet currently they harbor very little ferocity. That must be thanks to your contribution, no?"-Zhou Hui said.

—"No, this old man did nothing of the sort. At most, finding it uncanny, I merely restrained my kin from drawing near this place,"-the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree shook its head.-"However, you should understand full well what [reckless, death-seeking creatures](# "Literal CN: 作死 (zuòsǐ) — actively courting death / suicidal recklessness") humans can be."

True enough. If humans weren't like that, Mo Bei and Mo Xi would never have been born.

And beings similar to Mo Bei and Mo Xi existed in droves back in White Jade Capital. Due to their tender age, standard pedagogical curricula were entirely useless, leaving him at a loss as to how to handle them!

Remembering this, Zhou Hui felt a headache brewing.

Seeing Master's knitted brows, Mo Bei strove to be helpful, assuming Master was merely vexed by the yin spirits:

—"M-Master, when I made contact with the yin spirits just now, though I was affected, now that I've calmed down, I feel like I can sense their thoughts and emotions..."-Mo Bei said.

—"The thoughts of the yin spirits?"-Zhou Hui looked at his disciple.

Come to think of it, Mo Bei took yin spirits as her foundation and possessed the peerless Extreme Yin Constitution; she was the living being closest to yin spirits in existence, so achieving resonance with them was indeed within her power.

Yet because she was so easily assimilated, Zhou Hui only wished for her to stay as far from the yin spirits as possible.

Oblivious to Master's worry, Mo Bei nodded, her expression somewhat vacant:

—"I feel... those yin spirits are completely empty... Both memories and emotions feel hollow, just like... the original me."

—"Memories and emotions are empty? For a yin spirit, that is indeed rare. Most yin spirits are products of intense malice, akin to vengeful ghosts yet inferior even to them. After all, a vengeful ghost has a specific target for vengeance, and thus holds the possibility of being delivered into peace. But a yin spirit possesses no target; it is a mobile natural disaster,"-Zhou Hui assessed objectively.-"However, the yin spirits here arose purely from the sheer number and overwhelming power of the departed, which paradoxically makes them safer. With them as your foundation, you and Xi'er turned out so pure and kind. To be frank, when I learned you two were mingled with yin spirit components, I was thoroughly astonished!"

—"Master..."-Mo Bei muttered.

[Only you in the entire world would consider me and Mo Xi 'pure and kind.']

[The two standing right before your eyes in the previous cycle—one sold you out, and the other fell straight into demonic corruption!]

A heart full of complaints stuck in Mo Bei's throat.

Then again, [Master's rose-tinted mentor filter](# "Literal CN: 师尊的滤镜 (lǜjìng) — Master's lens/filter; modern slang for idealized perception") hadn't been built in a single day.

Zhou Hui ruffled his disciple's hair, musing in perplexity:

—"Still, that makes even less sense. No matter how pure and blank they were at birth, after enduring such vast ages, they shouldn't remain empty."-Zhou Hui noted. Even Mo Bei and Mo Xi had developed lives of their own.

This was a riddle even Zhou Hui could not solve, so Mo Bei naturally could not answer. However, she felt "Mo Xi" squirming frantically beneath her foot, seemingly trying to speak.

Mo Bei slightly eased the pressure of her boot.

—"Mo Bei, you damn... Fine, I get it, I won't insult you!"-"Mo Xi" gasped out, struggling.-"It's the ritual vessel! Master, it's that ritual vessel! The ritual vessel can wipe away memories and emotions, which is why Mo Bei, as the finished product, never remembers anything in any cycle, living like an absolute idiot—*mph mph mph!*"

Mo Bei ground her foot down, stomping "Mo Xi's" insults right back down his throat.

[—"The ritual vessel? So it really comes down to the ritual vessel?"](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"The original undertakers were cautious indeed, going to such lengths,"-Zhou Hui said, his expression turning ever graver.-"It is thanks to the ritual vessel repeatedly wiping clean their memories and emotions that these yin spirits remained so safe, is it not?"

—"Yet from now on, I fear they will be safe no longer,"-the Sacred Tree spoke with a heavy voice.

[Why?]

Mo Bei almost asked, before recalling that Master and the Sacred Tree had just been discussing how the ritual vessel could not be found.

This meant that from now on, the yin spirits could no longer be reset. Their devolution into a calamity was merely a matter of time.

Recalling the biting chill she had experienced when brushed by the yin spirits, Mo Bei could not help shivering.

—"Is there no way to destroy them? It has been so many years,"-the Sacred Tree asked. When it came to yin spirits, the Sacred Tree was clearly out of its depth.

—"Methods for eliminating yin spirits, setting aside violent annihilation, consist of planting purifying flora nearby and letting time grind them down,"-Zhou Hui glanced at the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, offering a troubled analysis.-"If I am not mistaken, the people of that era chose this location as the burial site in the hope that you would assist. Your divine fire is a natural counter to yin spirits... But they likely never anticipated that you would choose to shelter the demon race, deliberately bypassing this region. And the demonic energy of the demon race actually nourishes yin spirits, leaving the spirits here with no sign of wear whatsoever..."

—"Don't you humans go assigning bizarre duties to trees willy-nilly! At the very least, you ought to have told this old man beforehand!"-the Sacred Tree complained indignantly.

Yet complaining now was too late.

The problem returned to square one:

Where on earth had the "ritual vessel" suppressing the immortal remains and yin spirits gone?!

—"...Master, why don't you let me try looking for it?"-Mo Bei thought for a moment and suggested to Zhou Hui.-"Since both the yin spirits and the ritual vessel share an inexplicable connection with me, perhaps I can find it?"

—"However, you felt the touch of the yin spirits just now. You should know that with your cultivation level, it will be excruciating..."-Zhou Hui warned.

—"It doesn't matter, I can do it!"-Mo Bei insisted.

If anything, she urgently yearned to do something to atone for the foolish deeds of her past life.

Looking into his disciple's resolute eyes, Zhou Hui sighed:

—"Then give it a try. But remember: do not force yourself."

Speaking thus, Zhou Hui draped his moonlight over Mo Bei's shoulders. The moonlight coalesced into a moon-white fur cloak, bundling Mo Bei snugly within.

Mo Bei was certainly not so stubborn as to refuse Master's assistance.

—"I will,"-Mo Bei murmured. Feeling the soft, plush touch of the fur brushing against her cheek, she once more locked her gaze onto those nightmare "clouds" in the distance, and was instantly pulled inside.

Yet this time, Mo Bei was not overcome by negative emotions.

Naturally, the power of immortal yin spirits accumulated over tens of thousands of years was no joke. Even wrapped in the moonlight fur cloak, Mo Bei could still feel the soul-freezing chill and the negative emotions continuously welling up from the depths of her heart. But now, keenly aware that Master was right by her side, she was not so easily shaken.

Regrettably, Mo Bei's exploration yielded no progress whatsoever.

Just as she had sensed earlier, the interior of the yin spirits was "empty." Much like her former brain, it was void and vacant, incapable of thought and refusing to think. No matter which way she walked, boundless gray mist stretched in every direction.

Mo Bei did not know how long she lingered there. She saw nothing, and found nothing.

Until Master's voice rang out:

—"Bei'er, enough. You may return."

—"No, Master..."

Mo Bei wanted to persist, but in the next instant, she found herself standing once more upon the grassy expanse of Snow Glow Grass. That soul-freezing chill vanished once again, leaving only the cozy warmth of the moonlight cloak wrapped around her.

—"I could have kept going,"-Mo Bei sighed downcast.

—"I know. What I mean is that you have already found enough clues for me,"-Zhou Hui smiled gently.

Yet his smile carried a subtle heaviness:

—"Thanks to you, I was able to deeply perceive the interior of the yin spirits."

—"Then what about the ritual vessel...?"-Mo Bei asked.

—"It does not exist!"-Zhou Hui declared, raising his hand to catch a strand of moonlight.-"Thanks to Bei'er's aid, my spiritual energy was able to enter the yin spirits and perceive where the ritual vessel once resided. But now, that place is truly vacant—not only in space, but in time as well. It is entirely empty."

Zhou Hui stated with conviction:

—"That ritual vessel... from the very beginning, does not exist within the past or future of this timeline!"

What did that mean?

The ritual vessel had once existed, yet did not exist?

Mo Bei and "Mo Xi" were plunged into even deeper bewilderment.

The Sacred Tree, however, grasped Zhou Hui's meaning:

—"Are you saying it was taken outside of time? In other words, it was not stolen in this cycle?"-the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree asked.

—"It is possible,"-Zhou Hui nodded.-"Before this cycle, I was after all an ordinary mortal. Everything rewound could only take the entities of this world as its benchmark, unable to interfere with existence outside this world. If certain items were smuggled away to the Heavenly Realm or the Demon Realm, there is likely nothing I could do..."

["..."]-the Sacred Tree wore an expression of sheer speechlessness.

[In truth, the scope of what you *can* do is already preposterous enough.]

Zhou Hui muttered to himself, unheeding:

—"Yet that shouldn't be either. Even if I was unaware of the immortal remains and the ritual vessel, if an object of such immense mass were not restored, I ought to have noticed something amiss—unless they used a reasonable justification to make me take it for granted..."

Speaking up to this point, Zhou Hui's expression abruptly shifted. He turned his gaze toward Mo Bei.

He had just recalled a deeply unsettling matter:

Back when he still believed everything was dictated by the Heavenly Dao's script, hadn't he seen that Mo Bei and that so-called Child of Destiny had a child together? Mo Bei had even died in childbirth! And that child had been taken to the Immortal-Demon Realm by the Child of Destiny and the daughter of Jian Nuo and Qingyang Qingyu!

Now that everything had been rewound, the relevant figures had all been forcibly dragged back to this world by Zhou Hui, awaiting the unfolding of this cycle's "Destiny." But what about that "child"?

If he were "merely" Mo Bei's child, then before his mother gave birth to him, he naturally would not exist—and might never exist at all; everyone could pretend nothing had ever happened. But what if the "materials" composing that child were mixed with things Zhou Hui knew nothing about, and he was smuggled off to the Immortal-Demon Realm in the chaos?!

The "excess mass" might very well have remained behind in that manner.

At this thought, Zhou Hui's expression grew profoundly grim.

—"M-Master..."-Mo Bei felt a wave of panic under Zhou Hui's gaze. She asked nervously,-"What is it?"

Zhou Hui shook his head:

—"...No, it is nothing."

Not long ago, Mo Bei had suffered a severe blow from the memories of the previous cycle. If he told her now that she had unknowingly given birth to a child, and that the child might still be alive, abandoned in another realm, her mental state might collapse entirely.

Besides, this matter was far from certain.

That ritual vessel was connected to Mo Bei, yet what the other party truly wanted to smuggle away shouldn't have been that object.

Zhou Hui decided to investigate thoroughly first before considering whether to have a heart-to-heart with Mo Bei.

Mo Bei, however, did not believe it was "nothing."

If it had nothing to do with her, why would "Mo Xi" lose control again the moment they failed to find it, even wanting to kill her?

Clearly, Master had not come here from the very start to "save the world." He had come seeking this ritual vessel for her, Mo Bei. Thus, it was impossible that this had nothing to do with her.

Yet Mo Bei did not press Master.

She could vaguely guess that Master's reluctance to speak was no different from his refusal to let the authentic Mo Xi regain his memories—he feared hurting her. Right now, the (real) Mo Xi was still left atop the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, blissfully ignorant as he calibrated the demon race's Exhortation to Learning system! But compared to being hurt, Mo Bei feared even more that she would blunder into another catastrophic mistake in utter ignorance.

Mo Bei immediately pivoted to the other fellow who knew everything.

Transmitting her divine sense, she demanded of "Mo Xi":

[—"Tell me: what is the ritual vessel? What does it have to do with me? Why did you all want to find it?"](# "Speaker: Mo Bei")

As she spoke, she once again lightened the pressure of her boot on "Mo Xi."

Hearing Mo Bei's question, "Mo Xi" was startled at first, before breaking into a venomous sneer:

—"Are you certain you want to know?"-he asked, crimson blood mist spreading outward once again.

This time, Mo Bei did not defend herself, allowing the blood mist to seep into her body.

—"Bei'er..."-Zhou Hui sighed helplessly, having naturally noticed the twins' little maneuvers.

Yet considering that this "Mo Xi" had no knowledge of Mo Bei having borne a child and only knew of the ritual vessel, he did not intervene to stop them completely.

And so, Mo Bei witnessed "Mo Xi's" memories.

To be frank, if memories were not impossible to forge, Mo Bei could scarcely believe that "Mo Xi" loved her so deeply—even if terror of becoming like Mo Bei had been mingled in as motivation.

In that cycle, in order to reclaim Mo Bei and restore her true self, "Mo Xi" had done everything within his power.

He had not merely snatched Mo Bei away from the "protagonist" and forbidden them from making contact as depicted in the "script." He had even contributed his own blood and soul for Second Senior Brother Qu Qianxing to research, desperately seeking to understand the underlying mechanics of why Mo Bei had gone so madly obsessed over a worthless idiot.

Consequently, even without cultivating the secret manual Master had bestowed or treading the path of Carefree Palace, Qu Qianxing in that cycle had still discovered Mo Bei and Mo Xi's yin spirit lineage. Building upon that foundation, he had uncovered the hidden "mechanism" buried within Snow Glow Grass.

Yes, a mechanism.

When Carefree Palace stole yin spirits to manufacture Extreme Yin Constitutions, creating mere puppets was never the end of it. The perfect puppet had to be kept under absolute control.

They not only wanted the puppets to obey, but also wanted the cultivators who dual-cultivated with these puppets (cauldrons) to obey as well. They were the ultimate unscrupulous profiteers, killing two birds with one stone and [eating two dishes from one fish](# "Literal CN: 一鱼两吃 (yī yú liǎng chī) — cooking one fish in two ways; profiting twice from a single asset").

Yet cultivators who bought cauldrons were naturally no saints themselves. They routinely subjected cauldrons to comprehensive inspections. If any sinister trap were discovered, Carefree Palace's reputation would be ruined and endless trouble would follow.

Thus, Carefree Palace set their sights upon the [graveyard grass](# "Literal CN: 坟头草 (féntóu cǎo) — grass growing on a grave; dark slang for the dead").

Snow Glow Grass, as a mutated species symbiotic with immortal remains and yin spirits, had been permeated by the yin qi of the spirits year in and year out. It was practically an extension of the yin spirits, possessing the traits of frigid yin energy. Only because of its humble weed nature, the yin qi it emitted was practically negligible, making it appear harmless.

However, there was one specific state in which Snow Glow Grass could unleash tremendous power:

When it bloomed!

When Snow Glow Grass burst into blossom, all the yin qi it had accumulated would be discharged at once, constructing a secret realm mirroring its place of origin. Simultaneously, the power of the ritual vessel that likewise permeated (and suppressed) Snow Glow Grass would also be released to suppress that surge of yin qi.

In other words, amidst the dancing petals of blooming Snow Glow Grass, a puppet (cauldron) refined from yin spirits would lose all resolve and emotion—and in severe cases, have their memories wiped clean, undergoing a complete factory reset. Even the cultivator dual-cultivating alongside the cauldron, influenced by the cauldron's state, would have their divine consciousness eroded into a helpless puppet ripe for manipulation.

To make matters worse, Carefree Palace had specifically formulated a corresponding aphrodisiac to be paired with blooming Snow Glow Grass, allowing them to thoroughly seize control of the target's mind and soul while their divine consciousness was at its lowest ebb.

—"So my mind was controlled?"-Mo Bei asked in profound disgust upon reaching this part.

She inspected her own memories. There truly was a memory of Snow Glow Grass blooming, leading her to believe that fellow was her fated one.

Yet Mo Bei paused, rejecting the notion herself:

—"No, wait. That fellow didn't seem aware of the aphrodisiac, nor did he know how to control my mind. It seemed like an accidental consequence..."-Mo Bei muttered.

Because he was the "protagonist," of course. How could he possibly do wicked deeds on purpose?

Zhou Hui furrowed his brows.

Such convenient "coincidences" in the Heavenly Dao's script were precisely what disgusted him most.

Yet Mo Bei did not completely shirk responsibility onto external factors, merely muttering in fury:

—"In the end, I was just too stupid..."-Mo Bei grumbled. Even if she had been influenced, the fact that she had narrowly escaped being enslaved because the other party was too ignorant, only to actively rush into his arms anyway—if that wasn't sheer idiocy, what was?

—"Stupid to death, indeed,"-"Mo Xi" chimed in agreement.

Mo Bei glared at "Mo Xi," itching to stomp on him again. But recalling the memories she had just witnessed, she ultimately held her foot back.

"Mo Xi," however, [pushed his luck](# "Literal CN: 得寸进尺 (dé cùn jìn chǐ) — give an inch and take a yard / push one's luck"):

—"Because you were that stupid, to prevent you from being manipulated any further, we had to get our hands on the ritual vessel! Only that vessel can control and suppress the power of the yin spirits and immortal remains. And only if we hold that vessel can others be stopped from seizing your mind and using you as a lever against Master."

Indeed. It was precisely to keep Mo Bei's vulnerability from being exposed to the hands of others once again that Zhou Hui had accepted "Mo Xi's" account and come here to retrieve the ritual vessel.

Yet the ritual vessel was gone!

At the thought of this, "Mo Xi" showed signs of lapsing into demonic frenzy once more.

—"...Do we necessarily need the ritual vessel?"-Mo Bei suddenly spoke up.

While she loathed the aphrodisiac and the fated lover, she wasn't particularly fond of the ritual vessel "Mo Xi" mentioned either.

As an independent "person," Mo Bei hated anything that controlled her mind.

Even if it was supposedly for her own good.

For the sake of her own autonomy, Mo Bei mobilized her hollow noggin like never before. Finally, turning to Master, she said:

—"We don't need a ritual vessel. As long as the one who makes the Snow Glow Grass bloom is myself, and the one who uses the medicine to control me is myself, then I am my own master, right?"

—"I don't want anyone or anything controlling me ever again!"-Mo Bei declared.

Hearing Mo Bei say this, Zhou Hui paused, a flicker of gratification gracing his face.

—"It is exceedingly difficult. To execute secret arts and maintain control over yourself when your mind is thrown off balance means you must not only master secret arts, but also master every single aspect of your physical vessel,"-Zhou Hui cautioned.-"This means you cannot merely practice swordsmanship. You must study relevant mental techniques, body techniques, alchemy, medicine brewing, and body refinement courses. You will even need to study your Fifth Senior Brother's biological dissection until you thoroughly understand every nuance of the Extreme Yin Constitution, lest any error occur during execution."

Simply put, her coursework had multiplied tenfold in an instant.

And courses that required strenuous brainpower, no less.

Yet how should one put it? Master's solution was just so... predictably on-brand, wasn't it?

Bitterness welled in Mo Bei's mouth, yet she nodded vigorously:

—"I can do it!"-Mo Bei said.

Zhou Hui broke into a genuine smile.

—"Excellent! As soon as we return, I shall design your curriculum!"-Zhou Hui decided swiftly.-"Afterward, I will locate Carefree Palace to retrieve the medicine for you—"

—"That won't be necessary, Master! I encountered that 'fated lover' earlier. Although he was merely a fragment preserved by the Sacred Tree, he can serve perfectly well as a medicinal primer!"-"Mo Xi" blurted out in a panic.

He was genuinely terrified that Master would just casually wipe Carefree Palace off the map.

Naturally, "Mo Xi" was not pleading on Carefree Palace's behalf. Rather, at the "current stage," confronting Carefree Palace was far more trouble than it was worth. Moreover, Carefree Palace possessed immense utility value; it was best left untouched until the endgame.

That was what Second Senior Brother had once mentioned to "Mo Xi."

In fact, in the script, Carefree Palace had indeed wandered carefree all the way until the "protagonist" ascended.

"Mo Xi" wasn't sure whether to relay Qu Qianxing's words to Master. He had a sneaking suspicion that blabbing would only stack more crimes onto Second Senior Brother's rap sheet—

*Ah, then let's happily tattle after all!*

"Mo Xi" immediately prepared to sell out his Second Senior Brother. Yet before he could open his mouth, he was interrupted:

—"Hold on a moment, Immortal Venerable! Don't act as though every problem has been solved!"

The Fire Abyss Sacred Tree eyed the master and disciple with deep suspicion:

—"The most crucial issue hasn't been resolved at all?!"

[The most crucial?]

[Wasn't Mo Bei the most crucial thing?]

Zhou Hui looked at the Sacred Tree in surprise.

The Sacred Tree pointed toward the distant yin spirits:

—"Without the ritual vessel to suppress them, those yin spirits cannot have their resentment wiped away! Sooner or later, they will grow into a natural disaster! Isn't *that* the most crucial issue?!"

—"Ah, that!"-Zhou Hui responded as if only just remembering.

The Sacred Tree watched him in rising despair, wondering whether this human was truly reliable. Facing a catastrophe that could reach cataclysmic proportions, how could he seem so entirely unbothered?

Zhou Hui even added:

—"However, it is currently still blank, is it not?"

—"Being blank right now is useless! As a human, you should know that yin spirits accumulate resentment far faster than you can imagine! Especially now that the ritual vessel is lost, it will eventually constitute a disaster comparable to a demon invasion—or worse, stacking one upon the other, it will make a demon invasion even easier! Not to mention, who knows what nefarious schemes the thief who stole the ritual vessel harbors!"-the Sacred Tree emphasized. It refused to believe Zhou Hui could be so short-sighted as to fail to foresee such a horrifying future.

—"No, that is not what I meant. Naturally, I know leaving them unattended would be disastrous. But why would we leave them unattended?"-Zhou Hui said. His eyes burned with eager fervor as he looked at the yin spirits, and then back at the Sacred Tree.

A terrible premonition gripped the Sacred Tree.

Yet Zhou Hui patted its shoulder—due to the Sacred Tree's towering stature, Zhou Hui actually had to stand on tiptoe to reach it:

—"This mass of yin spirits, these immortal remains... are they not the most ideal location to expand your [server cache](# "Literal CN: 缓存 (huǎncún) — cache / buffer; modern computing terminology applied to pedagogical cultivation arrays")?"-Zhou Hui asked.
