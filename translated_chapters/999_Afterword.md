# Author's Afterword (作者有话说)

This story has finally come to an end.

To everyone who accompanied me all the way to this point, thank you from the bottom of my heart. And at the same time, I offer my deepest apologies to every single one of you (*prostrates in apology*).

I was truly a useless mess. After running a high fever from COVID-19 and enduring the sudden family upheaval that followed, I sank into a state where I sat staring blankly at my outline, completely unable to write.

As anyone could tell just from reading the synopsis, this novel was originally conceived as an archetypal [crematorium master-disciple story](# "Literal CN: 火葬场师徒文 (huǒzàngchǎng shītú wén) — a popular web novel subgenre where disciples mistreat, neglect, or betray their master, only to suffer agonizing regret ('the crematorium') and grovel desperately to make amends after the master withdraws or dies. In this novel, the trope is famously inverted and deconstructed"). Yet because the disciples turned out to be completely [nonflammable](# "Literal CN: 不可燃 (bù kěrán) — author's comedic observation playing on the crematorium trope: Zhou Hui's disciples were far too pragmatic, thick-skinned, or eccentric to combust with melodramatic guilt, leaving nothing to fuel the fiery angst of a standard crematorium novel"), the fire refused to catch in the slightest; and by midway through the story, Master's character incorporated elements of an idealized elder. But in the end, none of that bore any connection to reality. As for my father, he was a quintessential East Asian father. I dislike using that label, yet the truth was that I didn't even feel particularly "close" to him. During the period when he fell ill, I was genuinely frantic with anxiety (thank you so much to everyone who prayed and wished him well back then—truly, thank you). But by the time his funeral arrived, I didn't even feel like crying anymore.

Yet ever since then, no matter what I tried, I could no longer write interpersonal relationships naturally. The closer the story drew to its conclusion, the more paralyzed my pen became—as if some internal string concerning familial affection, something my psyche refused to consciously acknowledge, had completely snapped.

Perhaps it really was just the lingering effects of post-illness brain fog.

Back then, I thought to myself that it might be better to just wrap this novel up quickly and bring it to a swift close. But with so many planted foreshadowing threads left unresolved, the thought of abandoning them felt utterly unacceptable. So I bit the bullet and pushed forward, dragging things out as I persisted. And it was thanks to all of you who continued reading chapter by chapter that I didn't feel as though I was merely indulging in empty self-gratification.

In the end, this novel was able to conclude in a state I feel relatively satisfied with. I resolved virtually all the foreshadowing threads I could call to mind, and I didn't allow the characters to be warped by the turmoil in my own emotional state. At the very least, I feel it has reached a truly fulfilling, well-rounded completion.

Reaching this point, I must once again express my deepest gratitude to all of you who read to the very end. It was all of you who gave me the courage to finish writing it.

Thank you.
