# Chapter 127: Resolve (第127章 决意)

Ran Deng could not stop weeping.

In truth, weeping was already the gentlest outlet she could choose at this moment.

After all, the memories of seven lifetimes had been crammed into her mind all at once, and the vast majority of these recollections comprised the most agonizing, regret-drenched moments of her past. Even for someone like Ran Deng, after tumbling through seven consecutive cycles of reincarnation, her deepest emotions could hardly remain trivialized at the level of: 'I don't want to do homework, I don't want to cultivate, and thinking about inheriting the family business gives me so much stress.'

Especially after witnessing Master forfeit his ascension six times for the sake of these disciples of his.

The solitary exception was the lifetime where Master was entirely absent, supplanted by an impostor! And when the Heavenly Spirit Sect was exterminated in that life, she had still accomplished nothing, watching helplessly as the fellow disciples she was supposed to shepherd to safety were slaughtered before her eyes.

And yet, in every single cycle, she had remained utterly oblivious, heartlessly wanting only to [lie flat](# "Literal CN: 躺平 (tǎngpíng) — slacking off / giving up on ambition").

Ran Deng was consumed by remorse—remorse for the useless self who achieved nothing across seven lifetimes;

Ran Deng was consumed by hatred—hatred for the ignorant self who knew nothing across seven lifetimes;

These regrets and loathings stacked upon one another, driving Ran Deng nearly to the brink of wanting to slaughter her own incompetent self. Regrettably, in every incarnation, it was only at the final, irreversible juncture—spurred by desperation, grief, and vengeance—that she would recover the memories of her previous lifetimes. Then, mere seconds, minutes, quarters of an hour—or at most, less than a day—later, life would swiftly rewind, purging her memories clean, only for her to restart another lazy, unremarkable existence from scratch.

Realizing this, Ran Deng's tears flowed even more uncontrollably.

Until...

Until that familiar, gentle voice called out:

—"...Ah-Ran? Ran Deng, Deng'er!"

Accompanying that voice, Ran Deng struggled to raise her red, swollen eyes. Facing her was the only countenance capable of setting her heart at peace, currently wearing an expression of deep concern.

Yet, at the conclusion of every reincarnation in her memories, it was also this very face that wore a helpless yet resolute expression, relinquishing everything he ought to have possessed to choose starting all over again.

In that instant, Ran Deng forcefully swallowed back her tears:

—"M-Master... *cough, cough*!"-she managed, choking on her own tears.

—"Ah-Ran, what happened?"-Zhou Hui asked again, his worry evident.

Only then did Ran Deng realize that Master had not arrived in person. Had Zhou Hui come here in the flesh, he would have immediately noticed the issue with the steps and deduced what had transpired. Instead, he had projected his image across space via a spell.

Seeing his disciple fail to return after so long, he had likely projected his divine sense to inspect the situation, only to find his third disciple [crying her eyes out](# "Literal CN: 哭成了狗 (kū chéng le gǒu) — crying like a dog / weeping uncontrollably").

Zhou Hui looked Ran Deng over with anxious eyes. Seeing no signs of external trauma, he inquired:

—"Were you... bullied?"

As Zhou Hui asked, he shifted his gaze to cast a glance at the little black lamb nearby.

The little black lamb had just circled Ran Deng several times. Finding that his junior sister did nothing but weep, completely ignoring him with zero sign of stopping anytime soon, he had trotted back over to gnaw on the little white lamb.

The little white lamb was still skewered upon the lantern pole Ran Deng had hurled.

Even having been reduced to a roast lamb, it remained tenaciously alive, frantically trying to pull the lantern pole from its jaws with its hooves, contorting its body into a bizarre, unnatural posture.

Constrained by the collar Zhou Hui had affixed from the outset, however, it was incapable of morphing into anything other than a sheep. Harassed relentlessly by the little black lamb, breaking free was completely out of the question. After having several patches of fleece chewed away and a hole chomped straight through its ear, the little white lamb simply abandoned its attempts to pull the lantern pole out. Instead, clamping down with all its might, it snapped a hairline crack right into the pole.

It continued thrashing desperately, seemingly determined to chomp the lantern pole to splinters and break free before the black lamb devoured it whole.

When Zhou Hui looked over, this was the utterly chaotic spectacle that met his eyes.

—"What on earth have you two been doing?"-Zhou Hui asked, the scene almost too painful to watch.

—"Ying, ying!"-seeing Master look his way, the little black lamb hurriedly delivered a sharp kick to the white lamb.

He made it abundantly clear: the one who made Junior Sister cry was this fellow, not him.

Yet the fellow stubbornly refused to utter a single human word. One couldn't tell if he was still trying to [bury his head in the sand](# "Literal CN: 掩耳盗铃 (yǎn'ěr-dàolíng) — plugging one's ears while stealing a bell / self-deception"), foolishly believing Ran Deng would mistake the white lamb for 'a monster that transformed into Eldest Senior Brother to deceive her' without ever connecting the little black lamb to the genuine Eldest Senior Brother.

He probably assumed Ran Deng's intellect was roughly on par with his own!

Zhou Hui could not comprehend the thought process of that sheep brain in the slightest. With Ran Deng weeping so terribly, deciphering what had happened seemed impossible without being physically present. He had no choice but to set aside his ongoing research on the Ascension Stairway and sigh:

—"How about this: I'll come over—"

—"Don't!"-Ran Deng cried out sharply.

Her shrill outcry startled both Zhou Hui and the little black lamb.

They turned toward Ran Deng in astonishment.

Ran Deng also recognized her breach of composure. Wiping her eyes with her sleeve, she said to Zhou Hui in a flurry:

—"Master, there is no need. I can handle it myself."

Yes, she could do this!

Though she had remained lazy and incompetent in this life as well, things had not yet reached an irreversible stage! She could not continue leaning entirely on Master for everything as she had in the past.

At the very least, here—under circumstances where Master had already eradicated every enemy in advance—she could manage on her own.

Ran Deng resolved firmly within her heart.

As if worried Master would doubt her capability, she briskly reported everything she had encountered along the way.

Yet out of some unspoken consideration, she omitted two crucial details: what 'Eldest Senior Brother' had told her about this portion of the Ascension Stairway reenacting traumatic memories, and the fact that she had regained the recollections of seven lifetimes.

It must be because she didn't want Master to worry any more than he already did!

Ran Deng reasoned to herself.

Deftly steering the conversation away, she looked at the white lamb and probed:

—"It's just that I don't know the origin of this demon that took Eldest Senior Brother's form. It appears virtually identical to this 'spiritual pet' of yours, Master?"

To claim they shared no relation felt practically impossible.

Upon hearing Ran Deng's words, the little black lamb promptly landed a ferocious kick on the white lamb and backed away several steps, signaling with all his might that he had zero connection to this wicked little lamb that had tried to eat Junior Sister—none whatsoever!

Yet this [guilty protestation](# "Literal CN: 此地无银 (cǐ dì wú yín) — from the idiom 'No 300 taels of silver buried here', meaning an obvious giveaway / protesting too much") served only to solidify Ran Deng's suspicions.

In reality, having read through the entire collection of the Library Pavilion, Ran Deng had already formed several conjectures regarding the true identities of the black and white lambs, several of which were infinitesimally close to the truth.

Ran Deng merely had no desire to confront the little black lamb here and cause unnecessary complications.

She had already wasted quite enough time.

Bringing up the white lamb now was solely to divert Master from paying too much attention to her 'unusual state.'

Having failed Master across seven lifetimes, demanding his comfort now would make her far too much of a scoundrel.

It had to be said that among Zhou Hui's disciples, Ran Deng's emotional intelligence was indisputably among the very highest; she understood her master profoundly well.

—"Did the white lamb state that location is the core of Carefree Palace's laboratory here?"-Zhou Hui's attention was indeed drawn by Ran Deng's words.

—"I believe this place should be it as well,"-Ran Deng affirmed.

As she spoke, she raised her hand, and the palace lantern wobbled as it flew over to her side.

Whether it was because Ran Deng had rolled down too quickly, or because she had maintained a steady supply of spiritual energy while crying with utter absorption, the lantern's flame had miraculously remained unextinguished.

—"Well done,"-Zhou Hui commended.

Ran Deng's lips twitched slightly.

Under normal circumstances, she would have beamed with joy at Master's praise, but now she felt only shame.

She understood full well that even if it was framed as an examination, Master having her refine the lantern with spiritual energy was for her own benefit, designed to temper and elevate her cultivation. Yet just minutes ago, she had grumbled about what a nuisance the lantern was.

—"I merely accomplished what Master requested,"-Ran Deng said through stinging, hot eyes.

She directed the palace lantern toward the opposite side of the chamber, boosting the supply of spiritual energy to illuminate their surroundings.

Thus, the subterranean chamber beneath the stairs revealed its 'true countenance.'

It was a textbook palace chamber belonging to Carefree Palace:

Colossal,

Palatial,

And reeking of spirit stones.

—"Carefree Palace truly adores this [nouveau-riche](# "Literal CN: 暴发户 (bàofāhù) — parvenu / tacky display of unearned wealth") aura of ostentation,"-Ran Deng mocked coldly.

Evidently, the chaotic squalor of the outer buildings was reserved solely for 'experimental specimens' and 'low-level staff.' Even deep beneath the floor of the Abyss Sea, the cadres of Carefree Palace insisted on maintaining their lavish, decadent lifestyle.

The individuals in charge of this facility, however, would likely never be seen again.

Across the hall's floor lay several unmistakable silhouettes of human-shaped ash—the carbonized residues left behind after being blasted by high-temperature celestial thunder. Scattered near these scorch marks were numerous high-grade magical artifacts and storage pouches. Judging by these remnants, their former cultivation realms and status were self-evident: they had been none other than the high-ranking leadership of this outpost.

Then again, by any realm's moral code, anyone running this sort of research facility could hardly escape the fate of being [struck down by lightning](# "Literal CN: 天打雷劈 (tiān dǎ léi pī) — divine retribution by lightning strike").

Ran Deng glanced at the ashes once and looked away with complete disinterest.

The little black lamb, on the other hand, trotted over merrily to scoop up every magical artifact and storage pouch.

His hoarding addiction showed zero sign of improvement even after turning into a sheep.

What Ran Deng truly wanted Master to examine was the apparatus situated at the center of the hall:

A colossal pill-refining cauldron.

Peering through the glazed crystal viewport at the cauldron's core, the fire within had long gone cold, yet a mass of pitch-black liquid could still be seen bubbling violently. Running along the cauldron's exterior were various pipelines connected in an utterly un-Xianxia fashion, apparently designed to channel the black water to diverse locations. It exuded an industrial aesthetic—something Zhou Hui would likely characterize as '[steampunk cultivation](# "Literal CN: 蒸汽修仙 (zhēngqì xiūxiān) — industrial magic engineering")'.

Behind the cauldron stood an elevated incense altar, upon which rested an enshrined command tablet inscribed with peculiar, archaic characters.

This was actually a commonplace sight within pill-refining chambers.

After all, pill-refinement success rates were an utterly metaphysical gamble. No matter how seasoned an alchemy grandmaster might be, concocting top-tier spirit pills always carried the risk of failure. Consequently, regardless of what they believed, alchemists would invariably set up an incense altar and prostrate themselves in every conceivable posture, purely for peace of mind.

For instance, in the Heavenly Spirit Sect's alchemy hall, Lu Shulong had set up a spirit tablet of Yuwen Xuan to worship—

Yes, a spirit tablet of Yuwen Xuan! Not a tablet of Zhou Hui.

Lu Shulong's reasoning was that 'Eldest Senior Brother appears to possess far better luck.' Yet even with the little black lamb's intellect, he felt as though his fifth junior brother was laying a death curse on him every single time he bowed before that tablet.

[I knew it! I really should eat that dragon!]

Recalling this grievance, Yuwen Xuan felt completely justified.

Then again, what wicked intentions could a little dragon harbor? Ordinary alchemists naturally enshrined whichever entity they deemed most significant!

The command tablet here, for instance, was undoubtedly the 'formation eye' governing everything in this facility.

Just as the white lamb had stated: so long as one refined this command tablet, they could seize absolute control over this region.

Ran Deng had sensed the peculiar aura of this space the moment she descended.

Even when overwhelmed by uncontrollable weeping upon regaining seven lifetimes of memories, as a formation master, she could never overlook the bizarre anomaly here.

In truth, had Zhou Hui been physically present, he too would have detected it instantly.

—"Hmph. To enshrine it in such a conspicuous spot?"-Zhou Hui snorted coldly.-"They certainly are brazen!"

Actually, it wasn't particularly brazen.

Ran Deng reasoned.

Had Master not scorched the entire sector with celestial thunder beforehand, merely finding this subterranean chamber would have been a near-fatal endeavor. Once inside, intruders still faced the trials of the Ascension Stairway, not to mention an entrenched garrison of Carefree Palace's high-tier cultivators, demons, and monstrosities lying in wait.

Even if one managed to overcome all those hurdles, seizing control was hardly trivial.

Ran Deng stared at the command tablet.

Zhou Hui naturally noticed the tablet as well:

—"The sole remaining issue is this command tablet. Forcibly refining it requires considerable cultivation. Ah-Ran, your cultivation is not yet sufficient. In the end, it would be better to let—"

—"I can do it!"-Ran Deng raised her voice.

[—"?"](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"I can do it,"-Ran Deng said, softening her tone.

Zhou Hui fell silent for a moment, pondering how best to soothe his disciple and persuade her to yield.

Cultivation aside, Ah-Ran had clearly undergone some profound emotional turmoil just moments ago; her current mental state was far from stable, making her ill-suited for such a demanding undertaking.

Yet before Zhou Hui could assemble his words, he watched Ran Deng wrap her arms around the palace lantern and close her eyes, the spiritual energy throughout her entire frame surging into violent circulation.

As her spiritual energy cycled, a flame-shaped brilliance ignited upon Ran Deng's brow.

Witnessing this, the little white lamb, which had been secretly plotting its escape, halted its covert struggles and stared at Ran Deng with glowing eyes.

The little black lamb did the same, staring dumbfounded at Ran Deng—specifically at the flame between her eyebrows—as crystalline drool leaked from the corner of its mouth. Snapping back to his senses a second later, he gulped the drool back down with a hard swallow and indignantly jerked his head away from Ran Deng. Yet the instant he spotted the identical covetous expression in the white lamb's eyes, the little black lamb lunged forward and took a vicious bite, tearing off another clump of its fleece.

The two lambs descended into a brawl once more—or rather, the little black lamb unilaterally bullied the skewered white lamb kebab.

Watching Ran Deng's actions, however, Zhou Hui's expression turned stern:

—"Wait! Ran Deng, what are you doing?!"

—"Using soul fire to refine the palace lantern, then using the lantern's flame to refine the command tablet,"-Ran Deng explained.

This was the method the white lamb had proposed.

Having come this far, Ran Deng understood that relying solely on herself, this was the only viable path to refine the command tablet.

—"Do you have any idea that once this begins, it cannot be stopped?!"-Zhou Hui warned sternly.-"At that point, refining the command tablet will be a trivial matter; the true trouble will be that very lantern!"

—"...Once the lantern is ignited, I must refine the lantern to completion as well, correct?"-Ran Deng said.

Ran Deng understood perfectly:

Refining the command tablet would only require a day or two. As long as her actions were swift, she could seize total control of this 'experimental facility' and rescue everyone before those children suffered harm.

Yet because her cultivation fell short, she had to ignite the palace lantern with her soul fire to amplify her strength and refine the tablet.

Once ignited by soul fire, the lantern could no longer be treated like ordinary spiritual energy, where letting the flame go out carried no consequence.

The soul fire would burn ceaselessly, and the lantern would undergo continuous refinement. This process would relentlessly temper Ran Deng's spiritual energy, meridians, and dantian over and over, until the lantern became an inseparable part of her very being.

In other words, for the sake of a command tablet that took only a couple of days to refine, Ran Deng was committing to an immensely protracted ordeal to refine a lantern.

This was precisely why Ran Deng had rejected 'Eldest Senior Brother's' suggestion earlier without a second thought.

—"Precisely,"-Zhou Hui warned with absolute severity.-"The material for that palace lantern I gave you is Thousand-Year Yarrow, which Patriarch Jiuzhai spent nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine days refining by gathering the power of the Heavenly Dao. If you merely temper it with spiritual energy, you can halt at any moment even if you fail! But if you ignite it with soul fire, you will not be able to sleep or eat for at least nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine days, unable to cease for even a single breath—"

—"I know!"-Ran Deng said.-"Master, when you first gave this to me, this was what you truly wished for in your heart, wasn't it? But you knew I couldn't... no, you knew I was a slacker who would never want to do it, so you set the requirement at a mere 'passing grade.'"

As Ran Deng spoke, she lifted her gaze.

Her eyes were swollen and bloodshot from crying, unable to recover in the short term, yet the resolve blazing within them was firmer than it had ever been in her life.

—"This time, I want to score full marks."

[I... don't want to be a hindrance holding Master back ever again!]

[This time, I want to protect everyone who once protected me and allowed me to live through seven lifetimes of happiness!]

***

—"What on earth happened to Ah-Ran?"-Zhou Hui subsequently questioned the little black lamb.

In the end, he had not stopped Ran Deng—nor was there any need to stop her. It was rare enough for his disciple to desire self-improvement; what reason was there to stand in her way?

Yet Zhou Hui could not help feeling concerned.

He had no idea where Ran Deng's fierce resolve had originated. If she had merely been influenced by the Ascension Stairway into making a reckless, impulsive choice, matters would be dire.

Nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine days!

Even Zhou Hui viewed that as an arduous trial. For Ran Deng, who couldn't even survive a single day without sleeping, this trial was hardly about refining a palace lantern—it was practically refining her own soul and body.

Zhou Hui simply could not imagine his third disciple enduring such an ordeal, let alone volunteering for it.

The little black lamb was clearly incredulous as well.

He shook his head vigorously, then planted a hoof onto the other half of his soul, questioning whether Ran Deng had been bewitched by this creature.

—"No, Ah-Ran does not appear to be under external control,"-Zhou Hui remarked, casting a worried look toward her.

Ah-Ran had already ignited the palace lantern with her soul fire. A flame blazed brilliantly at the center of her brow, the overflowing sparks forming a bead-like chain of light that tethered directly to the flame within the palatial lantern.

Observing the pure, unblemished radiance of this soul fire was proof enough that Ran Deng had not been corrupted by any sorcery.

Yet this spiritual connection also dictated that over the next nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine days, Ran Deng would have to keep the flame burning while refining each stalk of yarrow grass woven into the lantern one by one, transmuting the entire palace lantern into an extension of her soul fire.

Could she truly endure it?

Zhou Hui harbored genuine concern; after all, this was the same Ran Deng whose indolence had remained unchanged across seven lifetimes.

Yet Ran Deng appeared completely set on her course. At this moment, she methodically laid down a protective ward around the cauldron to prevent any living creature—including the little black lamb—from disturbing her, before settling into a cross-legged meditative posture to commence refining the command tablet.

When the moment came to begin, however, she grew slightly anxious:

—"Master, if I run out of time, what will happen to those children—"

—"Do not worry. Take your time,"-Zhou Hui reassured her.

Though he was concerned, whenever his disciples resolved upon a course of action—so long as it was reasonable—he would render his utmost support.

This time was no different.

Zhou Hui raised his hand, expanding the perimeter of his spiritual projection.

Prior to this, Yuwen Xuan and Ran Deng could only perceive Zhou Hui alone. Now, the scenery unfolding behind Zhou Hui was laid bare before their sight, causing their eyes to widen in shock.

Across the Ascension Stairway surrounding Zhou Hui, diverse characters and runes were densely inscribed, coalescing into a colossal formation.

While the disciples understood Master would never idle during their absence, they hadn't imagined Zhou Hui would take things to such an astronomical extent. One could scarcely fathom how he had inscribed such intricate, esoteric runes in such a brief span of time.

Zhou Hui stood atop these inscriptions, spiritual energy flowing out from where his feet rested, coursing along the steps to ignite every single rune into a dazzling glow.

Beneath his boots lay the sole remaining unlit glyph—

The core of the formation array.

This was precisely why Zhou Hui had not immediately teleported to Ran Deng's side upon seeing her weep. In the past, had he seen Ran Deng crying like that, he would never have settled for a mere projection. But at present, the spiritual power of the entire formation was being anchored by Zhou Hui. Had he left for Ran Deng's location, while his efforts wouldn't be completely ruined, a significant portion of his accumulated energy would be lost.

Seeing this, Ran Deng felt secretly relieved that she had dissuaded Master from rushing over, yet found herself perplexed:

—"Master, what are you doing...?"

—"Because conducting research on the Ascension Stairway in a place like this is far too inefficient, not to mention exceptionally hazardous,"-Zhou Hui explained.-"Although my celestial thunder eradicated the demons here, it simultaneously created a vacuum of demonic energy in this vicinity, which will soon draw in denser demonic miasma and other monsters. Therefore, I made a small decision: I intend to pack up this entire place and take it with me."

Pack it up and take it with him...

Ran Deng glanced at the little black lamb.

She suddenly realized that Eldest Senior Brother's hoarding compulsion wasn't without precedent.

Reflecting on it carefully, Master's track record of 'packing up and carting away' ancient landmarks was extensive: the Thought Pavilion last time, White Jade Capital, and now the Ascension Stairway.

Carefree Palace was going to die of sheer apoplexy!

—"Eh? In that case, did I just meddle unnecessarily?"-Ran Deng asked belatedly.

A moment ago, her head had been filled with thoughts of scoring full marks, yet Master didn't even need her to...

—"No, I need it! Very much so!"-Zhou Hui naturally would not pour cold water over an earnest child at a moment like this. Gazing at Ran Deng with expectant eyes, he said,-"Although I can pack it up and cart it away, doing so will consume substantial karmic fortune and strength, and I have yet to determine a suitable place to house it. But if you can obtain control over this area, perhaps I can set a far loftier standard—"

—"Perhaps we can return it to its original position!"

—"Its original position?"-Ran Deng echoed, subconsciously glancing toward the heavens above.

—"Indeed. Therefore, the restoration of this Ascension Stairway rests upon your shoulders!"-Zhou Hui said with a gentle smile.

—"Master..." [Suddenly, the pressure is immense.]-Ran Deng's lips twitched.

This time she truly had no intention of slacking off, but this was the Ascension Stairway! The Ascension Stairway!

Entrusting a Golden Core cultivator with the responsibility of restoring the position of the Ascension Stairway—was that really wise?!

—"Do not fret; take your time,"-Zhou Hui said in his characteristically calm, measured cadence.-"I share this with you not to rush you, but to ensure you do not worry about those children or anything else lingering here. The characters of my formation can temporarily suspend the flow of time within this space. All you need to do now is focus your concentration, refine that command tablet, and secure ownership of this domain."

—"...Yes,"-Ran Deng nodded.

Indeed, what was she fretting over?

Regardless of the path she chose, Master would always have prepared contingency plans for every conceivable scenario; she would never need to worry about losing her way.

Ah, it was precisely because of this that she had been spoiled rotten across seven lifetimes!

With that thought, Ran Deng concentrated her mind entirely, tuning out all external sounds.

The flame upon her forehead blazed with even greater luminescence.

Watching Ran Deng's actions, Zhou Hui refrained from disturbing her further, instructing the little black lamb instead:

—"Guard your junior sister well."

—"Ying." [I *am* guarding her properly!]

—"Furthermore, do not allow your other half to cause trouble,"-Zhou Hui added.

Accompanying his admonition, an object tumbled from his projection onto the stone floor. While Zhou Hui himself was bound to the array and unable to cross the distance in person, teleporting a small item across the projection was well within his capabilities.

It was a muzzle.

One that Guan Ye had forged on a previous occasion, which Zhou Hui had reduced down to the size of a lamb.

Seeing the muzzle, the little black lamb was startled at first. But the moment he realized it was not intended for him, an expression of malicious glee spread across his ovine features. Clamping it gleefully between his teeth, he bounded toward the little white lamb.

Turning his head, Zhou Hui cast another concerned glance at Ran Deng's back—resembling a parent seeing their child off into the examination hall. Moments later, a faint, proud smile graced his lips.

***

What?

Did Ran Deng succeed?

That Ascension Stairway, hanging lofty in the firmament and visible once more to all across the [Five Domains and Six Abysses](# "Literal CN: 五境六渊 (wǔjìng liùyuān) — the complete mortal and subterranean cosmos"), told the entire story.
