# Chapter 232: Divine Authority

—"Bujuan, are you alright?"-Peilan asked nervously.

His gaze wavered slightly, subconsciously drifting to the side, simply unable to look directly upon Zhou Hui's true form.

There was no helping it; at this moment, Zhou Hui was far too sacred.

Yes, sacred!

Ever since that bizarre chewing sound began echoing, the surrounding power had begun surging, pouring into Zhou Hui's body. And that power was so pure and concentrated that had anyone else touched even a droplet, they would have instantly exploded and perished.

Even Zhou Hui, having absorbed that power, could not digest it all in a single instant, causing it to leak outward like a radiant aura of sacred brilliance—so dazzling that even Mahayana cultivators could not bear to look upon him directly.

This, of course, was hardly a good thing.

As a renowned physician in the cultivation realm, Peilan had witnessed far too many instances where absorbing excessive spiritual energy resulted in a cultivator's foundation being utterly ruined. He knew all too well the peril Zhou Hui was facing. [Even in the most extreme hypothetical scenario](# "Literal CN: taking ten thousand steps back (退一万步说)"), even if Bujuan managed to absorb all of that power, it remained uncertain whether Bujuan or the divine power would end up in the driver's seat.

In fact, the overflow of Zhou Hui's power had already begun displaying distinctly ominous tendencies.

Out of the corner of his eye, Peilan could see light coalescing above Zhou Hui's crown into a dazzling, magnificent, sacred corona. Yet around that corona, boughs resembling tree branches sprouted and extended upward, bearing luminous beads resembling leaves and blossoms that drifted down in soft, scattering showers.

And beneath the hem of his robes, energy tangled into thick clusters of light—resembling tentacles, yet even more akin to the taproots of an ancient primordial tree—locking firmly onto the sea of spiritual energy below, insatiably demanding ever more power beyond all control.

All of this caused irreversible changes to Zhou Hui's physical vessel.

Leaving aside how Zhou Hui's originally fair skin had begun emitting a pearlescent white luminescence, shrouding his surroundings in a luminous veil that masked his expressions—be they gentle or freezing cold—behind it, what was far more lethal was his silver-white hair. It had completely fused with the energy, billowing behind him like the vast aerial roots of a titanic plant, binding him directly into the surrounding fabric of space.

—"He won't turn into a tree like the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, will he?"-Tao Zhu mumbled worriedly.

Back when the high-tier cultivators of this realm held private gatherings, they used to gossip behind his back: with Bujuan's peerless cultivation and terrifying power, failing ascension over and over, could it be that his connection to the world was simply too deep, and that he might eventually turn into a giant tree? Who could have imagined that today, those idle words might actually become [a careless prediction coming true as a grim curse](# "Literal CN: a casual remark turning into an omen/prophecy (一语成谶)")?

Realizing this, Tao Zhu felt his scalp tingle.

Cherishing his "deep friendship" with Bujuan was certainly one factor, but at this very moment, for reasons unknown, what popped into Tao Zhu's mind were the smiling faces of Bujuan's eldest and youngest disciples.

For various reasons, among all of Bujuan's disciples, the ones Tao Zhu had dealt with the most were Yuwen Xuan and Mo Xi. Though he did not know—and certainly had no desire to know—what Bujuan's other disciples were like, the sheer conduct of just these two had already made him [keep a polite but terrified distance](# "Literal CN: revering from afar but unable to comply (敬而敏/敬而不敏)"). Zhou Hui had once tried to defend them to Tao Zhu, claiming that among his disciples, only Yuwen Xuan was a bit overly eccentric while Mo Xi was simply shy around strangers, which was why those two were somewhat impolite toward outsiders—an explanation that had only given Tao Zhu an even worse stomachache. He could not fathom what Bujuan's flock of disciples would do if their master ended up turning into a tree in a godforsaken place like this.

—"What should we do?"-Tao Zhu asked, looking around in a panic.

He had noticed that it was only after that eerie chewing sound began that Bujuan started assimilating with the power. Coupled with what Bujuan had mentioned earlier—that he had briefly entertained the notion of taking a seat at the table to dine—even though Bujuan had shaken off that urge, for some reason that chewing sound had substituted for Bujuan, devouring the meal and converting it into power.

Tao Zhu believed he had found the solution:

—"We just need to interrupt the 'feeding', right?!"-he declared, stepping forward to overturn the low table.

—"Don't move recklessly!"-Jiu Zhai barked at that moment.

It was hard to imagine that his shriveled, dried-orange body could produce such a thunderous shout, startling both Tao Zhu and Peilan into a stupor.

—"Are you certain you want to interrupt this?"-Jiu Zhai sternly interrogated the two.-"Right now Bujuan is ascending... no, not merely ascending, he could become a god in a single bound! At such a momentous juncture, are you sure you want to interrupt?"

—"But if this continues, he will be overtaken by the power and lose his human emotions..."-Peilan hesitated.

—"No, Bujuan is assimilating with the power splendidly; that the power is being absorbed by him is the best proof,"-Jiu Zhai refuted.-"As for you feeling he has become distant and unfamiliar, that is a necessary 'sacrifice'. If one chooses to become a deity, one must inevitably cast something aside—human desires, for instance."

Tao Zhu and Peilan grumbled inwardly at this lecture about having to [extinguish human desires](# "Literal CN: eliminate human desire (灭人欲) — referencing the Neo-Confucian doctrine of preserving heavenly principles and eradicating human desires"):

[...As if Bujuan hasn't extinguished his desires enough already!]

[Ever since this guy set foot upon the immortal path, has he had any desire other than cultivating?]

[Even that tiny sliver of humanity in his later years was reserved solely for his disciples!]

Yet having been lectured by Jiu Zhai like that, they hesitated once more.

In their stereotypical impression, Bujuan had always been obsessed with ascension—after all, they had known Bujuan for thousands of years, and Zhou Hui had only recently given up on ascending. If there was a method to ascend by shedding emotions, who could guarantee Bujuan wouldn't experiment on himself? Not to mention [ascending to the heavens in a single bound](# "Literal CN: reaching the heavens in one step (一步登天)") into godhood. Since the other party was very likely doing this "voluntarily," would they truly be ruining his grand fortune?

Regarding this, only Sikong Yao harbored some skepticism:

—"Bujuan... does he really want to become a god?"-he asked.

Without a second thought, Jiu Zhai replied:

—"Nonsense! No one can refuse—"

—"No. I don't want to,"-a voice spoke.

The voice did not come from Zhou Hui's physical body, but emanated from a luminous bead that had drifted down from him.

—"My apologies. If I speak through my true form, it might harm you, so I can only converse like this,"-the luminous bead said.-"However, Jiu Zhai, do not make decisions on my behalf. I have no desire to become a god, and for the current phase, I have already abandoned ascension."

Yet Jiu Zhai remained unconvinced:

—"Are you certain you are Bujuan's true consciousness, and not the humanity he stripped away?"-he questioned.

[—"Then allow me to prove it."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

[—"Please be careful."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

As the luminous bead spoke, the sacred, radiant "true form" seemed to shift its pupils ever so slightly, casting its gaze upon the group.

In that instant, everyone's minds went completely blank.

By the time their senses returned, they found themselves kneeling before Bujuan.

However, they suffered no harm. On the contrary, their power surged to the brim, receiving a substantial elevation. This sensation of surging power—so extraordinarily rare at high cultivation stages—produced an almost addictive rush, making one feel that even becoming Bujuan's devout believer would be a small price to pay to experience it again.

Fortunately, Zhou Hui the "person" harbored no such inclination, and the companions present were far too familiar with him, granting them some degree of immunity and allowing them to quickly regain their composure.

Through this, Peilan and Tao Zhu also confirmed that Bujuan could indeed master this terrifying power.

Peilan actually felt a pang of regret:

—"If that is the case, why abandon godhood?"-he asked.

—"I do not possess the awareness of a deity. Furthermore, I do not believe living beings truly need gods,"-Zhou Hui spoke through the luminous bead.

Peilan wore a baffled expression, clearly failing to understand.

Tao Zhu, on the other hand, expressed complete understanding:

—"Makes sense! I've felt that way for a long time,"-Tao Zhu chimed in.-"Someone like you, who goes into seclusion for hundreds or thousands of years at the drop of a hat, barely interacts with people, and couldn't even figure out how to be an official in the mortal realm—if you ascended to the Celestial Realm just to hole up and cultivate for spiritual energy, fine. But if you went and took office in the Heavenly Court, governing the futures of all living beings across the three thousand worlds, just thinking about it is terrifying! Let alone becoming a god on top of that—that would make anyone fear for the future of every world! Now that you've seen through it yourself, very good, very good."

[It feels like you're not just roasting Bujuan, you've insulted ninety percent of the immortals and gods across both the cultivation world and the Heavenly Court!]

Peilan rolled his eyes.

—"Then, Bujuan, what do you plan to do?"-Peilan asked Zhou Hui's... luminous bead.

He still could not look directly at Zhou Hui's true form.

Zhou Hui also felt his current state was rather cumbersome:

—"In theory, I ought to redistribute this power. After all, it was never meant to be held by an individual, but returned to the various worlds. However, recklessly releasing it all at once without fully understanding the situation could easily cause a catastrophe. We should conduct a more rigorous data analysis..."-Zhou Hui said.

[—"Get to the point!"](# "Speaker: Peilan")

[—"You've performed spiritual channeling surgeries before, haven't you? I need your help to siphon and channel this power out."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"I can help, but power of this magnitude..."-Peilan naturally would not refuse, though he hesitated.

—"I will provide you with the methodology; it only requires some simple procedures on your part,"-Zhou Hui said.

More luminous beads gathered before Peilan, condensing into dense arrays of text.

Peilan felt a headache coming on just looking at it.

He knew Bujuan's "simple" was never actually simple.

Yet as he examined the text closely, he immediately became completely absorbed. [Birds of a feather flock together](# "Literal CN: things of a kind come together (物以类聚)"); Peilan, after all, was a wild fox who had cultivated a Dao Bone entirely on his own. Given an opportunity to understand and treat a "god," Peilan would grit his teeth and try even if it killed him.

—"There is one more matter,"-Zhou Hui continued.

By this point, Peilan was completely deaf to outside words, though Zhou Hui had not been addressing him in the first place.

Several luminous beads circled around Tao Zhu and the others, sighing:

—"In my current state, I am unable to move. I fear I must ask you all to search for Ran Zhen."

—"Ah?"-Tao Zhu gasped.

Clearly, he had forgotten their original purpose once again.

Small wonder; ever since entering this mystic realm, incident after incident had piled up, making Ran Zhen seem rather insignificant by comparison.

Of course, that being said, leaving him unfound was out of the question.

Leaving aside past ties with the Ran clan, even purely for the sake of his third disciple, Zhou Hui had to find him.

—"Wait, are *we* going?"-Sikong Yao raised an objection, casting a sidelong glance at Tao Zhu and Jiu Zhai.

—"You got a problem with that?"-Tao Zhu asked, puffing out his belly.

Of course Sikong Yao had a problem.

From the very beginning, he had felt that this "mystic realm exploration team"—aside from Bujuan and Ruwang—was a motley crew of old men, fat men, and bald-feathered geezers. It hardly looked like a proper expedition team. And sure enough, look how long they had been in the mystic realm! Ruwang and Bujuan were both incapacitated from advancing for various reasons, and now the team's only healer had to stay behind. That left two... no, one and a half (Jiu Zhai was merely an avatar, so he only counted as half). How was a squad of one and a half combat-useless deadweights supposed to press forward?!

Yet before Sikong Yao could voice his complaints, Tao Zhu beat him to the punch with sheer disgust.

He clearly shared Sikong Yao's sentiments:

—"I don't want to go either! Setting aside Jiu Zhai the [deadweight](# "Literal CN: oil-drag bottle (拖油瓶) — colloquial term for a burdensome dependent or baggage"), I can't stomach a Carefree Palace grandmaster! Anyone who has survived in the Abyssal Ocean knows that the more desperate the situation, the less you should cooperate with Fallen Immortal Palace—Carefree Palace—or you won't even know how you died!"-Not to mention this guy held a grudge against Bujuan.

—"I have already severed ties with Carefree Palace!"-Sikong Yao defended himself, though he immediately felt a pang of regret—speaking like that made it sound as though he actually wanted to go.

—"Even if you're not with Carefree Palace, that doesn't guarantee you're on our side. Even setting everything else aside, your character is completely unreliable,"-Tao Zhu continued ranting.-"The moment danger strikes, you'll definitely abandon us and run for your life!"

[...I want to abandon you right now.]

Sikong Yao thought.

—"Enough arguing,"-Zhou Hui intervened.

[—"Boss Tao, you can actually place your trust in Lord Sikong. His greatest virtue is that he is not short-sighted. Between immediate danger and a catastrophic future, he has always known how to choose. In that regard, you needn't worry about betrayal from him."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

[Is that a threat?]

[That's definitely a threat!]

[If I don't do this, I'll be facing a 'catastrophic future', won't I?!]

Sikong Yao looked toward the "deity" in sheer terror, his soul stung by the divine light, forcing him to avert his gaze in haste.

Zhou Hui then reassured Sikong Yao:

—"Lord Sikong, you can also place your trust in Boss Tao. As the master of the Abyssal Ocean Society, his combat power... even if his combat strength isn't much to speak of, his luck stat is indisputable."

—"Wait a minute, Bujuan! What kind of talk is that?! You make it sound like everything I have came from dumb luck!"-Tao Zhu protested.

Yet Sikong Yao was genuinely swayed.

Luck.

That was precisely what Sikong Yao had always craved, but never possessed.

Putting it that way, it was true! Sikong Yao did not have deep ties with Tao Zhu, but he knew that ever since Tao Zhu [made his debut](# "Literal CN: came out of seclusion / debuted (出道) — humorous pop-idol phrasing applied to a cultivator") in the cultivation world, he had possessed a golden toad in his belly. It was that golden toad that ensured Tao Zhu never lacked spirit stones on his cultivation journey, sailing smoothly—a cultivation path every cultivator dreamed of. Aside from his less-than-flattering physique, and precisely because of that physique, almost nobody ever suspected there was a golden toad in his belly. People even pitied him for being possessed by a demonic beast, and nobody ever coveted his power.

Sikong Yao felt a pang of envy; he too longed for such a smooth-sailing life.

Still, having a companion with that attribute made accepting such a deadweight considerably easier.

Zhou Hui finally addressed Jiu Zhai:

—"Jiu Zhai, since you chose this world and chose to stand by my side, I believe you will assist us, correct?"

Jiu Zhai fell silent for a moment.

He bowed his head reverently toward Zhou Hui's true form:

—"Since this is the command of a deity, even the Celestial Emperor would not dare disobey. What does a humble divination cultivator like me count for?"

[—"I told you, I am not becoming a god."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"..."-Jiu Zhai feigned deafness, taking the initiative to ask:—"Then, how shall we find Ran Zhen?"

—"With the power I currently wield, it is sufficient to see through every checkpoint of this mystic realm, including Ran Zhen's condition,"-Zhou Hui replied.-"I can send you to Ran Zhen's vicinity."

—"Then why don't you bring him back directly..."-Tao Zhu asked strangely.

Yet he shut his mouth immediately.

Because the luminous beads drifting from Zhou Hui's true form coalesced into a light screen, displaying the condition of Ran Zhen and his party.

If the banquet atop the sea of clouds resembled an intoxicating celestial paradise, then the place where Ran Zhen and the others were located was pure hell.

Blood mist churned everywhere. Hideous demonic abominations prowled about, occasionally slurping a mouthful of fresh blood from a living person or tearing off a chunk of flesh. The only "humans" there were Ran Zhen and several members of Dongli Courtyard. With hollow, glazed eyes, they gathered around a sacrificial altar-like structure, fanatically stacking pebbles onto the altar as if building something, utterly disregarding that their bodies were mangled, torn, and covered in wounds.

—"Do they still have consciousness? No... are they even alive?"-Sikong Yao asked in dread.

—"Still alive, but if this continues, it's hard to say,"-Zhou Hui said, frowning.

He shouldn't have trusted a single word from that "Heavenly Dao script."

Because Ran Deng's bloodline technique indicated that her father was still alive, and the "Heavenly Dao script" stated that Ran Zhen had opened the mystic realm and sent invitations to cultivators across the realm to explore it, Zhou Hui had assumed the time had not yet arrived, and that Ran Zhen would be living well until then. Thus he had not investigated deeply. But looking at this situation, in no way could it be called "living well."

As for why Ran Zhen was still alive in the script, having obtained divine power, Zhou Hui could now see through the underlying "settings" behind the script:

First, Zhou Hui realized he had underestimated the role of Siwu from the Court of Ceremonies.

He had assumed Siwu received Ran Zhen's message and went to the Abyssal Ocean to inspect the ruins merely to die there and deliver the Immortal Slaying Sword to the "protagonist," serving as foreshadowing for the annihilation of the Heavenly Spirit Sect. But the fall of a Mahayana cultivator just one step away from ascension could never be so simple. The other purpose of Siwu's death was to mark the entrance of the mystic realm, establishing a link between the sealed mystic realm and the outside world.

And that link was Ran Zhen and Ran Deng.

The Ran clan, because of their short lifespans throughout generations, had been forced to adopt a special cultivation inheritance technique that linked their soul-fire. As long as the Ran bloodline was not extinguished, the soul-fire would never go out.

Thus, Zhou Hui could sense through Ran Deng's soul-fire that her father was alive. Conversely, as long as Ran Deng was alive, she continuously sustained Ran Zhen's life via the soul-fire. In fact, if necessary, one could kill Ran Deng to "resurrect" Ran Zhen!

In this way, the reason why Ran Deng—who never caused trouble, was quite attractive, and was a rare array grandmaster—was mysteriously assigned the role of a villainous female extra completely unrelated to her, was excluded from the "protagonist's harem," and was fated to die, was finally explained!

Thinking back, even in the Heavenly Dao script, it only wrote that Ran Zhen sought revenge, without mentioning any other members of Dongli Courtyard; clearly, they had all perished in the mystic realm.

Zhou Hui felt a pang of chagrin at his own oversight.

Regrettably, he could not personally go and rescue them to make up for the mistake:

[—"If I spared no expense, I could pull them out immediately. But the demonic qi there is far too severe. With my current power, touching even a speck would cause immediate corruption..."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"So, we just need to go in and bring them out?"-Sikong Yao grasped the crux immediately.

He seemed to share an unspoken (and entirely one-sided) rapport with Zhou Hui.

—"Precisely,"-Zhou Hui agreed.-"I can send you nearby, and as long as you escort them out of the contaminated zone, I can bring you all back to a safe place."

This plan was far safer than trekking blindly across an incomprehensible mystic realm. Sikong Yao and Tao Zhu exchanged glances and nodded in agreement.

Without wasting words, Zhou Hui teleported the three away in an instant.

All at once, the vast, empty cloud terrace was left with only Zhou Hui and Peilan, along with that irritating chewing sound.

Amid the chewing sound, the delicacies upon the sacrificial altar were nearly devoured clean.

Zhou Hui suddenly caught himself musing:

[Speaking of which, the mountain gate hasn't been renovated in quite some time.]

Especially that peach tree before the mountain gate.

That peach tree looked ancient and colossal, but it actually possessed no extraordinary heritage; it was an ordinary mortal tree.

Back when Zhou Hui chose the valley of Heavenly Spirit Mountain to establish the sect, he had thought about chopping it down to plant some rarer spiritual trees. However, he discovered the spiritual energy in that patch of soil was chaotic—spiritual trees struggled to grow there, let alone mortal trees. Yet that peach tree seed had somehow taken root in that hostile earth, driving its taproots downward, struggling desperately to survive. Zhou Hui felt a pang of compassion. Furthermore, that year's peaches had tasted quite good; Yuwen Xuan had eaten with peach juice smearing his entire mouth, clamoring that he wanted to eat them forever. Thus Zhou Hui kept the peach tree, diverted a spiritual vein to nourish it, and carefully watered it with spirit springs, allowing it to flourish, grow tall and massive—to the point where hanging dozens of people from a single branch was no trouble at all, earning praise even from the wood-spirit elders of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree.

Regrettably, a mortal tree was a mortal tree after all; no matter how hard it tried, the peach tree never developed spiritual sentience.

Having obtained divine power now, Zhou Hui suddenly thought that sparing a tiny sliver for that peach tree wouldn't be bad. Even if it could not grant the peach tree sapience, he could develop other functionalities for it.

For instance: binding capabilities, entangling capabilities, illusion-crafting capabilities... letting a certain [famished ghost](# "Literal CN: hungry ghost / starved ghost (饿死鬼) — referring to someone with an insatiable appetite") who hung from the tree complaining of boredom taste what it felt like to constantly eat, eat, eat, until he was literally stuffed to death!

The "deity's" malice was so vivid and palpable that Peilan, engrossed in reading, shivered violently.

Peilan stroked his balding, bristling tail, looking left and right. Unable to look at Zhou Hui's true form directly, he called out tremulously:

[—"B-Bujuan?"](# "Speaker: Peilan")

—"Yes?"-Zhou Hui returned to his senses in an instant, asking gently:—"Are you ready?"

—"...Ready,"-Peilan didn't dare ask further. Heaven only knew what heinous fiend could provoke such raw malice from the perpetually even-tempered Bujuan! He merely confirmed repeatedly:—"Are you truly going to do that?"

Peilan had just skimmed through the "treatment plan" provided by Zhou Hui.

Though he couldn't say he had completely mastered it, he could tell that Bujuan, just as he had claimed, had zero attachment to divine authority and power. In fact, he looked upon it with distaste, almost eager to disperse it immediately.

Peilan was rather worried about this, advising:

—"Even if you don't want it now, you shouldn't distribute it. Just siphon it out and store it."

—"I *am* going to store it! Right now the data is insufficient, and I don't know who to grant it to,"-Zhou Hui said, lifting his head to gaze upward.-"However, someone is scheming after this power, and someone is scheming after our world. That is why I decided to store the bulk of it while channeling a portion outward. Besides, doing so reduces the burden on me."

Peilan disagreed:

—"Yet divine power is different from spiritual energy. It is a form of power, but it is also a type of permission. If you distribute it to others, even just a tiny sliver, they will obtain divine authority and cause endless strife. If you ask me, if you absolutely must siphon it off to someone, you should select strictly from among your disciples."

—"If I do that, wouldn't I still be a 'god'? A god who determines the fate of others,"-Zhou Hui said. He paused, then added:—"That is precisely how the Heavenly Court partitioned things."

Having obtained divine power, combined with intelligence regarding the "Genesis Machine," Zhou Hui now understood a great many things.

For example: "permissions."

On that machine, in order to better distribute and suppress the energy of the cosmic explosion, the various functions inherently required certain clearance permissions based on operational duty. However, the "passwords" of an advanced civilization were not rigid numeric codes. Furthermore, such a facility could not be permanently guarded by personnel; only if a universe-level extinction event occurred would they risk sending people back into the epicenter of such an explosion. To prevent future generations from losing the password, the advanced civilization adopted a more flexible and dynamic "key": as long as a "suitable sentient lifeform" fulfilled the criteria, they would obtain certain clearance permissions—which became known as "divine authority."

Back during the era of the Demon Court, things were somewhat simpler. The demons were rather straightforward: strength ruled supreme.

Though they also discovered the existence of divine authority, they primarily focused on fulfilling the criteria for permissions. Whoever held the permissions was a god, the king of the Demon Court, the big boss. They did not prevent weaker demons from striving toward it.

Once the human race took power, however, human scheming multiplied.

The Heavenly Court believed that the conditions for achieving permissions should never be revealed to outsiders. If a primordial god happened to inadvertently meet the conditions and grasp the rules, the Heavenly Court would [co-opt and assimilate](# "Literal CN: grant imperial amnesty / pacify (招安/诏安)") them if possible and strip their permissions; if they could not be co-opted, they were eliminated. Even if it meant losing the chance to learn the criteria, they would never allow others to know that permissions even existed. And the high-ranking figures of the Heavenly Court who held permissions created "secondary permissions"—namely, True Immortals who cultivated through celestial energy. They further stripped down some basic functions into spiritual energy and cultivators. And of course, that was not the bottom tier; the lowest tier were mortals with zero permissions. By downgrading permissions layer by layer, the Heavenly Court's supremacy became impregnable, enduring for myriad generations.

Reflecting on this, Zhou Hui felt only bitter irony.

—"Everyone's destiny ought to be placed in their own hands,"-Zhou Hui said.

—"Is that so?"-Peilan flicked his tail. As a member of the demon race—the most 'egalitarian' of races—and as a physician who was supposed to be the most benevolent, he held a different view:—"Yet you do not know how low living beings can sink. In order to survive, driven by desire, the sheer absurdity of the choices they can make would astound you."

It was not surprising for Peilan to say this; although the fall of the Demon Court was partly due to the rise of the human race, ultimately it was because of their own doing.

Zhou Hui smiled:

—"That is why I haven't handed over all the power! Because before they exercise those permissions, they must be taught their responsibilities and obligations."

[—"Teach them until they master it!"](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui | Literal CN: teach until they know how / teach until it clicks (教到会为止) — Professor Zhou Hui's pedagogical motto")

—"Since you put it that way,"-Peilan sighed, harboring a sliver of curiosity as to what outcome Zhou Hui's choice would bring:—"Then let us begin!"
