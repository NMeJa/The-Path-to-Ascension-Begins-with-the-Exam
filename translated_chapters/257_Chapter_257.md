# Chapter 257: Three Hundred Years Later (Part 8)

—"Realm Protector Li, what are you reading?!"-Master of Automata Ji asked excitedly.

He was still clutching the newly purchased spellcraft journals tightly to his chest.

Although the vendor had thrown in an exquisite storage bangle because Ji Gongbu had subscribed to various newspapers, periodicals, and magazines for over a century in a single transaction—a bangle spacious enough to store every volume he purchased and capable of summoning a translucent screen at any moment to search and read any section of his archived collection—Ji Gongbu simply preferred the tactile sensation of reading physical paper. He felt it was far better suited for repeated flipping and study, particularly the paper on spatial spellcraft, which struck right at the heart of his pain points.

Earlier, while riding the large-scale teleportation array, questions had bubbled up in Ji Gongbu's mind one after another. A few had found answers, but those very answers had spawned even more questions, leaving him itching to immediately pay a visit to the paper's author—

Ran Deng... Ran?

He recalled that the Department of Works once had a staggeringly brilliant senior formation grandmaster who had presided over the monumental project of [Severing Heaven and Earth](# "Literal CN: 绝地天通 (jué dì tiān tōng) — ancient mythical project cutting off passage between the mortal and celestial realms") to isolate the upper and lower realms. Regrettably, for reasons unknown, that senior had abruptly ceased appearing in public. Rumors claimed he had offended Lord Tian Yan and was banished; other rumors claimed he had been reassigned to oversee a classified megaproject.

[Judging by this, it's likely the latter? The surname 'Ran' is hardly common, and to also be a formation grandmaster—even if not direct kin, she must belong to a collateral branch.]

Ji Gongbu pondered.

Emotionally, he was inclined to believe the latter—after all, if even a senior who had rendered monumental service could be [slaughtered like a donkey once it leaves the millstone](# "Literal CN: 卸磨杀驴 (xiè mò shā lǘ) — discarding someone after their usefulness expires"), his subconscious recoiled from the prospect of ever facing a similar fate.

Consequently, Ji Gongbu grew all the more convinced of the conclusion he and his colleagues had reached during their gossipy discussions: whether it was the large-scale teleportation array or this minor world, both were classified megaprojects orchestrated by the upper echelons of the Heavenly Court. They were almost certainly connected to the brewing succession struggle for the imperial throne, perhaps even involving the new governance policies of the next Celestial Emperor.

As a middling, low-ranking celestial official in the Department of Works, Ji Gongbu rationally wished to steer clear of such perilous entanglements. Yet he knew staying uninvolved was virtually impossible. Within the Celestial Court, playing the neutral faction was no easy feat—especially for someone of humble standing. Offending neither side invariably meant offending both. Yet picking a side left him completely at a loss. The previous Celestial Emperor had reigned for so long and sired so many offspring that most officials had lost track of how many imperial princes even existed, let alone which one to back. And here, at least, someone had extended an olive branch... hadn't they?

With that thought, Ji Gongbu managed to convince himself.

Without much hesitation, he settled on his allegiance. He decided to write a letter to this 'Mister Ran Deng'... No, that would be far too presumptuous. Perhaps submitting a manuscript to one of these journals first would be wiser? As it happened, he had accumulated quite a few insights into spatial spellcraft that could be compiled into an article—or, as the journals termed it, a "research paper." Whoever had conceived that term, it was infinitely more practical than the Immortal Realm's perennial empty debates and metaphysical sophistry.

Ji Gongbu's thoughts tumbled forward in rapid disarray, instantly outlining several potential paper topics.

As a rare practitioner among celestial officials who dealt with frontline application, bolstered by nearly a millennium of accumulated experience, he was hardly lacking in technical inspiration.

He immediately wanted to grab someone to workshop his topics and organize his thoughts.

Regrettably, the empty heads of his foolish, ignorant colleagues contained very little of practical substance. They were currently engrossed in discussing the wares they had purchased at Wanbao Pavilion, displaying zero interest in delving deeper into academic journals.

Still, it was understandable. Had he not emptied every spirit stone on his person at the Scripture Library and signed several installment-plan contracts, Ji Gongbu doubted he could have restrained his own hands from reaching for his purse either.

This was not because the cultivators were uncultured, but because the array of products at Wanbao Pavilion was overwhelmingly abundant!

Setting aside the myriad cloud chariots, elixir pills, and extradimensional storage spaces, there were dozens of distinct implements dedicated purely to beard grooming! There were even customized care regimens tailored specifically for flowing long beards versus bristling curly beards.

For a celestial official's coin pouch, this was nothing short of an absolute fatality.

The Celestial Realm was wonderful in every way except for the sheer monotony of its goods. After all, the cultivation world had never embraced commercialization, and the Heavenly Court operated on a strict system of planned allocation based on official rank. Take the universally lauded Heavenly Robes: although the weaving maidens' craftsmanship was magnificent and their magical attributes were maxed out, the garments they produced adhered to rigid standards dictated by rank, purpose, and protocol. They neither would nor could casually innovate with new cuts or styles.

Nor did celestial officials innovate much in lifestyle magical treasures.

To be sure, they possessed ample spells and servants to smooth over life's inconveniences, but certain needs were simply beyond their imagination. Beard care, for example—in officialdom, celestial officials faced rigid aesthetic expectations regarding their personal grooming.

Finally, from Ji Gongbu's perspective, he believed the staggering diversity of commodities here was strongly correlated with the existence of spellcraft journals.

It was not that celestial immortals never discussed immortal arts. On the contrary, official and private scholarly discussions were quite plentiful in the Celestial Realm—even the annual Flat Peach Banquet was effectively an academic symposium on lifespan-extending immortal arts.

It was just that once one attained immortality, scholarly discourse gravitated toward the esoteric and metaphysical, drifting into almost purely theoretical abstractions. Practitioners like Ji Gongbu, who actually applied theory to reality, did so mostly because their official duties forced their hands. Compounding this, most celestial officials lacked even combat needs, let alone any drive to innovate daily commodities.

With spellcraft journals, everything changed.

Once a theory was penned into an article, it achieved maximum dissemination. Even if the grandmaster who conceived it had no commercial intent, individuals with keen business acumen would naturally translate it into practical merchandise.

Ji Gongbu had already spotted several newly designed Qiankun storage pouches directly derived from spatial spells published in a recent issue.

Word had it that the patent dividends from such innovations were remarkably handsome.

Ji Gongbu's eyes burned with envy. Amidst a whirlpool of stray thoughts, his creative inspiration surged like a fountain, desperate for an outlet. Unfortunately, he could not simply abandon everything to rush back and draft a paper, nor could he find anyone to bounce ideas off. At last, his gaze settled on Realm Protector Li, who had remained taciturn the entire journey.

Come to think of it, Realm Protector Li had come at Ji Gongbu's invitation.

The two were not particularly close. It was merely that Wanhua Realm's material transport array had long been notoriously finicky; the Department of Works had spent several centuries (in Celestial Realm time) calibrating it, yet Realm Protector Li had never been satisfied. Hearing of this cross-realm large-scale teleportation array, Ji Gongbu thought he might find some inspiration and decided to visit, casually extending an invitation to Realm Protector Li as well.

He hadn't expected Realm Protector Li to attend in person, let alone trail behind them like a muted quail, not uttering a peep. Throughout the excursion, none of the dazzling novelties had piqued the viceroy's interest—until they reached the Scripture Library, where Li Lu casually picked up a book, flipped through it, and abruptly sprang to life, purchasing nearly half the library's stock.

[I had no idea Realm Protector Li was like me—a noble, scholarly soul unswayed by base material desires.]

Ji Gongbu thought, deeply moved, completely oblivious to how he had been drooling over every single item at Wanbao Pavilion only moments ago.

Instantly viewing Realm Protector Li as a kindred spirit, he approached:

—"I noticed you purchased quite a few books at the Scripture Library. You must have topics of interest you'd like to explore... as well?!"

Ji Gongbu jumped in fright.

Seeing Realm Protector Li lift bloodshot eyes, staring unblinkingly at him with a hollow, vacant gaze, he vaguely realized he had probably misunderstood.

Li Lu didn't look like someone who had discovered beloved treasures—he looked possessed!

"..." Sure enough, after holding that eerie stare for a full half-minute, Realm Protector Li abruptly asked:

—"Do you believe in... [unspeakable entities](# "Literal CN: 不可名状的存在 — incomprehensible, indescribable Lovecraftian entities")?"

—"Ah?"-Ji Gongbu blinked.

—"I mean..."-Realm Protector Li swallowed hard, his eyes unfocused, filled with deep-seated terror.-"Incomprehensible, imperceptible. It is clearly right there, saturating the entire space, yet even a Great Golden Immortal cannot pinpoint where it is. But the moment you snap back to your senses, every other living creature in the same room has already been scattered into disjointed chunks across the floor."

—"A ghost story?"-Ji Gongbu asked, taken aback.

By now, he had caught sight of the books Realm Protector Li was clutching. Their thickness bore no resemblance to academic spellcraft journals, and their gaudy, garish covers practically screamed trashy entertainment.

Clutching those volumes tight to his chest, Realm Protector Li stammered:

—"A story?! It's not a story! This is..."-[MONOLOGUE: My own lived experience!]

He shook his head in agony, yet dared not close his eyes, terrified of plunging right back into the nightmare that had haunted him for days.

Yes, over the past dozen-odd days—

A dozen-odd days? According to Celestial Realm calendar calculations, at most three days ago, he had abruptly received an emergency requisition from the Imperial Kitchens. They claimed provisions had run short at the Sky-Tour Banquet held in Xuanpu and demanded massive shipments of spiritual meat and celestial wine, leaving them no choice but to call upon Wanhua Realm—home to the finest beast-taming sects and the largest spirit beast ranches across the Three Thousand Worlds.

Normally, such matters never required a viceroy to handle them in person. But the requested volume was astronomical! Moreover, for a function as momentous as the Sky-Tour Banquet, ingredients were typically prepared well before the Celestial Emperor commenced his tour; running short at the eleventh hour—to the point where even the Imperial Kitchens' reserves were depleted, necessitating emergency purchases—was entirely unprecedented.

Although Realm Protector Li was a parachuted second-generation immortal, he was nowhere near as inept as Ji Gongbu assumed. Sensing something was amiss, he tapped his celestial connections and visited his second uncle, a Vice Minister in the Ministry of Rites, to discreetly gather intelligence.

And with that single step, he walked straight into hell.

The entire Ministry of Rites had become the private hunting ground of an unspeakable monster. Even the Minister of Rites, a supreme Golden Immortal, could neither pinpoint the creature's true form nor escape the opulent, palatial halls of his own ministry.

Even time had frozen within that horrific day. So desperate did the trapped immortals become that a gathering of aloof, refined celestials began exposing one another's darkest secrets, presenting evidence of past crimes, and were forced to enact the very [gu-pot ascension trials](# "Literal CN: 养蛊飞升 (yǎng gǔ fēi shēng) — forcing candidates into mortal combat so only the most ruthless survive") they had inflicted upon the lower realms, playing a frantic [Battle Royale](# "Literal CN: 大逃杀 (dà táo shā) — a deadly survival game where participants must eliminate each other"). The term "Battle Royale" was something Li Lu had only just learned from the storybooks. As an obedient, model second-generation immortal student, he had never touched any vulgar lower-realm entertainment prior to taking over Wanhua Realm—including popular fiction.

Thanks to that pristine innocence, Realm Protector Li harbored no notable sins to speak of, which allowed him to survive that infernal bloodbath. At least, that was what he chose to believe, rather than attributing his survival to the fact that his storage pouches were packed with hundreds of tons of choice spirit beast meat intended for the Imperial Kitchens.

Still, Li Lu's perception of the world—and of his celestial colleagues—had been completely shattered. He had spiraled into a total existential meltdown: *What is wrong with this world? Was everything I learned a lie? Is a Heavenly Court like this even worth defending?* In this shattered state, upon receiving Ji Gongbu's invitation, he hadn't even weighed its necessity; registering only that it was an official duty, he had muddle-headedly tagged along.

Throughout the journey, listening to his colleagues chatter about celestial gossip felt completely surreal.

Whatever the state of other departments, the Ministry of Rites had been rendered totally dysfunctional. What imperial succession struggle were they even babbling about? For that matter, was the Celestial Emperor himself truly clean? Did he even need to exist?!!

Trapped in this delayed adolescent rebellion and self-imposed isolation, Realm Protector Li had wandered into the Scripture Library and subconsciously picked up a novel.

After three centuries of development, the storybooks in the Scripture Library were no longer the pitiful handful Zhou Hui remembered.

After all, every incoming class harbored a contingent of distracted students who became addicted to novels. A portion of them inevitably picked up the brush themselves, exchanging drafts, founding literary societies, and launching serialized magazines. As educational reforms expanded and literacy spread among youth, the readership swelled exponentially. Nowadays, the storybooks added to the Scripture Library each year far outnumbered all academic treatises combined—and those were merely the ones deemed worthy of archival preservation. In reality, local cultivators were currently obsessed with reading serialized epics millions of characters long directly through transmission talismans.

The library's automated functionality had likewise evolved. Its bookshelves could now sense a reader's subconscious inclinations, pushing the most relevant titles directly before them.

Ji Gongbu, preoccupied with teleportation arrays, naturally received related spellcraft journals. Others received shopping guides or specialty commodity catalogs according to their interests. Realm Protector Li was a more complicated case: his mind was saturated with horrific, blood-soaked memories, so the shelves pushed supernatural horror and suspense novels into his hands.

Among them, a [rules-based weird tales](# "Literal CN: 规则怪谈 (guīzé guàitán) — horror stories built around surviving bizarre, arbitrary sets of rules") novel matched the "unspeakable entity" Li Lu had encountered by nearly eighty percent; almost every single survival rule lined up with his horrific experience.

Anyone else in his shoes would likely suspect the author was either the culprit or a copycat perpetrator. But Realm Protector Li had grown up in the sterile, cloistered environment of the Celestial Realm, having never read fiction. In his naive worldview, anything recorded in black and white upon paper was inevitably a form of factual knowledge.

What's more, the explanations penned (or fabricated) within made uncanny sense.

—"Obsession breeds inner demons, and for cultivators, what obsession could possibly surpass ascension? Emptiness attracts celestial fiends, and who is emptier than a second-generation immortal like me? Not to mention the twisted desires and filth spawned by boundless, stagnant millennia..."-Realm Protector Li's eyes were glazed, ostensibly speaking to Ji Gongbu but mostly muttering to himself.-"Could there be any place more fertile than the current Heavenly Court for breeding..."

—"Realm Protector Li!"-Ji Gongbu snapped sternly.

Even for someone of pristine celestial lineage like Li Lu, certain words were strictly forbidden.

Snatching the novel from Realm Protector Li's grasp, Ji Gongbu shook his head:

—"You've been reading far too much leisure fiction. If you keep this up, you'll end up being cast down to the mortal realm to undergo a corrective tribulation."

He didn't find this unusual in the slightest.

Second-generation immortals like Li Lu, whose starting line was other people's finish line, frequently developed discontent or even rebellious notions toward Heavenly Court policies during their formative years. Their elders and ascendant immortals simply chalked it up to having life too easy: throw them into the lower realms for a few harsh tribulations, and they would swiftly appreciate the Heavenly Court's benevolence.

Of course, unlike those [Children of Destiny](# "Literal CN: 天选之子 / 天命之子"), the tribulations for these rebellious nepo babies were custom-tailored.

Those who deemed the Heavenly Court corrupt were stripped of their divine status and cast into decaying mortal dynasties with immense talent, forced to experience the despair of powerlessness. Those who doubted the ascension system were blessed with coveted aptitudes and tossed into ruthless dog-eat-dog cultivation vats to witness the boundless greed of mortal cultivators. Those who sympathized with the lower realms were dropped into the most "lawless and free" mortal domains to taste the true savagery of freedom.

Upon returning from their tribulations, these second-generation immortals were thoroughly cured, transformed into staunch Heavenly Court loyalists. The stubborn ones experienced radical personality shifts, swinging violently from one extreme to another—either becoming hyper-zealous defenders of celestial hierarchy who wished to eradicate all lower realms, or having their souls hollowed out, cold and detached like walking phantoms on the verge of vanishing.

In Ji Gongbu's eyes, Li Lu's current mental state made him a prime candidate for tribulation therapy.

[Second-generation immortals, through and through...]

[Why on earth did I ever think he wanted to discuss spellcraft? Losing his mind over a couple of pulp novels—no wonder that transport corridor hasn't been fixed in centuries.]

Ji Gongbu grumbled inwardly.

Realm Protector Li neither knew nor cared what Ji Gongbu was thinking. Yet as the sole survivor of that harrowing hunting game, he had learned a thing or two; his earlier outburst had merely been a momentary loss of composure under prolonged psychological strain.

Ji Gongbu's rebuke shocked his mind back to sobriety. He quickly noticed the other celestial officials glancing their way.

Stiffening for an instant, Realm Protector Li smoothly reassembled his customary polite smile:

—"You said it yourself—they're just storybooks. Why so tense?"

—"But earlier, you..."-Ji Gongbu blinked in confusion.

[—"Storybooks?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")-another celestial official chimed in with mild curiosity.

—"I was merely summarizing the novel I just read,"-Realm Protector Li said with a light chuckle.-"Corrupted by demonic qi, immortals butchered one another, and not even True Immortals were spared, laying bare their ugliest depravities."

[—"Demonic qi? Golden Immortals? Depravity?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")-The celestial officials burst into laughter, as though hearing the most absurd joke imaginable.-[—"Only the lower realms could fabricate such ridiculous tales! Never mind that mortal scribblers haven't the faintest inkling of what a Golden Immortal truly is—how could any 'unclean filth' possibly exist within the Celestial Realm? Hahaha!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

Amidst their laughter, Ji Gongbu's expression softened as well, having found a neat explanation for Li Lu's erratic behavior:

—"Realm Protector Li, you scared the daylights out of me! I genuinely thought you were due for a mortal tribulation descent!"

[—"Pfft! A mortal tribulation? Ji Gongbu, do you take Lord Realm Protector for one of your good-for-nothing nephews?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

[—"Haha! How long were we even inside the Scripture Library? Even an immortal fairy-child wouldn't start yearning for the mortal dust after skimming two pages of fiction!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

The celestial officials' laughter grew even heartier.

Looking sheepish, Ji Gongbu figured he had overreacted. He shoved the confiscated book back into Li Lu's arms:

—"My apologies. I've been overly sensitive lately because of the trouble with the young ones back home. But your acting was far too convincing, Lord Realm Protector."

—"..."-Li Lu let out an open sneer.

This time, Ji Gongbu harbored no suspicions, assuming the young viceroy was simply mocking him for falling for a prank. Mumbling another curse about "damnable second-generation immortals" under his breath, he let the matter drop. But he had no idea that Li Lu's laughter was purely self-mocking.

For while the others were convinced that the Celestial Realm was free of impurity and that he was untainted, right upon the back of the hand Li Lu used to accept the book lay a bizarre, red-and-black sigil, radiating a horrifying, feral malice.

It was a brand left behind from that hunting game. To Li Lu's eyes, it was akin to an identification stamp burned onto spirit beasts awaiting slaughter, exuding an eerie aura distinct from ordinary demonic qi. Yet aside from himself, not one of these self-satisfied celestial officials had noticed it.

So what if he understood what was happening?

Himself, these officials, the Heavenly Court as a whole—all were doomed beyond salvation.

Li Lu thought numbly.

The brief thrill of finding answers in the novels vanished without a trace, replaced once more by suffocating despair.

As long as this mark remained indelible, he could never be saved...!!!

Li Lu's eyes suddenly snapped wide open.

He saw a diaphanous, veil-like appendage—shaped remarkably like a tentacle—brush across the back of his hand. It brought a cool, moist, slightly brackish touch. Though the sensation was somewhat unsettling, that grotesque red-and-black rune dissolved instantly, dispersing like a drop of ink swirled into clear water.

[MONOLOGUE: Impossible! Even a Golden Immortal couldn't cleanse it...!]

Li Lu nearly shrieked aloud.

Eagerly looking up to discover which exalted deity had resolved his affliction, he found that the creature trailing those slender gossamer "veils" was a colossal, translucent, mushroom-like entity.

Indeed, they were graceful and exquisite, akin to crystallized spiritual energy, but that didn't change the fact that they resembled gargantuan mushrooms—except that beneath the canopy hung several drifting tentacles rather than a stalk.

The other celestial officials naturally spotted such massive creatures as well:

[—"Look, what a massive jellyfish!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

—"Jellyfish?"-Realm Protector Li asked.

A celestial official glanced at Li Lu with a strange look, then remembered that the viceroy had been raised in the upper realms; even when attending banquets at the Dragon Palace, he likely traveled through exclusive VIP channels. Thus, the official explained:

—"A creature from the mortal sea. At least, it roughly counts as an animal, I suppose?"-Immortal cultivators rarely bothered studying species completely divorced from cultivation.

—"An animal?"-Realm Protector Li grew immensely intrigued, finding the term "jellyfish" laden with esoteric depth.-"Do you mean it is a divine beast of some sort? My apologies, I have never heard of it..."

—"It is only natural that you haven't, Lord Realm Protector,"-another official chuckled.-"They are no divine beasts, but rather ordinary mortal creatures found everywhere, incapable of even absorbing spiritual energy... Though one this massive is certainly a rare sight."

—"Ordinary jellyfish don't fly through the sky, either,"-the tour guide chimed in.

The guide had been present the entire time, conscientiously delivering explanations regardless of whether the celestial officials were listening:

—"In truth, they can also devour demonic qi. I am not sure if the esteemed immortals have noticed, but we have actually crossed into the Demon Realm."

This time, all the celestial officials were caught off guard, wearing identical expressions of bewildered ignorance:

[—"Devour demonic qi? How is that possible?!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

[—"Wait, this is the Demon Realm?! I haven't sensed a speck of demonic qi!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

[—"Granted, there is no spiritual energy here either. It feels more like the chaotic qi of the extraterrestrial void?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

[—"Mmm... looking at the surrounding terrain, it certainly bears the hallmarks of demonic erosion..."](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

Facing their confusion, the guide maintained his steady cadence:

—"Not long ago—three hundred years ago, to be precise—this world was plagued by demonic tides. Due to having fallen from the Heavenly Court, several breaches beneath the Abyssal Ocean led directly into the Demon Realm. And like many worlds troubled by demonic tides, even though our predecessors labored to seal those fissures, demonic qi would inevitably seep through the barriers, periodically triggering spiritual energy tides that drove swarms of demonic beasts to besiege our cities. To address this, our Untiring Immortal Venerable instructed his disciples to deploy extensive monitoring magical devices across the seabed to record demonic qi metrics..."

[—"There it is again, 'our Immortal Venerable,'"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")-someone muttered under their breath.

Throughout the journey, whether intentionally or not, their ears had been bombarded with "our Immortal Venerable" and "the Untiring Immortal Venerable." However dull-witted, they understood he was effectively the master of this realm. Yet here they were on the threshold of the Demon Realm without having caught a single glimpse of the Immortal Venerable himself. Evidently, he had zero intention of granting them an audience. A few officials harbored petty grievances, feeling that even if only for the sake of Realm Protector Li—whom they viewed as his peer—the host shouldn't be so arrogant.

Yet after witnessing the large-scale teleportation array, the academic papers at the Scripture Library, and the treasures at Wanbao Pavilion, most kept their heads down, not daring to voice their complaints aloud.

Not that anyone cared anyway.

Unperturbed, the guide continued:

—"...From this, the Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples observed anomalies in demonic qi circulation, noticing that while ordinary creatures could not survive in high-concentration demonic qi zones, jellyfish proliferated in immense numbers.

—"Because these mindless, spiritless creatures possess exceedingly simple anatomical structures, demonic erosion does not kill them immediately; instead, they scatter the demonic qi further, making eradication exceptionally troublesome.

—"However, before the disciples could devise a countermeasure, three hundred years ago following a certain war, Heavenly Spirit Sect's Untiring Immortal Venerable broke through his realm. His personal direct disciples all achieved profound enlightenment. His eldest disciple possesses a primordial bloodline, while his fifth disciple possesses a True Dragon bloodline. Their transcendence influenced the scaled and aquatic beasts of this realm, to the point where even mundane creatures like jellyfish developed devouring attributes—capable of consuming demonic qi and delaying that year's demonic tide.

—"Heavenly Spirit Sect immediately took note, capturing large quantities of jellyfish for research and genetic modification, adapting them to feed primarily on demonic qi, thereby successfully eradicating the demonic tides—"

By this point, none of the celestial officials dared wear expressions of contempt; they listened with rapt attention.

Demonic tides were by no means a problem isolated to a single world. Whenever demonic qi concentrations surpassed a critical threshold, catastrophe ensued. Consequently, it was tightly bound to celestial officials' official evaluations and KPIs.

In large worlds with dense mortal and cultivator populations, excessive mortal oppression spawned rampant cults, heretical cultivators went mad and expanded, and sect infighting caused mass casualties that summoned extraterrestrial fiends—all of which could trigger demonic tides. And that meant the supervising celestial official's political achievements for centuries would be wiped down to zero.

Hearing of an organism capable of eradicating demonic qi and stopping demonic tides, how could they not listen with rapt attention?

More than performance metrics, these bureaucratic veterans, adept at reading between the lines, were transfixed by those brief words: *The Immortal Venerable broke through, his disciples attained enlightenment, and their bloodline kin were cosmically influenced.*

As former ascenders, they knew that ordinary ascension or promotions within the celestial hierarchy yielded no such phenomenon. Even if ["even the chickens and dogs ascend to heaven,"](# "Literal CN: 一人得道，鸡犬升天 — when one person attains the Dao, even their domestic beasts rise to heaven") the scope of affected beings was strictly limited. To alter the bloodline of disciples, their kin, and an entire minor world's ecology could only signify one thing:

Comprehending the Heavenly Dao.

Ascending to Godhood.

The moment this dawned on them, whatever petty resentment they harbored over being snubbed melted away like morning frost.

*Haha! Expecting a 'God' or his divine kin to welcome us personally? If he didn't care to meet us, we certainly wouldn't want to shorten our own lifespans by imposing!*

These celestial officials became all the more convinced that this minor world was backed by the highest echelons of the Heavenly Court; otherwise, who would possess the qualifications to attain Godhood? It never crossed their minds that the casually mentioned "war," while involving demons, had primarily been waged against the Heavenly Court—annihilating its mightiest Heavenly General and the supreme head of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny. Nor did they suspect that the "primordial bloodline" influencing the kin was actually a Taotie—a genuine, ravenous ferocious beast, not some auspicious divine creature!

Naturally, the guide's pitch had been meticulously scripted from the start, specifically crafted to lead the officials to this exact conclusion.

After all, what wrong could a little jellyfish that consumed demonic qi possibly do?

The emphasis was on "devouring demonic qi," not "devouring"!

First impressions were paramount. Even if the officials later discovered that the great Immortal Venerable's eldest disciple was a Taotie, they would subconsciously view him as a domesticated, "good" ferocious beast that only devoured demonic qi and monsters—especially since he was paired alongside a True Dragon.

At least Realm Protector Li, who had narrowly avoided being devoured, never made the connection.

Not only did he not connect the dots, he asked eagerly:

—"So, are these jellyfish for sale?"

—"Eh?"-The guide, still immersed in his presentation, hadn't quite caught up.

Realm Protector Li pressed:

—"Name a price. I'll buy them!"

Before the guide could respond, the other celestial officials chimed in:

[—"Indeed, name your price!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

[—"How many spirit stones?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

[—"I want to buy some as well!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

Without question, deploying a few jellyfish to ward off demonic tides was an incomparably lucrative bargain.

Yet for the first time on this trip, the guide displayed a troubled expression:

—"My apologies, everyone. It's not that we are unwilling to sell, but this is the first time a non-spiritual organism capable of consuming demonic qi has ever appeared. It is difficult to predict what consequences introducing them to other worlds might bring. In the event that..."

To the celestial officials' ears, the guide's words sounded like a standard disclaimer. It was understandable enough: getting entangled with other worlds' demonic tides could easily pull one into war; an abundance of caution was only natural. But this did nothing to dampen their enthusiasm.

One official declared boldly:

[—"What does that matter? Since we are the ones buying, we will bear full responsibility if anything goes wrong!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official")

—"It doesn't work that way,"-the guide replied firmly.-"If something is sold as a commodity, we must guarantee its quality. You likely noticed earlier at Wanbao Pavilion that all merchandise sold in our world adheres to strict quality standards and comes with defined after-sales support."

The celestial officials had indeed noticed. However, neither the cultivation world nor the Heavenly Court had ever known such a concept as quality inspection. If someone took the wrong pill or used a defective artifact and perished, it was chalked up to fate—there was no such thing as post-incident warranty claims. Not to mention, many people's pills and artifacts were expired relics scavenged from ancient mystic realms of forgotten eras.

Naturally, as consumers, they wouldn't refuse excellent customer service, and celestial officials were no exception.

Besides, the guide hadn't shut the door completely.

—"There is no need for disappointment. In truth, our research on the [Demon-Devouring Jellyfish](# "Literal CN: 噬魔水母") has entered its final pilot phase,"-the guide said with a beaming smile.-"We are currently searching for candidate worlds with complex ecosystems and diverse species to conduct localized trials."

—"Isn't that perfect?!"-Realm Protector Li refused to yield.

He certainly had no intention of giving up. Having lived through that infernal ordeal, he wouldn't sleep soundly without bringing several jellyfish back. In fact, upon hearing of the jellyfish's origins, Li Lu had even harbored thoughts of buying—cough, inviting—the Immortal Venerable's eldest disciple home.

Now, however, Li Lu had an even better idea:

—"Our Wanhua Realm not only meets every criterion you mentioned, but we also boast the most comprehensive ranches across the Three Thousand Worlds! Any creature capable of surviving in our realm can thrive in almost any other world. If possible, I wish to collaborate with you on this."

—"Ah, but..."-The guide seemed completely unprepared for this turn of events, momentarily struck dumb.

Li Lu gave him no room to hesitate, declaring resolutely:

—"This project—I'm funding it!"

*

The celestial officials twittered like a flock of sparrows, overwhelming even the seasoned guide. In the commotion, two dazed, soul-shattered figures lingering at the periphery of the crowd went entirely unnoticed.

One was Ren Ran.

Seeing the battlefield where she had once bled transformed into a thriving metropolis—one where mortals and demons lived in tranquil harmony—had dealt the goddess a severe psychological shock. Ever since emerging from Wanbao Pavilion, she had drifted behind the crowd like a shadow puppet with severed strings, completely forgetting her divine mission.

As for the target of her mission, he was the other dazed wanderer.

Forced into it.

Initially, Long Tianming had been thrilled to wander alongside the celestial officials. Although he didn't have a single spirit stone and couldn't buy anything, the merchants never targeted him for sales pitches either. This allowed him to browse in undisturbed peace, freely test-driving various low-cost display models of advanced magical treasures that far exceeded his current cultivation realm.

As a transmigrator accustomed to modern electronics flagship stores, Long Tianming felt right at home in such retail shops.

Yet an inexplicable sense of unease continued to swell in his heart.

In transmigration web novels, transmigrators almost always transmigrated from advanced civilizations into lower-tier societies. While this partly stemmed from authors being unable to imagine worlds they had never seen, it was also because a "protagonist's" ideological foresight was often more potent than any golden finger. Consequently, whether one transmigrated into a cultivation world or a futuristic interstellar expanse, the societal regime was invariably a feudal empire.

After all, if a transmigrator could thrive under unfamiliar—or even existing—modern systems, why would they ever need to transmigrate?

Confronted by this bizarre cultivation world, Long Tianming felt utterly out of his depth—especially given the eerie familiarity of certain elements. But who in their right mind wanted to repeat the college entrance exams or civil service examinations? Long Tianming told himself his unease was perfectly normal, and so he had ignored it the entire journey.

Yet this bizarre dread had abruptly ballooned to a fever pitch. He couldn't pinpoint whether it had struck upon entering a particular corner of the city or aboard the sightseeing cloud chariot.

And the moment he heard the guide announce that they had entered the "Demon Realm," Long Tianming's heart nearly seized.

By this stage, even an inexplicably cocky soul like Long Tianming realized this dread wasn't originating from himself.

—"Old man, what on earth is wrong with you?"-Long Tianming asked his [portable Old Grandpa](# "Web Novel Trope: 随身老爷爷 (suíshēn lǎoyéye) — venerable master spirit mentoring the protagonist from within a ring or consciousness") in his mind.-"Ever since we entered this city, what have you been terrified of?"

"..."

Long Tianming waited patiently for a few minutes, but received no reply.

He couldn't take it anymore. Having the hairs on the back of his neck standing on end in perpetual dread was hardly a healthy psychological state. Not only was he incapable of concentrating, he genuinely feared he might drop dead from heart failure.

Straining his mind, he attempted to deduce the source of the panic:

—"Is it because of the 'Demon Realm'? But you weren't this frightened even in the Celestial Realm! Let's lay all our cards on the table: I know you're probably no benevolent saint. But trembling like this doesn't seem to be because we entered the Demon Realm. Isn't it for the same reason I'm uneasy—because things aren't turning out the way you envisioned?"

Presently, Long Tianming was speaking without the slightest filter.

He wasn't usually this reckless, but the crushing anxiety shredded his emotional stability, making his words sound like blatant mockery.

He actually kept rambling:

—"Come to think of it, they called this an Immortal-Demon battlefield, yet we haven't seen a single demon so far. Even I can tell something is off. Rather than a frontline, wouldn't you say this place has been completely brought under cultivator control? That's what you're terrified of, isn't it? But if you ask me, there's no need to be so hopeless, actually...!!!"

—"Shut up!"

The instant that roar erupted from the depths of his soul, Long Tianming found his panic vanishing—

His body had been hijacked; what was left to panic about?

Not that he felt nothing. Like a third-party observer, Long Tianming "watched" as his physical form began drifting forward like a wandering ghost.

—"What are you doing?"-Though stripped of a physical body to shudder with, Long Tianming finally realized what genuine terror felt like—especially as he recognized that this possession was far more absolute than when they had fled the Heavenly Court.

[The plot wasn't supposed to go like this!]

[Even if the portable Old Grandpa wanted to seize my body, that was supposed to happen in the late game once I reached full power! I haven't even officially begun cultivating yet—what's the point of taking over now?!]

Regrettably, the former Demon Emperor had zero intention of wasting words on him.

Long Tianming had guessed right: the Demon Emperor, reduced to a demon seed, was terrified. Terrified as never before.

Anyone who believed they had a chance to return to their home turf, only to discover it had been converted into enemy territory, would be just as hysterical.

Unlike the dull-witted celestial officials who believed they were merely on a sightseeing tour (while actually walking in to be sheared), the moment the Demon Emperor entered this newly erected city, he knew he had returned "home."

The ripples left in space by residual demonic qi, the scars etched into the earth by demonic tide erosion, even the faint, distinct scent of the demon race mingling in the spiritual atmosphere—all confirmed it.

Under normal circumstances, immortal cultivators could not survive long-term in such an environment. The chaotic spiritual energy alone would cause severe malfunctions in their spellcasting; only the presence of True Immortals could maintain a semblance of equilibrium on such a battlefield.

Even the omnipotent Untiring Immortal Venerable of the Heavenly Spirit Sect could not resolve this problem overnight.

Though Zhou Hui's unique cultivation art generated pure spiritual energy capable of purifying demonic corruption, even if he rooted himself in the Demon Realm, it would take millennia to fully cleanse a minor demonic territory. And his less-cultivated disciples would take even longer.

Fortunately, one could simply stack numbers.

While individual Foundation Establishment and Golden Core disciples practicing the Heavenly Spirit Mantra supplied limited spiritual energy, an overwhelming multitude could easily surpass Zhou Hui's purification rate—sparing Zhou Hui from having to serve as a glorified air purifier.

Zhou Hui had zero desire to become a localized air filter; he had far too many tasks on his plate. Thus, toward the Demon Realm frontier his disciples had inadvertently carved out, he adopted a two-pronged strategy.

On one hand, he scaled up sheer manpower. Heavenly Spirit Sect's Foundation Establishment and Golden Core disciples were mandated to garrison this city for several decades, permitted to depart only upon reaching Nascent Soul. This served to purify demonic qi, but it was also discovered that the Heavenly Spirit Mantra was truly worthy of the Untiring Immortal Venerable: it yielded the best cultivation efficiency precisely near the Demon Realm! Much like desert plants taking deep root in arid soil, spirit roots nurtured here grew exceptionally solid and resilient, with divine sense tempered to several times that of ordinary cultivators.

On the other hand, Zhou Hui commissioned mortal graduates from the academy's inaugural class to construct this city.

Unlike cultivators, mortals neither cultivated spiritual energy nor cast spells. As long as adequate warding formations and medical telemetry stations were established throughout the city to cleanse residual demonic qi from their bodies, their daily lives remained entirely unaffected. In fact, to mortals, the demonic crystals dropped by demons served just as effectively as spirit stones to power mechanical devices.

A brand-new tech tree was quietly sprouting.

Not that the celestial officials needed to know any of this.

Nor did the demon seed that was once the Demon Emperor need to know.

In truth, the demon seed currently puppeteering Long Tianming's flesh wanted to know nothing at all. It only wanted to flee as fast as possible—just as it had once fled from Qu Qianxing. It needed to escape this suffocating city immediately, and above all, put as much distance as possible between itself and those illogical marine organisms floating in midair. From those jellyfish, it sensed a cursed, horrifying aura—an aura so intimate it gave even demons nightmares.

Fortunately, because the hijacked vessel was sufficiently unique, those low-level creatures hadn't noticed it yet. And this was, after all, part of the Demon Realm; even if heavily purified, as the former sovereign of the demon race, it still possessed specialized escape arts.

The only nuisance was that the original soul inhabiting this vessel was far too noisy.

—"You can't do this!"-Long Tianming shrieked.

As the possessed party, he couldn't manipulate the demon seed's emotions the way the demon seed toyed with his. But sharing a single vessel meant that when the other party's intentions were overwhelmingly potent, he could still perceive them.

And knowing the creature's plan plunged Long Tianming into bottomless despair:

—"How could you self-detonate?! How could you detonate my body?! If you destroy me, you won't escape either!"

Yes—self-detonation.

Ever since fleeing the Celestial Realm, the demon seed had planned to use Long Tianming as a disposable tool. Surrounded by celestial officials, that resolve had hardened into certainty.

If ordinary escape routes were barred, it would simply use Long Tianming's death as a catalyst to blast open a bloody path. Even if such a terrorist strike failed to harm the celestial officials, it would suffice to sow mass panic among these pampered bureaucrats. Their furious demands for accountability would leave local cultivators scrambling to appease them, leaving no bandwidth to hunt down the demon seed.

As steadfast as the demon seed was in its resolve to flee, so too was its murderous intent toward Long Tianming.

—"Shut up!"-it snarled.-"Cooperate obediently and your death will be swift. Otherwise, I will make your agony unimaginable!"

—"No..."-Long Tianming had never experienced such naked, unvarnished malice. He finally realized what an idiotic mistake it had been to make a pact with an entity of unknown origins.

Even when his body was seized, the novelty of the situation had dulled his perception of danger; he hadn't grasped how truly dire his predicament was. Now, he felt the icy breath of death against his throat.

Long Tianming was genuinely terrified.

He wanted to curse in fury, he wanted to thrash wildly, he even wanted to beg pathetically. But a soul nurtured on pure, naive sheltered ignorance and fragile modern "pride" could only muster a toothless attempt at rational debate:

—"Y-You killing me won't do any good! My cultivation is so low I haven't even officially initiated. Even if I detonate, the blast radius will be tiny! It won't scratch those immortals in the slightest, and it'll just expose you..."

—"Don't sell yourself short,"-the demon seed sneered.-"Didn't you proclaim yourself the 'protagonist'? Wherever your delusion comes from, your destiny thread is indeed extraordinarily bizarre. It might actually cause those immortals a bit of trouble."

—"No..."-Long Tianming let out a despairing wail.

The demon seed paid him no heed, marching step by step toward the celestial officials:

—"The strong prey upon the weak. You should feel honored to offer your life for the great Demon Emperor..."

[—"Hmph, how arrogant."](# "Speaker: Mo Xi")

[—"Then, as the weaker party, it's only natural for you to surrender everything to us, right?"](# "Speaker: Mo Bei")

"!!!"

A cold male sneer and a cheerful female voice rang out simultaneously.

Sensing something amiss, the demon seed moved to abandon the vessel and flee instantly. But it was a beat too slow. Something freezing cold had already plunged into this freshly hijacked body, piercing clean through its heart.

A bone-chilling frost spread outward in an instant, as though the very air had frozen solid. The demon seed felt as if it had plummeted into an icy lake in the depths of winter. Every last sliver of its remaining demonic qi began congealing. It could neither move nor flee, much less carry out its plan to detonate the vessel.

The only action the demon seed could muster as the body collapsed was to roll its eyeballs to look at the "assailant."

It was a young girl, cold as a midwinter frost. She was drawing back a flexible sword that had impaled Long Tianming's heart, wiping away the demonic qi clinging to the steel—just like brushing away snowflakes drifting onto a blade on a snowy day.

A young woman like her should have been the cynosure of any crowd, yet not one of the celestial officials merely inches away took notice of her presence. It was as though the assassination unfolding right before their eyes simply did not exist.

As for the owner of this impaled vessel, he could only muster a foolish gasp:

—"So... beautiful..."

[Is that the fucking point, you idiot?!]

The demon seed wanted to roar in volcanic fury, but its final sliver of consciousness—alongside the physical vessel and its foolish original owner—sank into the frozen stillness of absolute zero.
