# Chapter 107: [Foiled](<# "Literal CN: 未遂 (wèisuì) — Unconsummated / Attempted (Unsuccessful) / Foiled; referring to Yuwen Xuan's botched plot to feed his other soul-half to Lord Siwu, caught red-handed by Zhou Hui">) (第107章 未遂)

[You're coming over...]

[Wouldn't it be faster if I went over instead?]
Zhou Hui thought.

Although both were in the Tribulation Transcendence stage, the mightier a supreme powerhouse was, the more distinct their areas of specialization became.

Although Zhou Hui's cultivation journey had been fraught with difficulties due to his humble origins, making it appear as though he were proficient in all things, his true primary domains were Time and Space. It was precisely because of this that he could create the *Seven Lives Transmigration Technique* and teleport instantaneously to any location within the cultivation world.

For him to travel to Patriarch Jiuzhai's domain was a matter of a mere blink.

Patriarch Jiuzhai, however, was fundamentally different—he was a [divination cultivator](<# "Literal CN: 卦修 (guàxiū) — hexagram / divination practitioner; one who reads cosmic trigrams and destiny">).

A pure one.

The kind who didn't even cultivate magical arts.

Not only did he forgo offensive spellcraft, but depending on the circumstances, he might surrender his own spirit roots, dharmic magic, five senses, internal organs, or even his soul as the "karmic price" to exchange for the divination insights he sought.

In other words, while this man could perceive the past, present, and future, and could deduce anyone's destiny, strictly in terms of his physical body, he was a total invalid.

For a Patriarch Jiuzhai like this to leave his immortal cave, depart the Southern Realm, and make a trip to the Central Domain, he could only rely on "fate."

If his luck was good, perhaps he would arrive the next instant. If his luck was bad, he might never arrive at all.

That was why Zhou Hui felt it would be far faster to go over to Patriarch Jiuzhai himself.

Yet after pondering for a moment, Zhou Hui dismissed the thought.

First, he hadn't yet figured out what had actually happened; even if he went over, he wouldn't be able to explain anything clearly.

Second, Patriarch Jiuzhai was not an impulsive man. Even if an unforeseen shift in providential fate had occurred beyond his calculations, he wouldn't immediately rush over in a panic. Furthermore, what had fluctuated was the providential fate of the Southern Realm; theoretically, Jiuzhai staying in the Southern Realm would allow him to better monitor the situation. Thus, there had to be an unavoidable reason why Jiuzhai had to come over in person.

In that case, waiting for him to arrive would suffice.

However, it wouldn't be passive waiting. Zhou Hui had no intention of idling away his time waiting for a Jiuzhai who might arrive who-knew-when—or might not arrive at all. What Zhou Hui cared about right now was discovering what had transpired.

Judging from Patriarch Jiuzhai's cross-examination, this matter was directly tied to him.

Zhou Hui turned the matter over in his mind. The only person in Fengqing City directly related to him at the moment was his direct disciple, Mo Bei.

At the thought that something might have happened to his youngest female disciple, Zhou Hui could no longer sit still. With a flash of his form, he vanished toward Fengqing City.

And as Zhou Hui departed, a pair of tiny black lamb ears poked out from the window of his study, trembling like rabbit ears.

*

[Master left Mount Tianling?!]

The little black lamb perked up its soft, fluffy ears in pleasant surprise.

Propping its front hooves on the windowsill, it strained to sense its surroundings.

In truth, Yuwen Xuan didn't even need to expend that much effort to be certain his master was no longer at the Heavenly Spirit Sect. After all, to a Taotie, Zhou Hui's presence was like a sprawling banquet table laden with an entire [Manchu-Han Imperial Feast](<# "Literal CN: 满汉全席 (Mǎnhàn Quánxí) — the legendary grand imperial banquet of the Qing dynasty, featuring over a hundred lavish dishes">). For a ravenous diner, ignoring it was nearly impossible. Now that this grand feast had abruptly vanished, he could sense the absence even with his eyes closed.

Yet Yuwen Xuan felt not the slightest hint of disappointment.

Like an [unruly brat](<# "Literal CN: 熊孩子 (xióng háizi) — bear child; modern slang for a mischievous, spoiled, or troublemaking kid">) eager to watch late-night television who just overheard that his parents were working overtime and wouldn't be home, he excitedly scampered around the study in a pitter-patter circle (*clack-clack-clack*), practically longing to burst into a celebratory dance.

[Ah, now is not the time to celebrate!]

[Even if the timing might not be fully ripe, such a rare opportunity doesn't come twice!]

[I have to get 'that thing' done before Master returns!]

Halting its dancing hooves, the little black lamb thought.

It trotted out of the study (*clack-clack-clack*) and darted into Zhou Hui's bedchamber.

Zhou Hui, having abruptly perceived the colossal, unusual surge of providential fate, had departed in great haste. Consequently, his bedchamber remained exactly as it had been during his meditation:

The cultivation-assisting incense was still smoldering, the prayer mat still retained residual warmth, and most importantly, the outer robe Zhou Hui habitually wore was draped over the folding screen directly across from the mat.

Due to his sudden departure, Zhou Hui had even forgotten to slip on his outer robe.

Seeing the robe, Yuwen Xuan's eyes lit up.

He immediately wanted to charge over. Before doing so, however, he did not forget to lick every inch of his wool, devouring all traces of his own scent and aura.

In an instant, Yuwen Xuan's aura dwindled into near-nothingness.

Though he remained physically present, even someone standing right beside him would struggle to detect his existence, let alone any lingering traces he left behind.

Only after properly "concealing" his aura did the little black lamb approach the folding screen. Leaping up with all its might, it clamped its teeth around the hem of the robe's sleeve and whipped its head back violently—

In a shower of motion, several storage pouches tumbled out from the sleeve.

Zhou Hui was in the habit of carrying his more important storage pouches on his person, tucked into the wide sleeves of his outer robe. This was also the reason he always took off his robe during prolonged meditation sessions.

Some storage pouches contained critical medicinal pills and magical treasures; others were unique in nature and might disrupt the efficiency of his meditation. In such instances, Zhou Hui never wore his outer robe.

All of the disciples were well aware of this habit of Zhou Hui's.

As Zhou Hui's eldest disciple, Yuwen Xuan knew it better than anyone.

Thus, Yuwen Xuan was certain that the object he sought had to be inside Master's sleeve.

Nudging through the scattered pouches with its hooves, the little black lamb promptly located a specific storage pouch.

A pouch completely devoid of aura.

A storage pouch that, despite having rested within Master's robe, bore not the faintest trace of scent.

Moreover, the mouth of this storage pouch was sealed tight with layered restrictions, as if terribly afraid that whatever was inside might break free.

Yuwen Xuan let out a sneering *bleat*.

He was certain this was precisely what he had been looking for.

He had thought it would take quite some time to steal it away, but this turned out to be remarkably convenient.

The little black lamb nudged the pouch aside with a hoof, gathered up the remaining pouches, and exerted tremendous effort to stuff them back into the sleeve of Zhou Hui's robe. It painstakingly adjusted the robe back to the exact drape it had seen upon entering, even using its own head to measure the hem from the floor to ensure the height was identical.

Only after completing all of this did the little black lamb, thoroughly pleased with itself, grab the storage pouch and scamper back to the study (*clack-clack-clack*).

With Master away, there was no need to be secretive about "playing with the model."

*Thud!* The little black lamb hopped onto the desk, making the wooden table rattle. The "model" of White Jade Capital, however, was fortified with Zhou Hui's spells; it was virtually indestructible and completely unaffected.

The little black lamb looked upon White Jade Capital with smug satisfaction.

At this moment, half of White Jade Capital was already blanketed in azure providential fate. In particular, the location of the White Jade Capital Academy emitted a faint, shimmering azure glow, as though something was coalescing within.

The little black lamb sniffed at the providential qi, sneezed, and disinterestedly looked away.

Luck and providential fate were utterly useless to a ferocious beast like a Taotie.

Ferocious beasts were entities born in defiance of the Heavenly Mandate. Consequently, neither positive nor negative karmic luck had much effect on them. Or rather, ferocious beasts were themselves part of negative providential calamity—whether mortals or cultivators, anyone who crossed paths with a ferocious beast had run into catastrophic misfortune. And being part of that "calamity," the beast finding unlucky prey to devour could, in a twisted sense, be considered its own "good luck."

Therefore, even a Taotie rarely consumed pure providential fate.

Last time, when Zhou Hui had taken them to the Immortal Arts Assembly to harvest providential qi, Yuwen Xuan had drooled on the sidelines only because Master had gifted that fate to Qu Qianxing and Qingyang Qingyu for their cultivation. Food in someone else's bowl always looked more appetizing, which was why Yuwen Xuan had craved a bite. Now that there were no rivals competing for food, Yuwen Xuan naturally looked down on anything outside his dietary wheelhouse with disdain.

The little black lamb even felt that this providential haze obstructed its view!

It flapped its front hooves, attempting to fan the providential mist away. Alas, providential fate was no ordinary fog, remaining entirely unmoved.

Giving up on the providential mist, the little black lamb extended a hoof and tapped the specific building it had wanted to [lay a hoof on](<# "Literal CN: 染蹄 (rǎntí) — stain its hooves; a humorous animal play on 'dip one's finger in' (染指) or 'taint with one's touch'">) earlier.

The structure immediately expanded, revealing the scenery within.

The building resembled an ordinary courtyard, complete with flowers, trees, and a pond situated in the center.

The water of the pond appeared crystal-clear, rippling gently in the breeze with an idyllic, pleasant charm.

Yet upon closer inspection, one would discover that the water cast no reflections whatsoever. Neither the trees along the bank nor the stone pedestals standing directly in the water reflected upon the surface. Even when the little black lamb leaned over, it could see no reflection of itself.

Yuwen Xuan, however, had clearly anticipated this.

Nudging the surface of the water with its hoof, it channeled its own spiritual power into the pool.

As if a bucket of ink had been poured directly beneath the lamb's hoof, wisps of inky blackness swirled outward, rapidly spreading across the entire pond until the pool turned pitch-black.

Once the water blackened, an ethereal panorama faintly materialized within.

The view looked like an aerial shot from high above, revealing shattered boulders, collapsed edifices, and the colossal skeletal remains of some unknown primordial leviathans—the whole area resembling an apocalyptic wasteland buried in darkness.

Had any other Central Domain cultivator been present, they would have been astounded by the sight:

The scenery within this "pond" was none other than a sector of the perilous Abyss Sea ruins... No, this was no mere "pond" at all. It was not only a [Thousand-Li Mirror](<# "Literal CN: 千里镜 (qiānlǐ jìng) — Thousand-Li Mirror; magical observation mirror capable of scrying across immense distances">) capable of observing distant horizons, but also an [omni-directional spatial teleportation array](<# "Literal CN: 不定向传送阵 (bù dìngxiàng chuánsòng zhèn) — non-directional / omni-directional teleportation formation capable of sending targets into unanchored coordinates">).

Back when White Jade Capital served as the center of the ancient cultivation realm, it possessed not only fixed teleportation arrays of various sizes leading to every corner of the world, but also these non-directional arrays capable of transporting targets to locations without fixed teleportation beacons.

Since Zhou Hui was researching and repairing White Jade Capital's teleportation networks, he naturally took an interest in these omni-directional arrays as well.

Even though he had no practical use for them himself, the Untiring Immortal Venerable never refused new knowledge.

Thus, during this period, Zhou Hui had carried out occasional repairs whenever he had free time, while Yuwen Xuan watched from the sidelines, concocting all manner of bizarre schemes.

Yet such teleportation could not be activated on a whim. It demanded far more spiritual energy than ordinary fixed-point arrays, as well as an intermediary medium to serve as a spatial anchor.

For example, right in the center of the Abyss Sea ruins within the pool, the very anchor Yuwen Xuan was tracking was on the move.

It was a cultivator.

With disheveled hair, his pristine white robes stained gray with blood and grime, he appeared utterly wretched. He barely looked like a Mahayana powerhouse, yet he was indeed a Mahayana grandmaster—specifically, the Ceremony Court Elder who had been taught a harsh lesson by the Untiring Immortal Venerable not long ago:

Lord Siwu!

Indeed, it was none other than the elder who had attempted to cause trouble at the Immortal Arts Assembly in Cloud City.

That day, amidst the chaos, the little black lamb had seized the opportunity to smear some [bone-ash patty crumbs](<# "Literal CN: 骨灰大饼渣渣 (gǔhuī dàbǐng zhāzhā) — crumbs from the flatbread baked with the ashes of Yuwen Xuan's past reincarnations">) mixed with the cremated remains of his past lifetimes onto Siwu. There was no spatial anchor more visceral or deeply imprinted than that, ensuring that no matter where Siwu wandered, Yuwen Xuan could locate him instantly.

Seeing Elder Siwu in such a pitiful state, however, even Yuwen Xuan was slightly surprised:

[—"What did he run into? How did he end up like that?"](<# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan">)

Theoretically, at the Mahayana stage, very few things in existence could harm him!

Yet while Yuwen Xuan was surprised, he had zero genuine interest in what had caused Siwu's misfortune. Leaving aside the fact that Master strictly forbade consuming human cultivators as food, even if Siwu were edible, he had no interest in learning where the prey had injured itself—much like a human wouldn't care what thoughts led a hare to crash headlong into a tree trunk.

Looking at the disheveled Siwu for a couple of seconds, Yuwen Xuan muttered dismissively:

—"Even better,"-he said.

With that, he dragged over the storage pouch he had painstakingly retrieved from Master's bedchamber with a hoof and inspected the restrictions on it.

[—"Such a simple restriction..."](<# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan">)

—"Then again, this is the biggest difference between me and 'you', isn't it?"-Yuwen Xuan scoffed, waving his hoof to trace a restriction-breaking talisman seal over the storage pouch.

Naturally, performing this with a cloven sheep's hoof was rather awkward, resulting in a talisman seal that looked suspiciously like a heart-shaped lamb footprint. Regardless, Yuwen Xuan managed to draw it out.

Having finished the talisman seal, Yuwen Xuan tossed the storage pouch into the "pond" as if holding a lit firecracker.

The pouch gradually sank into the "pond," drifting downward toward the Abyss Sea ruins beneath the waters.

Midway through the descent, however, the storage pouch swelled, growing larger and heavier until, like a body thrown from a great height, it slammed onto the ground with a dull *thud*.

Theoretically, falling from such an altitude, even a living body would be reduced to a corpse. Yet the enlarged pouch, now resembling a body bag, squirmed. A moment later, an adorable head covered in curly white wool poked out.

The instant the little black lamb saw that head, it erupted in incandescent fury:

—"Aha! Master really was secretly raising 'it'! He even turned 'it' into my likeness!"-the little black lamb bleated indignantly.

Indeed, the white lamb head poking out of the storage pouch was none other than Yuwen Xuan's... or rather, the Taotie's other soul-half.

It was only natural that they looked identical.

The little black lamb, however, completely ignored this fact, glaring murderously at the little white lamb on the other side of the pond as if it had [caught them red-handed in bed](<# "Literal CN: 捉奸在床 (zhuōjiān zài chuáng) — caught red-handed in adultery / caught in the act">). Had the other party not already been transported all the way to the Abyss Sea, he would have devoured it on the spot.

The little white lamb, however, was blissfully oblivious to its "other half" glaring at it.

Emitting plaintive *bleats*, it glanced left and right as if searching for something. Failing to find what it sought, its bleating took on an unmistakably disappointed lilt.

—"...Heh,"-the little black lamb scoffed.

Yet on second thought, he grew even more incensed:

['That fellow' doesn't have a shred of humanity! It doesn't even have the constraints of a human vessel—it's a ferocious beast that knows nothing but eating! Why on earth does it look so thoroughly domesticated now?! Just what did Master feed 'that thing'?!]

The little black lamb brooded in aggrieved jealousy, tears spilling uncontrollably down its cheeks in fat, pitter-patter drops.

—"Doesn't matter anymore. 'It' won't be around for long!"-the little black lamb muttered.

As if in response to this thought, the little white lamb abandoned its search for someone (correction: search for familiar food) and twitched its nose, sniffing the air as it prepared to forage for alternative prey.

Evidently, when it came to "food" in the white lamb's vicinity, nothing was more enticing to a Taotie than an injured Mahayana cultivator.

The little white lamb immediately trotted in the direction Siwu was heading, swiftly tracking down Siwu himself.

—"That's it, go on, eat him,"-the little black lamb urged under its breath.

[And then get smashed into smithereens by the Mahayana grandmaster!]

[That way, I can head over and reclaim the fragments of my 'other half'!]

This was the exact scheme Yuwen Xuan had cooked up back when he planted the crumbs of his bone-ash patty on Siwu:

Not only could he use his "other half" to teach the obnoxious Mahayana cultivator who had disrespected Master a harsh lesson, but he could also exploit this Mahayana grandmaster to obliterate his "other half," turning it into his own nourishment.

Of course, if that unfortunate Mahayana elder ended up having a chunk of flesh bitten off by the little white lamb, losing a bit of cultivation and soul, or even being devoured whole, that would purely be an "unforeseen accident." The one eating human flesh was the white lamb, not the black lamb. Therefore, even if the black lamb devoured the "cannibalistic white lamb" later on, it wouldn't count as the black lamb eating humans. The black lamb merely inheriting all the dharmic power and cultivation gained from the white lamb devouring a human was simply an "unavoidable consequence."

[—"What a fine calculation, killing two birds with one stone."](<# "Speaker: Zhou Hui — Literal CN: 一箭双雕 (yījiàn shuāngdiāo), shoot two hawks with one arrow / two birds with one stone">)

—"Right?! I really am a genius!"-the little black lamb boasted, tilting its head back smugly.

The moment the words left its mouth, it suddenly realized something was amiss.

[Master already went out. I'm the only one left in this cave-dwelling... right?]

[Then the person who just spoke to me was...]

Slowly, excruciatingly slowly, the little black lamb turned its head. Standing right behind it, smiling down at it, was its master.

There was, however, not a single trace of warmth in those eyes.

The little black lamb instantly let out a despairing squeak:

—"M-Master..."-it whimpered.

[Why did you come back so fast?!]

Zhou Hui had, of course, returned very quickly.

Upon arriving at Fengqing City and locating Mo Bei, he had quickly spotted Shui Huang'er beside her, around whom providential fate was converging, and confirmed the root cause.

Yet Zhou Hui had not immediately revealed himself to meet Shui Huang'er.

Certain "protocols" had to be observed.

For a Tribulation Transcendence supreme powerhouse to rush forward with unseemly eagerness would appear unbecoming to a guest from the Southern Realm; it was far better to let the other party come to him on her own.

Furthermore, he could use this window of time to do some homework in advance—adapting the introductory curriculum for the demon race would require a [billion](<# "Literal CN: 亿点点 (yì diǎndiǎn) — internet slang pun on 'a tiny bit' (一点点, yī diǎndiǎn) replacing 一 (one) with 亿 (a hundred million / a billion), meaning an absurd, overwhelming amount">) minor modifications.

On top of that, this served as a rare and invaluable opportunity to temper Mo Bei.

For all these reasons, Zhou Hui had swiftly departed Fengqing City, leaving Mo Bei to host Shui Huang'er on her own.

However, upon returning to his cave-dwelling, guided by an uncanny mentor's intuition, Zhou Hui had masked his spiritual pressure and presence, gliding silently over the Heavenly Spirit Sect to inspect what his disciples were up to.

None of the other disciples presented any issues. But in his own cave-dwelling, he had caught someone red-handed.

Zhou Hui sneered coldly:

—"Yuwen Xuan, your skills have certainly grown sharper by the day."

—"I didn't! I wasn't! I didn't do anything!"-the little black lamb fired off the [denial trio](<# "Literal CN: 否认三连 (fǒurèn sānlián) — modern Chinese internet meme: 'Not me, wasn't there, impossible / I didn't, I wasn't, I know nothing'">) in rapid succession, frantically attempting to block the view in the "pond" with its body.

This time, it did not attempt to flee.

Not because it didn't want to run, but because Yuwen Xuan knew full well when one could flee and when one could not.

Now was unequivocally a time when one could not.

Fleeing would only make Master even angrier!

If he ran away, he might lose his status as Master's disciple forever!

Still, even thinking with a sheep's brain, he knew what he had done today was far too out of line. It likely couldn't be resolved simply by taking a beating and hanging from the peach tree for a month. Unable to devise any other way to dodge punishment, Yuwen Xuan could only lie through his teeth:

[—"It went over there all by itself!"](<# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan">)

The moment he said it, Yuwen Xuan wanted to slap himself across the face with his own hooves.

How on earth could a remnant soul sealed inside a storage pouch go over there by itself?!

Yet having blurted it out, Yuwen Xuan could only bite the bullet and keep fabricating.

At this stage, he no longer harbored any hopes of escaping scot-free—he merely wanted a [sentence reduction](<# "Literal CN: 减刑 (jiǎnxíng) — sentence reduction / mitigating the penalty">). Thus, he admitted to a tiny sliver of guilt—or rather, mixed ten percent truth into his ninety percent lies:

—"I admit, I kind of wanted to teach that detestable Mahayana cultivator a lesson—who told him to disrespect you, Master? So last time, I stuck a little marker on him to bring him some bad luck, and today I was just secretly checking up on his status. After all, an almighty Mahayana grandmaster like him shouldn't be so easy to kill... Of course, I never wanted him dead! It was just... just at that very moment, that white lamb suddenly appeared!"

—"Oh?"-Zhou Hui crossed his arms, leaning back against the bookshelf behind him, wearing an expression that practically said, *Go on, let's see how you spin this.*

—"That white lamb was terrifying! Master, it genuinely wanted to eat me, *bleat*!"-the little black lamb sobbed, sounding so utterly sincere as tears pattered down once again.-"It charged right at me with ferocious malice! Although I wanted to eat it too, because you forbade it, Master, and because I'm different from it—I am your most obedient, well-behaved disciple—I held back! I didn't brawl with it, I just dodged to the side. Who knew it would run so fast that it slammed headfirst into the 'pond', got teleported to the Abyss Sea, and then set its sights on that Mahayana cultivator... *Bleat, sob!* This really wasn't entirely my fault!"

[Like hell it wasn't your fault.]

[Leaving aside whether that half-soul had the power to break the restrictions on the storage pouch, even if it broke out, would it have remembered to re-hang my robe back in its original position?!]

[Why is it that these children seem so clever, yet always tell such glaringly obvious lies?]

[And lying at a juncture like this only doubles the offense!]

The good mood Zhou Hui had felt over the Southern Realm's providential fate vanished completely in this instant. He looked coldly upon his eldest disciple, parting his lips to pronounce his sentence—only to hear a bloodcurdling scream echo from the "pond" behind the black lamb.

A scream from Siwu.

Zhou Hui paused in surprise. He remembered the man was a Mahayana cultivator; surely he wouldn't be eaten by a mere Taotie remnant soul, would he?

—"He couldn't have been eaten, could he?! *Bleat*!"-the little black lamb whipped its head around in sheer panic.

At this moment, it was even more anxious than Zhou Hui. It knew full well that if Siwu were truly devoured here by the white lamb... by its other soul-half, it would be virtually identical to being eaten by him. Master would never forgive him!

[Siwu! You're a Mahayana cultivator, for crying out loud! You have to stay alive!]

Yuwen Xuan prayed fervently, staring nervously into the "pond."

Then it let out a sigh of relief.

—"Master, this time it really has nothing to do with me!"-it wagged its stubby tail, announcing happily.

*Smack!* Zhou Hui slapped the back of its head.

—"Is this a time to be celebrating? By the looks of it, 'you' are about to be eaten as well!"

Indeed, on the other side of the "pond," somewhere deep within the Abyss Sea, both Siwu and the Taotie's remnant soul had simultaneously encountered mortal peril:

A demonic creature dominated their entire field of vision.

It was so colossal that it could practically swallow both Siwu and the little white lamb in a single gulp.

Even Zhou Hui was left slightly stunned by the sight:

—"Why on earth is there still a demonic beast like this beneath the Abyss Sea?!"
