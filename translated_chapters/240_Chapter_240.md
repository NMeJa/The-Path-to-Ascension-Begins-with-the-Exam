# Chapter 240: An Awkward Dragon

[← Previous Chapter](239_Chapter_239.md) | [Table of Contents](INDEX.md) | [Next Chapter →](241_Chapter_241.md)

[—"Beat him at his own game?"](# "Speaker: Lu Shulong")

Hearing this, Lu Shulong let out a scoffing chuckle.

Startled by the sudden sound, Ran Deng couldn't help whipping her head around to glare at him:

—"Why are you here?"-she demanded.

—"I've always been here,"-Lu Shulong said.

He truly had been by Ran Deng's side the entire time—namely, inside Water Mirror Tower.

Not only him, but Guan Ye and Mo Bei were also present.

However, they were in a state of separated divine souls:

While their physical bodies sat meditating before the water mirrors, their souls had plunged into the "illusion realm" on the other side of the water mirrors, continuously battling the demon race in their "future" states.

Thus, in theory, they shouldn't have had the bandwidth to attend to reality on this side, nor should they have been able to overhear the conversation on this end... right?

—"Don't play dumb—you know what I mean! I'm asking you: when did you 'come back'?"-Ran Deng pressed.

—"Not that long ago,"-Lu Shulong replied, blinking his autumn-water-limpid eyes with an aggrieved, delicate little pout.-"Could it be that you and Second Senior Brother were discussing something I'm not allowed to hear?"

—"Not really,"-Ran Deng muttered.

[Or was there? Shouldn't be, right?]

Ran Deng thought uncertainly.

Although there really didn't seem to be anything worth hiding from Lu Shulong, for reasons Ran Deng couldn't quite put her finger on, she simply didn't want to drag him into overly convoluted affairs.

—Perhaps it was because she had personally watched Master hatch this dragon from an egg? Or perhaps because, in theory, the duration he had spent as her youngest junior brother was actually longer than that of the current genuine Youngest Junior Brother, Mo Xi?

Ran Deng shook her head, yet subconsciously refrained from pursuing that topic further, instead following up on Lu Shulong's earlier chuckle:

—"I was just startled by your sudden laughter. Speaking of which, what are you laughing at? Did we say something funny?"-Ran Deng asked.

—"I'm laughing at Second Senior Brother,"-Lu Shulong admitted quite frankly.-"He actually said he's going to [beat him at his own game](# "Literal CN: 将计就计 (jiangji jiuji) — turning the enemy's scheme to one's own advantage")!"

—"Is there something wrong with that? Second Senior Brother is brilliant—it's not like he's Eldest Senior Brother,"-Ran Deng said, baffled by Lu Shulong's sense of humor.

—"If it were Eldest Senior Brother, I wouldn't be laughing at all; I'd only be fleeing as far away as possible,"-Lu Shulong replied dismissively.-"As for Second Senior Brother—brilliant as he may be, his brain and entrails are as immaculate as his pretty face; he can't stomach a single speck of ugly, underhanded filth. So forget about beating anyone at their own game: if he were even willing to use his brain to ponder a decent, respectable scheme instead of violently demolishing everything into scrap, I'd consider that a sign of respect toward his opponent!"

[Hearing that... why does it sound like you've been violently demolished into scrap yourself?!]

Ran Deng cast another strange look at Lu Shulong, only now noticing that he looked as though he had been gnawed on several times by Eldest Senior Brother—radiating an inexplicable wave of salty resentment ever since moments ago. Regrettably, now was hardly the time to pry.

For now, Ran Deng still defended Qu Qianxing:

—"Second Senior Brother does indeed hate evil as an enemy and despises conspiracies and underhanded tricks. But even Second Senior Brother would surely try utilizing a few [unorthodox tricks](# "Literal CN: 小道 (xiaodao) — minor paths / trickery") when confronting an enemy he cannot overcome, wouldn't he?"-Ran Deng argued.

—"An enemy he cannot overcome?"-Lu Shulong echoed.

—"Didn't you see them? Those celestial soldiers and generals..."-Ran Deng said, her expression solemn as she looked toward the battlefield reflected in the water mirror.

Lu Shulong followed her gaze.

However, his soul had already been inside the "illusion realm" earlier, and even now half of his divine sense still resided upon the bone dragon over there. How could he not know about the arrival of the celestial soldiers and generals? He simply hadn't approached them.

In fact, the bone dragon controlled by Lu Shulong had consistently been [farming the jungle](# "Gaming slang: 打野 (daye) — jungling / clearing neutral mobs on the edge of the map") on the "edge of the map."

This was because the bone dragon he controlled inexplicably repelled going toward the center of the battlefield—namely, the direction of White Jade Capital and the Ascending Heaven Stairway. Furthermore, Lu Shulong the dragon was rather [laid-back](# "Literal CN: 佛系 (foxi) — Buddha-like / detached / unbothered"). His agreeing to participate in the trial system had less to do with getting stronger and more to do with the fact that Guan Ye and his other fellow disciples were participating, and he didn't want to be left out. Enhancing his own power was hardly his first priority, so he naturally lacked any striving, competitive spirit, and didn't mind curling up in a remote corner to [slack off](# "Literal CN: 摸鱼 (moyu) — catching fish / slacking off on the job").

However, for Lu Shulong, this was already more than enough.

Discrepancies in aptitude existed among humans, to say nothing of unreasonable creatures like True Dragons. Even among divine beasts (and ferocious beasts) born from the spiritual energy of heaven and earth, there existed no creature more flawless than a True Dragon—let alone an uncontrollable glutton of a "defective product" like a Taotie.

The reason Zhou Hui had never discovered that Lu Shulong possessed the memories of the previous reincarnation cycles was certainly not because Zhou Hui didn't care about his disciple, nor was it because Lu Shulong's acting skills were transcendentally divine (though Lu Shulong's acting was indeed formidable—at the very least far superior to Mo Xi's petty, clever theatrics). Rather, it was because with a mortal vessel, even Zhou Hui fundamentally could not fathom just how profound the latent potential of a creature like a True Dragon truly was.

Even if a True Dragon merely lay flat and did nothing, as long as it had enough time, it would naturally grow into the most powerful existence between heaven and earth.

Not to mention, a True Dragon's concept of "time" was entirely different from that of humanity. As beings existing in a higher dimension in both physical body and divine soul, they were not bound by the "time" or "space" of a single dimension.

Therefore, even after several cycles of reincarnation—where Lu Shulong seemingly regrew from scratch in every lifetime—in reality, his memories had resided within the consciousness of his higher-dimensional true form all along. Unless Zhou Hui could break through dimensional boundaries, he could never truly erase those memories.

On the contrary, accompanying the reshaping and re-condensing lifetime after lifetime, Lu Shulong's physical vessel and divine sense were continuously purified and refined. Not only did he tangibly acquire cultivation equivalent to that accumulated time, but he was also forged to be far more potent and pure than ordinary True Dragons.

Naturally, this did not come entirely without a price.

Because he worried about making Master sad, what Lu Shulong had never spoken of was the sensation every time time was rewound: feeling the power he had already cultivated being stripped from his being, and his fully grown body being forcibly shrunk, reversed, and crammed back into a fertilized egg! It was virtually a process of having his tendons ripped out and his skin flayed, before every ounce of his flesh and blood was smashed into paste and then compressed. Even for a dragon, the agony made him wish for eternal slumber.

Compared to this excruciating pain, the increasingly colossal power accumulating across another dimension was hardly worth celebrating; in fact, Lu Shulong found it quite a nuisance—it made it increasingly difficult for him (before being exposed) to pretend to be a tiny dragon and act cute to Master.

Moreover, restricted by this world, he had been fundamentally incapable of mastering and wielding that surging power!

Fortunately, this dilemma had finally been resolved upon entering the trial system and transforming into the bone dragon.

Although this bone dragon was an imitation synthesized by the trial system based on the "prophecy," the trial system itself was a computational engine calculating the "closest possible future" by synthesizing all manner of data and archives. In other words, provided there was sufficient data, even an imitation generated by the trial system would approach reality to an infinitesimal degree.

Furthermore, this prophesied bone dragon seemingly possessed a certain degree of intrinsic connection to Lu Shulong from the start—it was not Lu Shulong, yet directly related to Lu Shulong—which was why it could barely be constructed within the trial system in the first place.

In the beginning, Lu Shulong's control over it had been quite clumsy. However, after a certain point in time—thinking back carefully, it seemed to be right after Eldest Senior Brother ran off (more precisely, after Yuwen Xuan participated in Thousand Beasts Hall's inheritance trial and obtained their bone flute)—the trial system abruptly calibrated the bone dragon, and Lu Shulong could sense a far deeper resonance.

Sure enough, Lu Shulong held a profound relationship with this bone dragon.

To be precise, this bone dragon was his biological father.

Lu Shulong could perceive that the other party's skeletal remains were pinned beneath Thousand Beasts Hall, sealed down by a single drop of True Immortal Blood. However, because True Dragons were naturally born from the spiritual energy of heaven and earth, reproduction did not strictly require sexual procreation; sufficient spiritual energy was all that was needed. In particular, with the presence of a powerhouse like Untiring Immortal Venerable near Thousand Beasts Hall—capable of independently constructing and reshaping spiritual environments—even though this realm was ill-suited for draconic legacy, those sealed skeletal remains still re-condensed essence blood and laid an egg. That egg was subsequently sniffed out by a certain gluttonous Taotie, dug up, discovered, and incubated—which became Lu Shulong.

[Ah, I knew it—I really was born of Master.]

Lu Shulong thought, happily rolling around amidst the cloud banks.

He deliberately ignored the cause and effect, not even bothering to look into the origin of this bone dragon's remains, selectively choosing only the parts he liked.

Yet regardless of what Lu Shulong thought, once his connection to the bone dragon became clear and the inheritance of power was established, Lu Shulong received all at once the methods for wielding power he had never previously mastered. The so-called True Dragon inheritance was wonderfully convenient in that regard.

What was even more "fortunate" was that at this critical juncture, a surge of power abruptly flooded into him.

Although for his True Dragon true form, this power could merely be considered icing on the cake, the divine authority embedded within it seemed custom-tailored for Lu Shulong, allowing him to control his colossal true body—which was potent enough to shatter this world—with far greater delicacy and finesse.

Most importantly, this power felt incomparably familiar to Lu Shulong.

It reminded him of an experience from his infancy: when Eldest Senior Brother had locked him inside an alchemy furnace, Master worried that opening the furnace recklessly might harm him—even though he was tougher to refine than the hardest ten-thousand-year dark crystal—and worried that he would be terrified and lonely inside the furnace. Master had spared no effort to project his own divine soul into the cauldron, carefully and tenderly guiding him out. In fact, that "game" had been so fun that Lu Shulong had chosen alchemy—a craft a True Dragon had zero need for—as his primary field of study!

This power also reminded him of the days before he had even hatched: during every reincarnation cycle inside the eggshell, he had been delicately, warmly enveloped and nurtured by Master's spiritual energy. Soaked in that spiritual nourishment, even the agony of being smashed to pieces and having his soul shattered at the end of every cycle was thoroughly healed. It had kept him from fearing that excruciating death. Indeed, that was the sole reason he had managed to endure the torment of dying in every cycle: because he knew that upon cracking through his shell once more, the first thing he would see was guaranteed to be Master's gentle smile.

Yes, there was no mistaking it: this was Master's power.

[—"But why?"](# "Speaker: Lu Shulong")

Just like Qu Qianxing and Ran Deng, Lu Shulong was thoroughly perplexed by this abruptly bestowed power.

Yet perplexed as he was, Lu Shulong naturally would never refuse any gift from Master. He unhesitatingly devoured and absorbed that surge of power, regardless of whether it was good or bad.

Besides, how could Master ever harm him?

After receiving that power, Lu Shulong's mastery over his primordial power leaped to an entirely new tier. Paired with the True Dragon legacy brought by the bone dragon, Lu Shulong instantly felt that his current self was terrifyingly strong—able to channel the power of a True Dragon without the slightest impediment, even across several dimensions.

Still somewhat incredulous, Lu Shulong gently swiped the bone dragon's claw toward nearby demonic creatures.

With that single swipe, a violent gale erupted all around, instantly grinding the demonic creatures into fine powder. Not only that, but demonic qi across a radius of a thousand li was swept clean in one breath—and along with it, a spatial rift in a certain corner where demons had been pouring out was completely purged.

—"That direction... seems to be the Blood Demon Palace..."-Lu Shulong mumbled, glancing toward the flattened spatial rift. He recalled that a fleeing demon had died there, plugging the spatial fissure, which subsequently gave rise to the territory of the Blood Demon Palace.-"Well, it shouldn't matter anyway. It's not the real 'past' after all—just an illusion realm."

Lu Shulong shook his head and tossed the matter to the back of his mind.

Then, he grew genuinely thrilled:

[I really can wield my power freely now!]

[The current me could probably beat Eldest Senior Brother until he bleats like a sheep!]

Carried away with smug delight, Lu Shulong tumbled several times through the sky. Ever since being subjected to the combined double beatdown from Eldest Senior Brother and Second Senior Brother in the third cycle, and then being completely refined by Fourth Senior Brother in the fourth cycle, he had never dared to get this swollen with arrogance. If Yuwen Xuan were truly standing in front of him right now, he might genuinely receive several claw swipes!

Regrettably (or fortunately?), Lu Shulong's inflated pride didn't last long before being blasted awake by the sound of thunder.

The thunder hadn't actually struck Lu Shulong; it was concentrated entirely in the central region of the Central Domain, dense as a torrential squall. Even gazing from the distant corners of the sky, it resembled a colossal golden bowl inverted over the heart of the earth, firmly encapsulating the Ascending Heaven Stairway and White Jade Capital—carrying an overwhelming momentum that threatened to wipe out everything within, whether demonic beasts or celestial soldiers and generals, in a single breath.

In theory, the only person here capable of commanding such a spectacle was Untiring Immortal Venerable. Yet relying on a True Dragon's acute sensitivity to spiritual energy, Lu Shulong felt that while those lightning bolts were as flawless as Master's heavenly lightning, they were far too dazzling and aggressive, utterly ruthless and uncompromising—lacking a touch of mercy, and brimming with a surplus of fury.

—"...Second Senior Brother."

Lu Shulong made the judgment in an instant.

Although in theory his Second Senior Brother did not possess such power, Lu Shulong still felt that this style of attack belonged to Qu Qianxing, and could only be Qu Qianxing.

Yet even with this conclusion reached in his heart, Lu Shulong still did not join the commotion near White Jade Capital. Flicking the bone dragon's tail, his divine soul returned to Water Mirror Tower instead.

Inside Water Mirror Tower, Ran Deng happened to be in a voice transmission with Qu Qianxing. Listening to their tone, they clearly knew vastly more than he did—why, they were already throwing down against the Heavenly Court.

[So that's why Master bestowed power?]

[Then again, makes sense. The only one Master ever plays favorites with is Eldest Senior Brother; other than that, he would never give power to just one disciple.]

Lu Shulong developed a slight misunderstanding.

At the same time, his spirits dampened considerably.

After all, in Lu Shulong's eyes, merely growing stronger on one's own wasn't true strength—as a True Dragon, becoming powerful was merely a matter of time. Only being able to defeat his martial siblings—especially those top two—counted as true strength!

But how on earth was he supposed to beat them?!

Never mind that Taotie: at least it was a ferocious beast of equal renown to a True Dragon, and was born before him, so not being able to beat it was normal enough. But what was up with that fellow Qu Qianxing? He was clearly a mere human! Having a perverted learning capacity was one thing, but why was his sheer talent on par with a True Dragon?! Even this time, the moment he received the power, he could immediately unleash it! Master had only demonstrated it in front of him a few times, right? How could he have mastered it just like that?! Fourth Senior Brother is still the cutest, *sob sob sob*!

With a stomach full of resentment, Lu Shulong had been solely occupied with roasting Qu Qianxing earlier, not paying much heed to any other presence.

Only now that Ran Deng brought them up did he actually bother to look at those celestial soldiers and generals.

Though in truth, those celestial soldiers and generals were hardly worthy of a proper glance from Lu Shulong anyway: caught between the demon army and a barrage of heavenly lightning, they looked utterly battered, disheveled, and exhausted. If one weren't told, one could scarcely tell they were supposed to be awe-inspiring celestial troops.

Fortunately, petulant as Lu Shulong was being, he wasn't about to derail serious business over a petty tantrum.

Even while roasting Qu Qianxing, he didn't underestimate the celestial soldiers and generals simply because Qu Qianxing had temporarily driven them into a corner. Gazing into the water mirror, Lu Shulong's eyes turned serious:

—"Celestial soldiers, huh? *Hmph*. So we've clashed with the Heavenly Court after all?"-Lu Shulong muttered.

—"'After all'?"-Ran Deng asked, surprised.

The sudden merger of the God-Demon battlefield with the illusion realm at their doorstep was an unforeseen emergency. Ran Deng remained completely in the dark regarding its origins, which was why she couldn't understand why Lu Shulong spoke as though he had known all along that the Heavenly Court would come.

Yet noticing the bone dragon controlled by Lu Shulong in the water mirror, she felt an epiphany strike:

—"Ah, right—that prophecy! The prophecy said we would wage war against the Heavenly Court. Could this be the origin of it?"-Ran Deng wondered aloud.

—"No, earlier,"-Lu Shulong said darkly. In his foul mood, he made no effort to conceal the grim suspicions he had harbored for a long time.-"I've felt that way for a long time. After all, other than the Heavenly Court, who else could force Master to restart time so many times, yet still leave the Heavenly Spirit Sect without a proper future?"

—"You mean..."-Ran Deng froze.

Seeing Ran Deng's expression, Lu Shulong also froze.

He abruptly realized that he didn't seem to have ever discussed past lifetimes with any of his fellow disciples—including his most trusted Fourth Senior Brother. Or rather, Fourth Senior Brother was the person Lu Shulong wanted to hide it from most, and he certainly didn't wish for his other martial siblings to recover their memories either, especially the memories of the third cycle. Fortunately, other than Eldest Senior Brother, the others shouldn't know... right?

Lu Shulong immediately wanted to gloss it over, but then heard Ran Deng murmur thoughtfully:

—"That makes sense too. Other than the Heavenly Court, there truly is no one who could push Master to such an extent."

—"You know?!"-Lu Shulong gasped, startled out of his wits.

He never imagined that Senior Sister, the biggest [salted fish](# "Slang: 咸鱼 (xianyu) — slacker / couch potato") of every lifetime, would actually have memories!

Wait, if even Senior Sister had recovered her memories, then Second Senior Brother...

Lu Shulong grew even more nervous. Blinking his autumn-water eyes, he probed Ran Deng in a soft, sweet cadence:

—"Senior Sister, you remembered too? How did you remember?"

—"It was an accident. It's a bit complicated to explain... Wait a minute, didn't you remember as well? How did you remember?"-Ran Deng countered.

However, she was oblivious to Lu Shulong's guilt and had zero interest in investigating the process of her fellow disciple recovering his memories. Instead, Lu Shulong's earlier conjecture occupied her entire mind.

Ran Deng shook her head, returning to the previous topic:

—"...Forget it, now is not the time for idle chatter. You mentioned the Heavenly Court just now—if it really is the Heavenly Court, what did they mean in the first lifetime?!"

—"The first lifetime? The very first cycle? Something from so long ago, I don't remember very clearly. Does Senior Sister remember something special?"-Lu Shulong asked, bewildered. He was still probing Ran Deng, though the question carried a genuine shred of curiosity. The first cycle hadn't involved the messy, tangled squabbles of later cycles; it was the cleanest—and Lu Shulong had died cleanly too, so he had never paid much attention to what happened in the first lifetime.

Ran Deng, however, was different.

She had barely participated in the chaos of the various cycles. Aside from the regret of burdening Master through her own death, Ran Deng was similar to the memory-less Qu Qianxing and Guan Ye, unable to offer Master useful intelligence—which was another source of her deep guilt. Yet precisely because Ran Deng hadn't been deeply embroiled in the disastrous nonsense stirred up by her martial siblings, she was able to stand from the perspective of an outsider, reasoning backwards from the very beginning.

Ran Deng murmured thoughtfully:

—"The first lifetime was naturally the most unique. That was the lifetime where Master came closest to ascension. If I recall correctly, even the Heavenly Gate opened!"

—"Is there a problem with that?"-Lu Shulong asked, confused.-"With Master's strength, ascending is a given sooner or later, isn't it? If anything, it's far stranger that every subsequent cycle became progressively more difficult for him."

—"Exactly. So why couldn't Master ascend?"-Ran Deng let out a heavy sigh.-"If everything we suffered was truly the Heavenly Court's decree, if it was 'Heaven's mandate' as you say, then at least in the first cycle, when the Heavenly Gate opened, Master earned the qualifications to ascend."

Ran Deng paused here, her voice turning hoarse:

—"Could it be that... because of us, Master chose to renounce ascension and rewind time back then, and thus earned the Heavenly Court's grudge?"

Reasoning up to this point, Ran Deng was already certain she had hit upon the truth:

After all, the Heavenly Gate wasn't something that opened at a whim. If every ascender knocked upon the Heavenly Gate only to tell them to "hold on a sec," where would the Heavenly Court's dignity be? Not to mention, the reason Master told the Heavenly Court to "hold on" was to reverse time, twist Heaven's mandate, and alter the Heavenly Dao—and he had done it a whole six times! Six entire times! Ran Deng figured that if she were the deity in charge of ascension in the Heavenly Court, even she wouldn't be able to resist dropping heavenly thunderbolts to blast him to death—Wait, that god had already been offed by Master? Then never mind!

Yet this meant that not only had they caused Master to reincarnate multiple times, but they had also severed Master's "future."

For a moment, Ran Deng was assaulted by a guilt even heavier than when she had first recovered her memories. The flame upon her forehead flared ablaze, threatening to set her entire divine soul on fire.

Regrettably, she was granted no time for weeping and repentance this time around. For beside her stood not the Master who would patiently comfort her, but her Fifth Junior Brother—who appeared melancholic and sensitive on the surface, but had absolutely zero connection to being empathetic or considerate.

Having listened to Ran Deng's analysis, Lu Shulong finally realized the gravity of the situation. Or rather, he had always known things were quite grave, but if the Heavenly Court was merely targeting his Heavenly Spirit Sect martial siblings—even including himself—Lu Shulong didn't care all that much. Only Master was his bottom line.

Lu Shulong let out a cold sneer:

—"Hold a grudge? Against Master? How dare they?!"

Though he merely said those words, his pupils abruptly shifted from serene circles, like the moon mirrored in autumn water, into vigilant, razor-sharp vertical slits. The entire draconic aura about him underwent a drastic transformation.

Ran Deng was startled by Lu Shulong's sudden aura. Subconsciously wanting to placate him, she parted her lips, but then changed her mind.

She didn't know if it was because this Fifth Junior Brother had been bullied by Eldest Senior Brother since childhood, but his personality was exceedingly [laid-back](# "Literal CN: 佛系 (foxi) — Buddha-like / detached / unbothered") (or so Ran Deng believed). His perspective on things was always somewhat bizarre, never grasping the key point. Sometimes he seemed so obedient he would die without a fuss if told to, yet other times he would throw baffling, petulant tantrums over trivial nonsense—just like moments ago.

If it were a squabble between fellow disciples, Ran Deng would naturally act as the peacemaker. But this was a common enemy!

And so, Ran Deng added fuel to the fire:

—"Why wouldn't they dare? That's the high and mighty Heavenly Court! Think about it carefully: even if purely for the sake of face, they would never accept someone who rejected them a second time. What's more, the reason Master renounced ascension was to overturn the fate they had ordained! I fear they regarded Master as someone who had to be eradicated—for instance, wasn't that world-destroying heavenly lightning in the fifth cycle meant to punish Master? Not to mention the cycle after that, where our Master was replaced outright by some bizarre impostor. Thinking back on it now, weren't they trying to thoroughly erase Master's very existence... *Oof*!"

Ran Deng abruptly let out a pained cry. It felt as though a dragon tail had smacked her squarely in the face; even her physical body throbbed in searing agony alongside her divine soul.

Naturally, Lu Shulong hadn't actually struck Ran Deng with a dragon tail, but it was essentially no different.

As Ran Deng's deduction unfolded, Lu Shulong was consumed by genuine, incandescent fury. He completely failed to rein in his draconic pressure, causing it to materialize and lash out at Ran Deng.

Not only Ran Deng, but the surrounding water mirrors were also impacted. The surfaces rippled violently, and several mirrors emitted sounds like shattering colored glaze, even developing hairline fractures that should never appear upon water.

Even Mo Xi, far away at the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, sensed the anomaly within his Water Mirror Tower.

Through one of the mirrors, his voice squawked in alarm:

—"Eldest Senior Sister, did something happen?! I feel spiritual energy over at Water Mirror Tower going completely haywire... Fifth Senior Brother?! Fuck! What the hell are you two doing?!"

—"...Nothing,"-Ran Deng answered, clutching her cheek.

Yet she had no time to explain anything to Mo Xi, merely turning back to Lu Shulong:

—"In any case, just like the 'prophecy' Mo Bei spoke of, the Heavenly Court has become our enemy. Fortunately, none of us have turned into the worst versions depicted in that prophecy yet, so there's still time. As long as we cooperate with Second Senior Brother, I think what we should do right now is... *Aaaah*!"

*BOOM—!*

Her words were cut short a second time. This time it was thunder—and not merely thunder.

For before that terrifying thunderclap could even detonate, a blinding white light radiating from the nearby water mirrors had already scorched Ran Deng's eyes.

With her cultivation, she originally wouldn't have been able to withstand such brilliance. Fortunately, ever since returning from the Ascending Heaven Stairway, Ran Deng had been perpetually scorched by her soul-fire, so she hadn't dared to be as chronically lazy with her cultivation and Master's homework as in the past. That diligence proved useful now: faintly sensing that this power was not directed at her—and even held a subtle protective grace—Ran Deng hastily stabilized her soul-fire and circulated her spiritual energy, successfully weathering the collateral backlash of the lightning and thunder.

The mirrors of Water Mirror Tower, however, were not nearly so resilient. Already fractured, they now blew apart entirely, turning Water Mirror Tower into a tower of water.

On the other side, Mo Xi's life plaque let out a shriek. While that gadget was only supposed to act up when Mo Xi was in mortal peril, Ran Deng was certain it was just Mo Xi throwing a fit to demand an explanation.

Ran Deng still had no time to care about Mo Xi. Looking around in panic, she asked nervously:

—"What happened?!"

Could celestial soldiers and generals have invaded the Heavenly Spirit Sect?!

—"Relax, it's not that bad,"-Lu Shulong sneered, as though reading Ran Deng's mind. Yet his draconic transformation was even more pronounced than before, to the point where a long dragon tail was now exposed.

With a flick of his tail, the droplets of water splattered across Water Mirror Tower—including the ones that had just exploded—instantly froze in midair. The water mirror surfaces settled into tranquility, once more reflecting the scene from the "illusion realm" on the other side.

Regrettably, visibility remained wretched, for lightning crackled and thunder roared violently on the opposite side, making it impossible to discern whether the figures beyond were human or ghost.

Lu Shulong flicked his tail, picking out the single mirror among them that was barely legible—the very mirror containing the "culprit."

Within the thunder and lightning, Qu Qianxing stood with an icy visage, golden electrical arcs enveloping his entire body—[physically, dazzlingly hand"some"—quite literally glowing with brilliant light](# "Wordplay: 漂亮 (piaoliang) means 'handsome/pretty', but 亮 (liang) literally means 'bright/luminous' — he is handsome, and quite literally glowing like a lightbulb").

—"So this is Second Senior Brother's 'beating him at his own game,'"-Lu Shulong mocked coldly.-"His head really is just a decorative ornament, isn't it?!"

—"*Ugh*..."

Having laid down a mountain of groundwork in hopes of getting Lu Shulong to cooperate properly with Qu Qianxing, Ran Deng let out a groan of pure agony.
