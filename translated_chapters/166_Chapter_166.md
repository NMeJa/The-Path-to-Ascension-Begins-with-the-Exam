# Chapter 166: Splashes

—"Pay it no mind. The one hanging from the peach tree is our sect's Eldest Senior Brother,"-the senior brothers and sisters of the Heavenly Spirit Sect said kindly and genially to the fresh recruits.-"Look at it long enough and you'll get used to it."

Was that the sort of thing anyone could ever get used to?!

The new recruits lifted their heads in awe and trepidation, looking toward the topmost boughs of the ancient peach tree closest to the mountain gate. There, their legendary Eldest Senior Brother hung suspended, precisely like a banner fluttering in the wind.

No, not "precisely like"—he literally *was* a banner.

Yuwen Xuan had been impaled clean through by a spear of light, pinned securely to the highest bough. For a Taotie whose hide had been thick and rough even in infancy, however, such an injury amounted to very little—to say nothing of the fact that he had now attained the Void Refinement stage. Even an ordinary human cultivator at this realm would hardly perish easily from an attack of this caliber. Besides, Zhou Hui had no intention of killing his own disciple; that spear of light was purely meant to pin him in place, rendering Yuwen Xuan utterly immobile. Most importantly, however, a brilliant red banner hung draped across Yuwen Xuan's body.

A banner emblazoned with the words:

> "New Academic Year, New Starting Point, New Endeavor, New Harvest!"

Even without wind, this banner billowed and fluttered on its own, unfurled wide across midair in a painfully eye-catching display. Whether someone was flying past overhead or walking along the ground below, they could not resist pausing to look. And naturally, as they looked at the banner, their eyes inevitably landed on Yuwen Xuan as well.

One could say [the damage was negligible, but the humiliation was absolute](# "Meme: 伤害不大，侮辱性极强 (Low damage, maximum disrespect / extremely insulting)").

Not only the new recruits, but even the returning students felt their eyelids twitch and their hearts palpitate at the sight.

Though the "freshmen" from the previous year had learned over the past twelve months that "failing an exam means getting hung from a tree" was merely an unfounded rumor, seeing their Eldest Senior Brother publicly exhibited on the tree the moment the new term began still made them mutter uneasily in their hearts:

[Could the sect actually be preparing to roll out this disciplinary measure for real...?]

After all, when they calmed down and thought about it with their brains, last year's terrifying spectacle—being tied into a butterfly bow—was physically impossible for ordinary mortals. Yet even so, they had lived in trembling apprehension for an entire year. But Eldest Senior Brother's current predicament this year? That was something they *could* accomplish!

An ordinary person with zero cultivation, wrapped in a banner and hung up for a day or two, probably wouldn't die either.

It was just... humiliating!

Utterly, excruciatingly humiliating!

From a certain perspective, perhaps being folded into a bow and hung up was actually preferable. At first glance, a bow was visually horrifying and grotesque, which paradoxically meant nobody focused much on the unlucky bastard's face. But in Yuwen Xuan's current posture, everyone across the entire sect felt they had never had a better, clearer opportunity to gaze upon the face of their perennially elusive Eldest Senior Brother.

He was undeniably handsome, sure—but didn't that only make it all the more humiliating?!

Even though it was early September, when [the autumn tiger's heat raged fierce and scorching](# "Cultural term: 秋老虎 (Autumn Tiger) — late-summer/early-autumn scorching heatwave"), every disciple couldn't help but clutch their light robes tighter around their chests, silently forging their academic resolutions for the year: strive with all their might never to end up in Yuwen Xuan's shoes.

If even the senior students reacted this way, the new recruits were naturally all the more terrified.

Fortunately, the number of incoming freshmen this year was not as large as the year before.

—Make no mistake: it was not that fewer people had come to seek entry at the mountain gate this year. Quite the contrary. With the convening of the Immortal Arts Assembly and the publication of the *White Jade Capital Daily* alongside the academic journal *Arts & Sorcery*, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had not only maxed out its prestige among high-ranking cultivators, but had also become a holy land in the hearts of low-ranking cultivators.

Even before the previous term's final examinations had taken place, young cultivators eager to join the sect had already begun converging on the Heavenly Spirit Mountain Range in droves. This had caused several bustling bazaars to spring up along the perimeter of Mount Tianling, making their eventual evolution into full-fledged cultivation cities only a matter of time.

Yet no matter how thoroughly Zhou Hui believed in [education for all without discrimination](# "Literal CN: 有教无类 (yǒujiào wúlèi) — Confucius's classic pedagogical philosophy that education should be accessible to everyone regardless of background or status"), it was physically impossible to accept every single applicant among such a staggering multitude.

Consequently, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had actually cranked up the difficulty of this year's "Entrance Examination," placing a strict cap on recruitment quotas so that only applicants who attained a specific score threshold were permitted entry.

The world held no objections to this whatsoever; on the contrary, everyone felt that this was precisely how it ought to be.

Seeking to become a disciple under the Untiring Immortal Venerable had never been an easy feat to begin with. People discussed the entrance examination with immense relish, and some cultivators who harbored zero intention of actually joining the Heavenly Spirit Sect even showed up purely to take the exam for fun. To this phenomenon, Mo Xi had commented that [in a world this vast, you really can find any kind of person (psychopath)](# "Chinese slang: 天下之大，什么鸟/精神病都有 — in a world this vast, every sort of weirdo exists").

Naturally, there were also many who felt thoroughly disheartened by the tightened recruitment quotas of the Heavenly Spirit Sect.

Fortunately, there was no need for them to despair overmuch. Standing in inverse proportion to the decrease in direct sect admissions was the dramatic expansion of the White Jade Capital Academies.

In fact, ever since the "Spirit Root Aptitude Test," the White Jade Capital Academies had grandly entered the purview of the wider cultivation realm, earning widespread prestige and high esteem.

This was especially true for small-to-midsize cultivation clans.

Most of these clans relied on private in-house tutor academies. However, cultivators of genuine competence rarely lingered at home to teach, meaning educational quality could never be guaranteed. Their next generation had to rely entirely on luck when it came to their spirit roots. Yet smaller clans lacked the demographic sheer numbers of the grand cultivation aristocracies; if several generations passed without producing a single high-talent seed, the entire lineage would inevitably backslide into ordinary mortals.

They were naturally unwilling to let their houses wither into obscurity, yet their clan resources were insufficient to license the official Heavenly Spirit Sect standardized textbooks—and the Heavenly Spirit Sect refused to license its curriculum to sects and clans that could not guarantee pedagogical standards. Thus, these clans set their sights squarely on the White Jade Capital Academies.

If they could send their offspring into the academies, even children with inferior spirit roots—or no spirit roots at all—could learn techniques and foundational skills usually reserved exclusively for cultivators.

Armed with these, their chances of being accepted when petitioning smaller sects would surge dramatically.

Not to mention the fact that if a student demonstrated outstanding academic performance and exemplary conduct within the academy, they could earn direct promotion to the Heavenly Spirit Sect even with slightly lesser spirit roots. Even prestigious, large cultivation clans coveted this pipeline with green-eyed envy. Furthermore, rogue cultivators who had failed the direct sect entrance exams in previous years immediately realized that the White Jade Capital Academy was a golden shortcut to entering the Heavenly Spirit Sect.

Consequently, ever since the Spirit Root Aptitude Test, countless people had been actively inquiring about enrollment procedures for the White Jade Capital Academy.

The academies, for their part, turned no one away.

For that year's autumn semester, the number of White Jade Capital Academy branches had surged by over a hundred percent. It was said that the upcoming spring intake would expand even further, adhering to a policy of admitting all eligible students without exception, whether mortal or cultivator.

Furthermore, direct admission from the White Jade Capital Academy was no longer exclusive to the Heavenly Spirit Sect. Sects expressing interest could, after passing accreditation by the "Central Domain Cultivation Sect Accreditation Council" chaired by the Untiring Immortal Venerable, directly recruit graduates from the White Jade Capital Academy. For instance, Eternal Villa—which had historically recruited exclusively from its own bloodline—had become one of the direct-recruitment partner sects following accreditation. Another example was the Thousand Harmony Gang, which similarly practiced an open-door policy; they openly declared that any graduate of the White Jade Capital Academy would be fast-tracked directly into their managerial ranks upon joining.

This made the White Jade Capital Academies all the more irresistibly appealing.

Ah, the narrative was straying a bit far.

Setting aside the rapidly expanding academies for the moment, the new disciples who managed to enter the Heavenly Spirit Sect this term could truly be called the elite of the elite. With few exceptions, most of them had conducted exhaustive research into the Heavenly Spirit Sect prior to their arrival, including several standout pupils who had earned direct promotion from the academies.

During their prior intelligence gathering, it was inevitable that they had caught wind of certain peculiar rumors.

And the most outrageous rumor of all now seemed to be proving its concrete, undeniable truth right before their very eyes:

[—"I heard long ago that if you fail an exam in the Heavenly Spirit Sect, you get hung from a tree. To think it's actually true."](# "Speaker: New Recruit")

[—"Huh?! There's really a disciplinary measure like that?!"](# "Speaker: New Recruit")

[—"Even the Eldest Senior Brother is hanging up there—could there be any mistake?!"](# "Speaker: New Recruit")

[—"Too brutal! Absolutely barbaric!"](# "Speaker: New Recruit")

[—"Honestly, if I had to be hung up like that, I'd rather just die."](# "Speaker: New Recruit")

—the new recruits gasped in hushed lamentations, staring up with trembling fear at the Eldest Senior Brother perched high in the peach tree.

Several of them even pressed their palms together in devout worship, murmuring silent prayers under their breath—invariably along the lines of "Please let me pass my exams," and the like. It completely escaped their minds to consider what on earth a fellow student hanging from a tree could possibly bless them with.

* * *

Regarding the fact that their Eldest Senior Brother was once again [hung up on the tree for the umpteenth time](# "Chinese internet meme: 又双叒叕 (yòu shuāng ruò zhuó) — stacking the character 又 (again) 1, 2, 3, and 4 times to emphasize something happening over and over and over again"), in contrast to the returning students' unease and the fresh recruits' terror, the direct disciples displayed a distinct, utter lack of sibling love, offering nothing but gleeful schadenfreude.

—"*Jie-jie!* I knew nothing good would come of Master looking for him,"-Guan Ye chuckled, swirling a tiny wine cup in his hand.

He was currently seated alongside Lu Shulong in a small gazebo perched on the mountainside path leading toward Zhou Hui's immortal cave.

The scenery from this vantage point was superb: not only did it offer a panoramic bird's-eye view of half the sect, but it faced directly toward the mountain gate, allowing an unobstructed view of the ancient peach tree—and naturally, of their Eldest Senior Brother suspended upon it.

Even when not specifically paying their respects to Master, Guan Ye and Lu Shulong often enjoyed sitting here to catch the breeze and sip a drink or two.

Mo Xi happened to be strolling past with Mo Bei in tow and ambled over to beg for two cups of fruit juice.

—"Drinking wine stunts your growth,"-Guan Ye stated plainly when pouring them.

Mm. They were merely here for their ordinary daily leisure; catching sight of Eldest Senior Brother was purely coincidental.

They definitely had *not* come here on purpose!

Still, drinking always required conversation, so it was only natural that talk would drift to their Eldest Senior Brother!

—"I wonder what Eldest Senior Brother did this time. It seemed like Master was originally prepared to let him off the hook,"-Lu Shulong remarked.

—"Hee-hee, whatever it was, it certainly wasn't anything good,"-Mo Xi said, making zero effort to conceal his glee.-"Still, those bizarre rumors praising Eldest Senior Brother can finally be put to rest, can't they? Second Senior Brother looked thoroughly annoyed by them."

[No, the one who found them most annoying was definitely you,] Mo Bei thought deadpan, sipping her fruit juice with an impassive face.

Upon hearing Mo Xi's words, however, Guan Ye—who had also been smiling moments before—developed a rather subtle, complicated expression.

Lu Shulong immediately picked up on the shift in Guan Ye's demeanor:

—"What is it? Is something amiss?"-Lu Shulong asked in confusion.

—"No... it seems this matter didn't turn out the way you all hoped. It hasn't diminished the outside world's evaluation of Eldest Senior Brother at all,"-Guan Ye frowned, shaking his head.-"If anything, after this incident, his reputation has ascended to yet another level."

[—"Huh?!"](# "Speakers: Lu Shulong, Mo Xi, and Mo Bei")

Everyone else present froze in bewilderment. Even Mo Bei held her cup with both hands, her eyes widening in disbelief.

Mo Xi sputtered in utter incredulity:

[—"Why?! Do they have holes in their brains?!"](# "Speaker: Mo Xi")

* * *

The grand powerhouses of the other sects were naturally not "brain-damaged," as Mo Xi so eloquently put it.

Rather than saying their opinion of Yuwen Xuan had risen again, it was more accurate to say that it had transformed into profound sympathy for him—and bone-deep dread of the Untiring Immortal Venerable.

After all, these senior figures had no clue why Yuwen Xuan had been hung up in the first place. Much like the White Jade Capital, the affair of the Langhuan Forbidden Grounds could never be revealed to the public before it had been thoroughly restructured and brought under control.

Combined with the various rumors circulating within the Heavenly Spirit Sect, these outsiders inevitably jumped to their own conclusions:

[—"Why did the Immortal Venerable hang his eldest disciple from a tree?"](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"I heard it's a Heavenly Spirit Sect tradition. Anyone whose grades fail to meet standard gets hung up."](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"Unsatisfactory? Yuwen Xuan?! Didn't he just advance to the Void Refinement stage?! A Void Refinement powerhouse barely over two hundred years old! If that counts as unsatisfactory, my disciples should line up to bash their heads against a wall!"](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"...There's no need to drag your own disciples into the comparison, is there? Besides, word has it that while the Heavenly Spirit Sect's eldest disciple advances rapidly in raw cultivation, his academic coursework is atrocious. What's more, this breakthrough of his was far too sudden—some suspect he rushed it purely to steal the limelight from his junior brother."](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"As cultivators, isn't raw cultivation the single most vital metric?!"](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"Your thinking is biased. If one neglects foundational theory, they are bound to suffer bitter hardship down the road."](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"To say nothing of the fact that this is the Untiring Immortal Venerable's disciple! With the Immortal Venerable's temperament, how could he ever tolerate a disciple cutting corners or relying on opportunistic tricks?"](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"Still, isn't that standard far too punishingly strict?!"](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"Indeed..."](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

The cultivators broke into a collective chorus of sighs and laments.

According to prevailing stereotypes, a "diligent, hardworking cultivator" was inevitably a "stern, ruthlessly demanding master." None of them found it odd in the least that Zhou Hui would discipline his own disciple with an iron fist.

Some masters were even tempted to copy this homework:

[—"Still, setting aside the disciple's aptitudes for a moment, hanging them from a tree actually strikes me as quite a marvelous disciplinary method."](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"How on earth is that marvelous?!"](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"I find it infinitely superior to the Cliff of Reflection. When I threw my disciple into ours, he ended up bewitched by a sealed ancient fiend and suffered a cultivation deviation!"](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"That's just because your sect never swept and sanitized your Cliff of Reflection! Our water dungeon has never had that problem—aside from the fact that not a single disciple soaked in it ever repented, and every last one of them defected from the sect."](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"...To be frank, if it were me, I'd have defected too."](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"A punishment failing to induce genuine reflection is a catastrophic flaw. When I locked my boy in the solitary confinement chamber, he emerged completely unrepentant."](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"Unrepentant is already fortunate! Would you believe that when my disciple was banished, he actually started farming in the back mountain?! He even dragged a whole gang of his junior brothers out to plant crops with him!"](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

—a circle of masters commiserated in sorrowful lamentation.

Evidently, how to properly discipline an unruly, rebellious disciple was a perennial headache shared by masters across the world.

If the punishment was too light, it had zero effect; if it was too heavy, the disciple would simply pack up and flee the sect. And interspersed in between were sudden disasters like demonic temptations, bizarre fated encounters, and sudden power spikes, making effective discipline an excruciatingly delicate tightrope.

No wonder these grand powerhouses felt a sudden urge to "learn from the best."

Yet no matter how much they debated, when the conversation circled back to Yuwen Xuan, they could not help but sigh with lingering sympathy:

[—"Still, if my disciple possessed cultivation speed of that caliber, no matter what blunder he committed, I wouldn't have the heart to punish him."](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"Indeed! The Untiring Immortal Venerable's standards are simply too demanding."](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[—"Not everyone can be an all-around polymath like the Immortal Venerable himself. When a youth focuses wholeheartedly on arduous cultivation, it is only natural that certain areas will be neglected. There was no need to go this far—or at the very least, not right as the new disciples were entering the sect."](# "Speaker: Outside Sect Master")

[—"Precisely! Displayed like this, where is the Eldest Senior Brother supposed to put his face?!"](# "Speaker: Outside Elder")

[...It really isn't what any of you think,] Tao Zhu mused internally as he listened to the chatter.

As an "insider" who possessed firsthand knowledge of what had transpired, Boss Tao naturally held a rather different perspective from the crowd. Even so, he still found himself leaning toward sympathizing with Yuwen Xuan.

At this point, Boss Tao still had zero awareness that he had been brazenly played and used as a tool by Yuwen Xuan. He simply assumed that Untiring was displeased that his disciple had shared such an enormous secret with an outsider first.

[But your disciple only did it to give you a surprise!] Boss Tao thought, feeling a touch of indignant pity on the boy's behalf.

Right as he was lost in these thoughts, Qu Qianxing approached and delivered a message:

—"Boss Tao, Master requests your presence,"-Qu Qianxing said.

—"It has nothing to do with me!"-Boss Tao blurted out, instantly abandoning all thoughts of pleading for Yuwen Xuan as he [swiftly passed the buck](# "Literal CN: 甩锅 (shuǎiguō) — throwing off the pot / deflecting blame / passing the buck").-"That was entirely your Eldest Senior Brother acting willfully on his own! I'm just an innocent old senior who was duped and dragged along!"

He had heard the tales!

Not only did Untiring hang his eldest disciple from trees, but whenever he got truly furious, even the monster beasts who hung out with that eldest disciple were [punished through collective guilt](# "Cultural/legal term: 连坐 (liánzuò) — collective punishment / guilt by association") and hung up right alongside him!

For the past several days, Boss Tao had lived in perpetual dread that Untiring would haul him out and hang him from the tree as well.

Qu Qianxing blinked, utterly failing to grasp the source of Boss Tao's panic:

—"Master isn't looking to discuss Eldest Senior Brother with you,"-Qu Qianxing clarified.-"He wishes to consult you regarding a journey to the Southern Domain."

—"The Southern Domain?"-Tao Zhu echoed, his confusion only deepening.
