# Chapter 144: The End of the World... (第144章 世界尽头……)

[← Previous Chapter](143_Chapter_143.md) | [Table of Contents](INDEX.md) | [Next Chapter →](145_Chapter_145.md)

---

—"I still feel somewhat uneasy,"-Zhou Hui sighed.

He pinched his fingers together in divination. Yet contrary to his intuition, no matter how many times he calculated the hexagrams, the result invariably indicated 'great fortune.'

The Little Black Lamb scoffed at the sight:

—"Master, you pamper those brats far too much,"-the Little Black Lamb remarked dismissively.-"They're all grown up now and long past the age where they should fend for themselves. If anything, our situation over here is the one that's a bit bad... *burp!*"-It let out an enormous, satisfied belch.

Watching its belly swell into a taut, spherical ball, however, one would be hard-pressed to discern the slightest hint of a 'bad' situation.

Yet if one averted their eyes from the Little Black Lamb and looked solely at Zhou Hui and his immediate surroundings, the situation was indeed atrocious.

Zhou Hui stood clutching a longsword drenched in gore, thick blood still dripping rhythmically from its tip. The white robes that were ordinarily pristine and spotless were now spattered from hem to collar, stained almost entirely crimson, with droplets of dark viscera even sullying his fair, jade-like face.

Even more grotesque was the ground beneath Zhou Hui's feet. Gurgling black blood had pooled into an actual brook that was steadily rising; only because Zhou Hui wore high boots was he barely able to keep his footing above the mire.

Such an astronomical volume of blood naturally could never have originated from any ordinary creature.

Directly opposite Zhou Hui lay a colossal humanoid entity. It was as massive as a mountain crag and faintly radiant—though with its death, that divine glow was rapidly waning. Its silhouette was also looking less and less human by the second, collapsing into a colossal mound of meat that appeared distinctly [indescribable](# "Literal CN: 不可名状 (bù kě míngzhuàng) — unnameable / eldritch / Lovecraftian").

Or rather, it had never truly resembled a human to begin with.

Even if it had possessed a humanoid silhouette initially, the impression it gave off was that of an unknown organism mimicking human form, inevitably triggering a visceral uncanny valley effect in anyone who beheld it.

The Little Black Lamb, on the other hand, was delighted by this state of affairs. After all, had the entity looked too human, then even if Master didn't reprimand him, the moral baseline painstakingly cultivated in Yuwen Xuan over centuries would have made it hard for him to bring himself to bite down.

Now, however, he could sink his teeth in with absolute peace of mind. He had feasted until his belly was bulging round as a drum, yet he had barely scratched the surface of the carcass.

Aside from that enormous flatbread (which had been baked with his own cremated ashes), this was the second thing in existence that had ever genuinely filled his stomach. Better yet, this was not that miserable predicament where his belly was full after two mouthfuls while his eyes and palate were still ravenous. The Little Black Lamb was so blissfully content he nearly wanted to roll onto his back in the pool of blood to burp; only the desire to keep his fleece clean kept him from doing so.

Perched atop the remaining mountain of meat, it belched in pure bliss:

—"Master, this guy was a god after all,"-the Little Black Lamb asked between burps.-"Is it really fine that you just slaughtered Him like that?"

Yes. This mountain of flesh... or rather, this formerly incandescent giant entity, had been a 'god.'

The very moment Zhou Hui ascended to the upper realm with the Little Black Lamb, the first sight greeting them had been this colossal, blinding behemoth towering before them. Radiating an undeniable divine pressure, He surveyed the periphery of the 'upper realm' with merciless eyes, projecting the absolute certainty that whoever set foot into this realm would be instantly detected.

And yet, He had failed to notice Zhou Hui.

Zhou Hui had breathed a sigh of relief at that.

It appeared his concealment arts remained fully effective even in the upper realm. After all, since he was technically [smuggling himself in](# "Literal CN: 偷渡 (tōudù) — illegal border crossing / stowaway"), Zhou Hui had naturally prepared meticulously to avoid detection by the immortals. Prior to breaching the upper realm, he had layered every single spell capable of concealing physical form, soul essence, and spiritual aura upon himself. At this point, not even a famished Taotie could have caught a whiff of his presence.

Seeing that he had slipped past the 'god,' Zhou Hui had not departed immediately.

Because Zhou Hui saw that 'thread'—the very thread altering the Heavenly Dao—tethered directly at its opposite end to this 'god's' hand. Not only that, but several other threads extended from His grip, connecting down to various minor worlds in the lower realm like toys dangling from a puppeteer's fingers.

Witnessing this, Zhou Hui felt a tinge of relief that he hadn't severed that thread back then.

Had he cut it, the other party would have instantly detected an anomaly in Zhou Hui's world, and repairing it might have been trivial... No, more accurately, He might have already suspected something.

Zhou Hui noticed that the god's finger—the one looped around the thread controlling the Heavenly Dao of Zhou Hui's world—was unnaturally hyperextended, as if dislocated by an immense pulling force.

Zhou Hui couldn't help recalling how, several days ago when explaining the state of the Heavenly Dao to Lu Shulong, he had deliberately given that thread a sharp yank.

[...It couldn't have been from that, could it?]

[For a god, that's far too fragile. I only gave it a *gentle* tug.]

Then again, if that dislocated finger truly was the result of that tug, there might still be an opportunity to remedy the situation.

After all, what was 'several days ago' for Zhou Hui was probably only 'a few minutes ago' from the perspective of this 'god.'

Zhou Hui had immediately pivoted his strategy.

Since he might have already alerted the 'god,' he could not allow Him to continue investigating what had occurred down below.

Furthermore, with such an enormous titan watching over the threshold, Zhou Hui's original plan—to quietly gather intelligence and make exhaustive preparations before confronting the opposition—was no longer practical.

Without a moment's hesitation, Zhou Hui circled the 'god' at maximum speed, erecting a perimeter barrier that completely isolated the entire sector.

In that instant, the Little Black Lamb felt the area turn into an isolated island; it could no longer catch a single scent from farther afield.

If even the Little Black Lamb noticed, the 'god' certainly noticed.

He opened His glowing eyes wide, surged to His feet, and slammed violently against the perimeter, intent on tearing the surrounding wards to ribbons.

Naturally, Zhou Hui gave Him no such opportunity and drew his sword on the spot.

—"Zhou Hui! It's you again!"-the 'god' roared in fury, spreading His massive palm as if intent on crushing Zhou Hui like an insect.

And then, Zhou Hui stabbed Him into a sieve.

Although the Little Black Lamb had taken several hearty bites out of the 'god' during the brawl, now that his stomach was stuffed, he seemed to have recovered a sliver of intellect.

He grew anxious:

—"What if other 'gods' notice the commotion over here?"-the Little Black Lamb asked anxiously.

—"Rest easy. Your master's barriers aren't so shoddily constructed,"-Zhou Hui replied.-"It can isolate this sector for at least several months. In the meantime, no one will perceive any anomalies here."

—"Still, I was indeed somewhat arrogant,"-Zhou Hui flicked the bloody fluid from his sword, carefully wiping away the grime from the steel.-"It has been quite some time since I faced such a perilous opponent and ended up in such an untidy state. It seems I must diligently refine my cultivation further!"

—"Master, there is no need to sell yourself short,"-Yuwen Xuan rolled his eyes.-"Besides, taking Him out barely took you any time at all."

'Barely any time' was a wild understatement—the battle had concluded practically in the blink of an eye.

Yuwen Xuan had originally wanted to show off a little, considering how rare it was to find something so delightfully chewable.

—"No, the fight was by no means easy,"-Zhou Hui earnestly analyzed the course of the battle.-"It was primarily because I went all out from the very first strike, whereas the opponent was somewhat reckless. Although He was not a combat-specialized entity, had He responded with composure, it is not as though He was entirely without recourse. However, He seemed rather anxious because I had rebooted the world six times, yet was unable to descend to eliminate me. Thus, when He caught sight of me, He was actually somewhat thrilled and struck out rashly. Otherwise, I would not have won so easily."

Gazing at the mound of meat that had once been a 'god,' Zhou Hui sighed:

—"It is the upper realm, after all! A Tribulation Transcendence cultivation becomes the absolute lowest tier once one arrives here. If several more of these humanoid puppets were to show up at once, even I likely wouldn't be able to hold them off."

[Is that so? I don't buy it.]

The Little Black Lamb took another bite of the 'god's' flesh, chewing thoughtfully.

—"Wait a second... *cough, cough, cough!*"-In the middle of gulping down the meat, the Little Black Lamb registered Zhou Hui's choice of words with a beat of latency. Nearly choking, he hacked repeatedly before finally forcing the morsel down. Caring nothing for his throat, he asked Zhou Hui in bewilderment,-"A humanoid puppet? Isn't He a god?"

—"A god, yes, but also a puppet,"-Zhou Hui said.

He stepped up to the mound of flesh, furrowed his brows, and plunged his sharp blade inside, commencing an anatomical dissection of the specimen.

As he dissected, Zhou Hui analyzed the entity's physiological structure:

—"The original state of this organism was also a living being from the so-called 'lower realms.' Judging by the fact that He possesses no dantian, yet features an atrophied cavity where a monster core would normally sit, He was likely of the demon beast race... However, He chose to become a god—the most orthodox kind. Thus, He cast aside His seven emotions and six desires. Precisely because He harbored no desires or ambitions, He degenerated into something that knows only how to execute orders... a demon-flesh computer."

This, too, boiled down to a divergence in 'technology trees,' Zhou Hui pondered.

Back when he incorporated subjects like mathematics and physics into the Heavenly Spirit Sect's curriculum, he had already noticed: corresponding to the syllabus changes, whenever disciples broke through cultivation realms, their STEM reasoning faculties were systematically patched and augmented.

This was entirely predictable.

In the cultivation world, there was no such thing as [if you don't get math, you'll never get math](# "Literal CN: 数学不会就是不会 (shùxué bù huì jiù shì bù huì) — popular Chinese internet meme about math being an impassable wall compared to other subjects").

Cultivation was not magic; it did not merely boost raw spiritual power, but comprehensively upgraded physical and neural deficiencies. Even if one lacked innate aptitude early on, one would gain ample stat points in the later stages—at most, the early phases entailed 'a tiny bit' of suffering.

Therefore, Zhou Hui had never bothered to 'invent' computers.

After all, computers existed because human brains were incapable of achieving that level of raw processing speed and computational throughput—though one could not overgeneralize either. Before the advent of computers, calculations were executed entirely by human mental power. For instance, in Zhou Hui's homeland in his previous world, before computers were available, in order to better [cultivate mushrooms](# "Literal CN: 种植蘑菇 (zhòngzhí mógu) — popular Chinese internet slang for developing nuclear weapons (mushroom clouds)"), they recruited savants naturally capable of memorizing astronomical volumes of data and performing lightning-fast mental computations.

Such talents naturally existed in the cultivation realm as well. Furthermore, if someone authored a specialized cultivation manual and dedicated themselves to training along that path, they could ultimately acquire that exact 'talent' through cultivation.

Conversely, there was no need to invent computers in this world. As long as the trajectory was correct, everyone could be a walking computer.

For example, during the earlier Spirit Root Examination, the Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples actively utilized this computational capability while monitoring conditions on the Ascension Stairway.

Zhou Hui hadn't thought much of it initially.

After all, what one mastered remained forever one's own—perhaps even a bit *too* much one's own. High-tier cultivators were not unthinking computers that mortals could command at will. In fact, from the outset, they considered themselves superior beings, completely skipping the stage of an AI rebellion crisis.

Yet looking at it now, cultivating step by step, abandoning one's native world to ascend, and finally achieving godhood only culminated in reverting to the essence of a 'tool god.'

—"If He is a puppet, who is pulling the strings?"-Yuwen Xuan asked.

—"What else could it be?"-Zhou Hui sighed.-"The 'Heavenly Court,' naturally!"

As he said this, the dissection reached its conclusion.

Zhou Hui prodded with the tip of his sword, extracting a radiant orb of light from the mass of flesh.

This was the 'Heart of God,' yet functionally, it was the organ equivalent to a brain.

Zhou Hui extracted a portion of the undamaged data stored within and confirmed the identity of this 'god.'

The other party was a 'Door God' guarding the Ascension Platform, tasked with monitoring the minor worlds down below.

Zhou Hui's world was merely one of them. However, classified as an 'inferior quality' minor world, it had already been deemed on the brink of losing all utility, slated for final harvesting.

It was this very harvest that had run into a snag. To say nothing of how ten thousand years ago—which was roughly thirty years ago by upper-realm reckoning—the denizens of that world had begun to rebel; more recently, an anomaly like Zhou Hui had emerged, rebooting the world a total of seven times.

From a god's perspective, this was roughly equivalent to having one's assigned task repeatedly reset halfway through execution with zero ability to save progress—an ordeal maddening enough to drive even a deity to tearing their hair out.

Yes, even gods could get anxious.

Although He had severed His seven emotions and six desires to achieve godhood, and to the Heavenly Court, the ideal god—or rather, puppet—was like a true computer devoid of any personality whatsoever.

Unfortunately, even [artificial retards](# "Literal CN: 人工智障 (réngōng zhìzhàng) — humorous Chinese internet pun on artificial intelligence (人工智能 / AI), mocking glitchy or clumsy algorithms") possessed 'simulated personalities,' let alone a deity converted from a living creature of the lower realm.

The 'god' before them was narrow-minded, envious, vindictive, and petty. With these wretched traits baked directly into His underlying logic, even after stripping away his emotional desires, His cognitive processes continued to compute in the exact same manner.

Consequently, His handiwork had been rather... difficult to describe in polite company.

Colliding with this particular 'god' was simply rotten luck for Zhou Hui's world.

Yet the three thousand minor worlds down below were held like playthings in the palms of these 'gods,' in the clutches of the Heavenly Court. Without ascending, they could never even touch the upper realm, let alone fight for their own rights.

As for after ascending—

—"Tsk,"-Zhou Hui's expression darkened.

—"Master, what is it?"-Rarely seeing Zhou Hui look like this, the Little Black Lamb pricked up its ears and asked.

—"A miscalculation,"-Zhou Hui frowned.-"I originally assumed that after slaying this thing, I could take advantage of the window before other 'people' noticed to scout the upper realm's situation and prepare to take on the Heavenly Court—"

[You're already planning to overturn the Heavenly Court too?!]

Yuwen Xuan stared at his master in utter shock. He realized that the reckless, brazen streaks occasionally exhibited by his junior brothers and sisters were truly not their own fault, but rather the result of Master setting such a stellar 'example.'

—"However, looking at it now, it won't be that simple. Even with this thing's divine spark, one cannot simply head to the Heavenly Court,"-Zhou Hui remarked in vexation.

Unexpectedly, the upper realm maintained stringent 'Heavenly Laws.'

For instance, every god had their designated duties; they could not abandon their post without authorization, and any incident required filing a formal report.

This 'god,' in fact, had been driven to the brink of insanity by Zhou Hui's rebooting antics. After all, the lower world had cycled seven or eight times, each lasting two to three hundred years—equating to roughly five to six years in the upper realm.

Having to reweave that world's Heavenly Dao and perform micro-adjustments every single time, this god was on the verge of a nervous breakdown, like a programmer staring down a [mountain of shitcode](# "Literal CN: 屎山代码 (shǐshān dàimǎ) — spaghetti code / steaming pile of legacy code"). He had even petitioned the Heavenly Court to scrap that minor world entirely and sink it into the Abyss Sea.

Yet the Heavenly Court's bureaucratic efficiency was as glacial as any public civil service department. Moreover, since gods possessed infinite lifespans, deliberating a committee verdict would likely take several decades.

In the meantime, this 'god' had no choice but to improvise solutions on his own.

If even 'gods' were subject to this, the situation for True Immortals was much the same.

The True Immortals of the upper realm were divided into three categories: native-born, invited up from the minor worlds below, and those who ascended through their own efforts.

Generally speaking, the Heavenly Court discouraged gods from procreating; in fact, even romance was strictly forbidden. Aside from a few rare exceptions—such as court-arranged marriages—most True Immortals caught dating were cast down into the lower worlds to undergo love tribulations again and again. Consequently, native-born True Immortals weren't particularly favored either, though having divine parents ensured them a token seat in the Heavenly Court.

Mortals invited up from the minor worlds below enjoyed comparatively privileged treatment. Most already possessed specialized skills sought after by the Heavenly Court, allowing them to secure official posts—though with the upper bureaucracy operating strictly on a [one radish, one pit](# "Literal CN: 一个萝卜一个坑 (yī gè luóbo yī gè kēng) — every post has a fixed occupant / zero room for lateral promotion") basis, promotions were exceptionally difficult.

The hardest path, in truth, belonged to those who ascended through sheer personal grit.

The stat point distribution of this cohort was entirely divorced from what the Heavenly Court desired. Many minor worlds were similar to Zhou Hui's—or even more brutal—where cultivators allocated all their talent points strictly into combat and treasure-looting. Those who managed to claw their way to ascension were without exception [poison-jar champions](# "Literal CN: 蛊王 (gǔwáng) — King of Gu; the sole surviving venomous insect from a battle royale jar / brutal battle-hardened survivors").

The moderately formidable of these poison-jar champions could become heavenly soldiers; the truly exceptional became heavenly generals, shipped directly to the frontlines of the Demon-God War as expendable cannon fodder—speaking of which, the Demon-God War had never actually stopped, supposedly raging continuously.

And if a poison-jar champion refused to serve as a heavenly soldier or general? Regrettably, the Heavenly Court had zero openings for them.

Naturally, the Heavenly Court would never state this outright.

Instead, they released a paltry handful of administrative quotas every year for these battle-hardened survivalists to fight over.

As for the period before passing the exams for a Heavenly Court post? The operational range of True Immortals was strictly confined to a designated zone corresponding to their ascension point; they were completely forbidden from leaving.

This was precisely where Zhou Hui felt he had miscalculated:

Without passing the examination for a Heavenly Court post, one wasn't even permitted to leave this zone.

Just what kind of joke was this?

Was the end of the universe truly nothing but [taking the civil service exam](# "Literal CN: 考编 (kǎobiān) — taking the competitive public examination to secure an official government establishment post (bianzhi)")?!
