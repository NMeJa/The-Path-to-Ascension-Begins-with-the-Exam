# Chapter 79: A Fleeting Pinnacle of Life (第79章 转瞬即逝的人生巅峰)

Rou Niang worried that failing to produce "results" would cause her to be abandoned by Carefree Palace. Little did she know, the moment Carefree Palace made the arrangements to "contract" and "lie dormant," she had already been abandoned.

In truth, sleeper moles like Rou Niang had never been important from the very beginning.

At the end of the day, they were merely mortals without spirit roots who happened to possess pretty faces. Carefree Palace took them in solely to serve as decorative embellishments on gifts used to curry favor—yes, they couldn't even qualify as the main body of the gift itself—they were purely used to pad headcount numbers. After all, even low-tier attractive female cultivators weren't that easy to come by, whereas beautiful mortal maidens were vastly easier to procure. They were essentially equivalent to floral arrangements at banquets: disposable after a single use.

Furthermore, Carefree Palace never considered the elementary schools established by the Heavenly Spirit Sect to be particularly important in their own right.

In their eyes, the Untiring Immortal Venerable was finally moving to assert control over the Central Domain, and these mortal schools were merely a pretext for the Heavenly Spirit Sect to drive in political "wedges" and outpost nails. In fact, it wasn't just Carefree Palace that thought so; every other sect shared the exact same assumption, which was why so many immortal sects had rushed over during the New Year to "apologize" and pay homage.

Thus, discarding these hidden pawns planted in the schools was of zero consequence; the stewards and directors of Carefree Palace hardly even remembered they existed.

That was why, had Rou Niang not taken the initiative to ask, the higher-ups wouldn't have even bothered notifying her about the tactical retreat along the front lines.

What Carefree Palace truly prioritized were the Carefree Pavilions scattered throughout the lands.

Those Carefree Pavilions were Carefree Palace's commercial network outlets.

Wherever an immortal market existed, a Carefree Pavilion was guaranteed to stand. If it were a larger immortal city or a chaotic venue like a black market, then on the surface they maintained not only a Carefree Pavilion, but also shadow alter-egos like the Fallen Immortal Pavilion.

Backed by Carefree Palace with stable supply chains, these Carefree Pavilions could sell medicinal pills with guaranteed potency, offer after-sales maintenance for magical artifacts, and custom-order bespoke items from certified master artisans, thereby forging a premier brand of their own.

Consequently, whether it was disciples from prestigious sects venturing out for their first trials or wandering rogue cultivators roaming across the realms, whenever they wished to purchase vital pills or artifacts, their first choice was always Carefree Pavilion. Naturally, if they had harvested significant treasures that they wished to sell or auction, their foremost destination was likewise Carefree Pavilion.

Even in the Heavenly Dao's original script, whenever the "protagonist" embarked on a secret realm trial, he would make a beeline for Carefree Pavilion on a lavish shopping spree; when he unearthed secret treasures in realms that he couldn't personally use, he would entrust Carefree Pavilion to liquidate them, thus cultivating solid connections with numerous Carefree Pavilion stewards—and the grand treasure auction midway through the story was the absolute climax of the entire "novel."

Carefree Palace also utilized these commercial nodes to covertly harvest intelligence, procure "raw materials," command trade flow, manipulate market prices, and rake in astronomical profits.

Such indispensable strongholds were something Carefree Palace would naturally never abandon.

Even now, after their "Ancestral Deity" had suffered devastating trauma at the Heavenly Spirit Sect (Zhou Hui: ?), prompting Carefree Palace to temporarily avoid antagonizing the Untiring Immortal Venerable and adopt a strategic contraction, they still refused to surrender these outlets—let alone relinquish the colossal market behind them.

If anything, Carefree Palace tightened its grip over the Central Domain market with even greater vigilance!

They strictly audited the quality of pills and artifacts across their stores, rigorously vetted the origins of auction lots and acquired goods, halted all purchases of dubious items or abducted women, and upgraded customer security services to new heights—terrified of handing the Untiring Immortal Venerable a fresh pretext to smash all their storefronts to smithereens a second time.

Thus, the previous blockade of trade routes was buried without a word; goods were restocked onto the display shelves in abundance, for fear that cultivators might find empty shelves.

The cultivators of the Central Domain were completely bewildered by the sudden shift. Naturally, they were thrilled by Carefree Pavilion's elevated service and embarked on an impassioned shopping frenzy, dreading that supplies might dry up later—which inadvertently piled immense competitive pressure onto ordinary merchants.

Of course, none of this impacted the operations of the Immortal Fair.

After all, Zhou Hui's Immortal Fair had never permitted Carefree Pavilion to set up shop, and the commodities traded there differed fundamentally from ordinary immortal markets. No matter how Carefree Pavilion pivoted its commercial strategy, it couldn't graze a single hair on the Heavenly Spirit Sect.

Yet Mo Xi, the youngest junior brother of the Heavenly Spirit Sect, was profoundly displeased by this.

Very, very displeased.

—"We have to find a way to dig up those Carefree Pavilions by their roots,"-Mo Xi muttered, scowling.-"They're getting in the way of our Immortal Chess sales."

Yes: Immortal Chess (Trial Version) was slated to roll out across the entire Central Domain.

This whole affair traced back to the major sects coming to "pay New Year calls."

During the New Year celebrations, while the top-tier titans of the Central Domain cultivation realm vied before the Untiring Immortal Venerable for the prestige of "Central Domain Representative Cultivator," Mo Xi hadn't been idle either. He had hosted the middle-tier pillars of the Central Domain sects and smoothly pitched the distribution and promotional campaign for Immortal Chess.

After all, no matter how wildly popular Immortal Chess had been previously, that frenzy was confined strictly within the Heavenly Spirit Sect and at the Immortal Fair. Multitudes of cultivators who had never visited Mount Tianling had zero clue what it even was!

—"Since we are going to determine the 'Central Domain Representative Cultivators,' we must at least ensure that over seventy percent of the cultivators across the Central Domain know what Immortal Chess is first! Consider it an early publicity blitz to hype up the upcoming Exhibition Tournament,"-Mo Xi had argued with irresistible business logic.

Mo Xi had effortlessly persuaded those high-ranking guests from major sects and immortal cities, securing shelf space for Immortal Chess across every prominent immortal market and city.

Yet sales figures had fallen catastrophically short of Mo Xi's expectations.

To be precise, they were atrocious.

Mo Xi had overlooked one crucial prerequisite: within the Heavenly Spirit Sect and the Immortal Fair, a pervasive "chess-playing atmosphere" already existed. Bystanders watching others play could easily project themselves into the match and become thoroughly addicted.

Transported to retail storefronts in foreign territories without any gaming atmosphere, naturally nobody paid the slightest attention to this unfamiliar product.

In fact, even if cultivators noticed it, they still wouldn't buy it.

After all, under the primitive commercial conventions of the cultivation world—where even currency spirit stones had to possess tangible "practical utility"—what possible reason was there to spend money on a mere toy like Immortal Chess, which couldn't instantly boost one's cultivation realm?

Not to mention, the age-old tradition of the cultivation world was that the better an item was, the more deeply it was kept hidden behind the counter. The standard shopping procedure for customers entering a shop was to ask the proprietor, "Do you carry XXX here?", upon which the shopkeeper would fetch it from the back. Such a retail model was fundamentally incapable of expanding an audience beyond existing demand.

In comparison, Carefree Pavilion suddenly throwing a manic fit—or what seemed like throwing a fit in Mo Xi's eyes—by elevating service standards to consolidate their market share was actually the least significant contributing factor.

Xi'er didn't care. Xi'er was unhappy. Xi'er simply wanted to demolish every single Carefree Pavilion on the face of the earth.

And so, Mo Xi sought out his "Eldest Senior Brother":

—"Eldest Senior Brother, what do you think we should do?"-Mo Xi asked.

—"What... does that... have to do... with me?"-"Yuwen Xuan" replied slowly, his speech stuttering and lagging with unnatural pauses, like a transmission paper crane whose signal was suffering heavy interference.

Mo Xi found it odd, yet tugged at "Eldest Senior Brother's" hem while putting on his most adorable, pleading expression:

—"In our entire sect, neither senior brother nor senior sister is as clever as you. For a matter like this, who else could I turn to besides you?!"-Mo Xi pleaded.

He wasn't purely flattering him, either.

In the eyes of Mo Xi, the youngest junior brother: his sister Mo Bei was a reckless simpleton; Fifth Senior Brother Lu Shulong was gentle and naive; Fourth Senior Brother Guan Ye was stubborn and inflexible; Eldest Senior Sister Ran Deng was too lazy to use her brain; and Second Senior Brother Qu Qianxing was the literal embodiment of rules and regulations. Only Eldest Senior Brother Yuwen Xuan was the premier partner in crime to scheme "monumental undertakings" with—and if things went south, he could even help take the blame!

And while Yuwen Xuan routinely teased his youngest junior brother, whenever causing trouble was on the table, his interest surged above all else, and he never once refused.

In the past, schemes like hiring disciples to forge punishment copywork lines had always been co-conspired between Yuwen Xuan and Mo Xi.

Yet for some time now—Mo Xi reckoned it began right around when the grand auction concluded—Eldest Senior Brother had suddenly turned just as indolent as Eldest Senior Sister. He no longer stirred up trouble, nor did he wander around idling. Instead, he sat basking in the sun all day long, occasionally cradling that wandering, sick (X) pancake-toting lamb, looking for all the world like a retired old pensioner.

Had Master not explicitly guaranteed that there was nothing wrong with Eldest Senior Brother, Mo Xi would have genuinely suspected he had been [body-snatched](<# "Literal CN: 夺舍 (duoshe) — the Daoist/Xianxia concept of soul possession/taking over another's physical body">).

Right now, "Yuwen Xuan" was also cradling the little lamb, murmuring in a sluggish, drowsy voice:

—"Why must your thoughts always dwell... on such violent matters?"-"Yuwen Xuan" said slowly.-"Isn't a peaceful life... wonderful as it is?"

—"A peaceful life?!"-Mo Xi's cat-like eyes widened in sheer disbelief.-[—"Isn't your motto in life 'Give me scheming or give me death'?!"](<# "Literal CN: 不搞事毋宁死 (if I can't cause trouble, I'd rather die) — parody of Patrick Henry's famous quote 'Give me liberty or give me death'">)

—"When did I... ever have such a motto?"-"Yuwen Xuan" replied with a slight frown.

Yet even while questioning him, his countenance remained draped in [Buddhist-style calm](<# "Literal CN: 佛系 (Buddhist/zen-like) — Chinese internet buzzword for a detached, unbothered, indifferent mindset that refuses to compete or stress">), as though nothing in the mortal realm could shake his placid soul, his face even gracing an ethereal, serene smile:

—"Besides,"-he murmured,-"I feel that I have already reached... the [pinnacle of life](<# "Literal CN: 人生巅峰 (pinnacle of life) — Chinese internet meme for reaching the absolute peak of career/personal success">)."

Indeed! How blissful life was right now!

Eat his fill, then sleep; wake up, then eat again. Whenever he felt like it, he could slip into Master's immortal cave without getting scolded anymore, let alone being kicked out. When Master was in a good mood, he not only permitted him to snooze on his lap, but would even gently comb his fleece!

Yuwen Xuan felt that continuing this life for eternity would be completely fine. He no longer felt that gnawing emptiness in his stomach, nor that restless itch to stir up trouble just to distract himself.

Mo Xi obviously could not fathom what on earth Eldest Senior Brother's "pinnacle of life" entailed.

He merely thought he ought to seek out Master once more to inspect whether Eldest Senior Brother's brain had been fried by too much sun.

While he was at it, why not ask Master for guidance regarding the sales of "Immortal Chess"?

Originally, he hadn't wanted to trouble Master before achieving concrete results. Furthermore, going to consult Master meant he would at least have to draft a comprehensive proposal first to demonstrate how much homework he had put in...

Contemplating this, Mo Xi sighed aloud:

—"Since you have no ideas, Eldest Senior Brother, I have no choice but to go see Master,"-Mo Xi sighed.

—"Go on, then,"-"Yuwen Xuan" replied. Then, just as Mo Xi stood up, he added a beat too late:—"Wait a moment."

—"What is it?"-Mo Xi asked, still looking back at "Yuwen Xuan" with a lingering glint of anticipation.

Yet shattering his expectations, "Yuwen Xuan" shoved the little lamb right into Mo Xi's arms:

—"Hold it properly,"-"Yuwen Xuan" instructed.-"Carry it over to Master."

—"Why on earth?!"-Mo Xi protested instantly. He had zero desire to ferry such an exasperating pet over to Master!

Recalling the sight of this thing sprawling across Master's lap and licking sesame pancakes, Mo Xi even felt the savage urge to hurl it violently onto the flagstones.

Yet when Mo Xi's gaze locked with "Yuwen Xuan's" hollow, ethereal stare, an involuntary shiver ran down his spine. He had an ominous premonition that if he were to drop this sheep, catastrophic misfortune would surely befall him.

Resigned, Mo Xi had no choice but to begrudgingly cradle the little black lamb and trudge toward Master's immortal cave.

***

Zhou Hui happened to be "free" as well.

With the New Year concluded and the Heavenly Spirit Sect commencing its spring term, his hyper-competent second disciple managed the entire sect inside and out—including the elementary schools—in immaculate order. The Immortal Fair and the secret realms were overseen by Mo Xi, and there was still a comfortable buffer before the promised "Immortal Representative Exhibition Tournament" for the major sects—those sects were notoriously sluggish at holding their internal qualifiers. Consequently, Zhou Hui's schedule had entered an extraordinarily rare window of leisure.

Of course, for a [former involution champion](<# "Literal CN: (前)卷王 — someone who previously pushed hyper-competitive overwork to an extreme">), there were endless tasks he could undertake.

For instance, deciphering the true utility of the White Jade Capital jade seal, researching long-distance teleportation arrays, developing brand-new secret realms, and so forth—casually picking any of them could easily keep him occupied for years on end. Yet Zhou Hui decided to "relax" for once and admire some cute little animals.

He retrieved the storage pouch containing the Taotie Remnant Soul.

Yes: the other half of the Taotie Remnant Soul that had slumbered inside the colossal skull opposite the Blood Demon Hall, which in every past reincarnation cycle mutually devoured the soul inside Yuwen Xuan's body, originating from the very same ancient Taotie.

Back then, after Zhou Hui gave it a sound thrashing, he had casually stuffed it into a storage pouch and never let it out since. Now that he finally "had some spare time," he could thoroughly study (X) admire it.

Truth be told, Zhou Hui was genuinely intrigued by the primordial soul of an ancient ferocious beast.

After all, following the Great War of Gods and Demons, the pathways leading to higher planes like the Immortal-Demon Realm and the True Immortal Realm had been sealed shut. The spiritual energy of this mortal world was too depleted to nourish even a True Dragon, let alone ferocious beasts capable of casually devouring an entire world.

Yet these beasts possessed lineages far more ancient than True Immortals, entities that touched the very origins of the cosmos. Furthermore, it directly concerned the background and heritage of his eldest disciple, so it naturally warranted meticulous examination.

Pondering this, Zhou Hui unsealed the restriction on the storage pouch.

The storage pouch showed no reaction whatsoever, as if the Taotie Remnant Soul within didn't exist at all.

Zhou Hui extended his divine sense inside to inspect it, only to discover that the interior of the pouch had transformed into a gaping, bloody maw. Tier upon tier of jagged fangs snapped violently shut!

—"My, this bit of petty cleverness is truly identical to Xuan'er,"-Zhou Hui noted with a faint smile.

His divine sense neither fled nor evaded; instead, the very instant it was bitten, it morphed into a razor-sharp fishhook that hooked firmly into that bloody maw, and then began reeling the remnant soul out of the storage pouch bit by bit.

True to being the soul of a Taotie, it exhibited the quintessential disposition of prioritizing food over its very life.

It clamped down ruthlessly on Zhou Hui's strand of divine sense. Even after being dragged entirely out of the storage pouch, it refused to release its bite, enduring the sharp fishhook just to swallow the whole thing down.

To the lingering consciousness of the Taotie, this was unmistakably an incomparable delicacy—profound, heavy, and bursting with multi-layered flavor.

Zhou Hui neither knew nor cared what his divine sense tasted like. Watching the remnant soul being hauled out, he acted with lightning speed, slipping the magical artifact he had crafted during this period directly over the soul.

Instantly, that remnant soul began to take corporeal shape.

Its hue turned pristine white and its four limbs touched the floor, transforming into the likeness of a little white lamb. The small bell around its neck chimed crisply, appearing every bit as obedient and docile as the little black lamb Yuwen Xuan transformed into. Yet if one examined its eyes closely, they would discover pure, swirling chaos within; gazing into them for too long evoked a terrifying dread of being swallowed whole. If anyone were foolish enough to treat it as an ordinary lamb, they would undoubtedly be devoured until not a trace of bone marrow remained.

Of course, in Zhou Hui's presence, there was virtually no difference whatsoever.

He patted the "little white lamb's" head.

The "little white lamb" was nothing like Yuwen Xuan, who relished Zhou Hui's petting. It lowered its front hooves, locked its hind legs straight, perked up its fluffy little rear, and thrust forward its budding, ungrown horns in a full combat posture, emitting defiant, threatening [little bleats](<# "Literal CN: 嘤嘤 (ying-ying) — onomatopoeia for high-pitched, pathetic whining or cute crying/bleating">):

—"Ying-ying!"-the little white lamb bleated defiantly.

If only it were willing to let go of Zhou Hui's strand of divine sense while doing so.

—"Such a glutton. Whatever shall I do with you?"-Zhou Hui sighed softly.

He felt more and more that this remnant soul and Yuwen Xuan were one and the same entity. He recalled that during his eldest disciple's youth, a bit of tasty food could effortlessly "hook" the boy; either way, they were just like [snapping turtles clamping onto chopsticks](<# "Literal CN: 王八咬住了筷子 — folk saying for someone biting down with stubborn tenacity and refusing to let go under any circumstances">), refusing to unclamp their jaws no matter what.

Fortunately, as a multi-spirit-root cultivator, the sole silver lining for cultivators of Zhou Hui's caliber was that in order to replenish five times the spiritual energy, his divine sense had undergone relentless, grueling tempering. It was typically vastly deeper and more robust than that of single-root or dual-root practitioners—several times greater than peers of the same realm. Upon reaching the Tribulation Transcendence realm, it became outright monstrous: his sea of consciousness could genuinely be described as a boundless ocean, and an ocean dense with viscous, high-concentration spiritual power at that. Thus, even if Zhou Hui completely let loose his divine sense for the Taotie to feast on, it would probably take hundreds of years before it could ever finish eating.

Zhou Hui simply allowed this tiny Taotie to keep chewing on his divine sense. Meanwhile, seizing the opening, he linked his divine sense directly into the Taotie's soul, rifling through its ethereal depths to trace its origins.

As a remnant soul that had never undergone reincarnation, with any luck, it might retain memories from before and after its demise.

And so, cradling the "little white lamb," he began to meditate.

The "little white lamb" initially struggled for a moment, but seeing that Food (X) Zhou Hui didn't hinder its meal in the slightest, it simply nestled complacently in Zhou Hui's embrace and continued gnawing and tearing at that strand of divine sense.

Zhou Hui didn't just let it consume freely, however. He hardened his divine sense until it was extraordinarily resilient—to a degree where even a Taotie could not easily sever it. Consequently, after chewing for ages, the "little white lamb" was merely licking and savoring the flavor; yet because it was so delectable, it refused to unclench its jaws no matter what.

Zhou Hui, on the other hand, unearthed several fascinating revelations from within the Taotie's remnant soul.

For instance: the ancestral heritage of the Taotie, as well as its innate comprehension of worldly origins.

—"It seems I can modify this slightly for Xuan'er to practice, and as for Shulong's side..."-Zhou Hui murmured pensively.

How to properly educate Yuwen Xuan and Lu Shulong had always been a headache for Zhou Hui. After all, one harbored the soul of a Taotie, while the other possessed the physical body of a True Dragon. Although making do with Zhou Hui's self-created cultivation methods wasn't impossible for them to practice, it ran counter to their primal instincts; yet letting them run completely wild was equally irresponsible. Right now, this inheritance derived from the Taotie provided Zhou Hui with immense pedagogical inspiration.

He could also "incidentally" refine and optimize his own cultivation techniques, rendering them more versatile and efficient.

That was only the first layer of harvest.

Another unexpected harvest concerned the true nature of the original Taotie.

In general folklore, a Taotie was perceived as a mindless beast that knew only how to devour without a shred of reason. Left unchecked, they could devour an entire world hollow and even consume themselves. Yet this legend possessed an inherent logical flaw: if it were truly such an insatiably voracious monster, it and any given world could only exist mutually exclusively—making it an abomination far more horrifying than demonic fiends.

Yet examining the Taotie's divine soul revealed that they actually possessed moments of restraint. An ordinary Taotie understood the wisdom of [not draining the pond to catch all the fish](<# "Literal CN: 竭泽而渔 (draining the pond to fish) — idiom for exhausting resources with zero regard for sustainability">). When retaining their reason, they would never devour everything clean; instead, like Yuwen Xuan, they prioritized preserving the abundance and variety of their reserve larders. A world that a Taotie would completely devour had to be one utterly and irreversibly corrupted by demonic qi.

Such worlds were completely beyond salvation, and the Taotie would act as cosmic scavengers to consume them.

—Yet in this very process, the Taotie would inevitably be tainted by the demonic qi as well, gradually descending into madness and losing all self-control until it devoured its own flesh.

Consequently, had "someone" (?) exploited this very trait of the Taotie, forcing it to gorge on demonic qi, then transporting it to other worlds to slaughter it, thereby using it as a disposable filter to cleanse their own world... ?!!

Reading up to this point, Zhou Hui's eyes went wide.

His thoughts instantly flashed to the primordial source of demonic qi buried deep within the Central Domain.

All along, everyone had assumed that demonic miasma was the aftermath of the War of Gods and Demons, marvelling at just how many millions of demons must have perished to pollute the land to such an atrocious degree! Now it appeared the source was far more convoluted than anyone had imagined—it was entirely possible that someone (?) had used this world as an interdimensional toxic dump!

Speaking of which, just when had that Taotie died down there?

And which world was the one that required cleansing?

Zhou Hui thought with simmering indignation.

No matter how desperately he wished to ascend, this realm remained his homeland. He had zero desire to see it desecrated or treated like refuse by anyone.

Unfortunately, the Taotie's memories could provide him with no answers. This Taotie had been far too young; while alive and still ignorant of the cosmos, it had gorged on excessive demonic qi. After death, it had been cleaved into fragments—one part stuffed into a mortal human infant's body, while the other remained in deep slumber—leaving it utterly incapable of retaining cohesive memories.

—"Continuing like this bodes ill for both you and Xuan'er. I must devise a method to make the two of you whole again,"-Zhou Hui said, stroking the "little white lamb's" head.

The "little white lamb" was entirely oblivious to Zhou Hui's somber reflection, still happily chewing on Zhou Hui's strand of divine sense.

Presently, Zhou Hui's released divine sense enveloping his immortal cave detected someone striding over toward his quarters:

—"Little Black, oh Little Black, don't you think your master has been acting awfully strange?"-Mo Xi murmured, carrying the black lamb. Somehow or other, he had actually struck up a conversation with this sheep he despised.—"Eldest Senior Brother is an absolute tyrant who stirs up waves even when there's zero wind. But I have to admit, causing trouble is his only real redeeming quality. The current Eldest Senior Brother basking in his 'pinnacle of life' is utterly useless! He doesn't even cook for Master anymore; sooner or later, Fifth Senior Brother will completely replace him."

[What do you know, brat?! Right now, I don't even have to cook for Master—Master actively feeds me instead!]

[If this isn't the pinnacle of life, what is?!]

The little black lamb emitted indignant [little bleats](<# "Literal CN: 嘤嘤 (ying-ying) — onomatopoeia for high-pitched, pathetic whining or cute crying/bleating">) in defense:

—"Ying-ying!"-the little black lamb bleated indignantly.

Yet Mo Xi couldn't comprehend the little black lamb's cries, assuming it was agreeing with him:

—"As expected, you think so too, don't you? Looking at you closely, you're actually quite adorable,"-Mo Xi chuckled.

—"Ying,"-the little black lamb bleated, rolling its eyes.-*(I've always been adorable, you fool.)*

The little black lamb was just plotting whether it should deliver a swift hoof to this silly junior brother's shin once they reached Master's cave, when suddenly, a tantalizing aroma captured its attention.

The little black lamb twitched its nose, snapping its gaze toward the depths of Zhou Hui's immortal cave.

[Oh crap!]

Sensing Yuwen Xuan in his divine awareness, Zhou Hui scooped up the little white lamb in his embrace and frantically began shoving it back into the storage pouch.

After all, these two halves of the Taotie Remnant Soul existed in a state where [two supreme predators could never meet](<# "Literal CN: 王不见王 (kings do not meet kings) — Chinese chess (Xiangqi) rule where the two opposing generals cannot face each other directly across an open file; by extension, two supreme apex predators cannot cross paths">). If they crossed paths, they might very well devour each other on sight—and once fully merged, who knows if they might even trigger immediate ascension right on the spot! The situation would spiral into an absolute catastrophe.

Yet the "little white lamb" refused to comply.

It had unmistakably caught the scent of its "other half" as well. While clamping its jaws around Zhou Hui's divine sense and refusing to let go, its eyes were glued toward the cave entrance, drool practically dripping from its chin.

—"No! That is strictly not for eating!"-Zhou Hui warned, scowling.-"Or do you want me to retract my divine sense?!"

Alternating between stern threats and brute physical force, it took Zhou Hui quite an ordeal before he finally managed to stuff that lamb back into his storage pouch.

Just as he finished, he saw a black, bullet-like creature bolt headfirst through the door, galloping frantically around the room—leaping atop the low tea table one moment, burrowing beneath cabinets the next, evidently scouring every inch for something.

Mo Xi only came sprinting in moments later, panting for breath:

—"Why did you run off like that?!"-Mo Xi cried.-"Master, I'm so sorry! This sheep... The instant this sheep reached the entrance to your cave, it went completely berserk and charged inside! I couldn't hold it back at all!"

—"It's quite alright,"-Zhou Hui comforted Mo Xi, then beckoned toward the little black lamb:—"Come here."

The little black lamb was currently sniffing frantically around a potted spiritual herb, seemingly trying to verify something.

Zhou Hui didn't know whether to laugh or cry, feeling that this fellow had grown so large entirely in vain—he was fundamentally no different from that unenlightened other half of his soul.

He said:

—"Stop searching,"-Zhou Hui said, shaking his head.-"There is nothing to eat here!"

[Hmph, Master, you're lying to me! If there really wasn't 'food,' how would you know I was looking for food?!]

The little black lamb certainly did not lack IQ at times like this. It even performed an extended logical leap:

[Wait a moment... Something 'delicious' that Master insists 'isn't food'... In these kinds of situations, it's usually...!]

Yuwen Xuan instantly abandoned the flowerpot, scampered clumsily before Zhou Hui, and stretched out his tiny hooves to scramble onto Zhou Hui's knees.

Zhou Hui assumed it had finally surrendered its search. Under Mo Xi's none-too-friendly glare, he picked up the little lamb and nestled it in his lap.

Yet the little black lamb did not immediately sprawl across Zhou Hui's lap as usual. Instead, it nuzzled vigorously against Zhou Hui's robes, sensing that the "delicious" aroma had grown noticeably richer—yet upon closer sniffing, it seemed to consist entirely of its own scent, with nothing else discernible.

...Not being able to smell it was no surprise—nine times out of ten, Master had erased the scent of that "food"!

This feeling was absolutely unmistakable!

Based on past experience, Master had definitely taken in new [illicit lovers (junior disciples)](<# "Literal CN: 外遇 (extramarital affair) — Yuwen Xuan's comedic jealousy over Master taking in new younger disciples">)!

Yuwen Xuan thought in bitter, aggrieved sorrow.

Tears began rolling down from his eyes in fat, heavy drops: *pat, pat, pat*.

Mo Xi was thoroughly spooked by the little black lamb's silent, streaming tears. He asked in alarm:

—"Wh-What on earth happened to it?"-Mo Xi asked.

—"Pay no heed to him,"-Zhou Hui replied ruthlessly.-"Xi'er, did you come here for something?"

Mo Xi immediately cast aside all thoughts of the silly little lamb:

—"Ah, it's regarding Immortal Chess—"-Mo Xi blurted.
