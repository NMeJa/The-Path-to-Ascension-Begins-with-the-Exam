# Chapter 9: The Immortal Venerable's Scapegoat Disciples (第9章 仙尊家的冤种徒弟们)

[—"This is marvelous!"](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

On the other side of the room, Guan Ye was clutching the touch-screen catalog terminal, refusing to let go.

He had clearly developed a profound fascination with the book search catalog within the reference, tapping its screen incessantly. Catching sight of Zhou Hui walking over, he asked:

—"Master, was this truly created by mortals? Without relying on spells whatsoever?"-Guan Ye asked.

Yet before Zhou Hui could answer, Guan Ye began answering his own question:

—"Then again, while this device operates, I genuinely cannot detect the slightest trace of spiritual energy. It should indeed be operable by ordinary mortals. What is more, if spells were incorporated, certain functions could be made even better..."-Guan Ye muttered.

[—"Yet how did they achieve this?"](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

That last question was murmured entirely to himself.

Once Guan Ye sank into research mode, he shed his usual silence and lapsed into a muttering, chatterbox trance—which, if anything, made him look... even gloomier.

Zhou Hui did not interrupt him, merely watching his fourth disciple with an amused smile.

It took quite a while before Guan Ye finally snapped back to reality. His face instantly flushed crimson:

—"Master..."-Guan Ye stammered.

Only then did Zhou Hui explain:

—"This device is unquestionably an ordinary mortal artifact,"-Zhou Hui explained.-"Yet do not underestimate it. The realm that built it was a world of mortals. Because there was no disparity in cultivation, their public infrastructure and products were engineered to satisfy the demands of millions, tens of millions, or even hundreds of millions of people."

This was the exact antithesis of the cultivation world.

Despite the kaleidoscopic variety and godlike might of magical treasures across the cultivation realm, the vast majority were 'bespoke products' forged to satisfy a single hyper-specific need. Once the master no longer required the artifact, others might not even be able to wield it. In such cases, everything depended on 'karmic affinity'—waiting for whichever 'lucky fellow' happened to require that exact 'bespoke product's' functionality before it could ever be utilized.

To draw an analogy, it was much like the mobile games from Zhou Hui's previous world.

The realm Zhou Hui had transmigrated from was a demographic powerhouse. Their mobile games underwent [stress-testing](# "Literal CN: 压力测试 (yālì cèshì)") by tens or hundreds of thousands of users during the [open beta](# "Literal CN: 公测 (gōngcè) — open beta testing") phase alone, making overseas expansion comparatively effortless compared to the domestic market. Conversely, foreign AAA studios capable of crafting sublime single-player console masterpieces would watch their servers implode into total ruin if they launched a mobile title in that country without partnering with a domestic publisher.

Without undergoing rigorous stress-testing, a solitary creator could never even begin to imagine where the bugs would emerge.

Consequently, after experiencing public products that had been [open beta tested](# "Literal CN: 公测 (gōngcè) — open beta testing") by hundreds of millions of people, returning to the cultivation world left Zhou Hui increasingly uncomfortable with its bespoke clunkiness.

In the past, Zhou Hui would have simply endured inconveniences unrelated to cultivation. But now that he had firmly resolved to retire, he saw no harm in introducing a few overdue reforms.

Among them, the library—err, Library Pavilion—that he cared about most was merely the opening gambit.

Looking at Guan Ye, Zhou Hui said:

—"I do not demand that you forge an identical machine,"-Zhou Hui told Guan Ye.-"Without mastering the sciences of that world, you could not manufacture it anyway. However, you may reference its functions and make appropriate adaptations."

Having said that, Zhou Hui glanced over at Ran Deng, who was still lovingly caressing book spines along the shelves:

—"Nor am I demanding that you instantly construct a brand-new Library Pavilion,"-Zhou Hui added.-"For now, you need only renovate the sections housing cultivation manuals for Qi Condensation and Foundation Establishment. As for the detailed blueprints, I shall unveil them to you gradually."

—"Understood, Master,"-Ran Deng and Guan Ye replied respectfully, hastily reining in their earlier frivolity before their Master's concrete directives.

Besides, having witnessed the 'reference model,' they harbored zero objections and were even looking forward to the renovation works commencing as soon as possible.

Yet after receiving his orders, Guan Ye hesitated.

He hesitantly asked Zhou Hui:

—"Master, I took a look at the reference examples you placed in the System just now,"-Guan Ye asked hesitantly.-"And I did not seem to see... the Sundial Clock?"

—"I have not had the time to upload that yet. After all, I have not finished compiling the teaching materials, and the formal renovation of the Palace of Silence..."-Zhou Hui paused mid-sentence, his eyes narrowing slightly.-"Did Xi'er approach you?"

Zhou Hui had never intended to erect a clock tower within the Library Pavilion in the first place. That was a hardware fixture intended for the lecture hall—err, the Palace of Silence.

Guan Ye instantly looked rather awkward.

Without waiting for Guan Ye's response, Zhou Hui opened the quest system to check, immediately feeling a migraine brewing:

—"Why on earth did that boy accept so many quests?"-Zhou Hui remarked with a growing headache.-"While I assigned him to oversee the examination, fulfilling the completion criteria merely required preparing the venue and notifying all disciples."

[...Oh no, Master didn't even know?!]

[Then again, when that brat persuaded Second Senior Brother to scalp the exam papers, I felt something was fishy. To think he pushed all the blame onto Master, claiming it was all assigned quests!]

Guan Ye muttered inwardly.

Then again, Guan Ye was not at all surprised by Mo Xi's little tricks.

As for his youngest junior brother, to put his character flaw charitably, he was starved for affection and desperate for validation; to put it bluntly, he was [obsessed with grandstanding and chasing glory](# "Literal CN: 好大喜功 (hàodà xǐgōng) — ambitious for great achievements and fond of lavish display").

Though Mo Xi whined from morning till night that Second Senior Brother Qu Qianxing was slavishly obedient to Zhou Hui, the moment you gave him a chance to show off before Master, Mo Xi would gladly pluck the moon from the firmament.

Accepting a few extra quests was child's play to him.

Though Guan Ye roasted him endlessly in his heart, he kept it to himself. Instead, he stammered to cover for him:

—"Youngest Junior Brother is probably still unfamiliar with navigating the System,"-Guan Ye stammered, attempting to cover for him.-"Besides, the Sundial Clock was something I was rather curious about as well..."

—"Before stepping in here to see me, you had never even laid eyes on the System. How could you possibly be curious about a Sundial Clock listed in its quest log?"-Zhou Hui asked dryly.

Guan Ye was rendered completely speechless.

Observing the shifting emotions on his disciple's face, Zhou Hui let out a quiet sigh.

With Guan Ye's temperament, it was no wonder he had met the [most tragically unjust](# "Literal CN: 冤 (yuān) — wronged, unfair, suffering as an unfortunate scapegoat") and gruesome demise.

In the Heavenly Dao's script, because Guan Ye's cultivation was comparatively modest, Qu Qianxing had sent him away before the Heavenly Spirit Sect was exterminated, allowing him to escape disaster. Yet instead of concealing his identity to live out his days in peace, Guan Ye clung to his burning hatred for the protagonist. He apprenticed himself to an eccentric monster of an [evil cult](# "Original CN self-censored as '□□' for platform filters — evil sect / cult (邪派)") renowned for mechanism arts, willingly allowing the fiend to refine him into a living puppet. Obsessed with hunting the protagonist right up until the latter's ascension, Guan Ye ultimately suffered the fate of having his soul scattered and obliterated, while the mechanism manual he had painstakingly authored was reduced to a mere trophy in the protagonist's loot bag.

For a disciple such as him, Zhou Hui was undeniably most concerned.

Had it been anyone else seeking to rewrite Guan Ye's destiny, they would surely have forbidden Guan Ye from touching mechanism arts or artifact refinement ever again. But Zhou Hui took the opposite view.

Zhou Hui reasoned that in his past lives, he had never seriously tutored Guan Ye in artifact forging, yet in the Heavenly Dao's script, Guan Ye had still managed to unearth some evil cult old demon out of thin air. It proved that trying to dodge fate by avoidance was futile. Rather than running away, it was far better to instruct Guan Ye in the most rigorous, orthodox artifact-crafting techniques starting now, broadening his horizons so that his achievements would shatter the bounds of the Heavenly Dao's predetermined script.

That way, even if the Heavenly Dao still intended to use Guan Ye as a stepping stone, it would have to see whether its chosen Child of Destiny choked to death trying to swallow him whole!

—"I shall upload the blueprints for the Sundial Clock into the System shortly, along with the mechanical principles of clockwork. Take them and study them,"-Zhou Hui instructed Guan Ye.-"If you truly have time to spare, giving your youngest junior brother a hand is harmless enough."

—"Yes, Master,"-Guan Ye said, exhaling in relief.

As if suddenly remembering, Ran Deng chimed in:

—"In that case, Master, should we allocate some renovation funds to Youngest Junior Brother?"-Ran Deng asked.-"He seems rather strapped for cash."

Evidently, Guan Ye was not the only 'foreign aid' Mo Xi had sought out.

Zhou Hui smiled faintly:

—"Since he possessed the boldness to accept so many quests, he can naturally finish them on his own,"-Zhou Hui said.-"If there are things he cannot comprehend, giving him pointers is fine, but do not provide him with any tangible material assistance. Let us see the full measure of his talent!"

—"Understood,"-Ran Deng replied.

She and Guan Ye exchanged a look, silently observing a moment of mourning for their youngest junior brother in their hearts—another [unfortunate sucker](# "Literal CN: 冤种 (yuānzhǒng) — internet slang for unfortunate suckers taken advantage of") who had voluntarily walked into Master's trap.

*

[—"Ah, Master! We finally found you!"](# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan")

Just as master and disciples were preparing to discuss the Library Pavilion's renovation project in further detail, a boisterous shout came echoing through the air.

Zhou Hui arched a brow and looked up. Ripples like water stirred across the 'library.' In the next instant, a door materialized upon the glass ceiling that reflected the azure sky, and two strapping young men accompanied by a young girl leapt down from behind it.

The arrivals were none other than Zhou Hui's remaining three disciples:

Eldest Disciple Yuwen Xuan, Fifth Disciple Lu Shulong, and Sixth Disciple Mo Bei.

—"What brings you three here?"-Zhou Hui asked, puzzled. He did not recall summoning these disciples to his presence.

—"Someone came looking for you,"-Yuwen Xuan announced.

He rummaged through his robes and finally fished out a crumpled invitation card from his sleeve:

—"The Heavenly Dao Alliance is planning to pay you a visit,"-Yuwen Xuan said.

—"The Heavenly Dao Alliance?"-Zhou Hui hesitated, pinching the mangled card between two fingers with undisguised distaste.-"What business do they have with me?"

—"What else could it be? Nothing more than poking around to see how Master's heavenly tribulation went!"-Yuwen Xuan declared.

Though bluntly phrased, it was the unvarnished truth.

The so-called Heavenly Dao Alliance had very little to do with the 'Heavenly Dao' itself (probably). Rather, it was a coalition formed by several of the cultivation world's paramount sects, tasked with mediating inter-sect disputes, rallying cultivators to repel extraterrestrial demon incursions, and harmonizing relations between humans and non-humans—the grand affairs of state. In essence, it was the United Nations of the cultivation world, or in the popular fiction of Zhou Hui's previous world, the quintessential massive 'righteous faction'—ostensibly noble on the surface, riddled with shady underhanded dealings, and perpetually at odds with the protagonist.

Zhou Hui's relationship with them, however, was not particularly warm either.

After all, the Untiring Immortal Venerable did not hail from any prestigious orthodox lineage, nor did he possess any sectarian pedigree. He held zero entanglements with the Heavenly Dao Alliance. Yet precisely because of this, having an untethered Tribulation Transcendence titan who was 'bound by no karmic ties' perched atop the zenith of the cultivation world made outsiders deeply apprehensive. Even if Zhou Hui harbored no ambitions of global domination, accidentally crushing someone's prized flowers and medicinal herbs while transcending a tribulation was headache enough.

Therefore, following a heavenly tribulation of such apocalyptic proportions, the Heavenly Dao Alliance wanting to pay a visit and inspect the premises was an entirely predictable turn of events.

The only detail that faintly puzzled Zhou Hui was that he had never encountered this in his previous lives.

Yet upon inspecting the formal card in his hand, Zhou Hui realized this must have been a 'fixed event.' In his previous incarnations, he had immediately retreated into secluded cultivation, meaning the invitation was never handed to him directly; Qu Qianxing must have hosted them instead.

—Come to think of it, during those past cycles when Zhou Hui retreated into seclusion, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had been forged by Qu Qianxing into a dominant regional powerhouse. In the Heavenly Dao's script, the sect had even become an ally to a regional branch of the Heavenly Dao Alliance, expanding steadily until it was eventually 'privileged' to recruit an entity like the Child of Destiny himself. One had to wonder whether that had been a direct consequence of this very visit.

In any case, when the Heavenly Spirit Sect was wiped off the map, the Heavenly Dao Alliance that should have come to their rescue never made an appearance.

Zhou Hui's brows furrowed slightly at the realization.

Yuwen Xuan, completely unable to [read the room](# "Literal CN: 看眼色 (kàn yǎnsè) — read expressions; understand the atmosphere"), chimed in eagerly:

—"Master, since distinguished guests are on their way, regarding the sect-wide Grand Unified Exam..."-Yuwen Xuan trailed off hopefully.

Hearing this, Zhou Hui set down the visiting card, wearing a faint, inscrutable smile:

—"They are merely a few idle busybodies,"-Zhou Hui said.-"Is there any need to suspend our sect's vital business on their account?"

—"Haaah..."-Yuwen Xuan let out a groan of despair, muttering under his breath in a 'whisper':-"Since when is taking an exam considered vital business..."

—"Still, we must grant the Heavenly Dao Alliance some face,"-Zhou Hui ignored him entirely, turning his gaze toward his fifth and sixth disciples.-"Shulong, Bei'er, your other senior brothers and sisters are all occupied. I entrust the hospitality preparations to the two of you!"

—"Yes, Master,"-the two disciples responded in unison.

Upon hearing this, Yuwen Xuan nearly jumped out of his skin:

—"Master! That's not fair!"-Yuwen Xuan pleaded.-"A banquet is such a fun—er, vital affair, it should be entrusted to me! Besides, you assigned tasks to all my junior brothers and sisters—did you forget your very first disciple?! Master, you cannot [abandon the old for the new](# "Literal CN: 喜新厌旧 (xǐxīn yànjiù) — idiom meaning to love the new and loathe the old; casting aside old companions for novel ones")!"

Saying so, he shamelessly grinned and sidled up to Zhou Hui's side.

—"Have you finished copying your exam papers?"-Zhou Hui asked, frowning at him.

Yuwen Xuan's [punitive copying](# "Literal CN: penal copying (罚抄) — assigning lines or copying mistakes as academic punishment") was the most astronomical workload among all the disciples, given that his test score had approached zero asymptotically.

Yet Yuwen Xuan replied without batting an eye:

—"Copied them all!"-Yuwen Xuan declared proudly.

—"Heheh,"-Zhou Hui chuckled, reaching out to straighten Yuwen Xuan's collar.-"Your speed is faster than I imagined!"

Yuwen Xuan puffed his chest out with swagger:

—"Naturally!"-Yuwen Xuan boasted.-"Whatever task Master assigns, I will definitely complete faster than anyone else!"

—"Quite true. Having hired so many outer sect disciples, you could hardly fail to be swift,"-Zhou Hui murmured, his hands sliding gently upward to Yuwen Xuan's throat.

Feeling fingertips as warm and smooth as polished jade resting against his cervical vertebrae, Yuwen Xuan finally realized that doom was upon him:

—"Master, hear me out! It was Mo Xi who—*ghk!*"-Yuwen Xuan choked.

In the next instant, Yuwen Xuan heard a crisp *CRACK*.

It was the sound of his neck snapping.
