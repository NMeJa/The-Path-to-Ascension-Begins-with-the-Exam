# Chapter 164: The Heavenly Scripture

—"Sure enough, this is harder than I imagined,"-Zhou Hui said, his brow slightly furrowed.

While Yuwen Xuan was attempting to crack the gates of the Langhuan Forbidden Grounds to alter the Heavenly Scripture, his venerable master was actually doing something remarkably similar.

As a [practice drill](# "Literal CN: 练手 (liànshǒu) — practicing hands / warmup drill; testing one's skills on preliminary exercises before the real test"), Zhou Hui decided to infiltrate and modify the Heavenly Court's Heavenly Dao.

Hm? Significantly more difficult than modifying the Heavenly Dao of his own world? Wasn't that only natural?

When practicing and previewing coursework, tackling harder questions and exposing oneself to diverse problem types ensured that when encountering tricky complications during practical exams, one wouldn't panic.

At least, that was how Zhou Hui viewed it.

However, the Upper Realm was the Upper Realm after all. Right at the very outset, Zhou Hui ran into a problem:

—"[The bandwidth is far too low](# "Literal CN: 流量太低了 (liúliàng tài dī le) — 'the traffic/bandwidth is too low'; modern data-network metaphor applied to spiritual transmission channels"),"-Zhou Hui noted with a sigh.

This was a metaphor, and also an objective fact.

After mastering the method of writing the Heavenly Scripture, Zhou Hui attempted to use the "strings" by which the Heavenly Court manipulated this world to hack back in reverse—such as through Siwu, or through Goddess Ren Ran. The primary conduit, however, was meant to be the "Heavenly Robe" that Qu Qianxing had recently refined.

The original name of that "Heavenly Robe" was unrecorded, but he was a genuine, bona fide True Immortal. Because he possessed the ability to seize others' bodies, he had slipped past spatial boundaries to do whatever he pleased within the cultivation world—though he was likewise bound by the rules of this realm, and in the end, Qu Qianxing had refined him into a robe anyway.

Through it, Zhou Hui had obtained a certain level of administrative access.

Yet it was merely access permissions.

Originally, Zhou Hui had assumed that because this world had erected its own boundary during the Demon-God War, preventing the Upper Realm from directly intervening or transmitting its power "downward," the higher realm had resorted to unlucky saps like Siwu for remote control, allowing only unilateral transmissions from their end.

Yet surprisingly, upon gaining the permissions of the "Heavenly Robe," Zhou Hui discovered that the conduit leading "upward" was also exceptionally narrow.

Even an "internal employee" like the "Heavenly Robe" could not call upon greater power at will.

Not that the Heavenly Court was mistreating its staff—judging from the Heavenly Robe's authorization levels, there was plenty he could do; it was just that the pipe capable of channeling power was far too small.

The current reality was that not only was the cultivation realm keeping a tight lid against the Upper Realm for fear that the heavens would descend to interfere once more, but the Heavenly Court also seemed wary of the cultivation realm, erecting a firewall as if deathly afraid that something down below might crawl up.

In fact, once Zhou Hui decided to "smuggle across," what blocked him was precisely this defensive perimeter.

—"Is this to defend against demonic beasts invading from the cultivation world?"-Zhou Hui murmured softly.

Among the intelligence Qu Qianxing had extracted through the "Heavenly Robe," there was word that the Heavenly Court had already written off this world, planning to repurpose it as a buffer zone for the Demon Realm. Yet Zhou Hui felt this measure didn't look like an anti-demonic defense.

To resist demons, shouldn't they have granted their lower-realm employees far greater power, letting this world become a full-blown battlefield?

Rather than installing double-sided deadbolts and sealing everything off completely.

—"It couldn't possibly be to lock me out,"-Zhou Hui remarked with an airy chuckle.

Regardless of what the Heavenly Court intended, that was not the immediate priority. Setting that aside, if the power conduit was so narrow, Zhou Hui would have to seek out other pathways.

—"Ascending directly to the Upper Realm would actually be fastest, but modifying the Heavenly Dao that way would cause too much commotion and waste time... In theory, I recall there should be a place in this world capable of accomplishing this,"-Zhou Hui mused, jotting down possible locations on a sheet of paper. Suddenly, his brush paused.-"Ah, right. How could I have forgotten... the Heavenly Scripture!"

Deep in the Abyssal Ocean, a certain sheep sneezed.

He glanced around nervously, completely unable to fathom where that spine-chilling premonition had come from.

However, the "Heavenly Scripture" Zhou Hui spoke of had nothing whatsoever to do with the messy business Yuwen Xuan was plotting.

—Zhou Hui had no idea what his eldest disciple was up to. After all, Yuwen Xuan was a master at devouring his own aura to conceal his tracks. While Zhou Hui could certainly locate him if he insisted on searching, he wasn't paranoid enough to place his own disciples under perpetual surveillance.

The "Heavenly Scripture" Zhou Hui referred to was the one that had surfaced roughly a thousand years ago, in the aftermath of the Demon-God War.

Legend had it that an ancient sage had been granted permission to ascend to immortality; the Upper Realm lowered a Heavenly Scripture as a staircase to receive him, and that Heavenly Scripture was preserved in this realm by mortal cultivators as a sacred treasure. Through it, one could inspect this world's Heavenly Dao and foretell the future.

In the past, Daoist couple Mei and Zhu had used it to divine their child's fate, which had ultimately precipitated Su Xin's catastrophic expedition to the Central Domain.

Most of the time, that "Heavenly Scripture" was utilized exclusively by the Palace of Carefree Wandering. The "Heavenly Robe" clearly knew what it was, frequently using the guise of the Heavenly Scripture's prophecies to command the Palace of Carefree Wandering to act.

Looked at from the reverse perspective, since that Heavenly Scripture could display information transmitted from the Upper Realm, it was very likely the largest loophole available.

Zhou Hui decided to reverse-exploit that "Heavenly Scripture."

With a flicker of his form, he arrived in the Northern Domain, standing before an eighteen-story pagoda.

Nothing about the pagoda itself was particularly extraordinary; what was peculiar was its foundation. It did not rest upon ordinary stone masonry or an earthen platform, but upon paper—wafer-thin sheets of paper.

Yes—several folded sheets of paper unfurled into the air, forming paper steps that rustled and whistled in the howling wind, looking as though they might tear into shreds and blow away at any second. Yet the towering pagoda weighed down upon these sheets like a colossal paperweight, pinning them utterly motionless.

This was the so-called "Heavenly Scripture Pagoda."

Although Zhou Hui knew of this place, he had never actually stepped inside.

He had always held little fondness for notions like "destiny." He possessed zero interest in squandering spiritual energy to inspect some so-called Heavenly Scripture to foresee the future. Now, if they allowed him to dismantle the Heavenly Scripture for research, Zhou Hui might have found it mildly intriguing; but the cultivators of the mortal realm would obviously never permit Zhou Hui to do such a thing, nor did Zhou Hui have any justification to go that far.

Now, however, he seemed to have one.

Zhou Hui walked toward the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda and stepped straight into its interior.

The cultivators stationed around the pagoda showed not the slightest reaction.

They weren't letting Zhou Hui through on purpose, of course; they simply hadn't perceived anyone entering at all. Even when Zhou Hui pushed open the grand gates of the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda in broad daylight, only one cultivator whose cultivation had reached the Mahayana stage looked up with a blank, bewildered expression.

—"I thought I heard the sound of the main doors just now,"-he said, looking right through the wide-open gates of the pagoda as though seeing nothing at all.

The other Mahayana cultivator was no different—if anything, even more oblivious:

—"You probably misheard,"-the other scoffed.-"The next opening day is still a long ways off!"

—"True enough,"-the first cultivator nodded, turning his eyes away.

It was hardly surprising their vigilance was so lax.

Aside from the brief era shortly after the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda was first completed, once cultivators discovered that the prophecies displayed by the scripture were excessively random and carried significant risks of self-harm, virtually no one was willing to squander their spiritual energy on such a gamble anymore—save for the Palace of Carefree Wandering.

Furthermore, there were neither spirit stones nor medicinal pills within the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda; the greatest magical treasure was the pagoda itself. Not even petty scoundrels would bother approaching.

The cultivators rotating on guard duty at the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda were dispatched by the Heavenly Dao Alliance—essentially an idle, precautionary assignment—so security was by no means tight.

In truth, even if they had focused their senses to the absolute utmost, detecting Zhou Hui was an impossibility. And taking ten thousand steps back, for an entity at Zhou Hui's level, whatever intentions he harbored, it was frankly far better for the guards not to notice him in the first place.

And so, Zhou Hui entered the main gates of the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda as casually as strolling into his own immortal abode.

The internal structure of the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda was equally bizarre.

From the outside, this eighteen-story pagoda stood perched atop paper steps. By all conventional logic, one ought to climb upward; yet upon actually stepping inside, one discovered that the towering structure possessed only a single floor. Soaring walls loomed on all four sides, painted with colorful murals depicting the origins of the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda, radiating an intense aura of oppression.

Yet right in the center surrounded by these high walls lay a pool of water. Walking to the edge of the pool and tossing in a substantial sum of spirit stones would reveal an inverted reflection beneath the water's surface: a mirrored staircase spiraling downward.

Following this descending staircase all the way to the bottom led to a single great hall.

The walls of this hall were covered in colorful murals similar to those on the floor above, but without any discernible theme—a chaotic clash of pigments that appeared eerie and disordered. In the very middle of the hall, a massive [viewing speculum](# "Literal CN: 窥镜 (kuījìng) — viewing mirror / speculum / viewing device") was angled downward toward the floor.

And the floor here was made neither of wood nor stone pavers, but of a vast expanse of paper—the wafer-thin, brittle kind. If an ordinary mortal were to step upon it, every single footstep would punch a hole straight through. Colossal characters covered the paper, yet like the murals on the walls, they were bizarre and unintelligible. One had to walk to the viewing mirror in the center—yes, walk; flying was strictly forbidden within the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda, meaning one had to tread lightly enough across the delicate paper without damaging it—and observe the ground through the mirror to decipher the writing below clearly.

In such an eerie place, without formidable cultivation, anyone entering would see nothing at all; on the contrary, they would be swallowed whole by the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda.

Naturally, such sinister peculiarities were completely irrelevant to Zhou Hui.

With a single teleportation, he was already standing inside this uncanny hall.

—"Huh? There's writing?"-Zhou Hui said, looking at the paper floor with a puzzled expression.

Under ordinary circumstances, the "floor" naturally had writing.

Cultivators only entered to read "prophecies," so they naturally picked the specific times when text manifested upon the Heavenly Scripture to step inside.

However, this also indicated that for the vast majority of the time, the scripture within the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda was a [wordless Heavenly Scripture](# "Literal CN: 无字天书 (wúzì tiānshū) — wordless heavenly scripture / blank heavenly book; legendary scriptures that reveal text only at designated times or under specific conditions").

Not only did text appear on this Heavenly Scripture strictly at scheduled intervals, but one also had to toss in an immense quantity of spirit stones or channel vast amounts of spiritual energy at the entrance pool to unlock the staircase, and then descend step by step with heavy ceremonial formality.

Yet Zhou Hui had arrived intending to use the Heavenly Scripture as an exploit channel to counter-hack the Upper Realm. He couldn't care less whether there was writing on the Heavenly Scripture or not, so he obeyed none of the customary rules, disregarded every restriction, and simply teleported straight down.

Even so, writing had manifested upon the Heavenly Scripture.

—"Is this normal?"-Zhou Hui murmured, uncertain.

He had never entered before, having only read some scattered records concerning the Heavenly Scripture Pagoda.

Yet even Daoist couple Mei and Zhu had strictly obeyed the protocols when they came here. Moreover, given how eerie this Heavenly Scripture Pagoda was, the guardians couldn't possibly watch inside around the clock; they only entered for a brief patrol during shift rotations. No one knew what state an empty Heavenly Scripture Pagoda remained in during ordinary hours, so naturally there were no relevant records.

Thus, Zhou Hui couldn't be certain whether the scene before him was some kind of strange anomaly.

Still, looking at these characters, Zhou Hui couldn't help but feel a flicker of curiosity.

Although he hadn't come to read the Heavenly Scripture, he nevertheless walked up to the viewing mirror, picked it up, and peered inside.

Through the viewing mirror, the twisted glyphs on the ground squirmed like tadpoles. Yet after a dizzying wriggle, they finally assembled into words that even an ordinary mortal could read. It was just that the content was rather absurd:

> "Help!"
>
> "Help!"
>
> "Help!"

Beneath the three lines written in blood, crooked strokes scribbled in a child's frantic scrawl read:

> "The sheep and the toad are trying to eat me!"

Zhou Hui stared at this, unable to help letting out a completely dumbfounded:

[—"Huh?"](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")
