# Chapter 256: Three Hundred Years Later (Part 7)

Having sat through the entire suite of promotional advertisements, the relatively long teleportation transit finally concluded, and Long Tianming's entourage at last arrived at their destination.

Long Tianming immediately shoved aside his bewilderment over what *Immortal Chess* even was. Finding his footing, he nervously surveyed his surroundings, eager to witness what the supposedly "brutal" Immortal-Demon frontlines were truly like.

His very first impression, however, was "familiarity."

It was far too familiar.

A spacious and bright concourse, a spotless and orderly environment, bustling and boisterous crowds, and punctual public address broadcasts echoing overhead. If the Heavenly Gate's transit hub had already given Long Tianming an inexplicable sense of déjà vu, then this place felt completely like a high-speed railway station—specifically, a high-speed rail station during peak holiday travel rush.

Meanwhile, the "platform" where Long Tianming stood was rather peculiar.

Exquisite and opulent, for some reason it was also the smallest and most deserted. Perched at an elevated height, it seemed deliberately engineered to provide an overarching bird's-eye view of the entire transit hub.

The other platforms, by contrast, were as vast as public plazas. As white light flared in orderly succession, crowds packed shoulder-to-shoulder appeared and filed out in single file.

No, it wasn't merely a crowd of "humans."

At least in Long Tianming's understanding, a five-meter-tall creature sprouting wooden branches could hardly be called human; a humanoid orb of light straining not to blind onlookers could scarcely qualify as human; and a rolling fluidic structure was even less of a human. Beyond those, there was a host of bizarre, half-human, half-beast beings, alongside classic spirit folks sporting animal ears and trailing tails.

Yet no matter how outlandish these creatures looked, they all obediently followed the designated pedestrian walkways off the platforms, only displaying their unique supernatural prowess—taking flight or tunneling through the earth—once they stepped out the main exit.

In comparison, humans were far more understated. Aside from a few who flipped onto flying swords the instant they stepped outside to ride the wind in style, most simply continued on foot. Out on the exterior plaza, however, a steady stream of diverse cloud carriages could be seen ferrying passengers back and forth, alongside carriages drawn by celestial cranes and similar beasts. All were built to virtually identical specifications—clearly the result of strict regulatory limits, given the colossal sign at the entrance reading:

> "Parking of large spiritual pet carriages strictly prohibited. Note: [Especially Taotie and dragons](# "Humorous Heavenly Spirit Sect traffic regulation targeting Eldest Senior Brother Yuwen Xuan and Fifth Senior Brother Lu Shulong / the True Dragon clan")."

[Why the hell are Taotie and dragons singled out?!]

[Wait, are there actually Taotie and dragons here?!]

Long Tianming's eyes went wide as saucers.

Confronted by this spectacle that felt simultaneously familiar and alien, his perception of the cultivation realm was completely overhauled once more.

Firmly believing he was the "protagonist," Long Tianming had always viewed the post-transmigration cultivation world through a "higher-dimensional" lens. He had been utterly convinced that by relying on his (self-perceived) superior cognition, his (self-perceived) broader horizons, and his (self-perceived) richer reservoir of knowledge, he could easily hold the upper hand over the natives. Yet traveling this far, he vaguely realized that the cultivation world was not the barbaric, backward wilderness depicted in web novels. On the contrary, under certain conditions, immortal spellcraft achieved technological leaps far faster than mundane science.

Take teleportation arrays, for instance.

Even in the Celestial Realm, spatial teleportation was considered high-level methodology. Not only was the research grueling, but the construction difficulty was astronomical, requiring adjustments for countless environmental variables. Yet once completed, it was an infrastructure far more convenient than high-speed trains, airplanes, or even starships.

None of this, however, dented Long Tianming's peculiar confidence in the slightest.

Male transmigrators were simply like this: whether thrown back into the primitive slash-and-burn stone age or thrust into an era of interstellar voyages, they somehow maintained the unshakeable certainty that the entire universe revolved around them. It was truly enviable.

Yet sensing the shift in Long Tianming's perception, the demon seed within his core contracted violently, radiating intense unease. This sudden wave of anxiety caused Long Tianming's heart to palpitate, prompting him to glance around in inexplicable worry.

No one noticed Long Tianming's oddity. Everyone, including Renran, was thoroughly captivated by the bustling transit concourse. After all, none of them had ever witnessed a [population explosion](# "Literal CN: 人口爆发 (censored as 人□□发) — rapid population boom brought about by advancements in modern medicine and technology") triggered by rising standards of technology and healthcare. Even in the most prosperous worlds, populations remained strangely limited; a city of a million souls was already considered a titanic metropolis, with mortals forming the base. Consequently, witnessing such dense, flowing rivers of people was quite a novelty in their eyes.

Nevertheless, the celestial officials relished the "distinguished" seclusion of their private corridor, regarding it as the proper treatment befitting upper immortals. Their gaze toward the crowd carried a touch of haughty scrutiny:

[—"So many people... Is this a frontline garrison? Why are you even enlisting the demon race?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official A")

[—"Not just demons—the vast majority are mortals! Your recruitment pool is looking rather dismal!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official B")

[—"Mortals?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official C")

As the officials murmured amongst themselves, one addressed the guide directly:

—"You shouldn't be drafting mortals,"-the official said, though his reasoning stemmed by no means from any reverence for mortal life.-"Mortals below the Nascent Soul stage have volatile mentalities and possess zero defensive measures. They are exceedingly prone to being seduced by demons, turning into breeding grounds for demonic corruption. Thus, they are unqualified even as cannon fodder—they might very well end up aiding the demon race."

They held deep, bitter lessons regarding this reality.

Hearing this, the guide merely smiled, clearly accustomed to such remarks from upper-realm officials.

—"No matter. These mortals will not come into direct contact with the demons. Furthermore, your lordships appear to have misunderstood: this is not the frontline, but rather the transit station of the largest city situated near the outpost. We could hardly be so reckless as to send you noble lords straight into harm's way! And these mortals and spirit beasts naturally aren't warriors—they are travelers freely coming and going."

Travelers?

What possible business could mortals have "touring" near the frontlines?

The celestial officials grew only more perplexed.

Yet the guide had no intention of offering exhaustive explanations to these [immortal bumpkins](# "Literal CN: 仙巴佬 (xiānbǎlǎo) — bumpkin immortals; lower-realm slang mocking cloistered, out-of-touch celestial officials"):

—"Once your lordships enter the city, the specifics will become clear."

Saying so, he turned toward Long Tianming and Renran:

—"Junior Brother, and Junior Sister, I still have duties to attend to here and won't be escorting you to the academy. Please wait in the VIP lounge; someone will come to receive you shortly."

—"Can't we come along?"-Renran spoke up abruptly.

She still could not resign herself to letting the "Child of Destiny" remain in Untiring's territory just like this. Although the Heavenly Gate was hardly any better, it was at least the Celestial Realm; who knew if an opportunity might arise to break free from Untiring's control?

Renran could only brazenly press her demand. Fortunately, shamelessly wheedling was second nature to her:

—"Such a rare opportunity—I want to see the God-Demon frontlines with my own eyes."

Saying so, completely heedless of the guide's reaction, she turned toward the celestial officials:

—"The immortal lords wouldn't mind, would they?"

Given Renran's familiarity with her celestial colleagues, no matter what they secretly thought, they always maintained a veneer of gracious reasonableness in public and would never harshly refuse a little girl's request. Renran was equally certain that even her fellow officials could not see through her disguise; her mastery lay in time spellcraft, so when she willed herself to be a little girl, her current physical vessel was an authentic, genuine little girl.

Sure enough, those celestial officials detected nothing amiss about Renran and did not refuse her plea.

Only one rather meddlesome official cautioned her:

—"We certainly don't mind. However, little girl, the frontlines are hardly a pleasant excursion; I fear you will regret it."

[Wouldn't that be perfect?]

If she could scare the "Child of Destiny" so badly that he refused to ever leave the Celestial Realm again, that would be ideal!

Occupied with such thoughts, Renran never once stopped to consider what possible use a "Child of Destiny" terrified of leaving the Celestial Realm would actually be.

—"Is it truly that frightening?"-Renran recited in an affected, [monotone deadpan](# "Literal CN: 捧读 (pěngdú) — reading woodenly from a script without genuine emotion"), pointedly turning toward Long Tianming.-"Yet our young master gave up his opportunity to cultivate at the Heavenly Gate solely to eradicate the demons; shouldn't he visit the actual field to take a look?"

[—"He actually gave up the opportunity to cultivate at the Heavenly Gate?!"](# "Speaker: Astonished Celestial Official")

The celestial officials wore stunned expressions, sizing Long Tianming up anew.

Although they had no clue that Long Tianming was a Child of Destiny, could see nothing remarkable about him, and didn't even know the true background of the Heavenly Gate's academy or what on earth it taught, it didn't matter. Regardless of whether an educational reform had taken place, tossing away an opportunity to study in a superior institution—even if "superior" was purely subjective—was universally viewed as a lamentable blunder. And that meddlesome itch to lecture and mentor others was a habit one never shed even after becoming a celestial official; if anything, it only grew more virulent.

The celestial officials immediately reached complete consensus with Renran:

[—"Too foolish!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official A")

[—"How could anyone abandon cultivation?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official B")

[—"Do you have any idea how excruciatingly difficult it is to ascend from the lower realms to the Celestial Realm?! How could you toss it away so casually!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Official C")

Watching the situation degenerate once more into [*Rescuing Out-of-School Children 2*](# "Literal CN: 《拯救辍学儿童2》 — humorous callback to the rural educational outreach in Chapter 250"), Long Tianming cast a deeply aggrieved glare at Renran and tried to argue:

—"I didn't... I know..."

[—"You know nothing! No matter what, cultivation is of paramount importance for youth like you!"](# "Speaker: Stern Celestial Official")

The celestial officials found this unremarkable. In their view, spoiled "immortal second-generation" nepo-babies who failed to appreciate the Celestial Realm's supremacy and refused to cultivate made up the majority. (Naturally, they assumed that Long Tianming, who had teleported down alongside them from the upper realm, was a pampered immortal brat; in the Celestial Realm, only such nepo-babies possessed negligible cultivation yet wandered about unchecked—one of the reasons the officials felt the Celestial Realm was going downhill).

The officials ceased lecturing Long Tianming, turning instead to direct their reproach at the innocent guide:

—"No matter how short-handed you are, you shouldn't swindle a child into coming here!"-Their indignation this time was far more heartfelt.

—"I decided this of my own accord; no one swindled me..."-Long Tianming issued a protest that went completely ignored.

—"We do not endorse arbitrarily altering one's chosen enrollment either, not to mention this matter isn't finalized yet, is it?"-The guide offered a wry smile.-"In truth, even if this young lady hadn't asked, once arriving at the academy, we would still escort these youths on a frontline orientation tour first. Only after they understand what their choice entails and what they must study do we allow them to make their definitive decision."

[—"They haven't even embarked upon cultivation yet; what meaningful decision could they possibly make?"](# "Speaker: Skeptical Celestial Official")-an official muttered.

Evidently, the celestial officials were unsatisfied with the guide's explanation and still harbored doubts regarding his motives.

Nevertheless, even as celestial officials, they could not overstep their authority to police "other departments"—or at least what they presumed to be another division of the Celestial Court. Furthermore, since the child was supposedly a "volunteer," the best they could do was align with Renran's reasoning: let the kid suffer a little adversity so he would finally appreciate the virtue of schooling.

Thus, an official declared:

—"In that case, there is no need for redundant steps; simply let him accompany us on our tour."

[Even if you say so, the academy's student tours follow a specialized protocol after school opens! How could we possibly take an unprepared mortal child directly into the Demon Realm?!]

The guide wanted to object, but seeing the celestial officials glaring at him in unison—like a troop of thieving monkeys on Mount Ling preparing to mug a passing traveler, looking as if they would tear him to shreds if a single negative word left his lips—he could ultimately only stammer:

—"I... I will check with my superiors."

He hastily contacted the local academy. Fortunately, as if having anticipated this scenario long ago, the academy not only granted special clearance but transmitted an entirely customized, specially routed itinerary.

[Isn't this preparation a bit *too* exhaustive? Could there be something exceptional about them?]

Puzzled, the guide couldn't help casting several inquisitive glances at the celestial officials, and especially at Long Tianming.

The celestial officials thought nothing of it, regarding such VIP treatment as only natural, merely prodding:

[—"Well? What's the verdict?"](# "Speaker: Impatient Celestial Official")

—"Guests' requests naturally meet no refusal,"-the guide said, swallowing his lingering doubts and humoring the officials, smoothly gesturing for them to proceed.

Their actual route differed little from the original plan:

—"Honored guests, please step this way."

Following the guide's lead, the tour group pressed forward. Long Tianming tagged along somewhat awkwardly, pointedly ignoring Renran.

He harbored a faint resentment toward his "prospective harem recruit," feeling her antics had just humiliated him, yet tagging along on a VIP tour with high-ranking celestial officials seemed advantageous. His feelings were thoroughly tangled.

Fortunately, his fretfulness did not last long before they reached the terminal's exit gates.

Yet this appeared to be an exit corridor exclusive to special platforms; unlike the public concourses, it was virtually deserted. Furthermore, the portal seemed situated at an immense altitude—long before nearing the doorway, one could feel the whistling celestial astral winds buffet against them.

—"The private cloud carriage prepared exclusively for your lordships is ready,"-the guide explained.-"Next, we will board it to tour the entirety of the city before traveling onward to the boundary of this landmass."

Hearing this, an official mumbled impatiently:

[—"What is there to tour in a lower-realm mortal city?"](# "Speaker: Haughty Celestial Official")

Immediately, another official jabbed him in the ribs with an elbow, signaling him to keep his mouth shut.

In truth, the officials' primary objective had already been fulfilled.

They had come to inspect the teleportation array; arriving in the lower realm via the array and returning to the Celestial Realm would formally constitute a completed inspection. But celestial officials never conducted business so abruptly. Having arrived in an alien world, bureaucratic protocol dictated that they tour the realm, meet—and dine—with local administrators, and inspect the arduous frontlines before calling it a day.

As far as the officials were concerned, they had arrived fully prepared to "endure hardship."

The guide merely smiled without a word, making an inviting gesture to let the officials step through the portal first—and then listened with immense satisfaction as they gasped in varying pitches of shock:

[—"Ah!"](# "Speaker: Shocked Celestial Official A")

[—"By the Sovereign Heavens..."](# "Speaker: Shocked Celestial Official B")

[—"!! Th-this—"](# "Speaker: Stunned Celestial Official C")

[What on earth is that commotion?]

Curious, Long Tianming followed them out—and let out an involuntary gasp of his own:

—"*Whoa!*"

Before his eyes, sword-like skyscrapers hung inverted from the "sky," every glass curtain wall cascading with waterfalls of neon luminescence. Advertising talismans drifted through the open air, rolling out vivid displays of alluring next-generation magical treasures.

Between the towering monoliths, transit networks crisscrossed in dazzling arrays, optical rails webbed as densely as biological meridians.

Compact spirit swords darted through the gaps between buildings, dragging slender golden vapor trails behind them. Colossal cloud carriages cruised languidly, while psionic automata swept deftly overhead in formations as disciplined as migrating wild geese.

The entire city operated like a colossal, precision-engineered array: skyscrapers served as its foundational pillars, optical rails as its conductive circuitry.

For a split second, Long Tianming thought he had transmigrated once again.

Transmigrated into the far future.

—"How does it look?"-The guide patted his shoulder, chuckling in an undertone.-"Pay no mind to those ignorant upper-realm folks. I must say, choosing to come here rather than lingering at the Heavenly Gate was a remarkably astute move. But mind you, qualifying to stay in the academies here is hundreds of times harder than at the Heavenly Gate!"

—"Difficulty in *what*?!"-Long Tianming asked in sheer panic.

The guide did not answer him, taking a few strides forward to announce to the petrified celestial officials:

—"Honored guests, welcome to the border metropolis forged jointly by mortals and cultivators: Eleventh City."

***

—"So the 'Child of Destiny' tagged along to the 'frontline base'?"-Zhou Hui inquired.

He had followed close behind, arriving in this metropolis on their heels.

This time, however, Zhou Hui chose not to shadow them directly. After all, stalking to Renran's degree bordered on perverse; no matter how inflated Long Tianming's ego was, he was bound to realize something was amiss—or at least, one would hope so. If the boy noticed absolutely nothing, Zhou Hui would genuinely begin suspecting his brain had been devoured clean by the demon seed.

In any case, Long Tianming would ultimately have to report to the academy. Zhou Hui was far more interested in observing his academic and cultivation performance anyway, and had gone straight to the academy campus—only to discover that Long Tianming had yet to check in, having wandered off with the celestial officials to the Immortal-Demon frontlines instead.

—"While Lady Renran instigated the diversion, the inclination of the celestial officials cannot be overlooked,"-Yao'er reported.

Having naturally grasped the situation instantly, he pulled up the transit platform's surveillance footage to present the scene to Zhou Hui.

—"They aren't bad at all; quite warmhearted toward juniors,"-Zhou Hui remarked, sounding surprisingly appreciative.

—"That is solely because the subject presents zero threat and due to certain misunderstandings. Had it been prospective ascenders seeking entry into the Celestial Court, they would wear an entirely different face,"-the Genesis Machine exposed without the slightest polite hesitation.

As the original creator of the Heavenly Court, it was intimately acquainted with the systemic defects of its own handiwork—having even contemplated rebooting civilization altogether, which explained why it had defected to Zhou Hui with such crisp decisiveness.

Zhou Hui was understanding:

—"Defending self-interest is instinctual. If anything, given the Heavenly Court's ruthless ascension quotas, the fact that they still preserve the instinct to shelter newcomers is already commendable."

This was also one of his core motivations for opening the celestial tour corridors.

Beyond establishing diplomatic ties with other minor worlds, these ascenders hailing from various realms represented prime human talent. The mere fact that they had achieved ascension proved they were the pinnacle prodigies of their respective worlds; it was merely because the Heavenly Court had operated for so long and reached near-total stagnation that these elites were left to idle away their lives or get discarded into frontline meatgrinders.

Since the Heavenly Court didn't want them, he would gladly take them off their hands.

Naturally, only after meticulous screening and rigorous examinations.

—"How are the celestial officials who arrived on this delegation?"-Having touched upon the topic, Zhou Hui inquired with interest.

—"Their caliber is fairly decent: comprehension and capability both meet standards, and their dispositions fall within trainable parameters. They qualify as candidates for employment contracts,"-Yao'er answered promptly, having clearly monitored their movements throughout.-"On another note, their personal savings are rather substantial. Thus far, they have expended five hundred thousand high-grade spirit stones across Wanbao Pavilion, the Beacon Relay, and the Eleventh Branch of the Scripture Library. Furthermore, running short on liquid funds, they have either bartered precious items, issued promissory notes, or signed long-term supply contracts promising local specialties from their governed minor worlds."

Zhou Hui: "..."

In truth, he had long harbored suspicions that the Celestial Realm's systematic exploitation of the lower realms owed a considerable debt to his youngest disciple—or perhaps the machine had initiated it altogether. After all, the xianxia novels used as its training corpus were brimming with celestial conspiracies; it was already showing boundless mercy by not generating a fashionable Lovecraftian cosmic horror Heavenly Court.

Unaware of his master's complicated musings, the Genesis Machine simply continued reporting:

—"Among them, two celestial officials warrant deeper observation. The first is Master of Automata Ji Gongbu, whom we made a special effort to 'invite.' He has already purchased every single spellcraft academic journal published over the past two centuries from the Scripture Library and opted into our long-term subscription service. Judging by his inclination, the probability of successfully recruiting him is exceptionally high."

—"Very good,"-Zhou Hui nodded.

Formation and mechanism grandmasters were the highest priority of this recruitment drive. After all, relying solely on their own internal manpower to construct large-scale arrays for multiple worlds was stretching them thin. In fact, Ran Deng had been playing sick for half a year already—she would rather be strung up from the peach tree than accept any more workload.

Moreover, dispatching personnel to construct arrays in other worlds would proceed far more smoothly with an official from the Heavenly Court's Department of Works endorsing the permits.

—"The other notable individual is Realm Protector Li Lu of Wanhua Realm,"-Yao'er continued.

—"A Realm Protector? And from Wanhua Realm?"-Zhou Hui remarked, surprised.

By now, Zhou Hui was no longer in the bewildered state of when he had first ascended to the Celestial Realm, not even knowing which way to turn. Aside from the near-omniscient Genesis Machine, even the Heavenly Gate academy gathered ample intelligence for him.

Yet compared to the hollowed-out Heavenly Court, Zhou Hui was far more invested in data regarding other realms. Wanhua Realm happened to be one of the largest and most resource-abundant worlds among the Three Thousand Realms. If he could tap into its reserves, Zhou Hui could construct high-speed, direct transit arrays linking every realm and domain across the entire cosmos.

Regrettably, the Heavenly Court would never overlook such a prized territory.

In truth, worlds capable of perpetually yielding precious resources were placed under direct celestial governance, with pureblood True Immortals parachuted down as viceroys—the so-called Realm Protectors.

Transposed into a mortal imperial dynasty, such figures would roughly hold titles akin to Prince of a vassal state.

Zhou Hui was angling for fish, yet he hadn't anticipated hooking such a massive catch.

—"Have they finally noticed something is amiss?"-Zhou Hui's tone actually carried a hint of gratification.

—"Deduced through standard logic: impossible,"-the Genesis Machine asserted.-"Had they genuinely detected an anomaly, they would have taken immediate military action rather than launching an investigation, and they certainly would not have dispatched a 'noble' Realm Protector to grace a petty lower-realm backwater in person."

—"Hmm..."-Zhou Hui sighed.-"Then for what purpose did this noble Realm Protector journey to our humble lower-realm backwater?"

—"...Apologies, Master. I am unable to compute in depth; I feel as though I was bitten."

—"Ah?"-Zhou Hui asked, taken aback.

—"I can only infer based on his behavioral telemetry: he appears to have been dragged along unexpectedly by an acquaintance. Throughout the journey, he has been weighed down by heavy anxieties, lost entirely in his own thoughts. He took no part in his companions' discussions, and was exceedingly stingy, making not a single purchase. Only upon reaching the Scripture Library did he seem to come to his senses, purchasing massive volumes of horror and thriller novels—specifically Lovecraftian subgenres featuring encounters with unspeakable, incomprehensible entities."

—"Ah???"-Zhou Hui stared, utterly flabbergasted.

—"Furthermore, he is suspected of bearing [Eldest Senior Brother's predatory prey mark](# "Literal CN: 大师兄猎食标记 — ominous spiritual signature branded by Yuwen Xuan upon prime food targets he plans to devour later")."

—"Ah,"-Zhou Hui exhaled, sudden realization washing over him.
