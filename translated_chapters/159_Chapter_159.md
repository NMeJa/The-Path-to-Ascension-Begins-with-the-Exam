# Chapter 159: True Form (第159章 本体)

[← Previous Chapter](158_Chapter_158.md) | [Table of Contents](INDEX.md) | [Next Chapter →](160_Chapter_160.md)

Yuwen Xuan swore that he wasn't [starting shit](# "Slang: 搞事 (gǎoshì) — causing trouble / stirring up shit").

He was merely... a little bloated from overeating.

In the physical sense.

Overeaten.

After all, it was a rare occasion where Master had personally taken him out for a feast! And it had been such a colossal entity. As a Taotie cub—and one that was half human at that—he had actually managed to eat his fill, and had even sneakily brought a sizable haul back with him.

Therefore, upon returning to his own cave dwelling from Master's quarters, Yuwen Xuan set about organizing the foodstuffs he had brought home.

Zhou Hui had always assumed that Yuwen Xuan's food-collecting habit and stockpiling obsession were innate Taotie instincts, but that wasn't actually true. While greed and gluttony certainly belonged to the nature of a Taotie, just as a Taotie had no notion of beast-taming, ordinary Taoties didn't hoard food either. They simply devoured without the slightest restraint, unbothered even if they gorged themselves to bursting.

Yuwen Xuan's hoarding instinct, in fact, stemmed from his human side.

Consequently, knowledge regarding how to dress game, categorize it, and stockpile it for a rainy day was the single domain of study Yuwen Xuan had mastered with absolute perfection.

One could see this clearly from the way Yuwen Xuan processed his food.

He hermetically jarred the god's aura into separate boxes, labeled each with the date, and stowed them neatly away;

He sliced up the divine radiance into measured portions and conducted experiments on several creatures of differing behavioral classifications. Once he verified which species the radiance proved most efficacious upon, he tucked a small sliver of light into that creature's mustard-seed space, meticulously recording detailed annotations;

Likewise, divine blood, divine flesh, and divine bone were sorted, cataloged, and scrupulously preserved according to their individual physical properties.

Regrettably, whenever Yuwen Xuan engaged in such endeavors, he was almost always stashing contraband snacks behind Master's back, and Zhou Hui was hardly going to design an exam specifically on this subject. Otherwise, in the realm of "Classification and Preservation of Diverse Foodstuffs," Yuwen Xuan would have undoubtedly scored top marks.

Ah, though there was a slight imperfection.

It would have been much better had he not been sneaking bites the entire time he was putting things into storage.

While slicing, he couldn't resist licking the blade; while weighing portions, he would nibble off a tiny corner; while testing effects, he casually grabbed one of the "experimental subjects" and turned it into barbecue; and while stowing items into inventory, he casually retrieved a bottle of fine baijiu aged for a long time to sip alongside his fresh emergency rations.

By the time he had finally finished packing everything away in textbook order, he had thoroughly stuffed himself to the gills.

To tell the truth, Yuwen Xuan felt a mild pang of regret at that moment.

After all, when he was just a fluffy little lamb, sticking his belly out and flopping foolishly across the floor wouldn't draw any judgment. But now that he had resumed his 1.9-meter-tall adult male form, sprawling [belly-up like an overturned turtle](# "Literal CN: 四仰八叉 (sì yǎng bā chā) — sprawled out on one's back with arms and legs spread wide") was rather undignified.

—"It's all the fault of this Taotie bloodline..."-Yuwen Xuan muttered.

He knew full well that Master would give him a sound lecture if he overheard that excuse, but he had zero intention of changing his ways.

With such a convenient excuse ready-made to indulge himself, why on earth would he change?

Contemplating this, Yuwen Xuan let out a luxurious yawn and shut his eyes.

To an outside observer, he had simply fallen asleep.

Much like legendary beasts such as the Kunpeng, once he had eaten and drunk his fill, he would slip into a tranquil slumber, effortlessly converting consumed nourishment into cultivation base mid-sleep, breaking through his original realm without breaking a sweat.

Yet in reality, Yuwen Xuan rarely enjoyed truly peaceful sleep.

Whenever he ceased thinking and closed his eyes, starvation would lick at his stomach like tongues of fire. In those moments, Yuwen Xuan desperately yearned to surrender his body to primal instinct and devour everything in sight. Yet he had never yielded. Instead, he would circulate the cultivation technique Master had taught him over and over again. When converting food into cultivation through means other than his physical stomach, he could experience a sensation of fulfillment akin to having eaten his fill.

Thus, undeniably, while Yuwen Xuan's rapid cultivation advancements owed much to his ferocious beast lineage, as the human portion of his being, he had cultivated with utmost diligence. This was precisely why, even after turning into a sheep—[er, transforming back into a Taotie](# "Humorous correction in raw text: 变成了羊（x）变回了饕餮 — crossed-out sheep joke")—he had never once lost his sanity.

The human side of Yuwen Xuan remained rather predominant.

Yet this time was unlike any occasion before.

This time, he was genuinely full. It was not the hollow sensation of tricking his belly with two lifetimes' worth of his own cremated ashes, but the feeling of stuffing his gut to the brim with pure divine power. Spared from the torment of gnawing hunger at long last, Yuwen Xuan drifted off into deep, unhindered slumber.

Only, at the exact instant he fell "asleep," somewhere in the so-called Upper Realm—in a higher dimension—a colossal pair of pupils resembling twin moons abruptly snapped open.

Those pupils were a murky, chaotic crimson, harboring madness twisted to the absolute extreme:

*Hungry hungry hungry...*

*Eat eat eat eat eat...*

*Master...*

*Food food food...*

In a fraction of a second, the sprawling, chaotic torrent of madness nearly shredded the consciousness named "Yuwen Xuan" into oblivion.

Fortunately, "Yuwen Xuan" was not confronting this frenzy head-on. Rather, it felt like a dream... no, he was literally "dreaming." Separated by a layer, a spatial membrane shielded him from direct fusion, and nestled amidst that chaos were memory fragments familiar to "Yuwen Xuan"—serving as immovable anchor points that stabilized the existence of the consciousness named "Yuwen Xuan."

Thus, Yuwen Xuan steadied his footing amidst the turmoil, and a sliver of clarity blossomed within those mad, dark-red eyes.

Directing these eyes, Yuwen Xuan peered downward toward "himself."

It was a titanic, ferocious beast. It bore thick, curling horns reminiscent of an argali sheep, yet possessed not an ounce of an herbivore's gentle docility. If anything, its colossal physique, tangled pelt, and tiger-like razor talons were savage to a terrifying degree.

—"'White Lamb'!"-Yuwen Xuan recognized the entity's identity.

Although his brain hadn't been the sharpest during his stint as the Black Lamb, half of his soul had hailed from this very origin after all. As long as his cognitive faculties were functioning, deducing his current predicament based on the circumstances was child's play:

To be precise, this was the true form of the "Taotie."

Whether the "Black Lamb" or the "White Lamb," both traced their origins back to this being.

However, the Black Lamb was intermingled with half a human soul and regarded itself as the human Yuwen Xuan, creating a separation from the true form. The White Lamb's soul, on the other hand, was directly tethered to the true form, capable of accessing the entirety of its memories at any given moment.

Hence, Yuwen Xuan had identified it as the "White Lamb."

At this moment, Yuwen Xuan's train of thought carried a bit of cognitive inertia from his days as the Black Lamb—or rather, these were thoughts buried deep in his mind all along. Back when he lived as an ordinary human, growing older had taught him to disguise them somewhat, but whenever his Taotie instincts gained the upper hand, he couldn't be bothered to hide anything:

—"What an absolute eyesore! So ugly and decrepit, yet you had the nerve to disguise yourself as me and deceive Master!"-Yuwen Xuan spat in disgust.

Indeed, Yuwen Xuan would never concede that this "White Lamb" was himself.

Even if their souls stemmed from the very same source, he had been born fair and square from a human mother's womb—he was someone who actually had a mother! The three characters of his name, "Yuwen Xuan," were the ultimate proof, to say nothing of his upbringing being painstakingly nurtured by Master. How could he possibly be lumped together with an uneducated, feral Taotie that had neither mother nor master?!

Harboring such indignant thoughts, Yuwen Xuan took a vicious, vindictive bite straight out of the Taotie's soul.

—"Ying!"-the Taotie's soul let out a wail akin to an infant's cry. It attempted to struggle, yet found itself completely immobilized.

Looking closely, the Taotie's soul was shockingly imprisoned.

Not only was it rigidly restrained, but it even wore an iron muzzle clamped tightly over its maw.

Yuwen Xuan found this setup remarkably familiar:

[Isn't this my own signature handiwork?! Not long ago, this was exactly how I tied up the 'White Lamb' when it tried to eat Junior Sister!]

*So binding the split soul down below causes the true form up above to be bound in the exact same manner!*

Filled with pleasant surprise, Yuwen Xuan took several more savage bites.

Consequently, he became even more bloated.

For a Taotie, there was no delicacy in existence that could satisfy the appetite more effectively than a Taotie's own true form. Not to mention, he had only managed to link with the true form through this dreamscape because he had been full to begin with—now, he was stuffed to the absolute bursting point.

Yet strangely, a sizable portion of the Taotie's soul did not resist Yuwen Xuan's devouring in the slightest.

If anything, a massive cluster of unbound soul-matter surged toward Yuwen Xuan with desperate eagerness, itching to merge into one with him.

Yuwen Xuan's mouth was crammed full in an instant, and then he realized what this was:

The remnant souls and memories of his previous seven lifetimes!

As the Eldest Disciple—surely because he was the Eldest Disciple, and definitely not because he was such a prolific troublemaker that he stuck his nose into every single mess—whether regarding the memories of the prior seven cycles, the True Immortal's conspiracy, or countless other secrets, Master had never concealed them from Yuwen Xuan the way he did with the younger disciples.

Thus, Yuwen Xuan had been privy to quite a lot of information.

Only, his memories had never returned. Even after consuming two lifetimes' worth of his own cremated ashes, there hadn't been the faintest flicker of recollection.

Now, Yuwen Xuan finally understood:

The reason he lacked those memories was because they had been preserved right here within the true form.

No—not "preserved." They had been eaten.

This was a reality that even Zhou Hui had been powerless to alter.

Because even for Zhou Hui, prior to this, his temporal reversals had only applied to his own minor world. No matter how peerless Zhou Hui's might was, he could not rewind time across all three thousand minor worlds alongside the higher-dimensional "Great World" that encompassed the Heavenly Court.

And throughout the previous seven lifetimes, Yuwen Xuan had "ascended" in every single one of them.

Under such circumstances, every time Zhou Hui rewound the world's timeline, he could only seize whatever fraction of ascended mass he could reach and forcibly drag it back. Fortunately, the current Yuwen Xuan was still "lightweight," possessing merely Soul Formation stage cultivation. Had a Tribulation Transcending powerhouse like Jian Haoge ascended, even if Zhou Hui reversed the entire world, he would never have been able to reel such a "heavy" amount of mass back intact.

Even so, the Taotie true form had stubbornly refused:

*How could food swallowed into one's belly ever be regurgitated?*

Therefore, each time Yuwen Xuan's soul was yanked back, a portion of it—especially everything pertaining to his memories—was swallowed whole, while an even greater concentration of ferocious beast essence was stuffed into the soul before sending it back down, thereby tightening the true form's gravitational pull and dominance over Yuwen Xuan's soul.

In other words, when it came to Yuwen Xuan, Zhou Hui had genuinely raised him in vain each time.

With every iteration, not only did Master have to start from scratch, but the human percentage grew progressively smaller, while the ferocious beast portion grew larger and larger.

However, the Taotie had failed to realize that this habit was actually "devouring" its own true form in return.

The portions of Yuwen Xuan swallowed into its gut had naturally refused to vanish quietly. Even having been eaten, they refused to be digested, stubbornly devouring and assimilating the Taotie's own flesh and soul in reverse.

And this cycle had played out seven times!

A full seven lifetimes!

Had it been only a single lifetime, that tiny spark of humanity—no matter how unwilling—would have inevitably been subsumed into the beast's overwhelming soul. But after seven cycles, the vast majority of the Taotie's soul had instead been conquered and occupied by "Yuwen Xuan's" willpower. Even the meager sliver of soul that had escaped being eaten had developed an intense curiosity toward "Master"—which had manifested as the White Lamb.

Now, Yuwen Xuan had returned to the true form once more. Unlike every previous time, he stood upon a dream plane buffered by a spatial membrane, gazing down at the bound, helpless Taotie true form.

This setup was no different from Yuwen Xuan sitting at a banquet table, staring down at an all-you-can-eat Taotie spread.

Those long-lost memories and remnant souls rolled with jubilant ecstasy, dying to be devoured so they could return to their original master consciousness.

Ironically, Yuwen Xuan was the one groaning from indigestion.

—"*Ying ying*, I can't eat another bite..."-Yuwen Xuan whimpered pathetically.

To force words like that from the mouth of a Taotie was a cosmic rarity.

Yet Yuwen Xuan continued to force himself to gorge.

This time, it wasn't out of a Taotie's bottomless greed, but because Yuwen Xuan knew: although these remnant souls and memories were once part of his being, and had clung stubbornly to "Master" as the anchor of their self-awareness so they wouldn't be digested, they were still trapped inside the Taotie's body. In this chaotic state without a unified brain, they still faced the peril of dissipating into nothingness. Only by returning to his own belly... er, his own body, would they be completely safe.

Furthermore, only by devouring these could he grow stronger than the Taotie, positioning himself to swallow the beast whole down the road.

Besides, Yuwen Xuan was genuinely curious about what had transpired across those previous seven lifetimes.

He hoped he could be of greater use to Master—definitely not because he harbored a sneaking suspicion that he hadn't disappeared so innocently in past cycles, and might have committed some unspeakable blunder that required him to [destroy the corpse and wipe away all traces](# "Humorous correction in raw text: 毁尸灭迹（x）毁灭证据 — slip of the tongue for destroying evidence")—er, destroy the evidence before Master found out!

Fortified with such solemn resolve, Yuwen Xuan strained with all his might to reabsorb the human portions of himself that had been devoured.

Even if his belly burst at the seams, he wouldn't hesitate.

Fortunately, before he could truly gorge himself to death, a slender wisp of aura suddenly wound around him, encircling and shielding Yuwen Xuan.

—"Master?"-Yuwen Xuan murmured in realization.

He struggled to grasp hold of that strand of aura and peered "downward."

Though he was adrift in a dreamscape, Yuwen Xuan occupied a higher dimension. Looking back toward the cultivation realm he inhabited felt like peering down through the rippling surface of clear water.

Different minor worlds glowed below with varied hues, resembling stars glittering beneath a flowing river.

In truth, if one stood inside a minor world and looked "upward," it was much the same.

Whether the Golden Crow, the Jade Rabbit, or the astral constellations, all were dimensional projections refracted across cosmic boundaries, casting diverse radiances down below to foster varied worlds.

Yuwen Xuan saw that the minor world he knew best of all was currently flickering with kaleidoscopic lights, looking rather like a ball of yarn mangled by an overenthusiastic cat.

And tracing that slender thread of aura, Yuwen Xuan could faintly overhear the voices of his Master and a certain "person" conversing:

[—"Lord Immortal Venerable, are you certain you wish to proceed thus? By doing so, you will surrender absolute dominion over this world, perhaps even forfeit your divine godhead..."](# "Speaker: Goddess of the Miniature World")

[—"It matters not. I was never a deity to begin with. The history of this world was forged collectively by its living beings from the outset. The Heavenly Dao of my ideals ought likewise to be woven by their own hands."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"That is truly..."-the feminine voice sighed in wonder.

Yuwen Xuan grasped the crux of their conversation immediately: it appeared Master was finally recompiling the Heavenly Dao.

Although he had known from the very beginning that Master intended to reweave the Heavenly Dao, he hadn't anticipated he would undertake it so swiftly.

What was more, judging by the exchange, Master didn't even plan to crown himself the supreme deity of that world—[Tch, the cultivation realm clearly lacks its own god, Master could easily be the Supreme God! That way, as a divine prince, I could run wild and do whatever the hell I wanted!]-Yuwen Xuan thought resentfully, mourning his missed opportunity to live as a [god nepo baby](# "Literal CN: 神二代 (shén-èrdài) — child of a deity; play on 'rich second-generation' (富二代) who inherits power without working"). Yet Zhou Hui was unmistakably committed to returning the authority of scripting the Heavenly Dao back into the hands of its myriad living beings.

Oh well. That was indeed precisely the sort of thing Master would do.

—"I suppose I've been saved by this as well!"-Yuwen Xuan muttered, gripping that wisp of aura tightly.

Evidently, in Zhou Hui's conceptualization, his eldest disciple Yuwen Xuan was counted among the "myriad living beings" of that world, equally qualified to become a weaver of the Heavenly Dao.

And so, Yuwen Xuan had received this wisp of divine aura.

Moments earlier, Yuwen Xuan had been agonizing over how to digest everything, terrified the dream might terminate at any second. But now, fortified with this tether, he could treat his remnant souls, memories, and even portions of the Taotie just like the divine remains—systematically categorizing and storing them away! For a long time to come, he wouldn't have to fret about an empty stomach ever again.

—"As expected of Master!"-Yuwen Xuan exclaimed.

No sooner said than done. Before his dream faded, Yuwen Xuan laboriously transferred this portion across the dimensional tether.

However, even if not fully digested on the spot, that was the accumulated power and cultivation base of seven full lifetimes! Consequently, when this torrent of power surged back into his physical form, Yuwen Xuan—who had already been teetering on the cusp of Soul Formation Peak—plunged headlong into a full-scale breakthrough.

Right at that juncture, Guan Ye and Mo Bei had arrived looking for him. Yuwen Xuan had awakened in the nick of time and [flexed shamelessly](# "Slang: 装了个X (zhuāng le gè X / 装逼) — put on an epic show / struck a cool pose to flex") before his junior brother and sister.

And thus, colossal thunderclouds coalesced across the sky.

Yuwen Xuan's breakthrough lightning tribulation had arrived.
