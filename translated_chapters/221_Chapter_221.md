# Chapter 221: A Measure of Strength

—"The trial system?... Master, I..."-Guan Ye began, then listened attentively before nodding:—"Ah, yes? Mmhmm! The external interface certainly poses no problem, and our spiritual energy reserves are more than sufficient."

—"Understood. I shall contact Mo Xi immediately!"

Guan Ye replied earnestly, watching Master's figure vanish from the water mirror just as abruptly as he had appeared.

Guan Ye could not help letting out a heavy sigh.

Observing his expression, Ran Deng—who had been sneaking around and furtively peeking over from the corner the entire time—grew even more frantic. Creeping up behind Guan Ye's chair, despite knowing full well no one else was present, she lowered her voice to an anxious whisper:

—"Did Master find out what we did to the trial system?"

She paused, unable to suppress a torrent of bitter complaints:

—"It must be Yuwen Xuan again! Who knows what lunatic stunt he pulled—the Sect Master of Thousand Beasts Hall fell into demonic corruption the second their match concluded! Master seemed to treat the matter with supreme gravity, personally heading to the arena to investigate and even borrowing my soul-fire! After all that, when I was heading over just now, I actually saw Eldest Senior Brother vaulting over the mountain gate with luggage strapped to his back, making a run for it! How could he just flee like that?! How on earth did he even manage to escape?! I bet everything that he sold us out!"

Ran Deng's agitation surged, looking as though if Yuwen Xuan were standing before her this very second, she would string him up by his ankles and whip him within an inch of his life.

—In truth, ever since Yuwen Xuan had [gilded the lily](# "Idiom: 画蛇添足 — drawing feet on a painted snake; ruining things by adding unnecessary meddling") with his "clever" postscript to the "Case of Qu Qianxing's Missing Heavenly Robe," Ran Deng had harbored an overwhelming desire to do exactly that. Alas, she had found no opportunity, nor could she actually defeat Yuwen Xuan in a brawl, leaving her to stew in powerless, impotent rage.

At this moment, she remained utterly convinced that every calamity in existence was Yuwen Xuan's fault. Regrettably, she remained just as powerless against him as ever.

Just moments ago, upon witnessing Yuwen Xuan's "jailbreak," she had originally intended to tail him. Yet rather than taking the main gates, Yuwen Xuan had scrambled across the boughs of the ancient peach tree. Immediately afterward, Ran Deng had lost all trace of him—as if their Eldest Senior Brother had been devoured whole by the ancient tree in mid-climb, vanishing without leaving so much as a speck of bone behind.

Left with no other recourse, Ran Deng had hurried over to the Water Mirror Tower, only to overhear Guan Ye conversing with Master. Though she hadn't caught the granular specifics, it vaguely revolved around the "trial system," sending her anxieties skyrocketing through the ceiling.

She unilaterally declared in total despair:

—"It's over. Master knows. What are we going to do?!"

[It is not at all what you think; Master merely assigned additional work.]

[Besides, if you were this terrified, why did you goof off with them in the first place, and why do you routinely dare to skip your homework?]

Guan Ye had originally intended to reply with these very words. Yet watching Ran Deng chatter away in frantic hysterics, an abrupt urge seized him to withhold the truth.

He had always found his fellow martial siblings' reactions toward Eldest Senior Brother profoundly entertaining.

While Eldest Senior Brother habitually relinquished all claims to basic human decency, surely there was no need for them to be quite so neurotic!

Musing thus, the corners of Guan Ye's mouth involuntarily twitched upward into an eerie sneer:

—"Jie jie jie... If Master has indeed discovered it, what would Senior Sister like to do?"

—"What else is there to do? First string oneself up on the peach tree to see if—"-Ran Deng blurted out, only to freeze mid-sentence:—"...Wait a moment. *'Jie jie jie'*?"

Ran Deng rounded on Guan Ye with a furious glare. Guan Ye's lips twitched awkwardly.

—"Splendid! Even *you* are mocking me now!"-Ran Deng fumed, spinning on her heel to storm toward the exit.

—"Senior Sister! Senior Sister Ran Deng! Forgive me, I was merely joking!"-Guan Ye hastily moved to block her path. While he occasionally suffered bizarre, impish spasms where he took perverse pleasure in frightening people, he genuinely had no desire to upset his martial sisters. Yet lacking the slightest knack for coaxing flustered maidens, he could only offer a dry, clumsy defense:—"B-But, I was not speaking entirely in jest."

—"Hmph?"-Ran Deng tilted her chin in haughty disdain, eyeing Guan Ye out of the corner of her eye.

She was not genuinely incensed; she simply knew Guan Ye's personality inside out, perfectly aware of how to make her Fourth Junior Brother squirm in flustered panic.

However, Guan Ye's next words instantly evaporated every shred of her playful mood.

Guan Ye confessed honestly:

—"While Master does not appear to have noticed for the time being, he is preparing to implement an 'expansion pack' for the trial system."

—"An expansion pack?"-Ran Deng repeated in confusion.

—"It seems Master feels the current combat simulations are not sufficiently intense, failing to accurately recreate historical events,"-Guan Ye explained:—"He intends to introduce a 'Campaign' mode to simulate the invading legions of the demonic horde..."

—"Stop, stop, stop! I get the picture!"-Ran Deng shrieked.

She had no inkling of what Master truly aimed to accomplish. Yet hearing this much, she was thoroughly convinced that regardless of what Master planned, his eventual discovery of the trial system's tampering was an absolute certainty!

Whenever Master assigned homework, there had never once been an occasion where he failed to inspect the submissions. The instant he stepped foot into the trial system, he would uncover precisely what had been transpiring inside!

So why on earth had Master suddenly remembered the trial system out of the blue?!

It was unquestionably all Yuwen Xuan's fault!

Ran Deng's mind burned once more with venomous grievance.

Even so, she had zero desire to voluntarily hang herself from the peach tree.

—"No, there is still time!"-Ran Deng attempted emergency damage control:—"While Master has merely assigned the 'homework' and has not yet sat down to grade it, all we have to do is retract the illicit components Master must never lay eyes on!"

—"Ah?"-Guan Ye blinked, having never even entertained such a notion:—"Are you not underestimating Master far too severely? Do you truly believe Master will fail to notice the tampering we performed?"

—"Of course he will notice! But as long as we claim it was merely a 'failed crafting experiment,' he will not pry too deeply,"-Ran Deng explained shrewdly.

Unlike Eldest Senior Brother who spent twenty-four hours a day practically begging for a beating, and incapable of the robotic obedience exhibited by Second Senior Brother, Ran Deng was a terminal, end-stage procrastinator who routinely delayed her assignments, blueprints, and array diagrams until the absolute deadline. Having spent years frolicking recklessly along the razor's edge of Master's bottom line, she possessed profound expertise in the fine art of [courting disaster](# "Lit: 作死 (zuòsǐ) — recklessly flirting with death or punishment"), intimately acquainted with the precise boundaries of Master's tolerance.

Scheming rapidly, Ran Deng offered tactical guidance:

—"We can even wait until Master enters the system, then casually report it in an offhand manner—nothing too detailed, merely mentioning that our curiosity was piqued by the architecture, so we conducted a few harmless experiments that consumed a fraction of sect resources... Ah, never mind, explaining this to you is an exercise in futility. I shall entrust Xiao Xi'er with this task later."

—"In short, suspend those 'BOSS battles' immediately and scrub the traces as thoroughly as possible,"-Ran Deng commanded.

For once, Guan Ye wore an uncharacteristically conflicted expression.

—"Can it not be done?"-Ran Deng asked:—"Is there some technical bottleneck?"

—"No. Though it would require some effort, halting the routines is not impossible,"-Guan Ye hesitated, shifting uncomfortably:—"However, stopping now would be... far too wasteful..."

—"Wasteful?"

Ran Deng stared at him in bewilderment. That was the last reason she had anticipated.

In truth, had Guan Ye protested with, *"This is deceiving Master, we must not do it!"*, Ran Deng would have found it far more aligned with her stereotypical impression of her Fourth Junior Brother. But where on earth did "wasteful" come from?

Guan Ye stammered haltingly:

—"Although not much time has elapsed, just as Youngest Junior Sister said... we seem to have obtained 'power' from the future."

As he spoke, fearing Ran Deng might disbelieve him, he formed a swift hand seal.

In tandem with Guan Ye's mudra, several shrill, harrowing wails of vengeful specters resonated through the Water Mirror Tower. The indistinct, shadowy silhouettes flickering behind the reflective panes—originally a "minor cantrip" Mo Xi devised to manipulate the scrying mirrors—oozed down from the glass like boiling pitch. Blending with the scattered scrap materials of broken artifacts, they coalesced in an instant into a jet-black "bone dragon" that continuously dripped greasy black oil from between its skeletal ribs, coiling through the rafters of the Water Mirror Tower.

—"Ew!"-Ran Deng squeaked, recoiling as a spark of flame erupted between her brows. It expanded into a radiant golden-crimson halo resembling a glass lantern shade, cloaking her entire figure.

Even so, a distinct chill seeped into Ran Deng's marrow.

—"Is this some manner of ghost-taming art?"-Ran Deng asked.

—"It is a synthesis of ghost-taming arts and automata mechanics,"-Guan Ye murmured, gazing up at the skeletal wyrm in sheer awe:—"I have never absorbed knowledge at such a blistering pace. It feels as though I already possessed this mastery long ago, and this is merely a quick revision cram before an examination."

Reaching this point, Guan Ye looked slightly abashed:

—"Ah, I know full well that every gain carries a cost; the more effortlessly something is attained, the steeper the hidden price tag. Yet these two disciplines offer immense utility to my craft. Furthermore, if these are skills that 'future me' was capable of mastering, then I am more than willing to pay whatever tuition is required to learn them."

—"Naturally, I also believe it is essential to inform Master and request that Mr. Ruwang appraise this ghost-taming discipline,"-Guan Ye added hastily.

Ran Deng's expression grew profoundly subtle.

She understood that once Guan Ye reached this stage of conviction, given his stubborn nature, nothing short of Master's incandescent fury would ever sway him.

After all, "power" was Guan Ye's eternal obsession.

Though both were ostensibly obedient honor students who [ground themselves into twisted crullers](# "Modern metaphor: 内卷成麻花 — intense academic rat-racing that twists one into a pretzel"), the underlying drivers behind her Second Senior Brother and Fourth Senior Brother were diametrically opposed.

Qu Qianxing studied diligently because of his innate character and moral disposition. He treated every endeavor with uniform, razor-sharp seriousness. Furthermore, endowed with peerless, heaven-defying talent, Qu Qianxing approached any subject with innate, unshakeable confidence; he never fretted over gains and losses out of a desperate hunger for a specific power, knowing that if one avenue was temporarily obstructed, he possessed the supreme confidence to carve out an alternate path to mastery.

Guan Ye was the polar opposite. Cursed with wretched natural talent, he clung fiercely to the belief that diligence could compensate for mediocrity, relying on agonizing exertion to bridge the gap in innate gifts. Even so, his academic journey had been exceptionally arduous, repeatedly set back by human-wrought disasters that forced him to retake his cultivation foundational modules multiple times. Consequently, he had zero faith in the existence of "effortless mastery"—such as what had transpired just now.

A Guan Ye like this harbored a ravenous, unyielding thirst for "power." While he would never intentionally stray onto demonic paths, once he deemed a certain power necessary, he would grip it with ferocious tenacity, heedless of the cost.

The terrifying part was that Master actually never objected.

Be it Yuwen Xuan's gluttony, Qu Qianxing's radicalism, Ran Deng's chronic indolence, Guan Ye's stubbornness, Lu Shulong's childishness, Mo Xi's greed, or Mo Bei's emotional void—so long as they did not sacrifice innocent lives and avoided inflicting authentic harm upon themselves, Master generally treated these traits as individual quirks, granting them broad latitude to express themselves.

> "Compared to an existence devoid of growth, an existence stripped of 'self' is far more terrifying," Master had once observed: "To live with a defined purpose is the most difficult endeavor of all."

Mmh... Ran Deng couldn't quite determine whether Master was genuinely profound or simply spoiling his disciples rotten.

In truth, what Guan Ye chose to study mattered little to her!

The real issue was that Guan Ye was pining after skills mastered by the *worst possible future version of himself*! Worse still, without Master's explicit consent, they had directly linked the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree's *Exhortation to Learning* system into the trial framework to run unauthorized simulations! And now he even intended to present it to Master as a [cooked rice fait accompli](# "Idiom: 生米煮成熟饭 — the raw rice is already cooked; what's done cannot be undone"), while soliciting guidance from an outside Tribulation Transcendence titan!

In any other orthodox sect across the mortal realm, any single one of those offenses would be branded as high treason against one's master!

Ran Deng felt that no matter how saintly Master's temper was, he would inevitably harbor some degree of displeasure.

Even if Master didn't take offense, he would surely issue a "token punishment."

Yet Ran Deng had zero appetite for "token punishments"! Thanks to the Heavenly Robe incident, she had already been saddled with managing hospitality and public relations for the entire Immortal Arts Assembly! She refused to be saddled with even more vexing administrative chores! What if Master found their unauthorized project impressive and sentenced her to study even more advanced curricula?!

Ran Deng parted her lips to object:

—"I—"

—"It is not merely myself; the younger siblings are progressing just as swiftly!"-Guan Ye cut in, sensing her imminent protest. Pushing his case once more, he added:—"Shulong and Youngest Junior Sister's combat power has expanded at an astounding rate."

As he spoke, he projected the tournament performances of Lu Shulong and Mo Bei in the Immortal Arts Assembly across the water mirror.

Among them, Mo Bei's display showed little discernible shift at first glance.

As a sword cultivator—and one belonging to the "invincible within her tier" echelon—she had zero need for elaborate tactical finesse during the opening stages of the grand finals, carving through her opposition like chopping melons and vegetables to secure victory after victory, making it impossible to gauge whether she had made tangible progress.

The focal point lay with Lu Shulong.

Because Guan Ye's skeletal wyrm bore a suspicious resemblance to Lu Shulong yet wasn't quite him, the *Exhortation to Learning* system had perpetually failed to simulate that specific future for Lu Shulong. Thus, in a fit of pique, Lu Shulong had executed a brute-force "grafting."

He had demanded that the bone dragon simulated from Guan Ye's "Darkened BOSS Guan Ye" data be grafted onto his own profile, allowing him to emulate that skeletal beast's skill tree.

Ran Deng privately wondered if Lu Shulong had suffered severe brain damage, dead set on becoming Guan Ye's personal mount... Ah well, to each their own; whether man or dragon, if Lu Shulong's supreme life aspiration was to serve as Guan Ye's steed, she could only offer her bewildered respect and heartfelt blessings.

What was truly staggering was that Lu Shulong had actually succeeded!

That very bone dragon, which had adamantly refused to simulate from Lu Shulong's dataset, had seamlessly synchronized with Lu Shulong once forced to utilize Guan Ye's data. Against all odds, Lu Shulong had authentically acquired the bone dragon's skill set!

Take the ongoing tournament match Guan Ye was currently broadcasting: Lu Shulong was clad in the newly forged silver-white shackles Guan Ye had prepared—which paradoxically resembled a majestic, imposing suit of armor. His innate, peerless draconic might was suppressed to the maximum threshold. Exploiting this apparent vulnerability, his opponent had opted for close-quarters melee combat, banking on the conventional wisdom that alchemists were notoriously inept at brawling. Yet before anyone could blink, Lu Shulong's unchained hand abruptly transmuted into a razor-sharp draconic claw, raking forward with savage velocity and nearly dicing the hapless challenger into five neat cutlets.

This manner of localized, pinpoint morphological transformation was a mastery Lu Shulong had never previously commanded.

—For transformed spirit beasts and demon clans, executing a flawless human transmutation was a formidable threshold in itself; furthermore, commanding precise, localized part-transformation while maintaining human form to unleash one's ancestral predatory advantages was a pinnacle art typically reserved for thousand-year-old fiends and myriad-year-old monsters.

Lu Shulong's circumstances were even more peculiar. Raised among mortals, though the dragon clan possessed ancestral hereditary memories, they encompassed only baseline magical arts. Consequently, while Lu Shulong was reasonably adept in human spellcraft, his True Dragon heritage had always lacked a critical puzzle piece. Assuming his draconic vessel had relied purely on primal instinct, utterly devoid of authentic draconic martial forms—a gap that even Zhou Hui was powerless to bridge.

In fact, Lu Shulong's recent half-draconic condition upon returning from the demon battlefield was an involuntary defensive reflex to resist demonic miasma, subconsciously deploying his dragon scales for protection, which had resulted in a botched transmutation that exposed his draconic lineage.

Now, it appeared the skeletal wyrm had provided Lu Shulong with that very final puzzle piece.

Throughout the remainder of the duel, Lu Shulong's playful instincts clearly took over. Ceasing all use of Heavenly Spirit Sect incantations and alchemical concoctions, he relied purely on the raw physical resilience of his suppressed dragon vessel, pummeling his opponent so thoroughly that the cultivator wept openly while forfeiting.

—"I ought to forge another set of manacles capable of sealing draconic qi all the way down to his fingernails; I wonder if that might properly constrain him,"-Guan Ye mused analytically.

—"..."-Ran Deng watched the conclusion of the match in stony silence before abruptly declaring:—"If you lot wish to forge ahead, then be my guest!"

—"Senior Sister?"-Guan Ye turned toward her with pleasant surprise.

—"Reflecting upon it carefully, this is entirely your business, is it not? In that wretched future, I alone was absent, nor did I possess any 'Darkened BOSS' transformation,"-and naturally, it held zero relevance to her own cultivation growth.

Ran Deng sneered coldly:

—"Therefore, I know nothing, I participated in nothing, and whatever atrocities you commit have zero bearing on me! I was never in the Water Mirror Tower to begin with! I am completely, utterly unaware of the situation regarding the trial system! I am merely an innocent senior sister who placed excessive trust in her fourth and seventh junior brothers, only to be foully deceived!"

Muttering this self-serving manifesto under her breath, she marched briskly toward the exit.

This time, she was genuinely departing.

Furthermore, as Ran Deng stepped across the threshold, Guan Ye distinctly overheard her spitting a vicious, muffled curse:

—"[Get stronger! Get stronger! Why don't you all just grind yourselves to death in the rat race?!](# "Modern slang: 卷死 (juǎnsǐ) — toxic academic competition ending in burnout")"

***

—"Achoo!"

Lu Shulong sneezed.

Naturally, True Dragons did not suffer from mortal colds; Lu Shulong's sneezing was invariably provoked by other cosmic catalysts.

—"Fourth Senior Brother must have watched my tournament match and been utterly captivated by my peerless valor,"-Lu Shulong proclaimed with supreme confidence, flashing a brisk peace sign toward the jade screen mounted high atop the open-air tavern.

That jade screen was double-sided: while broadcasting tournament footage, it simultaneously possessed recording and transmission faculties, capable of real-time monitoring of the tavern's patrons. Throughout the duration of the Immortal Arts Assembly, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had deployed countless such arrays across all major public venues.

Though administrative privileges for these "surveillance mirrors" were rigorously partitioned, to Guan Ye—the chief engineer responsible for the interior outfitting and public infrastructure of the entire sect—accessing them was child's play. If Guan Ye was indeed observing Lu Shulong, he would naturally catch the gesture.

—In truth, Guan Ye and Ran Deng had indeed been scrutinizing his every move.

Confronted with Lu Shulong's unbridled vanity, the patron seated opposite him saw zero issue with it, eagerly chiming in with honeyed flattery:

—"Naturally! Who could witness Your Draconic Eminence's transcendent performance without their soul bowing in absolute reverence?"

The speaker was an elegant, strikingly handsome middle-aged gentleman. Sporting sword-like brows and piercing dragon eyes, he was draped in pale robes embellished with crimson scale embroidery along the cuffs and hem, radiating an aura of natural, imposing authority. Regrettably, several rotund little critters—defying classification as fat fish, chubby dragons, or stubby squids—nestling atop his head, wriggling within his sleeves, and splashing around his boots thoroughly dismantled his majestic demeanor. The comical sight made it almost impossible to believe that he was actually one of the Dragon Princes hailing from the Boundless Ocean of the Eastern Domain:

Dansha.

Yet in the presence of an authentic True Dragon like Lu Shulong, the dragon lineages of the Eastern Domain dared not put on the slightest draconic airs.

Securing an audience to invite Lu Shulong out today had cost Dansha no small amount of effort.

At this moment, he strained his facial muscles to project an eager, fawning, yet unmistakably goofy smile, showering Lu Shulong with praises:

—"Over these past days, watching Your Highness Shulong's glorious exhibitions, the little ones of my household have all become your most devoted acolytes."

Saying so, he casually scooped up a chubby, unidentified creature by his feet—possessing a fish tail yet looking nothing like an ordinary fish—and held it out before Lu Shulong.

The creature stared up at Lu Shulong with wide, watery black eyes:

—"Yāng!"

Letting out a bizarre squeak, it flushed an instant, vibrant crimson. A skirt of tiny fleshy tendrils sprouted beneath its pudgy belly, paddling frantically as it scrambled in a frantic roll straight up into Dansha's voluminous sleeve.

—"Hahaha, the little fellow is shy,"-Dansha chuckled, attempting to fish it back out of his sleeve, though its slimy tail proved far too slick to grasp.

Lu Shulong regarded Dansha with icy indifference.

Paying zero heed to the chubby creatures, he inquired bluntly:

—"Dansha, what was your actual motive for inviting me out here?"

Hearing Lu Shulong address him, Dansha immediately abandoned his sleeve excavation, beaming broadly:

—"Naturally, it was to express my boundless gratitude for Your Eminence Shulong's salvation! Last time, had it not been for your intervention, this humble one would never have escaped the Abyssal Ocean. It was merely due to pressing constraints that I lacked the opportunity to formally tender my thanks—"

—"Get to the point,"-Lu Shulong interrupted, his face impassive.

Dansha's smile froze.

Lu Shulong pressed on with brutal detachment:

—"You did not go through all the trouble of summoning me here merely to babble pleasantries, did you?"

[How could offering my apologies be considered pleasantries?]

[Though admittedly, there was a tiny—well, massive—desire to curry favor!]

Dansha thought in deep, wounded grievance.

Lu Shulong ignored him completely, pouring himself a cup of fine spirit wine and taking slow, measured sips.

Against outsiders, Lu Shulong habitually maintained this icy demeanor.

Especially toward the aquatic clans of the Eastern Domain.

It was not that Lu Shulong harbored prejudice against eastern sea creatures. As an authentic True Dragon, he felt a natural, instinctual affinity for aquatic life over other races. In fact, during the third reincarnation cycle, having been dispatched by Master to be raised in the Eastern Domain, he had fostered a distinct sentiment for these distant ocean cousins.

Yet precisely because of that history, Lu Shulong understood the eastern sea clans all too intimately.

He knew that while aquatic clans appeared aloof, austere, and haughty—refusing close ties with human mortals—without a single exception, every last one of them possessed an embarrassingly substandard intellect. It made one wonder if this entire taxonomic family had landed headfirst on the pavement when descending into this world, scrambling their brains beyond repair.

Take the Dansha seated before him right now.

During the third reincarnation cycle, when Lu Shulong was delivered to the Eastern Domain, dragon custom dictated that bloodline elders instruct the youth in the sacred heritage of dragons. Yet because Lu Shulong was the sole surviving True Dragon in the cosmos, the Eastern Domain dragons dared not assume senior airs; they dispatched several venerable elders to instruct him, designating multiple dragon princes as his royal study companions.

Regrettably, Lu Shulong—retaining memories of the preceding two cycles, boiling with bitter resentment, and consumed solely with orchestrating havoc to draw Master's attention—had ordered those dragon princes around like mindless lackeys.

And that was when Lu Shulong witnessed the catastrophic destruction wrought by [pig teammates](# "Modern slang: 猪队友 (zhū duìyǒu) — bumbling, counterproductive allies who cause more harm than enemies").

Lu Shulong ordered them to guide him to the Central Domain to seek Master; the dragons managed to lose their way so catastrophically that they landed in the Northern Domain. Lu Shulong schemed to restore Second Senior Brother's past-life memories, cultivating memory-awakening Vermilion Fruits in Sky City; the duckweed the dragons brought along clogged the imperial city's drainage canals, resulting in Qu Qianxing hunting Lu Shulong across three thousand kilometers with drawn blades. Lu Shulong attempted to steal Eldest Senior Brother's magical treasures to frame Second Senior Brother; the dragons nearly delivered him straight into Eldest Senior Brother's simmering stew pot... An unbroken chronicle of blood, sweat, and tears!

To this day, whenever Lu Shulong laid eyes on Jian Nuo, his eyes turned scarlet with venomous envy:

[Why did Master assign Second Senior Brother a companion of that caliber, while I was consigned solely to associating with brainless invalids?!]

[Wuwuwu, Fourth Senior Brother, I miss you so much!]

To make matters worse, while attempting to rescue Dansha after he blundered blindly into Bi'an Tian, Lu Shulong had nearly been skinned alive by the third-cycle incarnation of Guan Ye!

Recalling these historical nightmares, Lu Shulong possessed zero desire to engage Dansha in prolonged discourse.

Moreover, this current incarnation of Dansha appeared virtually unchanged from the third cycle. Lu Shulong sensed that uttering more than two consecutive sentences would result in Dansha clinging to him like stubborn barnacles; better to maintain an icy demeanor. After all, Dansha lacked the courage to take offense.

Sure enough, exactly as Lu Shulong predicted, Dansha dared not harbor the slightest grievance.

In fact, so long as one was not a disciple of the Heavenly Spirit Sect, virtually no one in the world would ever take issue with Lu Shulong.

It was not that Lu Shulong wielded some terrifying magical coercion that intimidated the populace. Rather, merely sitting there, with silvery-blue locks cascading gently down his temples, exuding a melancholic, ethereal detachment, he commanded an innate aesthetic authority that made everything feel "as it rightfully should be."

Consequently, nearly every patron in the open-air tavern and every passerby strolling along the adjacent thoroughfares—young and old, male and female, across every species—could not help gazing upon Lu Shulong. Yet not a single soul dared approach to strike up a conversation; even as they passed by his table, they subconsciously lowered their voices to a hushed whisper, terrified of startling this peerless beauty.

[Behold the transcendent aura of a True Dragon!]

Dansha mused with inexplicable, swelling pride.

—"Speak!"-the True Dragon suddenly snapped, glaring at him in irritation:—"Why have you gone mute?!"

[Even the True Dragon's majestic wrath is breathtaking!]

Dansha admired inwardly. Even so, even he realized that failing to produce an authentic matter of "proper business" would genuinely incite Prince Shulong's wrath. Yet Dansha truly possessed no grave matters; he had merely fabricated a pretext to secure an audience with the True Dragon, yearning to pour out his adoration and praise his heroic performance in the tournament.

Alas, the prince seemed entirely unimpressed by his flattery.

Then again, how could a magnificent True Dragon ever care for a mortal assembly organized by the human race?

The sole reason His Highness had deigned to participate must surely be out of deference to his revered master, the Untiring Immortal Venerable!

While comforting himself with these thoughts, in a desperate bid to ward off Lu Shulong's mounting temper, Dansha racked his brain for a shred of "proper business." After Lu Shulong had polished off half a flagon of wine, Dansha miraculously dredged one up:

—"R-Right! Regarding that trial system,"-Dansha blurted out:—"This humble one has some feedback to report!"

—"You have feedback regarding the system engineered by my Fourth Senior Brother and Youngest Junior Brother?!"-Lu Shulong's eyes narrowed dangerously.

—"Certainly not! Absolutely not! So that was constructed by your martial brethren? Truly young and exceptionally talented!"-Dansha backpedaled frantically:—"H-However, I know not whether it is a fault of my own constitution, but after entering the simulations a few times, I keep experiencing vivid dreams... dreams regarding a demonic invasion and the like. You might not know this, but for our dragon race, dreaming is no ordinary phenomenon; rather, it is—"

—"I know,"-Lu Shulong interrupted, his gaze turning razor-sharp and deadly serious.

The dragon race did not simply "dream."

Or more precisely, dragons never experienced arbitrary, meaningless dreams like human mortals.

Their dreams were inherently tethered to the river of 'Time'—intimating events that would transpire in the future, events currently unfolding in the present, or ancient truths that had transpired in the past. Under no circumstance were they ever meaningless illusions.

This was indeed authentic "proper business."

Lu Shulong pressed Dansha for confirmation:

—"Could it be that due to the thematic framework of the trial system, your ancestral bloodline memories were stimulated, causing you to dream of the ancient God-Demon War?"

—"No, that is not it,"-Dansha shook his head:—"The God-Demon War is the God-Demon War. Though I was not yet hatched during that era, possessing draconic blood, I have inspected ancestral records; those chronicles would never coalesce into dreams. What I dreamed of was the *'future'*. I dreamed that the Central Domain was invaded by the demonic horde, completely falling to ruin, and demons flooded outward from the Central Domain into all other territories! In that dream, I even overheard scouts reporting that the Heavenly Spirit Sect was annihilated by the 'heroes' who rose to resist the demonic invasion..."-he trailed off, warily scrutinizing Lu Shulong's expression.

—"'Heroes'? Annihilated the Heavenly Spirit Sect?"-Lu Shulong asked.

—"Hahaha, naturally it was merely a dream! With Your Eminence present, alongside the Untiring Immortal Venerable, who could ever hope to lay waste to the Heavenly Spirit Sect?! Anyone watching the Immortal Arts Assembly understands that beyond a shadow of a doubt!"-Dansha immediately pivoted his tone:—"Though we dragons do not ordinarily dream, I suspect the trial system was simply far too lifelike, causing this humble one to hallucinate such an absurd, bizarre spectacle!"

...That was anything but absurd.

Judging by that description, that was precisely the catastrophe that had unfolded during the prior reincarnation cycles.

Particularly the sixth cycle.

Lu Shulong thought with a tight furrow of his brow.

Yet unlike himself, the dragon clans of the Eastern Domain had intermingled extensively, their divine blood severely diluted, tilting far closer to common spirit beasts. Consequently, their power was limited, rendering them incapable of preserving memories across temporal cycles.

Now Dansha—and of all dragons, this famously dim-witted Dansha—seemed to be recovering faint, hazy echoes of past cycles!

Could other cultivators across the realm be suffering similar symptoms?!
