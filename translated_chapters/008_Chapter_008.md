# Chapter 8: System (Pseudo) (第8章 系统（伪）)

Yes, a System.

Nowadays, the existence of a "System"—much like rebirth or transmigration—had virtually become standard-issue equipment for any protagonist.

Regrettably, Zhou Hui had never possessed such karmic fortune.

Though he had transmigrated and reborn several times, he had never once seen anything extra pop up in his sea of consciousness. Seen from that angle, it wasn't truly a regret; after all, he didn't even deign to use spirit stones to artificially boost his cultivation, let alone trust a System of unknown origin.

Besides, it was just a System. It wasn't as if Zhou Hui couldn't cobble one together himself.

In that other world, setting aside his accumulated background in aerospace engineering, back when he had taken part-time freelance gigs to pay for his studies, he had coded countless outsourced projects. Food delivery apps, short-video platforms, educational software, mobile games—he had worked on them all. After graduation, he had even been tempted by staggering salary offers and nearly jumped ship to a tech giant.

For a man like Zhou Hui, slapping together a [slacker system](# "Literal CN: salted fish system (咸鱼系统) — a system to assist in slacking off and taking it easy") to assist himself was child's play.

To be completely fair, Zhou Hui was well aware that his past behavior—such as deploying a mustard-seed spatial dimension to dilate time, building a dedicated question bank just to administer a baseline diagnostic test, rereading the entire library pavilion's collection from scratch just to draft textbooks, and holding a sect-wide Grand Unified Examination at the drop of a hat—even if it did nothing to advance his personal cultivation, hardly qualified as [slacking off](# "Literal CN: salted fish (咸鱼) — slang for lying flat and doing nothing") in the eyes of ordinary mortals.

Thus, building on the foundation of his *Retirement Schedule Plan*, Zhou Hui had programmed a System specifically to keep himself in check.

[Still, needing an auxiliary System just to supervise my daily schedule—my self-discipline really has deteriorated!]

[Looked at from that angle, I suppose this is the proper posture for retirement!]

The Untiring Immortal Venerable thought, thoroughly moved by his own logic.

Zhou Hui introduced it to his disciples with the proud air of an engineer unveiling a freshly developed automated control system for an extraterrestrial rover:

—"This is a supercomputer system I designed to assist in sect construction management and schedule the daily curriculum of our disciples... You don't follow? Then simply understand it as our sect's mountain-protecting grand formation. Not only can it guard and manage the sect's physical construction, but it will also issue quests to you on my behalf whenever necessary."-Zhou Hui explained.

[No, what kind of mountain-protecting grand formation hands out quests?!]

Ran Deng and Guan Ye inwardly cried out in horror.

Ignoring the subtle, twitching expressions on his two disciples' faces, Zhou Hui continued his demonstration:

—"Granted, this is only the alpha build. The learning module is still being compiled. In the future, I will distribute access to every disciple in the sect to track your exam scores, monitor study progress, compile rankings, and log everything in real time. It will be exceptionally convenient."-Zhou Hui said.

[Wait, you're planning to make those exams routine?!]

Both disciples froze on the spot, petrified.

Especially Ran Deng.

Although Ran Deng was the Heavenly Spirit Sect's Eldest Senior Sister, she was so lazy she barely seemed like Zhou Hui's disciple at all. Aside from sleeping, her daily routine consisted entirely of holing up in the Library Pavilion to read miscellany and pulp fiction. Her cultivation realm was passable, but her practical combat abilities lagged far behind her cultivation stage. Because of that, in the original script penned by the Heavenly Dao, even though Qu Qianxing had entrusted her with leading the junior disciples to safety during the sect's crisis, she had run far too slowly and ended up turned into cannon fodder by the Protagonist, reduced to ashes.

Presently, however, Ran Deng had zero sense of impending crisis.

The moment she heard words like "quests" and "exams," all that welled up inside her was a bottomless, crushing wave of exhaustion.

—"Master, are you... planning to renovate the entire sect? To carry out sweeping reforms?"-Ran Deng asked in a trembling voice, staring at the map displayed across the light screen. The Heavenly Spirit Sect's territory had never looked so terrifyingly vast.-"Why the sudden desire to do this?"

Zhou Hui patiently explained:

—"Didn't I mention it earlier? I no longer intend to ascend. I plan to properly enjoy my retirement—which is to say, mortal life. Naturally, I can't just make do and get by like before. I ought to renovate my immortal abode and improve my living environment."-Zhou Hui said.

—"...Starting with renovating the Palace of Silence and the Library Pavilion?"-Guan Ye asked, seeking hesitant confirmation.

—"Indeed, starting with the Library Pavilion,"-Zhou Hui said, tilting his head in mild puzzlement.-"Is there something amiss?"

[Wrong!]

[Everything is wrong about this!]

[In the mortal realm, a library pavilion is just a study, and the Palace of Silence, taking ten thousand steps back, is a classroom for schoolchildren!]

[When people talk about 'enjoying mortal life,' who on earth starts their top-priority home renovations with those two places?!]

Guan Ye roasted his master frantically in his heart. But perhaps because there were simply too many absurdities to roast at once, the words jammed in his throat, and not a single syllable managed to escape. He could only turn his gaze to his Eldest Senior Sister, hoping she would speak up on their behalf.

Ran Deng, however, opened her mouth and chirped:

—"The Library Pavilion is obviously the most important place—"-Ran Deng chimed in.

[Curses, I forgot. This woman is a bookworm with zero common sense who marinates in the Library Pavilion whenever she has free time.]

[She genuinely would think the Library Pavilion is the most important thing.]

Guan Ye was rendered entirely speechless.

However, for Ran Deng, her passion for reading was clearly no match for her burning desire to [slack off](# "Literal CN: catch fish (摸鱼) — slang for loafing around").

She was too lazy to even dust or organize the bookshelves in her own immortal cave; why would she ever willingly undertake the rebuilding of the entire Library Pavilion? What was worse, Master had coded a whole System specifically for this project! What if he dumped the renovation of the entire sect onto her shoulders down the line?!

Ran Deng offered a firm yet diplomatically tactful refusal:

—"Still, Master, I cannot imagine a library pavilion any better than the one you already have!"-Ran Deng said.

—"That is only because you have never seen better,"-Zhou Hui sighed.

He tapped across the glowing keyboard, bringing up [Building Mode], and under the [Primary Structures] category, selected the tab labeled [Library].

—"These are structures built by mortals. You may take them as reference,"-Zhou Hui explained.

Neither Ran Deng nor Guan Ye had any objections to referencing mortal architecture; in fact, it was standard practice across the cultivation world.

Because the cultivation world was sorely lacking in architects.

To be precise, professional architects simply did not exist among cultivators.

After all, everyone in the cultivation world sprinted headlong toward Ascension. Very few people bothered researching trivial chores like house construction. At most, cultivators might dabble in building techniques as an afterthought while studying array formations and feng shui.

Ran Deng, for instance, hailed from a rather prominent clan of array masters.

That was precisely why Ran Deng was so terrified Zhou Hui would toss the sect-wide renovation into her lap.

However, an array master's true vocation was ultimately arrays. Aside from a handful of naturally gifted exceptions, the structures designed by most array masters were functionally sound, but visually appalling under close scrutiny.

As a result, most cultivators preferred to purchase the bare defensive arrays alone, rarely ever buying pre-built dwellings from array masters. As for those ethereal jade pavilions, celestial terraces, and castles in the sky, more fastidious cultivators would patronize distinguished mortal architectural lineages, offering the prospect of cultivation guidance in exchange for bespoke estate designs. Cruder cultivators, meanwhile, would simply head into mortal cities, pack up whichever buildings caught their eye, and refine them with superior spiritual materials into portable pocket-mansion treasures. This had even spawned an entire industry, with similar magical artifacts sold at every cultivation bazaar.

Yet at this moment, when Ran Deng and Guan Ye caught a clear look at these so-called "references," both wore deeply strained, subtle expressions.

—"Master, where on earth did you find these references?"-Ran Deng asked, furrowing her brow.

—"Who sold them to you? How many spirit stones did they swindle from you?"-Guan Ye asked with profound concern.

It was hardly surprising the two disciples reacted that way.

Before their eyes, some of these "references" were brutally cubic, resembling cardboard boxes; others were rounded like eggs woven from silver bars; still others were twisted into pretzel-like corkscrews plated in gleaming, reflective "silver(?)"—each looking more bizarre and eccentric than the last.

Ran Deng and Guan Ye both seriously suspected their venerable Master had spent so many centuries detached from mundane affairs that he had been taken for a ride by mortal swindlers.

Zhou Hui merely smiled, offering no defense.

He hadn't expected disciples raised in the cultivation world to comprehend modern architectural art(?) in the first place. Truth be told, even after living through that world himself, he still couldn't comprehend it either.

—"Do not mind the exterior—that is precisely what I need you to improve upon. The focus lies within."-Zhou Hui said. Picking a "reference" at random, he tapped the keyboard.

In an instant, the surrounding air warped.

Thick, swirling mist engulfed Zhou Hui and his disciples. Within the vapor, colossal silhouettes flickered, stacking blocks together like toy bricks, and a towering edifice assembled itself in a heartbeat.

Within mere breaths, Zhou Hui and his disciples were already standing inside the building.

[An illusion?]

Ran Deng and Guan Ye thought, thoroughly unfazed.

Neither of them possessed particularly lofty cultivation realms, but as direct disciples of a Tribulation Transcendence Immortal Venerable, their horizons far exceeded those of ordinary cultivators. An illusion that could conjure a towering structure from flat ground was hardly novel to them; they knew that if their Master truly wished to, conjuring an entire city out of thin air in the blink of an eye would be trivial.

Yet the next moment, as the mist dissipated, both of their eyes widened in shock.

—"So many books!"-Ran Deng gasped, taking several swift steps toward the densely packed bookshelves.

—"...So many people,"-Guan Ye murmured, subconsciously edging closer to Zhou Hui's side.

Then the two of them began gazing around in sheer curiosity:

[—"Is the entire wall made of crystal? I thought only the demon clans of the ocean depths were so extravagant!"](# "Speaker: Ran Deng")

[—"Are so many books simply left out in the open for mortals to read at will? To think there exists anyone more wasteful than Master!"](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

[—"No, before that—can that many mortals even read?"](# "Speaker: Ran Deng")

[—"Even in Second Senior Brother's homeland, that grandest mortal imperial capital, there wouldn't be this many scholars, would there?"](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

Zhou Hui listened with a wry smile to his two disciples' discussion.

—"Naturally, anyone is permitted to read these books. They can even be borrowed and taken home,"-Zhou Hui said.-"Follow me."

Zhou Hui then guided his two disciples through the process of applying for a library card and checking out books.

—"This is magnificent!"-Ran Deng said, gazing enviously at the library's reading area.

This was a luxury the Library Pavilion had never possessed.

Given the sheer rarity and value of texts in the cultivation world, most cultivators treated books with an attitude focused entirely on "hoarding."

Even someone as "generous" as Zhou Hui had never considered carving out a communal reading area in the Library Pavilion for disciples to browse at leisure, to say nothing of optimizing reading comfort.

A spacious reading hall like this, bathed in bright, natural light, was the sanctuary Ran Deng had dreamed of her entire life.

She felt she could happily live here forever.

However, when Ran Deng eagerly pulled a volume of *Records of Mountains and Rivers* from the shelf, she discovered that only three or four pages actually contained text.

—"Master?"-Ran Deng looked at Zhou Hui, an expression of profound grievance on her face.

Zhou Hui shifted his gaze away with a guilty conscience:

—"Because it was a mortal book. I skimmed a few pages, deemed it useless, and stopped reading."-Zhou Hui admitted.

Ran Deng felt a deep pang of regret:

—"You should have flipped through every single book in this pavilion! With your divine sense, memorizing everything wouldn't take the slightest effort."-Ran Deng lamented.

—"When I traveled that mortal realm, there were far too many restrictions. I failed to seize the opportunity,"-Zhou Hui said, shaking his head.

To be precise, this library was reconstructed directly from Zhou Hui's memories.

In that "dream world," Zhou Hui had been a pure, unadulterated mortal. His stamina and time were limited. No matter how aggressively he [grinded](# "Literal CN: rolled / involuted (卷) — hyper-competitive studying or working"), it was impossible for him to read and memorize every book in an entire university library; naturally, he hadn't skimmed them all.

Zhou Hui himself felt quite a bit of regret over this. If he had browsed every volume back then, even if the "him in the dream" couldn't remember them, once he "awoke," he could have easily used spells to reproduce those hazy subconscious memories.

Take the book in Ran Deng's hands, for example. Back then, Zhou Hui had only casually flipped through it in the library. In the "dream," he naturally remembered nothing, yet after "waking," he had managed to excavate that exact sliver of memory.

Yet Zhou Hui's regret lasted only a fleeting moment before he brushed it aside:

—"Not memorizing them all matters little. The 'laws' of that mortal world do not apply to ours. [Blindly copying them](# "Literal CN: drawing a tiger using a cat as a model (照猫画虎) — imitating something poorly without understanding the essence") would only lead us astray."-Zhou Hui said.

—"Still, so many of these books look fascinating,"-Ran Deng muttered, unable to let it go.

She stealthily slid another book from the shelf: *After Faking His Death, the Aloof Master Ended Up with the Demon Venerable*. Judging purely by the title, it promised to be incredibly scandalous—er, thrilling! Alas, the pages inside were completely blank.

Evidently, her Master had merely glanced at the title before concluding the book was devoid of any academic merit, never sparing it a second thought or opening the cover.

Ran Deng felt thoroughly heartbroken.

Zhou Hui watched Ran Deng's crestfallen expression with a faint, knowing smirk:

—"I had originally hoped you would study the cataloging and classification system of this library to help me reorganize our Library Pavilion. But since you despise the task so vehemently, forget it. I can always assign it to another disciple—"-Zhou Hui said.

—"...No! Master!"-Ran Deng jumped straight up in a panic.

She was a chronic slacker, that was true. But if she could transform the interior of the Library Pavilion into such an ideal sanctuary, even Ran Deng was willing to put in genuine effort!

Not to mention, this "reference" contained so many fascinating books.

Although her Master only remembered a tiny fraction of them, that fraction alone was more than enough to hook Ran Deng's soul.

Clutching Zhou Hui's sleeve, Ran Deng pleaded desperately:

—"Master, please entrust the task of renovating the Library Pavilion to me! I feel that if I am separated from a place like this, I will wither and die!"-Ran Deng cried.

At that moment, Ran Deng had no inkling of the bottomless pit she was hurling herself into.

A bottomless pit named "Architecture."
