# Chapter 104: [Some Public Opinion](<# "Literal CN: 一些舆论 (yīxiē yúlùn) — Some Public Opinion; chapter title">) (第104章 一些舆论)

[Is Bei'er... truly the female lead?]

Zhou Hui wondered in bewilderment.

At the exact moment Mo Bei was enforcing tournament discipline, Zhou Hui had also been closely observing her situation in Fengqing City.

Consequently, when he witnessed blossoms of blood detonating across the Immortal Arts Assembly arena, he was somewhat taken aback.

For one, Zhou Hui realized that he truly did not understand his disciple well enough.

In his mind, Mo Bei had always been the most obedient, introverted child among all his disciples. He had never imagined her methods would be so... decisive.

As for the second reason, it was precisely the question that had crossed his mind:

Was it really appropriate for a "female lead" to do something like this?

Zhou Hui was not particularly well-versed in this genre of literature (?). However, having lived an entire lifetime in that other world, he had acquired some familiarity with its censorship mechanisms and popular aesthetic sensibilities. As far as he knew, works that could achieve widespread popularity over there generally had to possess [upright values](<# "Literal CN: 三观要正 (sānguān yào zhèng) — 'three views must be positive'; Chinese internet/censorship term for moral correctness in world view, values, and outlook">). This applied especially to the female lead: even if she was given an enchantress or villainess persona, she practically had to wear a chastity belt on her back at all times. As for slaughtering people, while seemingly less dire than losing one's chastity, she was still expected to undergo at least a brief inner struggle.

Yet Bei'er appeared to carry no psychological burden whatsoever.

On the other hand, the other one...

Zhou Hui lifted his eyes and looked across at the youth seated opposite him:

—"Argh, that idiot! Who told her to do that?!"-Mo Xi wailed, clutching his head as he stared at the blossoms of blood in the arena.-"Why did she have to strike so fast?! Wasn't the whole point of having her parade through the sky completely wasted?! The news that this girl is Master's direct disciple might not even have circulated properly yet! None of the various factions have had time to formulate countermeasures, or even a chance to react, and she's already started slaughtering people! This is going to offend everyone, damn it!"

—"[Cutting the Gordian knot with a swift blade](<# "Literal CN: 快刀斩乱麻 (kuài dāo zhǎn luàn má) — slicing tangled hemp with a sharp blade; solving a complex, messy problem decisively">) is a viable method in its own right,"-Zhou Hui said soothingly.-"It appears Bei'er excels at resolving problems using the simplest approach."

—"Isn't that a bit too simple?! She's going to make enemies out of a lot of people,"-Mo Xi protested.

He was not reproaching Mo Bei, but rather worrying on her behalf.

Present-day Fengqing City could be described as a murky swamp where [dragons and snakes mingled](<# "Literal CN: 鱼龙混杂 (yú lóng hùn zá) — fish and dragons mixed together; good and bad people tangled in a chaotic crowd">). There were undercover sleeper agents planted by the Palace of Carefree Wandering, demon factions from the Southern Domain, and branch offices of intricate escort agencies, cultivation sects, and commercial guilds—practically every walk of life was represented.

Compounding the issue, the City Lord of Fengqing City had long been nothing more than a puppet figurehead, completely incapable of exerting any meaningful control over them.

As a result, it was not that these factions were genuinely brazen enough to challenge the Untiring Immortal Venerable or sabotage the Immortal Arts Assembly. Rather, they had grown thoroughly accustomed to [fishing in murky waters](<# "Literal CN: 浑水摸鱼 (hún shuǐ mō yú) — taking advantage of confusion or chaos for personal gain">) and stirring up trouble whenever chaos presented an opportunity.

In their mindset, organizing illegal betting syndicates during the Immortal Arts Assembly, rigging matches with fake combat, and repeatedly testing the boundaries of the tournament's regulations did not even register as malicious behavior—it was simply [standard operating procedure](<# "Literal CN: 常规操作 (chángguī cāozuò) — routine operation / standard playbook">).

Thus, had Mo Xi been the one handling this affair—which had originally been his intention, given that he was the chief organizer of the Immortal Arts Assembly—merely invoking Master's name would have been sufficient to frighten off a fair share of troublemakers. From there, he could have methodically conducted an investigation into Fengqing City, winning over the obedient, ruthlessly suppressing the defiant, and ultimately consolidating full control of Fengqing City into his own hands.

In Mo Xi's view, Mo Bei's approach was far too radical.

With this single stroke, she had alienated practically every faction in the city. Those people would certainly not take this lying down; what if they sought retribution in the future? Did Mo Bei intend to never leave the Heavenly Spirit Sect for the rest of her life?

Furthermore, would slaying so many people cause Master to feel revulsion toward Mo Bei?

Master had never been fond of excessive bloodshed.

Beset by such worries, Mo Xi cautiously stole a glance at his master's expression. Only upon seeing that Zhou Hui showed no hint of displeasure did he quietly breathe a sigh of relief on Mo Bei's behalf.

Zhou Hui took no issue with Mo Bei's actions; in fact, there was even a trace of approval in his tone:
—"So what if she offended them? Our Heavenly Spirit Sect is more than capable of protecting her!"

As Zhou Hui said this, he failed to notice that his fiercely protective tone was identical to the description of Qu Qianxing's [family-shielding bias](<# "Literal CN: 护短 (hù duǎn) — fiercely shielding one's own people / unprincipled family protection">) recorded in the Heavenly Dao's script.

At that moment, an unfamiliar voice chimed in:

—"In that case, does Master not mind expanding publicity on this matter?"

Accompanied by these words, Ran Deng strolled into the room, followed by Guan Ye, whose face was bunched in a grim scowl.

—"Ah-Ran, Ye-zi, what brings you two here as well?"-Zhou Hui asked with a warm smile.

—"We've been keeping a close eye on the Immortal Arts Assembly all along!"-Ran Deng said, rolling her eyes playfully.-"We just heard someone say Little Junior Sister won her match. We originally came to celebrate, but we certainly didn't expect this kind of 'victory' method."

—"As expected, does Senior Sister also think she was too rash?"-Mo Xi asked, his brow furrowing.

—"Not at all. Personally, I think those bastards had it coming... *ahem*,"-Ran Deng began, before catching sight of Guan Ye's increasingly blackened complexion and coughing twice.-"Didn't Master just say it? [Cutting the knot with a swift blade](<# "Literal CN: 快刀斩乱麻 (kuài dāo zhǎn luàn má) — cutting tangled hemp with a sharp blade; decisive action">)! The Immortal Arts Assembly has already begun; we have no time to slowly untangle Fengqing City's internal politics. Isn't Bei'er's approach ideal? Not to mention, that city has a City Lord who has ruled for thousands of years; he won't be brought to heel so easily. Even if we managed to subdue the underworld factions, as long as he remains in place, subsequent maintenance costs would be exorbitant. It's far better to [enjoy the thrill and dip](<# "Literal CN: 爽完就跑 (shuǎng wán jiù pǎo) — modern slang: get one's kicks and run off / smash and dash without cleaning up the mess">)!"

Mo Xi's eyelids twitched. What Ran Deng had just refuted was precisely his original plan, though her reasoning was undeniably sound.

Mo Xi let out a cold snort:
—"Eldest Senior Sister is truly peerless at [armchair theorizing](<# "Literal CN: 纸上谈兵 (zhǐ shàng tán bīng) — discussing military tactics on paper; theoretical talk without practical execution">)."

[No shit. If I'm not doing armchair theorizing, am I supposed to actually go do it?]

[Whether it's slaughtering everyone in sight in one glorious spree or wheeling and dealing with underworld syndicates to pull strings from the shadows—neither of those is something a normal human being would ever do!]

[These twins truly are a pair of ghost dolls. They're wicked right down to their marrow.]

Ran Deng complained bitterly in her heart.

She was merely an engineering draftsman! There was no way she would ever take part in such terrifying, [dog-eat-dog underworld warfare](<# "Literal CN: 黑吃黑 (hēi chī hēi) — underworld crooks devouring other crooks">)!

In the end, it was Master who spoke up to dispel Mo Xi's lingering resentment:
—"Every individual has their own unique strengths. Everyone should simply use the problem-solving methodology they excel at. What Xi'er can achieve, Bei'er might not necessarily be able to accomplish."

[The unstated corollary being: 'What Bei'er can achieve, Xi'er might not necessarily be able to accomplish either,' right?]

Ran Deng mused.

She watched her brilliant (and thoroughly sinister) youngest junior brother pretend to be oblivious to the unspoken implication, putting on a silly, docile grin as if he had just received the highest praise from Master—as if Master would actually fall for that act.

Sure enough, Master paid no further attention to his youngest disciple's feigned kitty smile, turning his gaze toward Ran Deng instead:
—"Ah-Ran, what did you mean earlier by expanding publicity?"

—"I don't believe Little Junior Sister did anything wrong, but Little Junior Brother's concerns aren't unfounded either,"-Ran Deng said.-"Even if they don't dare offend our Heavenly Spirit Sect to our faces, having them spread malicious rumors behind Little Junior Sister's back would be quite troublesome. Therefore, we ought to [strike first to seize the initiative](<# "Literal CN: 先下手为强 / 先下手为快 (xiān xiàshǒu wéi kuài) — acting first to preempt the opponent">)."

[—"You mean?"](<# "Speaker: Zhou Hui">)

—"Master, I happen to have founded a newspaper capable of guiding public opinion,"-Ran Deng said, producing a folded publication.

Although she had not reported this project to Zhou Hui in advance, she had never intended to keep it hidden forever.

Mo Bei's current predicament happened to present the perfect opportunity for Ran Deng to formally register her newspaper with Master.

Naturally, the [proof copy](<# "Literal CN: 样刊 (yàngkān) — sample issue / advance proof copy printed before mass distribution">) Ran Deng presented was merely a preliminary edition. The scandalous columns she cherished the most had been tactfully omitted for the time being.

Unaware of Ran Deng's little scheme, Zhou Hui's eyes lit up upon beholding the newspaper.

—"Ah-Ran founded this? How truly rare."

[Why does everyone say that?! Is it really that miraculous when I occasionally want to do some work?!]

Ran Deng grumbled inwardly, forcing a bashful expression onto her face:
—"I merely wished to contribute something to the sect as well!"

Her acting skills fell far short of Mo Xi's, leaving her expression noticeably stiff.

Fortunately, Zhou Hui was not one to scrutinize such details:
—"I will have Qianxing approve a budget for you later."

—"NO!"-Ran Deng shrieked in terror.

Zhou Hui paused:

[—"?"](<# "Speaker: Zhou Hui">)

—"What I mean is, there's no need to trouble Second Senior Brother,"-Ran Deng scrambled to explain. The last thing she wanted was Qu Qianxing auditing her newspaper. Smoothly dusting off the excuse she had previously used to fob off Guan Ye, she added:—"This newspaper is entirely funded by the profits of A Cup of Sorrow. Master originally gave Little Junior Sister and me that allowance, but we really don't have many places to spend money anyway. The surplus happened to be just enough to launch this publication."

—"Ah-Ran, you are truly thoughtful,"-Zhou Hui said, genuinely moved.

He was well aware of how lazy this child could be.

For instance, while the milk tea shop had been his gift of "pocket money" to Ran Deng and Mo Bei, after contributing a little seed capital at the very beginning, Ran Deng had dumped all operational duties onto Mo Bei and Mo Bei's subordinate, Yu Feifei, leaving the milk tea shop looking like Mo Bei's solo enterprise.

Yet this time, even Zhou Hui had hardly considered the ramifications of public opinion. This was largely because the Untiring Immortal Venerable had never cared about external gossip, and thus had not contemplated what to do should outsiders slander Mo Bei. Fortunately, Ran Deng had anticipated it.

Not only had she anticipated it, but she had gone to such lengths for her junior sister!

Filled with such sentimental warmth, Zhou Hui had little desire to nitpick the rest of the layout. In truth, Zhou Hui was never inclined to find fault in the first place; he was simply delighted to see his disciples taking initiative:
—"In that case, I shall leave Bei'er's matter in your capable hands."

—"Understood, Master!"-Ran Deng chirped cheerfully.

With Master's explicit blessing, the supreme editorial authority over this newspaper was set in stone—no one could raise an objection now.

Even if Qu Qianxing saw the newspaper later and took offense, she could brazenly push back with Master's backing!

Amidst her delight, Ran Deng could not help feeling a pang of poetic irony:

Although it had started as an excuse to keep Qu Qianxing from sticking his nose in, *White Jade Capital Weekly* was indeed bankrolled by A Cup of Sorrow, and even its chief editor, Yu Feifei, had been poached directly from the milk tea shop. Having its premiere issue resolve Mo Bei's crisis felt like an exquisitely closed loop of karmic cause and effect.

—"However, if we follow my strategy, her current burden will become even heavier..."-Ran Deng remarked at last, turning her gaze toward the blood-soaked arena of Fengqing City.-"Should she happen to fail, the public backlash will be devastatingly severe."

—"Have faith in your little junior sister,"-Zhou Hui replied with absolute confidence in his disciple's prowess.-"She is more than capable of achieving it."

*

With Zhou Hui's authorization, *White Jade Capital Weekly* was rapidly rolled out across the realms.

Moreover, the entertainment value of this newspaper was leagues ahead of the academic journal *Arts & Sorcery*. The moment it hit the stands, it was met with rapturous acclaim. Even though its print run was dozens of times larger than that of *Arts & Sorcery*, demand still far outstripped supply.

After all, those currently studying *Arts & Sorcery* were predominantly high-level tournament contestants, whereas *White Jade Capital Weekly* was immensely entertaining simply for its gossip. In virtually no time, nearly anyone who was literate held a copy in their hands, with even ordinary mortals reading it with voracious enthusiasm.

This held true even in Fengqing City, the very epicenter of the event.

Originally, by no means did everyone understand the causes and consequences behind the incident. Given the prior chaotic climate of the Immortal Arts Assembly in Fengqing City, no respectable person would have ever considered going near that spectacle.

A perfectly respectable grand assembly had been degraded in Fengqing City into something resembling [underground illegal pit fighting](<# "Literal CN: 黑市打拳 (hēishì dǎquán) — black-market underground bare-knuckle fighting / cage matches">).

Particularly in recent days, rumors had circulated that a female demon had embarked on an indiscriminate slaughter in the arena, spilling so much blood that it was supposedly pouring out of the stadium. (In reality, given the bowl-shaped fortress architecture, achieving such a feat would require drowning the entire arena in blood, which was rather impractical.) As a consequence, people avoided the Immortal Arts Assembly like the plague. Fainthearted mortals scarcely dared step out their front doors, while certain self-proclaimed "righteous figures" were itching for action, plotting to subdue the demonic maiden.

It was precisely at this juncture that *White Jade Capital Weekly* suddenly blanketed the entire city.

[—"I told you she couldn't be some demonic fiend! How could those awe-inspiring celestial soldiers and generals belong to a demon? She's a bona fide direct disciple of White Jade Capital!"](<# "Speaker: Street Citizen">)

[—"Exactly! Those scoundrels turned the Immortal Arts Assembly into an illegal gambling racket—did they really think a Tribulation Transcendence titan was a toothless tiger? Look, he dispatched his direct disciple to clean house!"](<# "Speaker: Street Citizen">)

[—"No wonder those celestial soldiers suddenly raided the black market street! Splendidly done!"](<# "Speaker: Street Citizen">)

[—"As expected of the Untiring Immortal Venerable's disciple! Only at Foundation Establishment, yet she beat everyone until they were [pissing and shitting themselves in terror](<# "Literal CN: 屁滚尿流 (pì gǔn niào liú) — rolling with farts and flowing with urine; fleeing in sheer panicked terror">)!"](<# "Speaker: Street Citizen">)

[—"Magical treasures? You talk as if you wouldn't use a magical treasure bestowed by your own master! She is the direct disciple of a Tribulation Transcendence grandmaster—do you expect her to fight you barehanded without using treasures?! Besides, she is still offering everyone a fair opportunity!"](<# "Speaker: Street Citizen">)

[—"This Fairy Mo Bei is so adorably pretty, one look is enough to tell she's a good person."](<# "Speaker: Street Citizen">)

[—"Still, challenging everyone in the city like that... isn't she being a bit too arrogant?"](<# "Speaker: Street Citizen">)

...

In an instant, the streets and alleyways were abuzz with discussion over this matter.

Truth be told, this world was remarkably "quaint." At least when it came to information dissemination, people were far more inclined to place their faith in written words than in distorted word-of-mouth rumors.

—This was also because, within the cultivation world, language and written script were fundamentally forms of magical arts. When even an offhand curse could materialize into reality, anything committed to ink and paper carried the weight of a signed and sealed covenant. Its public credibility naturally far surpassed hearsay.

Furthermore, while certain distorted rumors had circulated previously in Fengqing City, they were mostly the muttered curses of disgruntled malcontents that had not yet gathered critical mass. Most residents in the city had personally witnessed the celestial soldiers and generals patrolling the sky; cross-referencing that spectacle with the newspaper's accounts, combined with the fact that those petty rumors crumbled under the slightest scrutiny, made it far easier for people to believe what they had seen with their own eyes.

Consequently, Fengqing City regained its former bustling vitality.

In fact, it surpassed what came before.

Previously, only well-informed, ambitious cultivators had harbored interest in the Immortal Arts Assembly. With the wide circulation of the newspaper, merchants and ordinary mortals became intrigued as well.

Even if they did not immediately head to the tournament grounds to spectate, they would enthusiastically discuss the latest developments with their neighbors.

Amidst this fervent atmosphere, no one noticed a passerby dressed as a servant purchasing several copies of the newspaper. Clasping them furtively against his chest as though terrified of being spotted, he scurried back to the City Lord's Estate.

—"Lord City Lord, the newspapers you requested have been purchased."

After making his report, the "passerby" delivered a copy before Feng Lang, the City Lord of Fengqing City.

Beholding that newspaper, Feng Lang's face twisted into an expression akin to a throbbing toothache:
—"Nobody saw you, did they?!"

—"Have no fear, this servant handled it with utmost discretion,"-the servant replied, before adding quizzically:—"However, why is My Lord so anxious that no one finds out? Is there something amiss with this paper?"

—"What the hell do you know?!"-Feng Lang cursed under his breath.

If word got out that he had purchased this paper, wouldn't that be tantamount to letting everyone know that the City Lord was aware of the Untiring Immortal Venerable's direct disciple arriving in Fengqing City? Under such circumstances, was he, as City Lord, supposed to host a reception for her or not?

As long as he remained "unaware," he could continue to [steal the bell while covering his ears](<# "Literal CN: 掩耳盗铃 (yǎn'ěr dàolíng) — plugging one's ears to steal a bell; deceiving oneself; burying one's head in the sand">).

Indeed, throughout the entire incident, the City Lord of Fengqing City had never shown his face. He had been [playing dead](<# "Literal CN: 装死 (zhuāng sǐ) — feigning death / turtling up to evade responsibility">).

To be precise, Feng Lang hiding inside the City Lord's Estate and refusing to step outside had not begun when Mo Bei arrived; it had started right after the Tianling Auction.

He had had no alternative. First, he had watched with his own eyes as Daoist Huang of the Thousand Harmony Gang and his cohorts dissolved into puddles of melted water right before him. Next, he had watched several of the city's prominent cultivation clans get blasted into ash by heavenly lightning. And then, he had heard that even the Palace of Carefree Wandering had adopted a retreat strategy. As a City Lord who had historically leaned toward Carefree Palace, he had naturally spent every waking hour in trembling terror, terrified that the Untiring Immortal Venerable would personally pay a house call to liquidate him.

Yet defecting to the Heavenly Spirit Sect was equally out of the question.

Leaving aside whether the Untiring Immortal Venerable would even be willing to take him in, Fengqing City's entanglements with Carefree Palace ran far too deep to easily sever. Furthermore, with Daoist Huang's gruesome fate serving as a cautionary tale, Feng Lang had no desire to melt into a puddle of bloody water.

Consequently, ever since the Heavenly Spirit Sect auction, he had maintained an erratic state of "secluded cultivation," minimizing his excursions as much as humanly possible.

In any event, this teleportation array had been guarded by his Feng family for generations. Even the Untiring Immortal Venerable would not risk ruining the Central Domain's final cross-domain teleportation array just to forcefully depose him as City Lord!

Of course, playing dead was one thing, but he could not truly ignore outside affairs—especially since Feng Lang held a stake in several lucrative businesses within Fengqing City.

In particular, the destruction of the gambling parlors by the "celestial soldiers" Mo Bei brought along had infuriated Feng Lang so severely that he had nearly lost his mind and rushed out to stake his life against Mo Bei.

Yet upon considering the death toll in the arena, followed by the sight of those brawny, formidable "celestial soldiers," Feng Lang had promptly sat back down.

[Damnable!]

[Why did that bi—that young lady have to strike so fast?!]

[If she had simply revealed her identity as the Untiring Immortal Venerable's direct disciple, we naturally would have reined in anything that displeased the Immortal Venerable! Why did she have to make things so dreadfully ugly?!]

For a time, Feng Lang genuinely suspected that the Untiring Immortal Venerable had deliberately sent his direct disciple to usurp Fengqing City.

Before he could formulate a counterstrategy, however, he heard that a publication called *White Jade Capital Weekly* was circulating, containing news regarding the Untiring Immortal Venerable's direct disciple. He had immediately dispatched a servant to covertly secure a copy.

Having finished reading the contents of the newspaper, he finally let out a long breath of relief and burst into raucous laughter:

—"Hahaha! It seems the Heavenly Spirit Sect truly only came to build a grand reputation for their own direct disciple!"-His tone dripped with mockery.-"However, if she thinks she can step on Fengqing City to make a name for herself, she had better see if she has the ability!"

—"My Lord?"-the servant asked blankly.-"Hasn't she already achieved quite a reputation by now?"

—"If she dies here, she'll become even more famous!"-Feng Lang declared in a chilling voice.-"Originally, had they not publicized it, some cowards might have hesitated to act out of deference to her status as the Untiring Immortal Venerable's disciple. But now that the Heavenly Spirit Sect has publicly announced her identity and broadcast her declarations at the Immortal Arts Assembly, the number of people itching to make a name for themselves by defeating her will skyrocket! Add to that those seeking vengeance and opportunists looking to fish in murky waters—in the coming days, the line of challengers stepping up to duel her will be endless!"

—"What if... she wins every single match?"

—"Hahaha! How could that be possible?! She is merely at Foundation Establishment! No matter how formidable that Immortal Venerable is, no matter how extraordinary the magical treasure he bestowed upon her, it cannot possibly be completely invincible! Otherwise, wouldn't the direct disciples of all major powerhouses reign supreme across the heavens?!"-Feng Lang laughed uproariously once more.

—"Then, My Lord, should we..."

—"We do nothing!"-Feng Lang cut in decisively.-"With the situation having reached this point, all we need to do is sit back and watch!"

In any case, so long as he refrained from making a move, neither side would have any justification to cause him trouble.

Hearing this, the servant cast a deeply meaningful gaze upon his master:

[Lord City Lord, you truly do [follow your heart](<# "Literal CN: 从心 (cóngxīn) — 'following one's heart'; homophonic pun for 怂 (sǒng), meaning a complete coward / gutless wimp">)!]

*

There are certain things where, if you believe they will happen, they will happen.

Evidently, Mo Bei's victory was one of them.

Although, precisely as the City Lord of Fengqing City had predicted, the number of challengers seeking out Mo Bei surged exponentially following the newspaper's publication, Mo Bei held the platform firmly, defeating every challenger who stepped forward.

Meanwhile, even as challengers multiplied, the proportion of "normal people" in the spectator stands steadily increased.

These "normal people" comprised cultivators who intended to compete legitimately through standard procedures, ordinary folk who simply wished to enjoy a fair match, and merchants conducting business through honest channels.

While the initial batch may have arrived merely to gawk after reading the newspaper, people swiftly realized that alongside Mo Bei's declaration of war against the "rule-breakers," the "celestial soldiers and generals" had assumed full control over the perimeter surrounding the arena. Even mortal women and children could walk through this area without the slightest concern for their personal safety. Furthermore, the immortals' duels were undeniably captivating to watch—even if other rings proved underwhelming, watching Mo Bei's sword dance alone was more than worth the price of admission, even if it was [a bit unsuitable for minors](<# "Literal CN: 少儿不宜 (shào'ér bù yí) — not suitable for children / rated 18+ due to graphic violence">).

Consequently, ever more people flocked to the Immortal Arts Assembly grounds, whether to spectate or to participate.

Merchants took keen notice of the commercial opportunities here, discovering that order in this zone was far superior to that of Fengqing City's regular markets, and the [protection fees (er, taxes)](<# "Literal CN: 保护费（X）抽税 (bǎohùfèi [X] chōushuì) — tongue-in-cheek crossed-out correction: protection racket money corrected to official taxation">) were noticeably lower. As a result, honest traders doing business here multiplied rapidly.

The only matter that caused them lingering unease was whether everything would instantly revert to the old chaos if Fairy Mo Bei happened to lose a match.

People began voluntarily joining Mo Bei's fan support squad, cheering her on at the top of their lungs.

As if in direct response to their hopes, Mo Bei never lost.

The first day, the second day, the third day... the fifteenth day.

After half a month, virtually no one doubted whether Mo Bei could be defeated anymore. Mo Bei's arena ring had overwhelmingly transformed into her absolute home court, packed wall-to-wall with fervent supporters cheering her name.

On this day, before the matches officially commenced, deafening cheers erupted from the stands:

[—"Mo Bei, keep fighting~~!"](<# "Speaker: Cheering Spectators">)

Truth be told, under such an overwhelming atmosphere, stepping onto the arena to challenge Mo Bei required no small amount of courage.

Mingling amidst the crowd, Shui Huang'er screamed along with the spectators, waving the magical firework sticks in her hands in rhythmic cadence, sending brilliant, dazzling sparks blazing into a luminous sea of light.

—"You're having quite the grand time, aren't you?"-Elder Tong Ling remarked in a deeply disgruntled tone.

—"What, do you still suspect Sister Mo Bei of being a yin spirit?"-Shui Huang'er asked dismissively.

—"She IS a yin spirit!"

—"Then she is a good yin spirit! You shouldn't harbor racial prejudice, Elder,"-Shui Huang'er chided.

[—"..."](<# "Speaker: Elder Tong Ling">)

Elder Tong Ling felt an intense urge to curse aloud, but in the end, he held his tongue, merely letting out a long sigh:
—"It is simply that this yin spirit is far too bizarre. Having slain so many people, how is it that she harbors not the slightest trace of ferocious malice? Not only is there no ferocious malice, but she doesn't even carry a shred of resentment. It is truly too strange,"-he reiterated.

This had been the precise reason the Elder had coldly observed from the sidelines over the past several days.

Under normal circumstances, if a yin spirit slaughtered living beings, its ferocious malice and karmic resentment would inevitably multiply. These negative energies would further erode its already meager sanity, ultimately causing it to degenerate into a bloodthirsty evil fiend—an entity that demanded extermination even more urgently than demonkin.

Yet Mo Bei was not like that at all. When engaged in combat, she experienced virtually zero emotional fluctuations, and thus nothing arose that could corrupt her reason.

—"Possessing an Extreme Yin soul, yet preserving her absolute sanity under such circumstances—doesn't that precisely prove she is stronger than anyone else?"-Shui Huang'er said, visibly enraptured.

Fixing her gaze upon Mo Bei once more with eyes burning with teal flame, she beheld that across these days of ceaseless combat, a tiny speck of golden brilliance was steadily condensing atop that pitch-dark soul.

That was a **[Sword Heart](<# "Literal CN: 剑心 (jiànxīn) — Sword Heart; the pure, indestructible crystallization of martial intent and swordsmanship condensed through combat">)**, coalesced from martial killing intent.

This was the very first time Shui Huang'er had ever witnessed a sword cultivator condense such an essence. She felt an urge to shriek in excitement once more:
—"Sister Mo Bei truly is the greatest!"

[...If you ask me, it's you spectators who have lost your minds on that yin spirit's behalf!] the Elder thought sourly.

Even so, he could not resist watching Mo Bei with growing intrigue. As a fellow spiritual entity, he could not help feeling his blood surge whenever this young girl fought, involuntarily longing to see just how far this yin spirit girl could go.

*

[One hundred and eighth...]

Mo Bei counted silently in her heart, disarming her opponent with a casual flick of her blade.

Her adversary this time was a handsome young man. His face pale, he glanced in the direction his flying sword had landed before clasping his hands in a respectful salute:
—"My skill is inferior; I willingly concede defeat."

Mo Bei gazed at him impassively, while a chorus of boos erupted from the spectator stands.

However, Mo Bei could sense that no one in the crowd wished for her to kill him, so she spared his life.

Over the past few days of matches, such "not-to-be-killed" fellows seemed to have grown ever more frequent. While she was largely indifferent, this assignment had presumably reached its conclusion, hadn't it?

Mo Bei mused internally.

She had never held any real criteria for judging combatants. Yet during this excursion, guided by the reactions of those around her, she felt as though she had begun to grasp the distinction between those who "ought to be killed" and those who "ought not to be killed."

More importantly, however, listening to the roaring voices cheering her on from the stands, Mo Bei felt as though her body—perpetually freezing for as long as she could remember—was growing ever so slightly warm.

Something within her seemed to be taking shape.

Nevertheless, seeing today's final challenger depart and no new challenger step forward, Mo Bei stepped off the platform without lingering in the slightest.

Several Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples who had accompanied her to Fengqing City immediately bustled over, bearing spiritual tea and fresh towels:

[—"As expected of Senior Sister Mo Bei, you are truly magnificent!"](<# "Speaker: Accompanying Disciples">)

[—"Nobody in Fengqing City can defeat you now!"](<# "Speaker: Accompanying Disciples">)

[—"Soon, no one will even dare challenge you!"](<# "Speaker: Accompanying Disciples">)

Listening to their praise, Mo Bei voiced the question lingering in her mind:
—"Do you believe my mission this time is complete?"

—"Of course! If this doesn't count as complete, how could anyone possibly do any better?"-one of them replied.

—"Very good,"-Mo Bei said, nodding with a small sigh of relief. Then, reaching into her storage pouch, she retrieved a stack of items and handed them out to everyone:—"Since this task has drawn to a close, we may proceed with the next one: your [midterm exams](<# "Literal CN: 期中考试 (qīzhōng kǎoshì) — midterm examination; Zhou Hui's standardized academic testing system striking even on external missions">)."

[—"M-Midterm exams?!"](<# "Speaker: Accompanying Disciples">)

The beaming smiles on the disciples' faces instantly froze solid.

Mo Bei looked at them with genuine bewilderment:
—"Naturally. You didn't truly believe that coming out on an assignment with me meant you wouldn't have to take exams, did you?"
