# Chapter 229: I Want to Sit at the Celestial Emperor's Table

—"Achoo..."

Yuwen Xuan sneezed, and instinctively gave his fleece a vigorous shake.

Only halfway through the motion did he suddenly realize that he was currently in human form rather than a lamb—strike that, a Taotie. He hastily coughed twice and cast a nervous glance around.

Fortunately, there was no one nearby.

Or rather, this vicinity looked like a place where no "humans" could possibly exist.

At this moment, Yuwen Xuan was perched far above a boundless sea of clouds. Above and below him stretched fish-scale cloud layers extending beyond the horizon. Yet a colossal entity—unclear whether a mountain peak or a gargantuan tree—pierced between the two cloud seas, and Yuwen Xuan was perched like an [argali](# "Literal CN: 盘羊 (panyang) - wild bighorn sheep") upon a nearly vertical cliff face.

Still, singular though this place was, however one looked at it, it certainly did not appear to be any road leading toward the Demon Realm, let alone the God-Demon battlefield.

—"I did tell Master I was going to find Second Brother, but I never said when,"-Yuwen Xuan muttered to himself.

—"He's a grown man; surely he can manage to stay alive a bit longer?"

—"As long as I fish him out before he dies, that's plenty!"

If anyone were to ask Yuwen Xuan, that was unquestionably how he would answer—before being promptly strung up from the peach tree by Master.

Alas, Yuwen Xuan had already been set loose; right now, there was no one around to keep him in line.

And so Yuwen Xuan harbored not the slightest intention of rushing to the front lines of the God-Demon battlefield to seek out his unlucky Second Senior Brother trapped amidst the carnage. Instead, following the Taotie's ancestral memories, he had rummaged here and pawed there, burrowing indiscriminately through fissures between worlds, until somehow or other, he wound up in this very place.

Though it sounded casual enough, this was in truth an exceptionally hazardous realm.

Its spiritual energy was outrageously abundant—so abundant that spiritual qi could no longer exist in the form of "gas," but had instead condensed into liquid.

Indeed, the two "seas of clouds" above Yuwen Xuan's head and beneath his feet were no ordinary vaporous mist, but the physical condensation of pure spiritual qi.

As for spiritual energy rich to such an extreme, within the sphere influenced by the "Genesis Machine," it virtually did not exist. It belonged to the more primordial, pristine regions of this universe. If Zhou Hui could set foot here, he would undoubtedly be transported with joy.

Regrettably, Zhou Hui could not reach this place.

Because excessive spiritual matter exerted mutual attraction and violent collision. The more powerful a spiritual entity, the less capable it was of surviving here; it would dissolve into this sea of spiritual energy in an instant. Naturally, mortals and ordinary beasts devoid of spiritual energy fared no better—before they could even draw near the sea of spiritual energy, they would be pulverized into meat paste by the sheer spiritual pressure.

The only beings capable of traversing the sea of spiritual energy were entities classified as divine beasts and ferocious beasts—such as True Dragons, Primordial Phoenixes, and...

Taotie.

Though they, too, inhaled and exhaled colossal volumes of spiritual qi, their physical vessels were unique. They could adapt to the environment of the spiritual sea, balancing the spiritual energy within and without their flesh, thereby surviving in this domain.

Yuwen Xuan had learned of this place from his "inherited memories" after devouring his true body.

Yet as a mere "cub," Yuwen Xuan did not dare venture any deeper into the spiritual sea. Deep within its depths lurked visibly far more terrifying behemoths—to say nothing of the rest, even bumping into a sub-adult Taotie would be more than he could chew. In truth, even from this distance, he could faintly detect dreadful, looming auras drifting from afar.

Consequently, Yuwen Xuan ascended with utmost caution along the perimeter of the spatial rift. After all, while he possessed an inveterate love for courting disaster, he had no wish to commit suicide. He still wanted to live tens of thousands of years more to act spoiled and whine at Master!

Fortunately, such regions were few and far between. That colossal existence—neither mountain nor tree—had merely happened to extend into the space beyond the machine's sphere of control. Furthermore, the portion leading into the machine's dominion had already been sheared away by someone, leaving behind an isolated cliff face adrift in the sea of spiritual energy. Relying on fierce beast instinct, Yuwen Xuan navigated the spiritual sea along the cliff, reached a fissure on the opposite end of the rock face, and squeezed his way through.

Thanks to his status as a cub, he managed it; had he been fully grown, he wouldn't even have fit through the crack.

Once through the fissure, the landscape before him opened up in breathtaking grandeur. Poking his head out and peering downward, Yuwen Xuan beheld gardens where jewels blossomed from the soil, pavilions forged of gold and jade, and a magnificent, towering sacrificial altar. The scenery was so resplendent it elicited involuntary sighs of wonder.

Yet to a Taotie, any sublime vista was merely [playing a zither to a sheep](# "Literal CN: 对羊弹琴 - a pun on 'playing a zither to an ox' (对牛弹琴), meaning wasting fine things on someone incapable of appreciating them").

What captivated a Taotie was, of course, solely gourmet food.

And amidst such magnificent scenery, delicacies capable of seducing a Taotie naturally abounded—in fact, for this place to have left an imprint within the Taotie's ancestral memories, there was evidently something here that had once allowed a Taotie to gorge itself to its heart's content.

Yuwen Xuan twitched his nostrils and discovered that the tantalizing aroma emanated from two distinct sources:

One came from the sacrificial altar. The sky-soaring fragrance almost caused the Taotie to lose all reason, tempting him to plunge down heedlessly and devour everything in sight. But the human portion of Yuwen Xuan reined in the impulse. Aided by the formidable endurance forged over centuries at Master's side, Yuwen Xuan preserved a sliver of rationality amidst the aroma's seduction to scrutinize the altar.

A solemn and grand ceremony was clearly underway atop the altar.

The melodious chimes of bronze bells carried across vast distances. Figures arrayed in lavish ceremonial robes bowed in disciplined cadence with the rhythm. The scent wafting from their flesh was virtually identical to the True Immortal Yuwen Xuan had consumed last time—yet compared to the fragrance billowing from the figure leading the procession, their scent amounted to nothing at all, akin to the difference between modest appetizers and the grand centerpiece course of a [Manchu-Han Imperial Feast](# "Literal CN: 满汉全席 (Manhan Quanxi) - legendary grand banquet of imperial Chinese cuisine").

However, even setting aside Master's ironclad baseline of "no eating people," Yuwen Xuan's human experience warned him that the sacrificial rite below was anything but ordinary. The "person" presiding over the ceremony was no benevolent soul either; the more fragrant they smelled, the more perilous they were to provoke. Forget a Taotie cub—even if a pack of prime adult Taotie charged down, they would likely find no easy victory.

To reiterate: Yuwen Xuan merely enjoyed courting death; he did not actually wish to die.

Having recognized that he could not devour the things—strike that, people—strike that, immortals below, he could only cast several reluctant, longing glances at the deities upon the altar, wipe a trail of saliva from the corner of his lips, and direct his gaze toward the other source stimulating his appetite.

The second source was far more normal.

In a garden not far from the altar, surrounding a mist-veiled cloud pool, several white jade low tables were arranged in precise order. A meticulous count would reveal that the number of tables corresponded exactly to the official participants in the ceremony, their placement strictly governed by rank. At present, no one was seated behind them; only handsome youths and fair maidens bustled between the tables, steadily arranging exquisite, steaming delicacies and celestial wines upon the jade surfaces.

Evidently, following that solemn sacrificial rite, a sumptuous banquet would unfold.

—"Hehe, my luck really isn't bad this time. Just taking a casual stroll, and I stumble upon a banquet. If I don't attend, wouldn't I be wasting an opportunity delivered by cosmic fortune?"-Yuwen Xuan murmured, licking his lips as he surveyed the banquet grounds.

He wasn't merely putting on airs; it truly was pure coincidence.

He didn't even know who was conducting what ritual atop that altar, and naturally had no idea a ceremony was scheduled for this hour. He hadn't even ventured here for food, but for entirely different business.

Yet whether through extraordinary luck or dreadful misfortune, someone had arrived before him and was actively hosting an event.

—"Just don't know who they are,"-Yuwen Xuan muttered, yet he didn't hesitate in the slightest.

Seeing that the ceremony atop the altar was nearing its conclusion and figures were beginning to file out in orderly sequence, he summoned a bank of cloud mist and soared downward.

Yet after flying only a short distance, Yuwen Xuan's keen senses detected a faint aura of a divine beast cloaking the entire mountain peak. It appeared intended merely as a deterrent rather than an aggressive barrier, but plunging in blindly would without question alert the guardian.

In the Taotie's memories of foraging here, there had been a "tough nut to crack" to contend with; in all likelihood, it was the master of this very aura.

—"Tch, useless,"-Yuwen Xuan scoffed at his original beast self without mercy.

Yet he did not recklessly dive down to antagonize the divine beast radiating that aura. Instead, bringing forth the survival skills honed to perfection when dealing with his own Master, he restrained every scrap of his aura and touched down silently upon the mountainside.

Ascending a few paces from this point, one could see a colossal paifang archway, inscribed with two grand characters:

> **Xuanpu**

In truth, Yuwen Xuan couldn't care less what this place was called; all that occupied his mind was how to slip inside.

And perhaps cosmic fortune truly smiled upon Yuwen Xuan today: just as he touched down at the mountain's base, a flock of "sheep" happened to amble leisurely through a side gate into the interior.

These "sheep" each bore four horns atop their heads, looking distinctly unlike ordinary mountain goats. Yet if he transformed into a hornless lamb and blended among them, he wouldn't look particularly out of place.

Yuwen Xuan realized this immediately. Halting in his tracks, he fixed the flock with several contemplative, predatory stares.

Under Yuwen Xuan's gaze, the sheep's fleece stood on end. Though they could not discern what manner of creature the aura-concealed Yuwen Xuan truly was, the instinctive dread born of the food chain filled them with primal unease. They hastened their steps, swarming hurriedly toward the side gate. But the side gate was narrow, and the flock was in such a panic that several sheep jammed tightly in the opening, bleating helpless cries that sounded entirely un-sheeplike.

At the spectacle, Yuwen Xuan let out a scoffing chuckle.

Paying no further heed to these foolish "sheep," he gave his sleeves a flick. His attire shifted to mirror the [sweeping robes and tall caps](# "Literal CN: 峨冠博带 (eguan bodai) - traditional formal ceremonial attire of high officials") worn by the figures performing the ritual on the altar. Only, his version retained his signature crimson-and-black palette, complete with the subtle embroidered Taotie motifs Master had stitched for him.

Dressed in this attire that looked both similar and decidedly distinct, and without bothering to disguise his facial features in the least, Yuwen Xuan strode boldly toward the main gate.

Though no one was entering or exiting, beneath the grand gate of Xuanpu stood a contingent of guards arrayed in standard Celestial Soldier armor. Their armor gleamed with brilliant golden luminescence; anyone with sufficient knowledge of the Heavenly Court would recognize from the intricate, floating relief patterns that these soldiers belonged to the Imperial Celestial Guards, directly subordinate to the Celestial Emperor, far removed from the [line-fillers](# "Literal CN: 填线宝宝 (tianxian baobao) - trench-fillers / cannon fodder, gaming slang for expendable frontline units") deployed on the God-Demon battlefield.

Yet Yuwen Xuan acted as if he hadn't seen these imposing celestial soldiers at all. Keeping his eyes straight ahead, he swaggered onward with chin held high.

Naturally, it was impossible for the celestial soldiers not to notice him.

Yuwen Xuan had merely veiled his aura to prevent others from assessing his cultivation realm; he hadn't cast invisibility. With a living, breathing person strutting inside so brazenly, they would have to be blind not to see him.

One of the celestial soldiers stepped forward to stop him:

—"Hey, you! Halt!"

Yuwen Xuan didn't force his way through; he simply lifted his chin and shot the soldier a cool, sidelong glance:

—"What?"

Yuwen Xuan's haughty attitude was instantly infuriating. The soldier frowned, about to interrogate him, but another soldier beside him yanked his sleeve, stepping forward to ask Yuwen Xuan with warm enthusiasm:

—"My Lord Immortal Official, have you also arrived to attend the Heaven-Worship Ceremony?"

Yuwen Xuan regarded him with a half-smile, sizing the soldier up:

—"By this hour, shouldn't any ceremony already be over? I'm merely here for the banquet to get something to eat,"-he said, not bothering to conceal his objective.

Hearing this, the soldier's smile turned even warmer:

—"Then you've arrived at the perfect time! The Sky-Tour Banquet is about to begin. Would you like us to guide you?"

[A Sky-Tour Banquet... wasn't it Heaven-Worship?]

Yuwen Xuan mused carelessly, nodding with equal indifference:

—"Lead the way,"-he said, his gaze already drifting toward the mist-shrouded cloud pool where the banquet was being set.

The soldier seemed even more confirmed in his suspicion. Casting a meaningful glance to the side, a pretty, sharp-witted celestial maid stepped forward at once, smiling sweetly as she led the way for Yuwen Xuan.

Only after their figures vanished from sight did the enthusiastic soldier drop the smile from his face. Releasing the guard who had initially attempted to interrogate Yuwen Xuan, he scolded:

—"Where are your eyes?! Where did all your etiquette go?!"

—"I—I was just following regulations!"-the first celestial soldier retorted with deep indignance:—"He's clearly not an immortal official participating in the Sky-Tour Ceremony! Is it really fine to let him walk in like that without even knowing who he is?!"

—"Heh, that's precisely why I say you're blind to the room!"-the now-frigid celestial soldier sneered:—"Those attending the Sky-Tour Ceremony are big shots, to be sure, but within this Heavenly Court, 'big shots' capable of swatting little grunts like us to death are a dime a dozen! And they're far more viciously troublesome than legitimate high-ranking officials."

Seeing his colleague's blank incomprehension, the frigid soldier illustrated:

—"Take that gentleman who just strolled in. You saw it, didn't you? He's wearing an immortal official's ceremonial robe with flawless tailoring—and high-grade tailoring at that—yet the colors and patterns are utterly aberrant, even sporting the design of a ferocious beast! What do you think that signifies?"

[—"Signifies that he's a fake?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Soldier")

—"Bullshit!"-another celestial soldier chimed in with a sharp rebuke:—"What kind of impersonator nails the most meticulous, agonizing clothing cuts down to the millimeter, only to screw up on the most glaringly obvious colors and patterns? Isn't that deliberately begging for trouble?!"

—"Precisely,"-the frigid soldier nodded:—"Those who treat the solemnity of official court attire like trash and slap bizarre patterns and shades all over it are, nine times out of ten, [Immortal Second Generations](# "Literal CN: 仙二代 (xian'erdai) - celestial nepo babies / scions of high-ranking immortals")! And mark my words—I'm not talking about those unregistered wild bastards sired secretly when immortal officials sneak down to the mortal realm. I mean immortals legitimately born in the Heavenly Court! You understand what they represent, don't you?"

The first celestial soldier parted his lips, unable to answer immediately.

Yet he certainly understood what it meant:

The Heavenly Court currently suffered from severe bureaucratic bloat. Even romance was strictly forbidden, let alone having children. Consequently, any young immortal born within the Heavenly Court either possessed a monstrous background or carried a unique "Heavenly Destiny." In either case, they were destined to occupy lofty heights in the future. Naturally, they had been coddled and pampered from birth, cultivating a haughty, unworldly demeanor that looked down upon all creation.

Recalling Yuwen Xuan's bearing, the soldier felt that he indeed possessed the arrogant, willful temperament characteristic of every celestial nepo baby he had ever encountered.

Yet he remained somewhat reluctant to yield:

—"Even if he's an Immortal Second Gen, letting him in without announcing his name and origin isn't right, is it? What if he disrupts the Sky-Tour Ceremony? He'll walk away scot-free, but we'll be doomed!"

—"Rest easy. People of his sort understand 'the rules' far better than we do. These nepo babies might be rebellious, but each one is sharp as a needle; they know exactly who they can offend and who they cannot,"-the frigid soldier scoffed:—"Didn't you hear him say it himself? He's merely here for the banquet. He even timed his arrival to the exact minute the Sky-Tour Ceremony wraps up. For all we know, he was specially invited by some grand household."

—"Is that so..."-the soldier murmured, thoroughly subdued, though a tiny sliver of curiosity remained:—"But who on earth is he?"

—"Tch, why do you need to know who he is so clearly?"-the frigid soldier retorted dismissively.

Yet as an Imperial Celestial Guard protecting the Celestial Emperor, he wasn't entirely irresponsible:

—"If you're genuinely that curious, you can ask Xiao Cui later,"-he added, nodding toward the celestial maid dispatched to lead Yuwen Xuan:—"If all goes smoothly, she might be able to coax it out of him."

[—"Actually, not knowing is fine. Those Immortal Second Gens are all massive headaches,"](# "Speaker: Celestial Soldier")-another soldier muttered.

—"Don't be so dismissive. A single meeting could easily spark a [karmic connection](# "Literal CN: 机缘 (jiyuan) - fortuitous encounter / celestial opportunity"). This gentleman possesses an [imposing, majestic bearing](# "Literal CN: 器宇轩昂 (qiyu xuan'ang) - dignified and formidable presence")—who knows, he might even be an Imperial Prince! Besides, following this Sky-Tour, isn't it rumored that His Majesty might finally select a successor? Could he have rushed here for that?"-speculated another.

[—"Ah, surely not?"](# "Speaker: Celestial Soldier")

[—"He does look handsome enough, but he seems terribly difficult to talk to."](# "Speaker: Celestial Soldier")

—"That caliber of temperament is precisely what an Imperial Prince looks like! Speaking of which, the Celestial Emperor has reigned for far too long. Over a dozen of his princes were banished to distant fiefdoms. Now, during this Sky-Tour, word suddenly spreads that he's choosing a successor—who knows which of them managed to rush back in time,"-whispered a guard.

[—"Hey, hey! Don't talk nonsense about such matters!"](# "Speaker: Celestial Soldier")

Even though the guards knew discussing this topic was strictly forbidden, the allure of court gossip was simply irresistible.

—"He might genuinely be one!"-at that moment, a celestial soldier abruptly disclosed:—"I saw this gentleman once before! Back then, he was together with Lord Tian Yan, and the two seemed exceedingly intimate!"

—"Ah, Lord Tian Yan?! Doesn't that make his chances of inheriting the throne even higher?!"-the soldiers gasped in unison.

Without realizing it, throughout this whirlwind of gossip, Yuwen Xuan had evolved from an "intruder of unknown origin" to an "arrogant Immortal Second Gen," and finally into an "Imperial Prince likely to inherit the throne of the Celestial Emperor, backed by the Ministry of Celestial Destiny."

The frigid yet perceptive soldier suddenly experienced a jolt of realization:

—"Stop talking! If it involves the Ministry of Celestial Destiny, this succession business might actually be for real! This Prince arrived 'in secret'—who knows, in the blink of an eye, he might end up ascending the throne just like the reigning Celestial Emperor!"

[How on earth does he look like he came 'in secret'...] a soldier mumbled inwardly, though he supposed that to an 'Imperial Prince', perhaps he truly believed strolling through the front gate was sneaking in to claim the throne.

—"In short, do not get dragged into this mess! Therefore—we never saw him!"-the guard declared with utmost gravity.

This proclamation was met with unanimous agreement from the Imperial Celestial Guards.

***

—"Your High—my Lord, the Sky-Tour Banquet is just ahead,"-the celestial maid Xiao Cui announced with deep reverence.

Though she hadn't participated in the guards' gossip, her thoughts over the walk had led her to a strikingly similar conclusion.

Yuwen Xuan had no inkling of this, nor had he deliberately deceived anyone. To tell the truth, right from the start, he had simply dropped three thunderous questions:

Question one had been:

—"Did you ascend from the lower realm?"-Yuwen Xuan had asked casually.

—"Yes,"-Xiao Cui had answered with graceful poise, though an uneasy flutter had already begun in her chest, dreading that he looked down on her. Native True Immortals of the Heavenly Court were often like that.

Then Yuwen Xuan had lobbed his second query:

—"Actually, I've always been curious about something: why are there still 'maids' in the Celestial Realm? Excluding the few who couldn't bear to part with their servants upon ascension, where did you all actually come from?"

—"This..."-Xiao Cui's smile had nearly shattered right there.

She felt increasingly certain his words carried deep, barbed implications, yet lacked any concrete proof.

And Yuwen Xuan's third question broke through her emotional defenses entirely:

—"After toiling so hard to ascend, only to end up a maidservant—are you really content with that?"

Well, the facts proved once more that Yuwen Xuan's emotional intelligence was practically non-existent. Whether it was his most revered Master or completely irrelevant strangers, whenever the mood struck him, he would [steamroll everyone indiscriminately](# "Literal CN: 平等的创亖所有人 - internet slang for emotionally mowing down / running over everyone equally").

Yet precisely because of his brazen, uninhibited attitude, the maid grew even less inclined to doubt his identity—nobody had ever witnessed an "infiltrator" this outrageous! She only wanted to maintain a respectful distance.

With that mindset, the young maid lost all appetite for fishing for information.

Consequently, throughout the remainder of the walk, she gleaned virtually no background intelligence on Yuwen Xuan. Instead, she was pelted with a relentless barrage of inquiries: What were the sacrificial offerings atop the altar, and were they tasty? How many children did the Celestial Emperor have to date? Had any formidable Demon Sovereigns been captured on the God-Demon battlefield recently? What was Lord Tian Yan of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny doing at the Sky-Tour Ceremony? What delicacies were being served at the banquet? Could those "sheep" outside be eaten? Could the divine beast guarding this place be eaten? What on earth was the Sky-Tour anyway? And so forth.

These questions were so chaotic and nonsensical, lacking even the most basic Heavenly Court common sense. Had anyone else asked, their identity would have been suspect immediately. But because Yuwen Xuan opened with three thunderous questions and his focus was all over the map—seeming far more interested in the sacrificial offerings than the Celestial Emperor himself—it made one assume that basic queries like "What is the Sky-Tour Ceremony?" were merely smoke screens deployed to mislead his listeners.

Xiao Cui, having been assigned to guide distinguished guests, was naturally the sort of person endowed with [hundreds of scheming calculations](# "Literal CN: 几百个心眼子 - having countless sharp, manipulative, calculating thoughts").

Her thoughts inevitably drifted toward the matter of the "Celestial Emperor's succession," and she grew increasingly convinced that he was an Imperial Prince. In this regard, it had to be said that Yuwen Xuan's human shell was excessively deceitful: his dashing features and bizarrely cheerful, radiant demeanor were an absolute lethal combination, charming and beguiling men and women alike. Even his nonsensical inquiries seemed like profound philosophical deliberations regarding the Heavenly Court's administrative structure!

Once that conviction took root, Xiao Cui no longer felt offended in the least, and even felt a certain degree of "esteem."

Her demeanor turned increasingly subservient:

—"This humble maid cannot accompany you further. Please feel free to enjoy yourself as you wish."

—"Oh."

Yuwen Xuan nodded indifferently, swaggering straight into the banquet hall as if strolling back into his own mountain cave dwelling.

Yet Xiao Cui didn't truly leave Yuwen Xuan unattended. Pulling aside a maid carrying serving dishes, she whispered a series of instructions.

Very soon, all the celestial maids and servants preparing the banquet learned of Yuwen Xuan's "identity."

If the gate guards knew how to read the room, the attendants serving wine and delicacies at high-ranking officials' banquets were even more adept. Before long, servants were attentively hovering around Yuwen Xuan's side.

Yuwen Xuan remained entirely unconcerned.

He had no idea that simply by walking through the door, he had inexplicably acquired the illustrious status of an Imperial Prince. All his thoughts were entirely fixated on the delicacies adorning the banquet tables. He spared only a sliver of attention to ask:

—"Where is Tian Yan's seat?"

—"This way, please,"-a celestial servant answered at once:—"Would you like to sit with Lord Tian Yan, my Lord? Shall you use the existing table, or should we arrange an additional one?"

—"The existing one is fine,"-Yuwen Xuan answered absently.

He followed the servant to Tian Yan's table.

As the chief official of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny, Tian Yan was an exceptionally vital immortal official; his seat was positioned right next to the head seat—the throne of the Celestial Emperor. His jade table was laden with far richer delicacies than the other tables.

However, in accordance with ceremonial etiquette, the food for the Celestial Emperor and for his high ministers was strictly differentiated. Whether due to different palates or rank, the dishes on the two sides differed vastly.

Yuwen Xuan didn't sit down at Tian Yan's table immediately.

His gaze darted back and forth between the Celestial Emperor's table and Tian Yan's table several times. The contrasting delicacies made it excruciatingly difficult to choose. Fortunately, as a human, he was already an adult—and so Yuwen Xuan decided: [he wanted them all!](# "Literal CN: 作为人类的部分他已经成年了，于是宇文轩决定，他全都要！ - homage to the famous meme: 小孩子才做选择，大人全都要 (Children make choices; adults take everything)")

With complete naturalness, Yuwen Xuan took a seat at the low table situated directly between the Celestial Emperor and Tian Yan—without even asking who that table belonged to!

The servants and maids exchanged meaningful glances, but none dared utter a peep.

They didn't dare disturb a figure of potentially monumental stature. Furthermore, they had far more pressing matters at hand:

The first "distinguished guest" had already arrived; could the other banquet guests be far behind? They had to finish preparing all the dishes before the guests arrived.

And thanks to the presence of a certain Taotie, that workload was about to become far, far heavier than they could ever imagine!
