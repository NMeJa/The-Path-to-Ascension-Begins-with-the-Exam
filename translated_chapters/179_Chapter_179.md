# Chapter 179: The Demon Race's Educational Reform

Regardless of Shui Huang'er's thoughts on the matter, the demons proved wholly incapable of resisting Mo Xi's "enticement."

Especially those little demons who had never wanted to attend school in the first place.

For example, the deer demon Zhu Zhu was one of them.

As a demon spirit born from an herbivorous beast, people invariably held a stereotyped impression of his kind, assuming them to be gentle, peace-loving, and averse to conflict.

The reality was quite the opposite.

Herbivorous demons were actually far more belligerent.

Even before the antlers atop his head had sprouted, Zhu Zhu had loved butting and tussling with his playmates. Once he entered the mating season, this instinct flared even fiercer: regardless of whether an attractive female was present, a brawl was an absolute must—otherwise, what was the point of growing antlers in the first place?

Consequently, when the Sacred Tree began "livestreaming" the human race's Immortal Arts Assembly, Zhu Zhu instantly became a devoted spectator.

Unlike those [longworms](# "Literal CN: 长虫 (chángchong) — colloquial term for snakes; here used humorously to describe snake demons worshipping dragons") who worshipped the Dragon Clan and instantly became die-hard fans of Lu Shulong, however, although Zhu Zhu acknowledged that dragons were undeniably formidable and magnificent, what he truly loved watching was the combat of ordinary humans:

[So frail humans can actually accomplish such feats?]

[Ah, they can genuinely overcome the strong through weakness?]

[If I learn the techniques of the human race, could I become even stronger?!]

Such were the thoughts that occupied Zhu Zhu's mind as the livestream began.

And so, just like all the other young demons swept up in a sudden rush of hot-blooded fervor, he dashed off to plead with his clan patriarch and enrolled in the "Demon School" initiative.

And then, he regretted it.

To be precise, exactly five minutes and thirty-six seconds after sitting down in the classroom—minutes and seconds were apparently a newly minted human system of time measurement, and Zhu Zhu felt that breaking a single [shichen](# "Literal CN: 时辰 (shíchen) — traditional Chinese two-hour unit of time") into two hours, one hundred and twenty minutes, and seven thousand two hundred seconds captured with exquisite accuracy his agonizing sense of [days crawling by like years](# "Literal CN: 度日如年 (dù rì rú nián) — Chinese idiom: days drag on like years; excruciating agony of boredom"), where every single passing second was pure torture—he already deeply regretted it!

Why had no one told him that before humans could even begin cultivating, they had to read so many books and memorize such impossibly difficult knowledge?!

[This is a scam!]

[An outright fraud!]

Zhu Zhu thought in aggrieved despair.

Fortunately(?), although herbivorous demons were belligerent, they also possessed strong submissiveness to hierarchy; once rules were firmly established, they faithfully obeyed. No matter how much Zhu Zhu despised attending class, he still reported to the classroom dutifully each day, thereby dodging the dreaded punishment of [being suspended from tree branches](# "Literal CN: 挂枝头 (guà zhītóu) — referencing Zhou Hui's academic punishment of suspending failing or truant students from tree boughs").

—His learning efficiency, however, was practically non-existent.

Or rather, the learning efficiency of the demon race had always been fundamentally inferior to that of humanity.

This was not racial discrimination, but an objective, undeniable fact.

After a demon became a spirit, it would almost inevitably assume humanoid form—or at least manifest a human head. This was not because demons were particularly fond of humanity, nor was it due to aesthetic taste; rather, in the absence of external interference such as spiritual qi, demonic qi, immortal power, or demonic arts, the human race was the only species that had evolved a dedicated "thinking organ" purely through bodily structure. In other words, the human brain was uniquely suited for cognition.

Demons assumed humanoid forms simply to conserve their demonic power while granting themselves a slight boost in intelligence.

Yet for youths suffering from academic aversion, they wished for nothing more than to be entirely rid of that organ called a brain.

In this regard, demons had a distinct advantage over humans.

A human youth who hated studying could hardly chop off his own head, but a demon youth who refused to listen to a lecture could simply shift his head back into a thoughtless beast skull.

In any case, whenever Zhu Zhu sat in that classroom, his mind was completely blank.

As a consequence, after merely a handful of lessons, his head reverted more and more into a literal stag head. At this rate, over the course of a single semester, his studies would only devolve backwards until he could no longer maintain even a semblance of humanoid form.

Of course, long before that happened, he was far more likely to fail his exams and end up suspended from the tree branches.

[Once every single bough of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree is draped full of suspended demons, even Lady Saintess will have no choice but to abandon this educational curriculum, right?]

Zhu Zhu eagerly looked forward to the arrival of that glorious day.

Regrettably, until that day arrived, he still had no choice but to sit obediently in the classroom.

[When on earth will this accursed class finally end?]

Zhu Zhu pondered with his dazed, spinning stag head, and just as he was on the verge of nodding off completely, something struck his forehead with pinpoint precision:

[—"You over there with the stag head—pay attention to the lecture!"](# "Speaker: Illusory Instructor")

[—"Ugh..."](# "Speaker: Zhu Zhu")

Zhu Zhu clutched his forehead and glared furiously at the lecturing "instructor."

It was an "illusory avatar."

The Heavenly Spirit Sect was already facing a severe shortage of teachers.

Originally, before the grand enrollment expansion, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had possessed relatively few disciples. Zhou Hui had rushed to train an emergency batch of instructors specifically for the White Jade Capital academies, but the pace of teacher training simply could not keep up with the explosive rate of expansion. Thanks to the stellar demonstration effect of the White Jade Capital academies, compounded by the new demands from both the demon race and northern sects, the Heavenly Spirit Sect's roster of student instructors had fallen into a catastrophic deficit.

Fortunately, the cultivation world possessed the cultivation world's unique solutions.

Cultivators could utilize specialized spells to craft illusory avatars capable of simulating their prototype's knowledge, personality, and even soul fluctuations with seventy to eighty percent fidelity. Whenever many cultivators struggled to find a suitable disciple to inherit their mantle, they often relied on this very technique to pass down their legacy.

Yet such illusory avatars were, after all, not the living individuals themselves; their cognitive capacity was strictly limited—though conversely, their crafting costs were remarkably low.

The more exceptional the prototype, the steeper the cost to fabricate its avatar. If one were to directly craft an avatar of Zhou Hui... no, one didn't even need Zhou Hui—merely producing an illusory avatar of Qu Qianxing required materials valuable enough to raise several Golden Core cultivators from scratch.

Therefore, in the demon academies, the standardized solution was to deploy illusory avatars modeled after the student instructors of the White Jade Capital academies.

Those student instructors possessed modest cultivation bases of their own, making their avatars comparatively easy to replicate, even if their behavioral patterns were somewhat rigid.

For instance, Zhu Zhu's "instructor" was perfectly capable of detecting obvious slacking or daydreaming among the students below and intervening promptly, but "he" lacked the nuanced emotional depth required to truly care about or comprehend what the students were actually thinking.

And students across the entire world were largely identical.

They might not know how to study, but they grasped their teacher's bottom line in an instant.

After receiving a flick to the forehead, Zhu Zhu naturally dared not stage an outright walkout or assault the "illusory instructor"—which would have landed him [suspended from the southeastern bough](# "Literal CN: 挂东南枝 (guà dōngnán zhī) — poetic idiom from 'Southeast the Peacocks Fly' meaning hanging oneself; here playing on Zhou Hui's punishment of hanging truant students from trees")—but he had no qualms about grinding the antlers atop his head, adopting an aggressive stance toward the "instructor," and even setting up a miniature silencing boundary around his desk, fully intending to block out the lecture entirely.

According to Zhu Zhu's hard-won experience, as long as his gaze remained fixed upon the "instructor," the avatar would judge him as "listening attentively," remaining completely oblivious to "inconsequential" petty fidgeting.

This time, however, before Zhu Zhu could finish his movements, his spellcasting was abruptly interrupted.

Zhu Zhu jolted in shock. Looking up, he found the "instructor" glaring at him with icy chill:

—"Didn't I tell you to listen carefully to the lecture?"-the "instructor" said.

—"...Yes,"-Zhu Zhu answered sullenly, hoping to muddle his way through.

Today, however, the "illusory instructor" seemed dead set on picking a fight with him:

—"If you are so unwilling to learn, why not simply quit studying altogether?"

Had a human child heard such words, they would surely have hung their head in shame or indignation!

In the lexicon of low-tier demonlings, however, the concept of "sarcasm" simply did not exist.

Hearing this, Zhu Zhu brightened up in sheer delight:

—"Really? I can?"-Zhu Zhu asked.

Still, Zhu Zhu retained a sliver of vigilance. Eyeing the "instructor's" cunning human features (a product of Zhu Zhu's deep-seated prejudice), he asked warily:

—"This isn't a trap, is it? You're not going to hang me from the branches, right?!"

—"It is no trap,"-the "instructor" sneered,-"However, there is a condition."

With those words, ripples spread across the blackboard behind the "instructor" like disturbed water. From the very center of those ripples, a colossal figure stepped forth.

It was a... deer.

A giant stag.

Standing over three meters tall, it bore a colossal pair of antlers spanning two meters across, adorned with golden ginkgo foliage and azure moss like a majestic canopy, while a pair of blood-red pupils radiated terrifying murderous intent.

Goosebumps erupted across Zhu Zhu's entire body in an instant:

[So incredibly majestic~~!]

[It's practically the exact image of my dream self... wait a second, that tail tipped with white speckles, that notch missing from the very tip of the antler, and that scent—why is it so uncannily identical to mine?!]

[Could it be...]

—"That is you—the you who has completed your studies,"-the "instructor" stated.

—"After completing my studies?!"-Zhu Zhu leaped to his feet, his ears and white-spotted stub of a tail shooting straight up in excitement,-"I can become like that?!"

In the midst of his leap, however, he slammed directly into his desk and chair.

Instantly, his harrowing experiences in class over the past few days came rushing back into his mind.

Even a [foolish roe deer](# "Literal CN: 傻狍子 (shǎ páozi) — Northern Chinese slang for someone gullible, curious, and easily startled, based on the famously slow-witted roe deer") learned from painful experience.

The hot blood rushing through Zhu Zhu's veins cooled down in a flash. Glaring at the "instructor" in tragic indignation, he cried:

—"Don't tell me that sitting obediently in this godforsaken place for decades will turn me into that! You won't fool me twice!"

—"I did not deceive you. Regardless, it matters not. Manifesting it was never meant to preach what you might achieve through study; it is merely a projection configured according to specific parameters. Only if you attain full mastery across every single subject could you possibly attain such form—though based on my pedagogical experience, you would be fortunate to even scrape a passing grade."

The "instructor" sighed with a rare touch of human nuance.

Zhu Zhu didn't understand the jargon, but Zhu Zhu knew he had just been deeply insulted.

Fortunately, the "instructor's" final words were crystal clear:

—"What I mean is, since you believe it to be nothing more than a scam, how about testing yourself against it in battle? If you can defeat this 'learned version of yourself,' thereby proving you possess no need for schooling whatsoever, I will permit you to leave this instant without wasting any further time here."

[If I beat it, I won't have to go to school?]

Zhu Zhu was instantly tempted.

He scrutinized that "idealized self."

*Gulp.* It looked terrifyingly strong.

[Still, I can't let myself be tricked! No matter how you look at it, that's still 'me', isn't it?]

[With those boring human courses, even if someone actually graduated with honors, could they really be that powerful? At most, it's just fancy, useless parlor tricks!]

Zhu Zhu successfully convinced himself.

At the tantalizing thought of never having to attend class again, he felt this was well worth a shot.

Zhu Zhu stamped his hooves and brandished his antlers:

—"Don't look down on me! Let's see what you've got!"-Zhu Zhu shouted.

And then, Zhu Zhu "died."

It couldn't even be called an exchange of blows. The moment he charged forward, the sheer disparity in body mass flattened him into a puddle of minced meat.

Naturally, within the demon race's domain, it was impossible to truly kill a demon.

After wriggling for a few moments, that puddle of "deer paste" kneaded itself back into the shape of a young stag. Oblivious to his undignified state, he whined to the "instructor" in tragic indignation:

—"That's unfair! That's cheating! There's no way I could ever grow into something that colossal!"

—"You can,"-the "instructor" replied, patting the neck of the giant stag beside him,-"Provided you thoroughly master gigantification body-refinement techniques, cultivating such a physique is entirely within reach."

Gigantification? Body refinement?

It seemed the lectures over the past couple of days had indeed touched upon something like that.

Zhu Zhu reflected, shamefully tempted for a fleeting second. Yet the moment he recalled the lecture materials that had turned his brains into mush, he decisively rejected the possibility:

—"Wh-who would want to study that anyway! That definitely isn't me!"

—"Very well, then we shall forfeit this subject,"-the "instructor" acquiesced with shocking amiability.

"He" snapped his fingers. The giant stag's frame immediately shrunk by half, looking considerably less oppressive, though still remarkably magnificent.

—"Do you wish to fight again?"-the "instructor" inquired.

—"Yes!"-Zhu Zhu answered without hesitation.

He reasoned that since this stag had suddenly shrunk so much and could no longer trample him flat, he could definitely take it!

And so, Zhu Zhu charged forward once more—

And was promptly pulverized into "deer paste" yet again.

This time, Zhu Zhu still failed to even lay a hoof on his "future self." A gust of wind swept by, and the gorgeous ginkgo leaves upon the antlers emitted an indescribable aroma that enveloped Zhu Zhu, causing him to melt away completely.

This time, before Zhu Zhu could even complain, the "instructor" explained:

—"This is the 'Spiritual Botany' course you voluntarily requested when classes first began. If mastered, this is the exact effect it produces."

Right—because he had seen humans overcome stronger foes using spiritual botany during the Immortal Arts Assembly, Zhu Zhu had specifically chosen it as an elective. But once the actual lessons started, the subject had proven excruciatingly dull.

—"No! I'm not studying that!"-Zhu Zhu wailed tearfully, even though completing the course looked ridiculously cool!

—"That works too,"-the "instructor" said, stroking the stag's head. The magnificent ginkgo foliage upon its antlers vanished.

Gazing at the bare antlers, Zhu Zhu felt a pang of regret, as though he had plucked away a piece of his own future with his bare hands.

[It was so massive and so handsome just a moment ago...]

—"Do you still wish to challenge it?"-the "instructor" asked once more.

—"Yes!"-Zhu Zhu ground his teeth.

And then, he "died" again and again.

There was a slight improvement this time: he died from an antler clash.

—"There were no tricks to this move; it was purely the result of refining the antlers,"-the "instructor" remarked.

—"I'm not refining any 'antlers'!"-Zhu Zhu lied through his teeth.

He watched as "his" exquisite antlers disappeared. Still, he could definitely win this time—

A fourth time...

A fifth time...

A sixth time...

Zhu Zhu "died" countless times.

Furthermore, the more possibilities he stripped away from his "future self," and the closer its strength and stature drew to his present self, the more brutally he died, and the more bitter his indignation became.

After all, that initial "ideal self" had been merely an abstraction; Zhu Zhu could hardly fathom transforming into such a grand being. But as he stripped away those possibilities piece by piece, narrowing "the future" down to a version of himself who had merely passed a specific course or mastered a particular spell, the skirmishes inevitably turned visceral and bloody—and his defeats stung with unbearable indignity.

Finally, after collapsing for the $N\text{th}$ time beneath the hooves of "the self who scraped a passing mark in this semester's Heart Cultivation Method," Zhu Zhu clung to the "instructor's" thigh in a mangled, bloody heap and bawled his eyes out:

—"'Instructor,' I want to go to school!"

*

Scenes of this exact nature played out across every corner of the Sacred Tree.

In their desperate bid to earn exemption from attending class, the young demons of the Sacred Tree died and revived in endless succession—and through that very crucible, awakened to the boundless possibilities of their own futures.

—"I believe this is the [correct way to experience](# "Literal CN: 正确打开方式 (zhèngquè dǎkāi fāngshì) — modern internet slang for the proper way to use or experience something") the 'trial of desire.' Merely using it to guard secret treasures is rather wasteful; for the sake of the demon race's future, I believe developing the children's latent potential is far more worthwhile.

—"Furthermore, from a pedagogical standpoint, simply suspending truants from tree boughs right from the start is far too crude. It fails to let students comprehend what they are truly missing out on, and may even deepen their aversion to learning.

—"To kindle their passion for study, one must take into account their natural aptitude, race, personal hobbies, and temperament, providing tailored guidance accordingly. Only then will a genuine desire to learn take root,"-thus concluded the pedagogue, Untiring Immortal Venerable Zhou Hui.

—"As expected of the Immortal Venerable,"-Shui Huang'er praised, her eyes sparkling with admiration.

Only Tao Zhu averted his gaze with an expression of profound speechlessness:

[So hanging them from tree branches is too crude, but slaughtering these brats countless times isn't crude at all, is that it?!]

[Just how on earth did Untiring's disciples survive all these years?!]
