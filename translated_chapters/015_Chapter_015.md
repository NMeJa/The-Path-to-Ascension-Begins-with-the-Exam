# Chapter 15: The Laws of Exam Joy

—"Master, does this test paper feel completely different from the one we took last time?"-Ran Deng asked, flipping through her examination paper.

Aside from his eldest disciple Yuwen Xuan, the ones who had also received Zhou Hui's special dispensation—being exempted from the examination to receive the guests—were his third disciple Ran Deng and fifth disciple Lu Shulong.

Originally, they were supposed to lead He Yong and his entourage to the Heavenly Spirit Sect's primary hall, the Hall of Embracing Emptiness. Several days prior, Lu Shulong and Mo Bei had already prepared a banquet there. However, Yuwen Xuan had barged in and meddled, resulting in them coming to the Palace of Silence anyway.

Adhering to the principle of [“since you're already here,”](# "Literal CN: 来都来了 (lái dōu lái le) — one of China's iconic Four Great Excuses: 'Since we've already come all this way, we might as well...'") Zhou Hui had handed each of the three disciples an exam paper as well, having them work on it.

Although their scores wouldn't be officially recorded, it was a good opportunity to gauge their general level and see whether they had made any progress.

Well, as of right now, it was abundantly clear that Yuwen Xuan had made absolutely zero progress.

Not only had he not improved, but he also appeared to have regressed.

Even a disciple who had just entered the Qi Condensation realm wouldn't answer the question "Which medicinal pill is suitable for Foundation Establishment?" with "Qi Condensation Pill."

[This won't do; just looking at this gives me a headache.]

[I can feel the injuries from the heavenly tribulation acting up again.]

Zhou Hui rubbed his forehead and swept his divine sense across the other two disciples' exam papers.

Theoretically, Lu Shulong had been in charge of preparing hospitality for the guests, so having him host the guests today without sitting for the exam seemed like a completely natural course of action. In reality, however, Zhou Hui felt that his fifth disciple, Lu Shulong, was an entity who could not be allowed to take standard exams for an entirely different reason.

It wasn't because Lu Shulong's fundamental common knowledge was as abysmal as Yuwen Xuan's. In truth, Lu Shulong's temperament was infinitely better than Yuwen Xuan's; he was an exceptionally obedient, sweet child. Consequently, after the previous exam, Lu Shulong had dutifully read through every single book in the Library Pavilion covering the Qi Condensation realm from start to finish. And then... and then there wasn't a single question he couldn't answer.

Indeed, Lu Shulong's test paper today was a perfect score.

At least up until now, Lu Shulong had not answered a single question wrong. One couldn't discern his weaknesses, nor could one tell where his specializations lay.

[What raw talent!]

Back when he was an "ordinary mortal," this was precisely the sort of rival that had given him the worst headaches.

The side of Zhou Hui that was an [Involution King](# "Literal CN: involution king (卷王) — modern slang for someone who out-works, out-studies, and out-grinds everyone else in hyper-competitive environments") let out a sigh, but shortly thereafter, he cheered up.

Although Zhou Hui disliked facing rivals like that, Lu Shulong was his own disciple! What was wrong with his own student becoming strong with effortless ease? Besides, Lu Shulong's potential clearly didn't stop here; he had evidently underestimated this disciple in the past. Moving forward, he could rest easy accelerating Shulong's study curriculum and ramping up his workload.

Zhou Hui looked at Lu Shulong with a benevolent, loving gaze—much like a farmer gazing upon a plump piglet he had raised with his own hands.

Lu Shulong instantly shivered from head to toe.

Baffled, he brushed aside his long, silvery-blue hair and touched the chilly nape of his neck. He glanced around in confusion but found no clue, because by that point, Zhou Hui's gaze had already shifted toward Ran Deng.

Ran Deng wasn't quite as "miraculous" as her senior brother or junior brother; she was merely an ordinary honor student.

Moreover, this girl had clearly slacked off.

Faced with identical problem types, however she had answered wrong last time was exactly how she got them wrong this time. She had clearly used the assignment of renovating the Library Pavilion as an excuse to return to her immortal cave and never review a single thing.

Nevertheless, the ordinarily exceptional Ran Deng possessed the attentiveness typical of an honor student.

Unlike Lu Shulong, who simply buried his head and answered questions without pausing, she had only completed a third of the exam before noticing that Zhou Hui's intention behind setting the questions this time differed from the previous exam.

—"Does it seem... somewhat easier?"-Ran Deng asked tentatively.

—"It is not easier. Over the past few days, I even expanded the question bank, adding more problems of greater difficulty and depth,"-Zhou Hui replied.-"The reason you perceive it as easier is likely because I instructed the System to collect data in real time, calculate which subjects each disciple excels at and is more willing to answer, and increased the weighting of those questions."

—"Weighting?"-Ran Deng looked completely at a loss, wearing an expression that said she heard the words but understood none of them.

—"Simply put, this examination makes it easier to identify your strengths,"-Zhou Hui said.

After all, the purpose of the exam and the target examinees were entirely different.

The subjects of the previous exam were the direct disciples and inner-sect elites. These individuals had been selected in the first place precisely because of their outstanding qualities; they possessed the self-awareness of an elite, brimming with confidence—perhaps even a bit overconfident. They had no need for anyone to remind them how exceptional they were.

Zhou Hui's [baseline assessment exam](# "Literal CN: bottom-feeling exam (摸底考试) — diagnostic/placement test given at the start of a school year to determine students' baseline competency") had been designed to pinpoint their shortcomings, see how much water their buckets could hold, and incidentally [tighten their discipline](# "Literal CN: 紧紧皮 (jǐn jǐn pí) — tighten their skin/discipline, a colloquial phrase for whipping lazy subordinates or students into shape") so they would take their future coursework seriously.

This examination, on the other hand, was the Grand Unified Examination, composed mostly of outer-sect disciples.

The natural aptitudes of these disciples were mediocre to begin with, and they split their time between working and studying, spending the majority of their hours performing menial labor. With disciples like these, rather than saying they had shortcomings or missing wooden staves in their buckets, it was more accurate to say that most of them were essentially shallow plates—completely incapable of holding water at all.

Nor did they lack blows to their morale; the highest aspiration for many among them was merely reaching Foundation Establishment to tack on a few extra decades of lifespan.

Disciples of this sort needed their strengths identified. Even if they had no standout strengths, one needed to locate a direction that sparked their interest and warranted sustained effort, thereby encouraging them to forge ahead.

Zhou Hui didn't explain all this in elaborate detail, merely saying:

—"It would be wonderful if everyone could derive joy from the examination."

—"Not happening, impossible, can't be done,"-Ran Deng fired off a rapid [denial trifecta](# "Literal CN: 否认三连 (fǒurèn sānlián) — modern Chinese internet meme: 'Not me, wasn't there, impossible / No, impossible, can't be done'").

She admitted that this exam was indeed "easier." As long as one powered through the foundational questions at the start, the subsequent problems dealing with mechanisms and formations—her strongest suits—multiplied. But excelling at something didn't mean she liked it! Just looking at those questions was enough to make her hair fall out from stress.

What kind of person could possibly enjoy taking exams?!

Ah, right. Probably only someone like Master!

Ran Deng looked at Zhou Hui helplessly.

Zhou Hui looked curiously at his disciple's contorted expression, not minding her denial in the slightest. With utter confidence, he said:

—"No matter. Even if they cannot derive joy from the test paper itself, I had Xi'er design another reward mechanism. It will surely make everyone very happy."

[Is that really true?]

Ran Deng thought dubiously.

In fact, she had felt for a long time that the things that brought Master joy were decidedly not things ordinary mortals could find joy in.

*

What Ran Deng failed to understand was that there existed a particular kind of joy known as [the scholar's joy](# "Literal CN: 学霸的快乐 (xuébà de kuàilè) — top students who genuinely find thrill and joy in solving hard academic problems").

Although most [slacker students](# "Literal CN: 学渣 (xuézhā) — academic underachievers") could never comprehend it, through big data, a portion of the students were able to catch a glimpse of it.

Leaving aside Qin Xian, who had already demonstrated his specialty from the very beginning, Xiang Rong was currently having quite a grand time:

[It's gradually getting smoother and smoother.]

[Hm? This question is related to tailoring, isn't it? I don't recall seeing this on earlier test papers!]

[Doesn't matter, I know how to do this! Who do you think actually did all that embroidery work for Yiyi in the Heavenly Clothing Workshop?!]

[Still, this question goes deeper than I expected. So inserting the needle like that had this kind of purpose behind it...]

[Should I aim to secure an inner-sect qualification? Otherwise, I won't even be able to help her out in the future.]

*

Meanwhile, Xiang Rong's twin sister Xiang Yi was having a similar experience, though her focus lay in a different direction:

[Reviewing really was effective—almost everything I studied is on the test!]

[Still, the creatures of the Abyssal Ocean are truly vast in number. Rote memorization alone isn't enough; one needs to understand their classification from the fundamentals, right? Back when I was processing materials, I already had a faint feeling about that.]

[Speaking of which, I seem to be rather adept at the categorized processing of spiritual energy?]

[Once the exam is over, should I direct my efforts toward that field...?]

[My cultivation realm... advanced!]

In shock, Xiang Yi inspected her dantian, discovering that from that single shift in mindset just now, her stalled cultivation realm had actually experienced a modest breakthrough.

Xiang Yi had actually always lacked self-confidence.

Although she was an inner-sect disciple and had opportunities to be instructed by direct disciples or even attend Untiring Immortal Venerable's open lectures, her cultivation was about the same as outer-sect Xiang Rong. In fact, the reason she was in the inner sect in the first place was that Xiang Rong felt a girl was more vulnerable to bullying in the outer sect, so he yielded the opportunity to her, and even sought Junior Brother Mo's help to get her into the Heavenly Clothing Workshop. In the end, she couldn't even properly manage the embroidery work that girls were expected to excel at, needing Xiang Rong's help.

It was only thanks to having done an extensive amount of revision beforehand and scouring mountains of reference materials, capped off by the sudden inspiration sparked by this test paper, that Xiang Yi had finally discovered where her true talent lay.

Her cultivation realm had finally advanced as well. Although it hadn't yet reached the point of increasing her overall cultivation level, she finally had a clear direction.

*

Although the vast majority of disciples were thoroughly enjoying having questions [fed to them](# "Literal CN: 喂题 (wèi tí) — algorithms feeding specific problem types to students"), and several had gained sudden inspiration from it, there were also those who had firmly set their course from the start and refused to be spoon-fed.

Gan Tang knitted her brows as she scrutinized her exam paper:

[Why are they all cultivation technique questions that I already reviewed? Clearly, right at the start, there were many knowledge points I didn't understand.]

[Immortal Venerable must have his reasons. Could it be that he deliberately gave everyone questions in their areas of strength?]

[...This is a bit troublesome. While I feel guilty toward Immortal Venerable's good intentions, compared to cultivation techniques I'm already good at, I would much rather develop toward beast-taming. After all...]

[What if I deliberately get a few cultivation technique questions wrong to see what happens?]

[Ah, sure enough, other types of questions are appearing. Now to carefully select the ones I want—]

Gan Tang pondered in deep concentration.

Naturally, there was no need to worry about a top student of her caliber; in the course of this mental exercise, the divine sense she had newly acquired upon reaching Foundation Establishment gradually condensed and solidified.

*

Candidates enjoying this sort of joy (?) were far from few.

In truth, as long as one had studied conscientiously and managed to survive the initial foundational problems with passable marks, most were able to find their areas of strength in the questions that followed. On the examination paper, this manifested as the difficulty of the problems increasing in depth, yet everyone felt things were flowing far more smoothly.

This phenomenon was made abundantly clear through the cheating tool brought by a certain "guest."

At this moment, Su Xin could no longer make heads or tails of Qin Xian's test paper. All he knew was that they were intricate, complex artifact refinement problems.

Owing to the initial misunderstanding, Su Xin even thought in astonishment:

[Can ordinary disciples (outlier) of the Heavenly Spirit Sect truly master artifact refinement problems of this magnitude?!]

Refusing to give up, Su Xin checked the others, only to discover that the exam papers of everyone he had previously peeked at seemed to have become exponentially harder.

—"Dammit! How is anyone supposed to keep doing this?!"-Su Xin flung aside the Light-Stealing Mirror, lifting his head in sheer exasperation.

And then, he abruptly noticed an exceedingly bizarre phenomenon.

The coral platforms were ascending!

No—to be precise, the coral platforms encased within bubbles belonging to other people were ascending!

Take Qin Xian, for instance. Initially, the bubble containing his coral platform had hovered in Su Xin's vicinity; now, however, it had already drifted to a vantage point more than a dozen meters higher into the air.

If Qin Xian alone wasn't obvious enough, during the time Su Xin had spent peeking around, the vast majority of people's bubbles had ascended by varying degrees. As a result, the coral platforms that had all hovered at roughly the same elevation at the start were now suspended at completely disparate heights throughout the hall.

There were very few coral platforms left lingering in their original positions like Su Xin's.

Most of the examinees had clearly failed to notice this. Even those who did notice probably couldn't fathom what was going on.

Yet Su Xin had been using the Light-Stealing Mirror to snoop everywhere. Correlating this sight with the varying performances of these examinees across their exam papers, an ominous premonition seized him.

Su Xin raised his brush and hastily, blindly scribbled more than a dozen random answers across his paper.

Naturally, every single one of these answers was dead wrong.

The critical issue was that the instant these dozen-plus incorrect answers appeared, his coral platform descended by a massive, visible chunk, sinking until it was practically scraping the floor of the Palace of Silence.

This coral platform was actually a [live floating 3D leaderboard](# "The coral platforms floating at heights corresponding directly to real-time test scores")!

It directly reflected an examinee's performance in real time!

What was truly lethal was that Su Xin only now discovered that at the very bottom of the Palace of Silence, near the entrance doors, an enormous crowd of what appeared to be sect handymen and servants was standing there for some reason, pointing and whispering as they watched the spectacle.

Su Xin's vision went completely dark:

[How is this any different from a [public execution](# "Literal CN: 公开处刑 (gōngkāi chùxíng) — internet meme for extreme public humiliation or being exposed on display")?!]

[Who on earth came up with such a wicked, sadistic idea?!]
