# Chapter 226: A Financier Who Can't Tell Stories Isn't a Good Programmer

Mo Xi (of this reincarnation cycle) was indeed writing a script.

He hadn't only just begun writing it now; rather, back when the very first secret realm opened for business, he had already started drafting—though back then it couldn't properly be considered a script, merely some onboarding copy and tutorial guidance.

Mo Xi was [driven like a duck onto a perch](# "Literal CN: 赶鸭子上架 (gǎnyā shàngjià) — forced to do something beyond one's ability or comfort zone; roped into it") as well. After all, whose fault was it that among his Master's seven disciples, not a single one was a humanities student?

No, Second Senior Brother could perhaps count as half of one. It was just that although Qu Qianxing bore the title of top imperial scholar, that head of his had zero connection to fantasy or romance. In the end, this copywriting duty inevitably fell upon the head of the youngest junior brother whom anyone could push around.

—"It still aligns with your major,"-Zhou Hui had evaluated thus:—"How can someone studying finance not know how to tell stories?"

Mo Xi felt this evaluation seemed steeped in thick stereotypes and professional bias. But how could his revered Master ever be such a prejudiced person? Therefore, studying finance (?) presumably truly required knowing how to spin a narrative.

And so, Mo Xi had dutifully begun pulling tall tales out of thin air.

Even so, the previous two secret realms—namely the Thousand Stars Secret Realm that entertained visiting cultivators and the Spirit Stone Secret Realm connecting directly to the Demon Realm—were actually fairly manageable. The volume of text hadn't been large, merely requiring explanations on gameplay mechanics (crossed out) how to utilize the secret realm's facilities and vanquish foes. Only when it came to this Immortal Arts Assembly's trial system depicting the God-Demon War did the required word count balloon exponentially.

Mo Xi not only needed to guide contestants in combat, but also had to popularize historical lore regarding the God-Demon War for everyone, and even devise reasonable plotlines to give participants a sense of "immersion."

Mo Xi felt his head was going bald. He had borrowed several editors from Ran Deng and barely managed to hammer the thing into shape.

Yet the fatal catch was that there was a portion Mo Xi had zero way of delegating to anyone else:

Namely, the dreadful states that the Heavenly Spirit Sect's direct disciples were slated to become in the "prophecy."

Their so-called "boss forms."

This state was not in Zhou Hui's plans, nor did it have anything to do with the God-Demon War; it belonged strictly to the [smuggled private goods](# "Literal CN: 私货 (sīhuò) — contraband/private stash; internet slang for secretly inserting one's personal headcanons, biases, or hidden agendas into an official work") that Mo Xi and the others had stealthily snuck in. Consequently, how to blend it plausibly into the "plot" was a monumental dilemma.

However, things having reached this stage, abandoning it was out of the question.

It wasn't because of the growth of his own strength—truth be told, compared to Guan Ye and the others, Mo Xi's harvest was the lowest. This wasn't due to a lack of competence, but because in the blackened "bad ending," Mo Xi and Mo Bei existed as immortal-skeleton puppet dolls whose source of power was thorough, absolute yin qi—which violently clashed with Mo Xi's current yang qi.

And precisely because of this, Mo Xi realized that perhaps that "prophecy" would never come to pass.

At least, not for him.

Because his Extreme Yin Constitution had already been thoroughly cleansed and inverted. Even if someone crippled his current spirit roots and crafted him into a puppet, it was impossible to revert to his original Extreme Yin Constitution, let alone transform into an immortal-skeleton puppet like that.

Naturally, this did not mean the "future" Mo Bei had witnessed was meaningless.

The explanation Mo Xi leaned toward was that Master, too, had witnessed such a "future." No matter how one looked at it, Master could never sit by and do nothing. No, rather: even if Master hadn't seen such a future, Master would never leave them to their fates. And the remediation of Mo Xi's constitution was one of the "fruits" of Master altering destiny.

Extrapolating from this perspective, Master was shouldering far too much.

And he never said a single word about it!

Reaching this thought, Mo Xi couldn't help but turn his head and glare at his own shadow.

—"Hee-hee, do you want to know what Master knows?"-in a daze, Mo Xi heard his own voice inquiring, the shadow seemingly coming to life.

Perhaps it was that other "himself."

—"No! That neither-male-nor-female freak isn't me at all!"-Mo Xi grumbled under his breath.

He had zero desire to acknowledge it! That "himself" who had inexplicably manifested beside Master that day.

Yet Mo Xi was not the type to flee reality. He clearly recognized that had his Extreme Yin Constitution not been remedied and had he continued growing unchecked, he very likely would have ended up looking just like that.

Moreover, it was also from that point onward that Mo Bei seemed to have undergone a shift.

A positive shift.

That fellow who always lived in her own world had grown proactive. For instance, sneaking their "private stash" into the trial system this time had been Mo Bei's idea.

Even if it was the worst conceivable future, she was now willing to confront it herself.

Therefore, as expected, something must have happened in places unknown to him, right?

Mo Xi despised the feeling of being the only one left behind. Even considering it from this angle, even if he couldn't extract much power from the trial system—not that the power was entirely useless; he could simply pass it to Mo Bei, as yin qi was immensely nourishing for her and could fully compensate for her current deficits in cultivation base—he still had to grasp sufficient information.

Viewed from this angle, even if the blackened future would not come to pass, it was imperative he understand why they could have become like that in the first place.

At the very least, he needed to know who the enemy was...

Wait a moment: in that future, they were fighting the Heavenly Court, weren't they? So the enemy was the Heavenly Court?

Mo Xi sat up straight and gripped his brush.

—"Why go to so much trouble?"-the voice of that other "Mo Xi" sounded once more:—"You could clearly just ask me. I know everything—everything regarding Master, and everything regarding 'me.'"

—"No,"-this time, Mo Xi rejected the offer with crisp finality:—"Matters Master is unwilling to speak of, I can pretend not to know, or I can investigate behind his back. The one thing I will never do is listen to the nonsense of an entity Master doesn't want me coming into contact with."

That day, two "Mo Xis" had existed. Clearly, the matter concerned him, yet Master hadn't offered a single explanation, blatantly not wanting them to have any intersection. If Mo Xi insisted on investigating and insisted on knowing, because it was ultimately "Mo Xi's" own business, Master would likely sigh helplessly and acquiesce in the end—after all, had Master wished to preclude all contact whatsoever, Master never would have let him discover this other self in the first place.

Mo Xi possessed the highest emotional intelligence among the direct disciples (or so he fancied himself); there was no way he couldn't see through Master's thoughts.

The reason he had never gotten to the bottom of it, aside from trusting Master's resolve, was because he wished to play the obedient, well-behaved child to keep Master happy.

Ah, speaking of this petty calculation...

Mo Xi curled his lips, inquiring mockingly:

—"Even if I were to ask you, would you truly be willing to risk displeasing Master just to tell me everything?"

Come to think of it, although that fellow continually emphasized "I know," he had shown zero inclination to proactively reveal anything to Mo Xi—like a bored cat chasing its own tail, merely playing around for amusement.

After all, to Mo Xi, compared to himself, Master's mood came first.

—"Hee..."-the other Mo Xi evidently expressed agreement.

Accompanied by a chuckle of relief, Mo Xi watched his shadow sway, its hue darkening into a deeper shade.

Simultaneously, a surge of power flowed into Mo Xi.

This was not direct raw power like that granted by the trial system, but rather an ethereal tether to the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree. Mo Xi could sense that he was now capable of wielding a portion of the Sacred Tree's spiritual energy, communicating far better with the tree spirits, and engineering the "trial system" with vastly greater ease and finesse.

Without question, this was power originating from that other Mo Xi.

—"Tsk, was that an actual test?"-Mo Xi complained dryly:—"Continuously claiming you're 'me' as well—who ever heard of someone testing themselves?"

Mo Xi would never admit that he himself was just that perverse and awkward!

Another matter also piqued his curiosity:

—"Still, why the ability to commune with the Sacred Tree?"

Looking at that other "himself" and that wretched, half-succumbed-to-demonic-corruption look, he had assumed the other's power would resemble Mo Bei's—all yin qi, or perhaps even demonic qi. He hadn't expected it to be communion with the Sacred Tree.

Naturally, having no inkling that he had undergone repeated reincarnation cycles, Mo Xi was unaware that this "Mo Xi" was the manifestation of the residual silhouette he had left upon the Sacred Tree during the sixth cycle. Having been purified by the Sacred Tree, it naturally would not taint itself with demonic qi; instead, owing to the nature of its manifestation, it could strengthen Mo Xi's connection to the Sacred Tree.

Of course, that Mo Xi knew none of this also meant the other party, true to Master's wishes, had not imparted a shred of memory to the "himself" of this reincarnation cycle, shielding Mo Xi from being tainted by the depravity and despair of bygone lifetimes that might corrupt his "innocence" in this life.

Hm? Did the word "innocence" have anything to do with Mo Xi?

Although Mo Xi himself didn't think so in the slightest, so long as Master thought so, that was all that mattered.

As for power, the "innocent Mo Xi" didn't truly care about where the power came from; he only cared about the results.

Thus, even though he couldn't fathom why the other's power was tied to the Sacred Tree, he still absorbed it without leaving behind a single drop.

Only after Mo Xi had finished absorbing the power did a voice speak:

—"Heh-heh. Is the final resting place for the 'you of this lifetime' to be the Sacred Tree?"-a voice chuckled.

—"My place is forever by Master's side,"-Mo Xi answered without a second thought.

Only after finishing his sentence did he realize that the voice he had just heard was no longer his own, but originated from another "person"—a supreme powerhouse, at that:

—"Lord Ruwang!"-Mo Xi blurted out, leaping up from his chair.

Rarely did he display respect that was so "sincere."

Even though they were all Zhou Hui's "friends"—the likes of Tao Zhu, Peilan, and Jiu Zhai—Mo Xi's attitude toward them, while polite enough, was merely a commercialized, performative courtesy designed to maintain his image as a good, obedient child in Master's eyes. In reality, he looked down on every single one of them.

Ruwang was different. Perhaps because the man was a Ghost Emperor, Mo Xi always harbored a sliver of dread.

Fortunately (?), Mo Xi had never run directly into Ruwang until now.

During this recent period, Ruwang had been residing at the Heavenly Spirit Sect while Mo Xi's true body remained here at the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree. And this time, when Ruwang suddenly appeared, his true body was over by the Genesis Machine; the one conversing with Mo Xi was merely a projection.

Even so, Mo Xi was still somewhat on edge:

—"Why has Your Majesty suddenly descended here?"-Mo Xi asked nervously:—"I heard you went out alongside Master... Ah, is Master well?"

[This brat only harbors feelings for his master and his sister, doesn't he?]

Ruwang thought coldly.

Back during the third reincarnation cycle, after Zhou Hui rescued Mo Bei and Mo Xi, he had fostered them with Ruwang Ghost Emperor. At the time, Mo Xi had taken Ruwang as his foster father—though it was of the extremely [plastic](# "Literal CN: 塑料 (sùliào) — internet slang for superficial, flimsy, or fake relationships (e.g., 'plastic brotherhood')") variety.

Owing to the educational defects of Fallen Immortal Palace, the original Mo Xi suffered from the foul habit of [fawning over the strong while trampling the weak](# "Literal CN: 踩低捧高 (cǎi dī pěng gāo) — snobbishly toadying to superiors while kicking those below"). For instance, back at the Heavenly Spirit Sect, Mo Xi's "bond" had originally been closest with his Eldest Senior Brother—not only because Yuwen Xuan was the strongest, but also because the other senior brothers and sisters had relatively milder tempers if offended, whereas Yuwen Xuan was the one person Mo Xi could never afford to cross. And once sent to Bi'an Tian, a realm swarming with vengeful wraiths, this trait of Mo Xi's grew even more pronounced. Terrified of being reduced to spectral dregs, he had braced himself, clung tenaciously to Ruwang, and insisted on taking him as his foster father.

In truth, Ruwang had doted on Mo Bei and Mo Xi quite a bit; after all, Untiring had sent them over.

Yet in his eyes, both of these little brats were cold-blooded and unfeeling. It was just that one wore ['stay the hell away from me'](# "Literal CN: 莫挨老娘 (mò āi lǎoniáng) — internet slang: 'don't touch this old lady / keep your hands off me'") plastered across her face, while the other babbled "Foster Father" this and "Foster Father" that with his mouth while scheming who knew what in his heart. These two personalities were far too prevalent in Bi'an Tian, where ghosts abounded and living humans were scarce—so prevalent that it annoyed him. Neither was nearly as endearing as the honest, earnest Guan Ye. Thus, his affection for these two little runts leaned more toward how one treated pet animals: giving them whatever they asked for, but refraining from teaching them too much.

—Nor was it appropriate to teach them.

Although Zhou Hui sending Mo Bei and Mo Xi to Bi'an Tian was partly due to their Extreme Yin Constitutions, he had by no means wished for them to lean into the "world's stereotypes" and cultivate the Extreme Yin Constitution into dark infamy. Rather, he had hoped Ruwang would help the two brats suppress the negative side-effects of that constitution. Hence, Ruwang hadn't dared impart his complete mantras to them.

Ruwang didn't know whether it was due to this disparate treatment that, in that lifetime, Mo Bei and Mo Xi were ultimately lured away by Yuwen Xuan to deal with Qu Qianxing.

Because of this, Ruwang always felt somewhat guilty toward his former master for failing to properly tutor the children he had sent. Yet at times Ruwang also thought that without Untiring's constraints, had he simply subjected the twins—especially Mo Xi—to a thorough course of discipline according to Bi'an Tian's ghost-taming principles, even if the brat weren't genuinely well-behaved, he would have been beaten into behaving.

In this lifetime, upon hearing that Mo Bei and Mo Xi were actually yin spirits, Ruwang felt this conviction even more strongly.

However, upon laying eyes on the Mo Xi of this cycle, an inward exclamation of surprise escaped Ruwang first. He noticed that Mo Xi no longer possessed an Extreme Yin Constitution. Yet pondering it closely, it wasn't surprising in the least:

[It seems Master Untiring truly wore his heart out fretting over this child!]

Regrettably, the Mo Xi of this cycle was not under his purview, so Ruwang gave no indication of it.

Ruwang didn't answer Mo Xi's question either, inquiring merely:

—"I hear you have written a play script?"

—"A play script?"-Mo Xi blinked in confusion. He wondered who had mentioned this to Ruwang, yet didn't dare ask outright, responding merely:—"Does Your Majesty mean the scenario scripts for the 'trial system'?"

—"Show Zhen—"-Ruwang demanded.

[Why did a homework inspector suddenly pop out of nowhere?!]

[A portion of my script cannot be seen by others—especially anyone connected to Master!]

Mo Xi thought nervously.

His large eyes rolled around, yet he did not refuse:

—"Certainly. I shall have them deliver the scripts over right away,"-Mo Xi said with a sunny, sweet smile, preparing to fetch the onboarding tutorials penned by others to brush him off.

Unfortunately, Ruwang understood Mo Xi far too well:

—"I want the one you wrote,"-Ruwang countered.

—"Eh?!"-Mo Xi instinctively shielded his glowing screen:—"I... I haven't written much of anything."

Seeing him like this, Ruwang understood:

—"Rest assured, Zhen will not tell your master what mischief you are up to this time,"-Ruwang said:—"Zhen merely wishes to ensure your script contains nothing that would harm yourselves or cause your master sorrow."

As for whether the brat courted death too excessively, prompting Untiring to discipline his disciple in person, Ruwang couldn't care less.

Hearing this, Mo Xi's expression flickered:

—"I'm not up to any mischief,"-Mo Xi protested.

Why did he get the nagging feeling that Ruwang already knew about what they were cooking up?

Yet hearing Ruwang's tone, it seemed he was genuinely considering Master's well-being, leaving Mo Xi somewhat hesitant.

—"Nor could my script possibly harm myself..."-as Mo Xi spoke, he abruptly realized something:—"Or could it be... that new 'server' Master just linked to the Langhuan Library possesses some special functionality?"

Ruwang cast Mo Xi an appreciative glance.

Although he didn't particularly care for Mo Xi's personality, this sharp cleverness of his was undeniably endearing.

Indeed. To Untiring, linking the Genesis Machine with the Langhuan Library was merely a contingency to secure his own access route to the Genesis Machine. Yet as long as that machine possessed even a sliver of power, it could turn the most preposterous script in Mo Xi's hands into reality.

Ruwang had come to audit the script precisely for this reason. If it was sufficiently intriguing, he wouldn't mind giving it a push; but if Mo Xi took things too far, he would intervene.

And when it touched upon potential hazards, Mo Xi didn't dare fool around either. Reluctantly, he surrendered the script:

—"However, mine isn't finished yet..."-Mo Xi admitted.

Rather, he felt fortunate it wasn't finished. Because he hadn't yet had time to insert too much plot regarding his blackened senior brothers and sisters, and Ruwang Ghost Emperor wasn't overly familiar with them as disciples, he should be able to muddle through... presumably?

Ruwang took the script with a half-smile. Flipping through several pages, he uttered a surprised "Oh?":

—"Surprisingly rational, isn't it?"-Ruwang remarked.

—"O-Of course it is,"-Mo Xi replied nervously:—"For any system, the most important thing is balance! Otherwise, there'd be far too many bugs."

These words caused Ruwang to glance at Mo Xi once more in surprise.

The Mo Xi he was familiar with was greedy, willful, and constantly demanding everything; he hadn't imagined that one day such words would emerge from the boy's mouth.

Ruwang glanced at the bewildered Mo Xi, then looked down at the script in his hand:

—"Operating according to this is remarkably coherent. If things go well, it might genuinely be achievable..."-Ruwang murmured softly.

[—"Making the future transpire in the past..."](# "Speaker: Ruwang Ghost Emperor")
