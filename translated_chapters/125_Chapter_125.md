# Chapter 125: The Trial of Ascension (第125章 升仙考验)

[—"Baaa?"](# "Speaker: Little Black Lamb")

The little black lamb's ears twitched.

Sensing something, it glanced back up the staircase, wondering why its junior sister had not descended yet.

However, that thought did not occupy much real estate in its little head. In a corner not far away, a colossal mushroom with an enormous pink cap caught its eye.

The mushroom stood as tall as an adult man, fleshy and plump, gleaming with a glossy sheen. The combination of a vibrant pink cap and a snowy white stalk looked utterly delectable.

Granted, growing in a place like this made it rather suspicious, to say the least.

The little black lamb crept forward cautiously, sniffing with vigilance, and then abruptly unhinged its jaws. Before the mushroom could even react, it swallowed the entire thing down in a single gulp.

—"Bleeegh... Ptui, ptui!"-the little black lamb spat violently the very next second.-"What on earth is this?! It tastes horrific, ptui!"

What it spat out was not mushroom flesh at all, but a puddle of foul, reeking black water.

To scour its palate clean, the little black lamb had no choice but to bite into the adjacent stone wall, chewing the rock slabs into pulverized gravel and spitting them out along with the vile black slurry.

Rarely was a Taotie—a beast known to devour literally anything—disgusted to such an extreme.

Zhou Hui could not help but glance over at it:

—"Didn't I tell you not to eat random things off the ground?"-Zhou Hui scolded.

—"Blargh!"-The little black lamb stuck out its tongue, tears welling in its eyes as it looked up at its Master.-"Master, my mouth feels terrible!"

Zhou Hui sighed.

Rummaging through his storage pouch, he retrieved a tin of mint-flavored medicinal paste, tossed it directly into his eldest disciple's maw, and pinched the scruff of its neck:

—"Don't swallow it, and don't spit it out."

Issuing the command, he transfigured a miniature battle-axe into a toothbrush, shoved it into his eldest disciple's mouth, and cast a cantrip to set the toothbrush vigorously scrubbing away at the black lamb's teeth, churning up a thick froth of dark-green bubbles.

Holding the toothbrushing little black lamb by the scruff with one hand, Zhou Hui raised his other hand and drew a crackling arc of heavenly lightning from the tip of his index finger, flicking it down upon the pool of black water.

Yet the black water did not dissipate; instead, it emitted an agonized, curdling groan.

—"Oh?"-Zhou Hui murmured, slightly surprised.

He summoned a white gourd and drew the black liquid inside, sealing it away.

—"Master, wha' i' thi' shtuff?"-the little black lamb asked, struggling through the foaming brush in its jaws.

—"It feels somewhat similar to an extract of negative emotions from a yin spirit, yet diverges from the ordinary; it appears capable of obscuring cognition,"-Zhou Hui deduced aloud.-"I once observed something resembling this on Xi'er. When I examined it back then, I assumed it was merely an innate facet of a yin spirit. But seeing that even heavenly thunder cannot eradicate it, matters are evidently far less simple than that."

Zhou Hui furrowed his brows in annoyance.

In a world where the Periodic Table of Elements was utterly useless, every material was an exotic substance unto itself. Even someone of the Untiring Immortal Venerable's vast erudition could not possibly identify every compound in existence.

In circumstances like this, one might have to resort to rather unconventional taxonomic methodologies to isolate the anomaly.

Pondering for a moment, Zhou Hui asked Yuwen Xuan:

—"Speaking of which, what is your impression of Mo Bei and Mo Xi? Do they seem delicious to you?"

The little black lamb's eyes instantly lit up:

—"You're giving me permission to—*glub-glub-bzzzt!*"-Its sentence dissolved into the whir of the toothbrush drilling against its teeth like an electric jackhammer.

—"Think carefully before you answer,"-Zhou Hui said with a gentle smile.

[So I still can't eat them!]

Wrenching free of the abhorrent toothbrush at last, the little black lamb drooped its ears:

—"I've always identified as a human! How could I possibly look upon newly arrived junior brothers and sisters and want to eat them? Probably because of that... I've never harbored that impulse toward those two."

—"That certainly wasn't your attitude toward Shulong,"-Zhou Hui noted.

—"He wasn't human! He didn't even appear before me in human form; he appeared looking like food!"-Mentioning this made the little black lamb want to weep.-"He was clearly an egg! An edible egg...!"

Tears began plinking down its snout once more.

—"Fine, setting Shulong aside, you haven't considered eating any of your other junior siblings, have you?"-Zhou Hui asked offhandedly.

—"Ah, well, Second Junior Brother looks the tastiest, second only to Little Fifth,"-the little black lamb mused.-"Next would be Youngest Junior Sister. Fourth Junior Brother falls somewhere in the middle—his meat quality is actually quite good, but that spine you grafted into his back, Master, completely ruins the appetite."

[...You've literally ranked them all on a gourmet deliciousness scale, and you still claim to identify as human?!]

Zhou Hui was about to give it a piece of his mind, but a sudden disturbance elsewhere pulled his attention away.

His divine sense detected that his third disciple seemed to have stepped off the stairway.

—In a treacherous place like the Abyssal Ocean, Zhou Hui naturally would never let his disciples wander off unsupervised. Both Yuwen Xuan and Ran Deng bore his divine sense imprints, allowing him to perceive their whereabouts at all times.

He had not expected the precaution to prove useful so quickly.

—"What is A-Ran doing?"-Zhou Hui murmured.

Zhou Hui unfurled a light screen, displaying Ran Deng walking slowly down a cramped alley flanked by twisted buildings.

—"No wonder she was taking so long!"-Delighted that someone else had diverted Master's reprimand, Yuwen Xuan piped up brightly.-"Wandering off in a place like this—she's being far too reckless!"

—"No, she isn't wandering aimlessly,"-Zhou Hui said, watching the palace lantern hovering ahead of Ran Deng.-"The fact that the lantern is drifting in that direction indicates that path is the 'correct road' for A-Ran."

—"But in our eyes, the only correct place is by your side, Master!"-Yuwen Xuan declared.

—"What sort of warped mindset is that? Stop pinning your entire futures onto your master. Your paths can only be chosen by yourselves,"-Zhou Hui chided, swatting his eldest disciple across the head.

Yuwen Xuan drooped his ears. A defiant pout crept onto his ovine face, though it was largely concealed by black fleece.

Zhou Hui had no time to lecture his eldest disciple right now; he gazed at his third disciple with a furrowed brow of concern.

—"Furthermore, from the looks of it, Ran Deng seems to be undergoing a trial,"-Zhou Hui mused aloud.

[—"A trial?"](# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan")

—"Didn't you notice?"-Zhou Hui countered.-"One of the materials comprising the Ascension Stairway is remarkably similar to the main thoroughfare of White Jade Capital... Or rather, the reverse: historically speaking, the main avenue of White Jade Capital was modeled after the Ascension Stairway. Stepping upon it triggers a trial akin to a Heart-Questioning Stone, interrogating one's true inclinations. Only those with unwavering wills who have clearly perceived their foundational convictions can walk to the very end."

In that regard, it aligned seamlessly with the folklore surrounding the Ascension Stairway.

According to legend, while anyone was theoretically capable of ascending to immortality via the Ascension Stairway, mortals who embarked upon the climb would encounter all manner of trials on the steps. One relatively famous tale told of a tyrant who believed himself worthy of standing shoulder-to-shoulder with true immortals. Yet he lacked even the stamina and physical endurance to ascend the stairway on his own two feet. Thus, he ordered his subjects to carry his palanquin up to the upper realm. After sacrificing the lives of countless commoners, ministers, aristocrats, and even his own imperial sons, the tyrant lost his mind to grandiose delusions on the final flight of steps, plunging from the heavenly stairs and splattering into a bloody paste.

Granted, it was merely one of those ubiquitous, boilerplate folk parables. Yet it held true that immortals in ancient folklore adored using such devices to cross-examine mortal hearts—so much so that many cultivators installed similar contraptions within their own secret realms.

Even Zhou Hui possessed pedagogical tools like the "Heart-Questioning Stone."

Consequently, the moment his feet had touched the Ascension Stairway, Zhou Hui had detected the peculiarities in its masonry, already wondering when his own "trial" would begin.

Yet the "trial" had conspicuously failed to materialize.

—"I assumed the Ascension Stairway had lost its efficacy,"-Zhou Hui muttered under his breath.-"After all, ascension is currently impossible... Besides, we are currently walking *downstairs*, aren't we? Don't the legends state that one must walk *upward* to face the trial?"

Yet Ran Deng had clearly fallen under its sway.

Or had he miscalculated? Perhaps because he had already cleared the main avenue of White Jade Capital, the trial was rendered moot for him?

Zhou Hui fell into contemplation.

—"Who knows what that girl is daydreaming about,"-the little black lamb said, wagging its head.-"These junior brothers and sisters are simply never worry-free."

[The one who gives me the most headaches is clearly you.]

—"Hush. This is a marvelous opportunity for A-Ran,"-Zhou Hui scolded. Even if ascension was off the table, [clarifying one's heart and beholding one's true nature](# "Literal CN: 明心见性 (míngxīn jiànxìng) — a Zen Buddhist and Daoist concept referring to perceiving one's original enlightened nature") was an indispensable step in cultivation. After weathering this, Ran Deng's mental state would advance by leaps and bounds, making it prime time for her to attempt breaking through her cultivation realm.

The only issue was the timing.

At any other juncture, Zhou Hui would be ecstatic that his disciple had encountered such a trial. But running into it right now was slightly unfortunate, for Ran Deng was simultaneously undergoing another 'exam':

Zhou Hui had tasked her with refining the palace lantern.

With two exams stacked on top of each other, it seemed A-Ran's odds of keeping that lantern intact were looking rather grim.

Nevertheless, Zhou Hui deemed this opportunity far too rare to disrupt her trial.

Considering the extenuating circumstances, perhaps he could arrange a [make-up exam](# "Literal CN: 补考 (bǔkǎo) — retake / make-up examination") for her later? Patriarch Jiuzhai would certainly nag his ear off, though—that old charlatan loved harping on about 'serendipity,' and would surely insist that failing this time was fate, meaning there would be no second chance.

As Zhou Hui brainstormed how to talk Patriarch Jiuzhai into it, his gaze suddenly snapped to his eldest disciple:

—"Why weren't you affected either?"

—"Why *should* I be affected?"-Sensing impending doom, the little black lamb thrashed in panic.-"I don't want to take any exams!"

—"Is it because your brain is simply too primitive?"-Zhou Hui asked, dangling the little black lamb and giving it a gentle shake. He felt as though he had overlooked something:

Upon the Ascension Stairway, all living beings were equal; all had to undergo the trial, so long as they possessed life—

*Splat!*

Zhou Hui abruptly released his grip, sending the little black lamb tumbling four limbs splayed onto the stone floor.

Scrambling to its hooves, it shook its head to lodge a heated protest, only to see its Master pull a storage pouch from his sleeve. Inverting it, the pouch proved entirely empty—save for a musky ovine stench it would never forget for the rest of its life:

Its very own scent.

The little black lamb's entire face crumpled:

—"That wretched little thing escaped again?! Wait a second... could it be undergoing the Ascension Stairway's trial right now? If it fails, will it die?"-That final question trembled with unadulterated hope.

—"According to folklore, those who failed the ascent plummeted from the stairway and dashed themselves to death. Though given that this segment is buried subterraneanly, whether it would splatter is difficult to say,"-Zhou Hui noted calmly.-"On the other hand, should it pass the trial, it is exceedingly likely to achieve full human form—and perhaps entirely replace you. At the very least, the Celestial Stairway selected it, not you."

The little black lamb's eyes bulged wide.

—"Considering how much junk you've devoured lately, you're drifting further and further away from human form, aren't you?"-Zhou Hui delivered the brutal reality check.-"Whereas that child stayed tucked inside the storage pouch, eating nothing. It will likely attain human form far swifter than you... Come to think of it, once it assumes human form, it won't be incapable of understanding human speech. Why don't I simply appoint it as my Eldest Disciple instead?"

—"...I-I know you're bluffing, Master! You're just tricking me into taking the exam!"-the little black lamb wailed, tears streaming uncontrollably down its fleece.-"I'm not falling for it!"

—"So you're not going?"-Zhou Hui asked placidly.

The little black lamb locked eyes with its Master for several agonizing seconds. With an indignant whip of its head, it bolted frantically down the stairs in the direction of Ran Deng.

*

Ran Deng had no inkling that her Master was already drafting a make-up exam for her.

Following behind the palace lantern, she advanced slowly between the tilted structures, scanning her surroundings with extreme caution.

Yet after walking for nearly half a shichen, Ran Deng had still failed to locate that black lamb. Were it not for the palace lantern continuing to drift into the depths of the alleyway, she would have assumed her eyes were playing tricks on her.

Ran Deng muttered to herself:

—"As expected, I should have gone to find Master first..."

*Bang!*

—"Who's there?!"-Ran Deng gasped, startled by the abrupt commotion.

Catching a shadowy figure flitting across her peripheral vision, she decisively hurled a bolt of lightning at it. Under normal circumstances, Ran Deng rarely wielded lightning arts. But since Master had just brought down heavenly thunder, she reasoned that lightning techniques would carry the greatest deterrent effect against entities in this subterranean abyss, so lightning it was.

Sure enough, as the lightning bolt slammed into the sloped pavement, the figure tottered out of concealment, trembling uncontrollably.

It was a cultivator merely at the Foundation Establishment stage.

Yet he wore a black face mask obscuring half his countenance, seamless black soft armor across his torso, and leather gloves upon his hands—swaddled so tightly from head to toe that he looked suspicious to the highest degree.

Catching sight of Ran Deng, the man prostrated himself in terror:

—"Spare me, Fairy! Mercy!"

—"Who are you?"-Ran Deng interrogated.

—"T-This humble one is a resident here,"-the man replied, his eyes darting frantically. He was unwilling to surrender even a name; one glance told anyone he was concealing something.

Ran Deng narrowed her eyes, scrutinizing him:

—"From Carefree Palace?"

—"H-How did you know?!"-the man stammered, his face draining of color.

—"I wasn't certain before, but I am now—this place truly belongs to Carefree Palace!"-Ran Deng sneered coldly.

In any case, an engineering feat of this magnitude beneath the Abyssal Ocean could only be financed by a handful of entities in this realm. Guessing Carefree Palace carried at least an eighty percent hit rate.

Yet the man reacted as though he had accidentally spilled a catastrophic state secret, kneeling upon the flagstones with a face like a mourner at a funeral.

Seeing his utterly miserable demeanor, Ran Deng was about to press him further when another series of sounds thudded from nearby:

*Thud!*

*Thud!*

*Thud, thud!*

This time she heard it clearer—it sounded like impacts striking a surface, echoing from a building not far away, originating from deep underground.

—"What is that?"-Ran Deng asked.

—"...Probably the children?"-the man answered after a hesitant pause.

—"Children?"-Ran Deng's eyes widened.

—"Yes. Since you already know this is Carefree Palace, you can probably deduce what they do down here,"-the man answered listlessly.-"Beneath that building, several children are locked away. Their overseers were likely eliminated by the Immortal Venerable's heavenly thunder earlier. With the guards gone, they are probably trying to break out."

—"Then what were you doing? Bolting them in?"-Ran Deng asked, eyeing him suspiciously.

She was not particularly worried about this man.

After all, what her Master had summoned earlier was authentic heavenly thunder.

Generally speaking, unless one was a high-tier cultivator with special defensive means, heavenly thunder was an impartial arbiter far stricter than any mortal judge.

Truly vile, irredeemable scoundrels could never survive a baptism of heavenly thunder. For this man to stand here completely unscathed proved he had yet to commit any deed that defied the Heavenly Dao—or rather, defied common moral decency.

Yet this man appeared to be on the verge of doing exactly that:

—"I... I was preparing to kill them,"-the man stammered.

[—"Excuse me?!"](# "Speaker: Ran Deng")

—"B-Because these children are entirely different from the ones the Immortal Venerable rescued from Fallen Immortal Palace! Their very birth was a sin!"-the man answered in a panicked rush.-"If they are released, they will swiftly become part of the Abyssal Ocean, or turn into something even more wicked!"

—"...Wait a moment. You've mentioned my Master twice now. You know who I am?"-Ran Deng finally caught the anomaly in his words.

The man shrank his shoulders, looking even more desolate:

—"Years ago, when the Immortal Venerable purged Fallen Immortal Palace, this humble one was present as well."

—"Fallen Immortal Palace... Ah, the time with Bei'er and the others?"-Ran Deng muttered to herself, staring at the man with an even more bizarre expression.-"Master spared your life back then, and you still went and joined Carefree Palace?!"

—"No! I originally belonged to Fallen Immortal Palace and had no idea about Carefree Palace! So after that incident... after the Immortal Venerable's divine retribution, I was on my way to seek refuge with relatives. Midway there, I was captured by envoys sent by Carefree Palace to investigate the wreckage, and promptly exiled down here!"

—"You are truly unlucky,"-Ran Deng remarked with heartfelt sympathy.

—"I feel the same way,"-the man wept.

[Clearly, surviving wasn't good luck at all!]

Yet Ran Deng had no desire to reminisce with him. Straightening her expression, she demanded:

—"What is going on with these children you mentioned? Explain in greater detail."

—"Those children..."-The unlucky wretch—no, Ah Huang—stammered, wearing a conflicted, agonizing expression.

Truth be told, Ah Huang bitterly regretted not finding a hole to hide in instead of doing "unnecessary deeds."

Still, perhaps it counted as lucky (?) that the one who suddenly appeared was not the Untiring Immortal Venerable himself, who hated evil like an enemy (Ah Huang's ingrained stereotype—whenever he had nightmares about the Immortal Venerable, the sky was always blotted out by apocalyptic lightning, a lifelong trauma). Instead, it was an apparently gentle fairy accompanying him.

—This was also why Ah Huang had never dared show his face before the Untiring Immortal Venerable. Even though he had heard the commotion years ago and knew the Immortal Venerable had rescued the children trafficked by Fallen Immortal Palace, as a "member of Fallen Immortal Palace," Ah Huang had been terrified that the Immortal Venerable would lump him together with the culprits and vaporize him. Lacking the courage to rebel and simply treating the job as an honest day's labor, he was indeed an accomplice by association. Ah Huang was honestly baffled as to why he hadn't been struck dead by lightning on either occasion.

Yet hiding this time was utterly pointless anyway.

This was the Abyssal Ocean! The deepest trenches of the Abyssal Ocean, where even Void Refinement powerhouses were not guaranteed to walk away in one piece!

He had been stationed here for several years, strictly adhering to the "work duties" assigned by Carefree Palace, never venturing anywhere beyond what was required. Although playing it safe had kept him alive all these years, Ah Huang knew that with the rest of Carefree Palace's personnel wiped out, a cultivator of his pathetic cultivation could never survive out there even if he managed to escape.

Conversely, even if he hid until Carefree Palace's "rescue reinforcements" arrived, being the sole survivor while everyone else lay dead would almost certainly earn him a death sentence anyway.

Thus, Ah Huang had stepped out, resolving to do the only "good deed" he could manage in his entire life.

At this thought, Ah Huang let out a hopeless sigh and confessed truthfully:

—"I am merely a servant tasked with feeding them; I know very little. I only vaguely overheard colleagues say that those children aren't human, but crossbred abominations of various demons and monsters. Carefree Palace rears them down here until they reach a certain stage of growth before taking them out..."

Speaking as he walked, he led Ran Deng toward a certain corner of the building complex.

Ran Deng glanced at the palace lantern. Seeing that the lantern was drifting along with him, she followed unhurriedly behind.

To Ran Deng's surprise, this "Carefree Palace servant" did not lead her directly into the containment chamber. Instead, he brought her to a secluded corner of the back annex and gestured toward a fissure in the floor, indicating she look down.

—"According to the 'rules,' I am forbidden from entering. Frankly, I think it's better not to go in anyway,"-Ah Huang explained.-"However, while cleaning utensils, I stumbled upon the fact that one can catch a glimpse of the interior from this crack..."

To be precise, Ah Huang's former colleagues had loved peeping through this very fissure. While Ah Huang knew about it, he made a conscious effort to stay far away from such things, instinctively sensing that peeking too much would cause him to vanish without a trace just like those colleagues.

It had been a wise decision.

Ran Deng shot a disgusted glance at the peeping crack, having no foolish intention of pressing her eye directly against it. Retrieving a candle from her pouch, she ignited it; the flickering candlelight cast a stark, vivid projection of the subterranean scene below.

It was... pure purgatory!

Hybrids of countless races, shapes, ages, and grotesque deformities were cultivated like [gu insects](# "Literal CN: 蛊虫 (gǔchóng) — venomous creatures bred in sealed jars to devour each other until only the most lethal survives") within the karst cavern below, swarming densely in squirming clusters. A single glance nearly made Ran Deng heave.

Most terrifying of all, sprawled directly beneath the fissure Ah Huang had pointed out was an apparently boneless child, peering upward through the crack. Had Ran Deng pressed her face against the gap, her gaze would have collided dead-on with those abyssal, pitch-black pupils!

Ran Deng retreated several steps, severing line of sight.

The child giggled, a chilling, eerie titter, and attempted to wedge a pale, boneless hand through the narrow slit:

—"Big Sister, come play with us~?"

Only then did Ah Huang realize there was a child right there. Yelping in terror, he collapsed backward onto the floor.

[Indeed, refusing to look was 100% the right call!]

Ran Deng took a long moment to steady her breathing and settle her stomach before turning to Ah Huang:

—"So you believe these children should not be allowed to live?"

—"I don't know, I just didn't want..."-Ah Huang said.-"Even if I do nothing, they will still die... I overheard my supervisor mention that this facility must never be discovered by outsiders, nor can anyone be permitted to seize them. Thus, a magical treasure in the Hall Master's residence monitors the entire complex. If the overseers perish, then after a certain period—though I don't know the exact timeframe—every creature bred here will enter a frenzied state of mutual slaughter until they slaughter every living being in sight!"

—"And so you resolved to kill them before they tear each other apart?"-Ran Deng asked.

—"Since death is inevitable, wouldn't a quick death be better...?"-Ah Huang said.-"Besides, I truly believe that if any of them were to escape, it would be catastrophic. Horrifically catastrophic."

—"Mmh,"-Ran Deng lapsed into silence.

She glanced back toward the fissure, from which echoed the horrific, lilting laughter of the child.

[No!]

[This situation is completely beyond my pay grade!]

[Go find Master!]

[Only Master is qualified to decide!]

[But... this man said there is a time limit!]

[I'm still carrying this palace lantern, and my speed is painfully slow. By the time I locate Master, these children will have already slaughtered each other—or worse, broken out.]

[In any case, they aren't human. It's not a big deal if a few die.]

[Not to mention they are such a horrific brood of monsters.]

[Just ignore it and go find Master...]

Ran Deng let out a heavy sigh.

Looking dispirited, she asked Ah Huang:

—"Where is the residence of that Hall Master you mentioned?"

Ah Huang looked at Ran Deng in shock.

Her choice... Did she intend to save these children?

But they weren't even human!

Ah Huang paused, his throat dry:

—"I... I don't know."

If he knew, he would not have chosen to euthanize them, but would have pondered how to disable the berserk protocol.

—"What a royal pain! I hate dealing with trouble, and I don't want to walk anymore,"-Ran Deng grumbled.

Ah Huang's eyes dimmed with disappointment.

Then he watched in bewilderment as the fairy turned to address her floating lantern:

—"...Do you know the way? Or do you think it's better to go find Master too?"

The straw-woven palace lantern bobbed up and down, as though nodding.

It began drifting toward a specific direction.

—"Damn it all,"-Ran Deng sighed.

Taking a few steps behind the palace lantern, she glanced back curiously at Ah Huang:

—"Aren't you coming?"

—"No. I will stay here. If they go mad... Ah, not that I believe you will fail, Fairy!"-Ah Huang stammered.

—"...You're actually a pretty decent guy,"-Ran Deng said with a faint smile.

Leaving behind the thoroughly baffled Ah Huang, Ran Deng finally rallied a shred of spirit, continuing her pursuit behind the palace lantern.
