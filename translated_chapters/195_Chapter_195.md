# Chapter 195: Accidents Will Always Happen

To say Guan Ye's luck was simply bad would be an understatement; it was downright abysmal.

—"Why on earth did you have to run into a one-in-a-hundred-billion probability?!"-Mo Xi shrieked.

At this moment, Mo Xi and Guan Ye were still lingering atop the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, undertaking the final maintenance of the Sacred Tree's pedagogical system.

This had been Guan Ye's personal initiative.

In his view, progress in artifact refinement came from everyday diligence and accumulation; [cramming at the eleventh hour](# "Literal CN: 临时抱佛脚 (lín shí bào fó jiǎo) — 'hugging the Buddha's feet in an emergency'; cramming at the eleventh hour") for a qualification examination was utterly meaningless. Staying behind to maintain the pedagogical system, on the other hand, killed two birds with one stone: on one hand, expanding the Sacred Tree system involved processing 'hardware', which required refining certain specialized materials—serving as a solid review of foundational artifact refinement knowledge points; on the other hand, it allowed him early hands-on exposure to the yin spirit 'cloud server' Master planned to construct, preparing him thoroughly for the next phase.

As someone cursed with trash spirit roots, Guan Ye required far more effort and time than anyone else just to keep pace with the others. Consequently, his study schedules and work agendas had always pursued the utmost in quality and efficiency.

In truth, at least prior to yesterday, Guan Ye had indeed achieved his objective:

While assisting the demon race in completing the construction of their study and examination system, he had maintained frequent, controlled contact with the immortal remains and yin spirits in small, repeated doses. By refining off-cut scraps of the immortal remains, he had reviewed the entire workflow of artifact refinement from start to finish, while gaining a comprehensive understanding and preview of the prerequisite items needed for the 'cloud server' Master planned to build alongside him.

Yes, everything had progressed without a hitch—prior to yesterday.

Just as the demon race's learning system had reached preliminary completion, poised for its first major open beta:

Be it this qualification examination or the opening ceremony of the Immortal Arts Assembly, both were scheduled to open up a 'livestream' for the demon race, enabling the demons to participate in online interactive features.

In other words, the qualification examination was slated to commence on this very day. Zhou Hui was supposed to teleport directly to the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree right before the exam to pick up Guan Ye and escort him to the venue.

As for Mo Xi and Mo Bei, they were to remain on the Sacred Tree: Mo Xi would monitor and maintain the newly deployed system, while Mo Bei would be responsible for supervising (crossed out) safeguarding Mo Xi's personal safety.

At least, that had been the original plan.

Yet at this critical juncture, the 'system'—which had passed repeated stress tests without a single glitch—encountered an anomalous bug.

The fortunate news was that the system was currently under maintenance, scheduled to open officially to the demon populace only at noon. Aside from a cluster of Wood Spirits currently maintaining the architecture, no laymen (demons) had been caught in this glitch.

The bad news was that Guan Ye was standing squarely at ground zero of this anomalous bug.

His light-screen had turned completely blue. The jade slips beside him used for auxiliary logging were covered in mangled, scrambled gibberish. Worst of all, dozens of translucent hands were reaching straight out of the tree trunk behind the light-screen—which served as the biological 'computer' hosting the demon learning system. Some clawed at Guan Ye's arms, some yanked on his robes, some clung to his waist, some curiously tugged at his exposed, tail-like external spine, and others had clamped directly onto his throat.

It was only thanks to Guan Ye's lightning-fast reflexes—snapping open a barrier of spiritual energy around himself the instant the ambush struck—that those hands were prevented from applying true force, narrowly saving him from being torn limb from limb.

Wrestling against the spectral grip, Guan Ye glared daggers at Mo Xi:

—"A one-in-a-hundred-billion probability? In other words, you *knew* this bug existed?!"-Guan Ye demanded.

—"Uh, well, the odds were minuscule..."-Mo Xi mumbled under his breath, scrambling to justify himself.-"It was because memory was running tight, so I borrowed a tiny chunk from the unprocessed yin spirits over there. Theoretically, with odds that microscopic, it should almost never trigger. Besides, thanks to the sheer numerical superiority of the demon population, this portion of yin spirits would naturally be purified anyway, saving us the trouble of pre-processing. So..."

—"What kind of garbage did you learn in probability theory?!"-Guan Ye barked.-"Probability is nothing more than a macroscopic statistic! For the poor bastard who gets hit by it, it's a hundred percent!"

Hence, gacha drop rates in games were mostly 'pseudo-probabilities'.

From an individual perspective, even with a fifty percent drop rate, there was still a chance of drawing absolutely nothing ten times in a row. This compelled even the most [scummy game designers](# "Literal CN: 狗策划 (gǒu cèhuà) — 'dog designers/planners'; common gaming slang for predatory or devious game developers") to implement systems where, if a player failed to pull a prize after a fixed number of rolls, the drop rate was forcefully dialed up—though some hopeless souls still failed to hit—or the prize was simply [handed out as a pity drop](# "Literal CN: 白给 (bái gěi) — 'given for free'; gaming slang for a pity handout or completely wasted effort").

Recalling that Mo Xi was also in charge of balancing the system values for Qianxing Hall and the secret realm at the Demon Realm entrance, Guan Ye felt a surge of profound dread regarding the reward payout mechanics of those secret realms.

—"Still, I figured an ordinary person wouldn't be *that* unlucky,"-Mo Xi muttered.

Even now, Mo Xi didn't truly believe he was at fault... Even if an error had been made, the only mistake was letting Fourth Senior Brother run the tests.

Where in the world could one find a flawless program? Especially under such a tight deadline! Pushing low-probability minor bugs to the backlog for post-launch patching was standard operating procedure for any scummy designer.

It was just that Mo Xi had noticed long ago: whenever some bizarre bug that had no business existing reared its head, the probability of it triggering skyrocketed the moment Fourth Senior Brother operated the controls...

Not only in programs, but in reality as well.

If Eldest Senior Brother pulled a prank by stuffing a handful of blisteringly spicy dumplings into a batch, the one who ended up biting into them was almost always Fourth Senior Brother. (As an aside, if spirit stones were hidden inside the dumplings instead, Lu Shulong would find them a hundred percent of the time; the auspicious fortune of the Dragon race was simply invincible.)

Of course, had Guan Ye been a lucky man, the tragedies of his past life would never have transpired in the first place.

Still, while Guan Ye's luck was poor, it had never been outrageously obvious—merely hovering around the level of a somewhat unfortunate normal human being, well within the baseline average among the Heavenly Spirit Sect's roster of hard-luck cases.

For a bug with such astronomical odds to actually trigger was something even Mo Xi hadn't anticipated.

—"Fourth Senior Brother, I'm coming to save you!"-Mo Xi shouted in a panic. While furiously attempting to sever the program execution (the attack vectors of the yin spirits), he shouted to the 'bodyguard' beside him:-"Hurry up and help!"

Mo Bei required no prompting. The very moment those spectral hands had emerged, she had already unsheathed her flexible soft sword and lashed out.

Unfortunately, her strikes proved completely ineffective against ten-thousand-year-old yin spirits.

Her blade failed to sever the spirits' limbs, nor did it freeze their movements. Instead, it coated Guan Ye's skin in a fine sheet of frost, chilling him so thoroughly that he broke into violent shivers.

—"L-Little Junior Sister, hold on... pause for a second,"-Guan Ye stammered, wrestling to control his chattering teeth.-"Your sword... is too cold... I can't take it."

—"...? Isn't it the yin spirits that are cold?"-Mo Bei didn't believe she was in the wrong either.

She had been 'frozen' by these yin spirits before and knew their bone-piercing chill intimately. She was convinced Fourth Senior Brother had merely conflated the corrosion of the yin spirits with her frost qi.

Lifting her sword, Mo Bei called out to Guan Ye:

—"Endure it for just a moment, Fourth Senior Brother. It'll be over soon,"-Mo Bei insisted.

—"No! It's not the yin spirits, it's you!"-Guan Ye accused.-"Don't forget you've already reached Golden Core! I can't withstand your spiritual energy... *urgh!!*"

[—"Hold your blade!"](# "Speaker: Boss Tao")

A barrier snapped into place in front of Guan Ye, parrying the strike just in the nick of time.

Mo Xi whipped his head around, crying out in pleasant surprise:

—"Boss Tao... and Lord Jiu Zhai? You're actually here! Looks like our luck isn't so bad after all."

No matter what, these two were a Mahayana powerhouse and a Tribulation Transcendent respectively.

—"It's not that your luck is good; it's because Jiu Zhai felt an 'epiphany of inspiration', so we came over,"-Boss Tao corrected him, casting a lingering glance of trepidation at Mo Bei.

Blocking that strike just now hadn't strained him to his limits, but it had certainly required deliberate effort.

Even though he wasn't a sword cultivator, for a Mahayana expert, requiring 'deliberate effort' was already the highest form of praise.

Especially when this girl was only at the Golden Core stage, wasn't she?

[The younger generation is truly terrifying!]

[Why is every single one of Zhou Hui's disciples an absolute monster?]

Tao Zhu sighed inwardly.

Mo Xi was oblivious to Tao Zhu's internal lamentations, nor did he care. His sole concern was getting these two heavyweights to lend a hand:

—"Please, save Fourth Senior Brother! You two can manage it, right?"

—"I can save him myself,"-Mo Bei retorted in dissatisfaction.-"As long as Fourth Senior Brother endures a little."

—"...I appreciate the sentiment,"-Guan Ye said stiffly, grappling with a pair of spectral arms currently attempting to strangle him.-"Compared to the yin spirits, I fear your sword intent far more."

—"How could that be?"-Mo Bei asked in astonishment.-"You can tolerate yin spirits better than that?"

[—"It is indeed peculiar. By normal tolerance metrics, one should find the corrosive chill of yin spirits far more unbearable."](# "Speaker: Old Ancestor Jiu Zhai")

Even Mo Bei and Mo Xi couldn't endure such corrosion.

Staring strangely at Guan Ye, Jiu Zhai muttered:

—"In this reincarnation cycle, you haven't studied ghost-controlling arts, have you?"

—"Ghost-controlling arts?"-Guan Ye possessed no memories of any prior reincarnation cycles; this was naturally the first time he had ever heard the phrase.

Old Ancestor Jiu Zhai, however, adopted a look of profound fascination:

—"No memories, yet possessing natural tolerance? A temporal residue? Or accumulated experience? Could this be the secret behind the Heavenly Spirit Sect's lineage...?"

—"What are you all doing?!"-Zhou Hui's voice rang out abruptly, right as Old Ancestor Jiu Zhai was circling Guan Ye and muttering incomprehensible nonsense to himself.

Everyone immediately watched as Old Ancestor Jiu Zhai tumbled away like a shriveled, dried orange peel.

Zhou Hui cast a bemused glance at Jiu Zhai's scurrying retreat.

—"Master, you've arrived!"-Guan Ye let out a genuine sigh of relief, before asking in bewilderment:-"I assumed you wouldn't come to pick me up for another few hours."

—"I suddenly felt a sense of unease,"-Zhou Hui said.-"What is the situation here?"

As he asked, he casually flicked his sleeve, and the spectral arms entangling Guan Ye vanished into thin air.

To be precise, they had been purified on the spot by Zhou Hui.

Zhou Hui inspected the frozen, blue-screened light display:

—"A bug caused by bridging into the yin spirits ahead of schedule?"

—"I'm sorry, Master,"-Mo Xi instantly tucked his tail between his legs in front of his master.

Zhou Hui did not reprimand him. Instead, he pulled up the control interface and spent a few minutes resolving the bug that Mo Xi had been temporarily unable to fix, optimizing the codebase along the way.

Watching from the side, Guan Ye and Mo Xi were filled with immense reverence—and a fair share of shame.

They had originally hoped to complete the system entirely on their own before Master arrived. Who could have foreseen that after smooth sailing all the way through, the final bug would bring everything crashing down right before launch?

—"Not bad. It seems you two worked quite diligently,"-Zhou Hui's standards weren't overly exacting.-"In that case, Ye-zi can return with me first. Xi'er, stick to the original plan and keep a close eye on things here. You should be able to handle the rest. If anything comes up, contact me directly via the water mirror."

—"Understood,"-Mo Xi replied, bowing his head.

—"Wait a moment,"-interjected the uninvolved Old Ancestor Jiu Zhai.

He peered suspiciously at Guan Ye's face, asking for confirmation:

—"If I recall correctly, you're taking him back for that artifact refiner examination or whatever, yes?"

[—"Indeed."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"Are you certain you want him refining artifacts today?"-Jiu Zhai asked.-"The physiognomy of his face spells supreme misfortune!"

—"Supreme misfortune?"-Zhou Hui turned his gaze to Guan Ye's face as well.

Zhou Hui possessed a dab of mastery in virtually everything, naturally including the arts of reading auras and physiognomy. It was just that in this particular discipline, he was indeed outmatched by an old fraud of a charlatan like Jiu Zhai:

—"His complexion is somewhat poor, but surely it isn't dire enough to warrant 'supreme misfortune'?"

[—"No, you don't understand. A crimson undertone lurks beneath his eyes, his spirit is adrift, and his qi is hollow. This is a concealed omen of supreme catastrophe."](# "Speaker: Old Ancestor Jiu Zhai")

[That just sounds like someone who pulled too many all-nighters.]

Zhou Hui complained inwardly.

Catching his breath, Jiu Zhai pressed on:

—"At its worst, this supreme misfortune could endanger his life... Ah, actually, no. His life-thread is exceptionally robust, protected by multiple benefactor lines. However, his fortune has been thoroughly drained. Forcing matters under such conditions will inevitably court disaster—such as artifact refinement. That craft relies heavily on fortune, doesn't it? In this state, the failure rate is astronomically high; I strongly advise against attempting it."

—"Ah, no wonder a bug with such microscopic odds triggered!"-Mo Xi was instantly convinced.-"But what do we do? The qualification exam is today! We can't postpone it just for Fourth Senior Brother, can we?"

—"Master, I want to take the exam,"-Guan Ye said, looking anxiously toward Zhou Hui.

The expression on his face was resolute; he clearly had no desire to miss the exam over something like this.

Observing Guan Ye's expression, Zhou Hui offered a gratified smile.

Yet that gratification was overshadowed by his concern for Guan Ye's well-being.

When all was said and done, an exam was merely a tool to verify academic results; it did not represent the knowledge itself. There was utterly no need to plunge his disciple into danger over a mere test.

It was only out of respect for Guan Ye's persistence that Zhou Hui refrained from turning him down outright.

Fortunately, while Zhou Hui's fortune-telling skills couldn't hold a candle to Jiu Zhai's, he now possessed a 'cheat code' of his own.

Zhou Hui blinked softly. His silver-gray irises shed their warmth and gentleness, turning as frigid and emotionless as glacial ice, reflecting the chaotic tangled threads of the Heavenly Dao within his gaze.

Normally, analyzing and deciphering heavenly destiny was an arduous undertaking. But this time, it was remarkably straightforward: peering closely, one could spot a slender trajectory of fate tethered straight into the heavens. Under the influence of this celestial trajectory, numerous fine threads in the surrounding vicinity had been dyed the exact inverse color—and Guan Ye's thread of destiny was right among them.

No, to be more precise, Guan Ye's thread of fate had been ensnared by that sky-piercing celestial trajectory, like a sapling coiled tightly in parasitic vines. The slender trunk appeared weighed down beyond endurance; it seemed that with the slightest gust of wind, the sapling would be dragged down by the lush, overgrown vines, tilting sideways until it snapped.

However, if it was *this* particular 'sapling', Zhou Hui knew just how resilient and formidable he truly was. Never mind an ordinary breeze—even a typhoon might not topple him. Against the malicious 'vines' clinging to his bark, the odds of pulling off a counter-kill were far from zero.

—"If you are determined, you may certainly proceed with the examination,"-Zhou Hui said.

Hearing those words, Guan Ye smiled. He knew this examination had been orchestrated by Ran Deng for his sake, and he had no wish to let down his Senior Sister's good intentions. Naturally, Guan Ye also admitted to himself that hearing about the influence of 'fortune' had sparked a streak of stubborn defiance within him, making him care all the more about this qualification exam.

—"However,"-Master's pivot sent Guan Ye's heart leaping into his throat, as he heard Master instruct him:-"Do not push yourself beyond your limits. If at any point you feel it is inappropriate to continue the exam, stop immediately. Do not obsess over your score! And above all, do not [penny-pinch only to lose a fortune](# "Literal CN: 因小失大 (yīn xiǎo shī dà) — 'losing the big picture for small gains'; suffering a major loss out of petty obstinacy")!"

—"Yes, Master,"-Guan Ye breathed a sigh of relief, answering earnestly.-"If I run into trouble, I will weigh the consequences carefully."

Zhou Hui nodded, then turned his gaze toward Jiu Zhai.

—"Jiu Zhai, are you coming along to observe the exam?"-Zhou Hui asked.-"You might witness something unexpected."

—"Whew, spare me,"-Jiu Zhai declined without a shred of hesitation.-"While I am somewhat curious, look at how decrepit I've become! A simple slip and fall could claim my old life. So please, don't drag an old man like me into whatever happens next—I still wish to live long enough to see the future further down the road!"

[It seems Jiu Zhai truly knows about the 'omens' Ruwang mentioned!]

Zhou Hui thought to himself. Placing a hand on Guan Ye's shoulder and giving Mo Xi a few final instructions, he prepared to return to the Heavenly Spirit Sect.

Boss Tao finally snapped back to his senses, frantically grabbing a fistful of Zhou Hui's sleeve:

—"Hold on! Take me with you! It's that old bastard's business if he has no interest, but you dragged me all the way to the Southern Domain by force in the first place! I want to go back! Whether it's the Immortal Arts Assembly or this year's Immortal Market, I refuse to miss out on any of it, ahhh!"-Boss Tao howled.

*

By the time Zhou Hui returned to the Heavenly Spirit Sect with Guan Ye and Boss Tao in tow, the sect grounds were already teeming with bustling activity.

Truth be told, owing to the Immortal Arts Assembly, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had been lively all year round. But with the concurrent commencement of the qualification examinations and the assembly's opening ceremony, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had officially entered its peak human traffic season.

Naturally, not all visitors were as boorishly insolent as Goddess Ren Ran. The vast majority of guests were more than willing to abide by the Heavenly Spirit Sect's rules, and in return, they naturally received the courteous treatment due to honored guests.

Such as, for instance, the freedom to tour all public areas of the Heavenly Spirit Sect.

Fairy Maogui was having a wonderful time exploring.

Maogui hailed from Moon-Welcoming Valley, one of the most renowned sects in the Northern Domain and the largest alchemy sect across the entire cultivation realm. As the Grand Elder of the valley and a Mahayana Late Stage powerhouse, Maogui was universally recognized across the cultivation world as one of the prime candidates most likely to advance to Tribulation Transcendence after figures like the Untiring Immortal Venerable.

Yet Fairy Maogui harbored bitter grievances known only to herself.

Although she possessed a supreme Heavenly Spirit Root and a cultivation talent comparable to divine mythical beasts—her cultivation base swelled even while eating and sleeping—her fragile mortal vessel could not withstand such an overwhelming influx of spiritual energy. As a child, she had nearly perished in infancy. Even though great fortune led her to cross paths with the valley's sect master at the time, allowing her to embark upon the path of cultivation, an incurable ailment had taken root in her constitution.

In ordinary times, this constitutional ailment was manageable enough, sustained through nourishing medicinal pills. But with every breakthrough tribulation lightning, this defect became the fatal Achilles' heel targeted by the heavenly wrath, leaving her hanging by a thread between life and death on every occasion. Advancing directly to Tribulation Transcendence under such circumstances meant being tested by lightning tribulations at every turn, whether one ascended or not—a virtual death sentence.

Thus, Fairy Maogui had remained stuck at Mahayana Late Stage. Having searched the ends of the earth, she had found no cure. She had even pulled strings to petition the Heavenly Court for a celestial edict through Administrator Siwu of the Heavenly Dao Alliance, only to learn that her affliction was incurable unless her physical vessel was reshaped using a Dao Bone. That solution, of course, was an absolute non-starter. Leaving aside the sheer impossibility of finding a Dao Bone in the first place, even if she knew who possessed one, it would have to be excised alive from another person's living flesh. Were she to commit such an atrocity, would she be cultivating the path of immortals, or the path of devils?!

Having rejected the celestial edict, Maogui was left utterly helpless. For all these years, she could only hide deep within Moon-Welcoming Valley concocting pills, hoping against hope that one day she might invent a cure through her own research.

It was amidst such wasting of years that word suddenly arrived from the Central Domain: the Untiring Immortal Venerable had restored the Ascension Stairway! Only then was a spark of newfound hope rekindled in Fairy Maogui's heart:

Could it be possible to bypass the Tribulation Transcendence stage entirely via the Ascension Stairway?

Even if she couldn't, having reached the Mahayana stage, she ought to at least catch a glimpse of what the Heavenly Court looked like, shouldn't she?

Nourishing such hopes, Fairy Maogui had departed Moon-Welcoming Valley for the first time in nearly a thousand years. Boarding the grand treasure ship constructed by Sky City and the Heavenly Dao Alliance, she arrived at the Heavenly Spirit Sect in the Central Domain.

Having come for the Ascension Stairway, Maogui was naturally not foolish enough to tag along with Ren Ran in crashing the Untiring Immortal Venerable's mountain gate.

In fact, she had remained aboard the treasure ship on that day, disembarking only when the Heavenly Spirit Sect's third disciple, Ran Deng, formally opened the gates to welcome guests. She cooperatively proceeded to the guest accommodations arranged by the Heavenly Spirit Sect, and without the slightest airs of a Mahayana powerhouse, followed the disciple acting as a tour guide to visit the sect's open areas—even though the Untiring Immortal Venerable himself likely didn't care who participated in these proceedings.

Fairy Maogui originally had zero interest in hospitality tours. She had come solely for the Ascension Stairway and had no desire for superfluous pleasantries. Her original plan had been to pay formal respects to the Untiring Immortal Venerable first, dispatch her disciples to handle the ceremonial formalities—such as attending the opening ceremony of the Immortal Arts Assembly—and once all the tedious etiquette concluded and the Untiring Immortal Venerable had some free time, formally request permission to view the Ascension Stairway.

Such was standard operating procedure for major events hosted by cultivation sects.

Yet after Goddess Ren Ran willfully refused to dock the treasure ship as instructed and barged straight into the mountain gate, Fairy Maogui felt the Untiring Immortal Venerable's impression of the Northern Domain had plummeted to rock bottom. Little wonder she was so anxious: for high-tier cultivators, entering the territory of a peer without prior greeting was tantamount to an invasion. Had it been a Tribulation Transcendent with a shorter fuse—such as Ruwang, for instance—if any Tribulation Transcendent (save for Zhou Hui) dared step into the Western Domain without announcing themselves, they would likely find themselves playing ['Ghostly Mist Navigation'](# "Literal CN: 鬼雾迷航 (guǐ wù mí háng) — parodies lost in ghostly mist / spatial shipwreck; getting trapped and disoriented in lethal netherworld fog").

Ren Ran enraging Zhou Hui was her own funeral, but if the Untiring Immortal Venerable developed a prejudice against all Northern Domain cultivators as a result, that would be disastrous.

To demonstrate that they were not in cahoots with Goddess Ren Ran, the high-tier cultivators of the Northern Domain could not voice the slightest dissatisfaction over the Untiring Immortal Venerable failing to grant them an immediate audience. Instead, they had to step up in person, following the guides through the entirety of the welcoming itinerary.

As it turned out, this guided tour blew open the doors to an entirely new world.

Especially within the Immortal Market.

Compared to the previous year, the Heavenly Spirit Sect's Immortal Market had doubled in size once again. Not only had cultivators and mortals from the Central Domain converged here, but well-informed sects and merchants from other domains had also set up shop, adapting to local customs with various innovations.

Maogui's Moon-Welcoming Valley was one of them.

They had always maintained apothecary branches across major immortal cities, so opening a pavilion in the Immortal Market was only natural. Having arrived at the Immortal Market, Fairy Maogui naturally wished to inspect her own sect's apothecary.

As it turned out, before she could even reach the alchemy pavilion, she was swept away and separated by the surging throng of the marketplace.

This was not the guide's fault, but rather a problem intrinsic to Fairy Maogui herself.

Fairy Maogui had been frail and sickly since childhood. When she was still a mortal, she had been forbidden from venturing outdoors or mingling with crowds. Once she became a cultivator, she spent every waking day sequestered in alchemy chambers. As the prized prodigy of the realm's premier alchemy sect, she never had to step outside to obtain materials, nor did she ever need to venture out for practical training. By the time her cultivation realm climbed to the heavens, the rare spirit herbs and miraculous plants she required were found only in the most enigmatic and perilous secret realms—places even further removed from mortal crowds.

A Fairy Maogui such as this was setting foot inside an open marketplace for the very first time in her life!

Although the Heavenly Spirit Sect's bazaar was far more orderly than conventional markets, the densely packed sea of people still gave Fairy Maogui a fright, leaving her at a total loss as to how to proceed. On top of that, before entering the market, she had reined in her Mahayana spiritual pressure to 'avoid startling ordinary folks', masking her aura down to roughly the Foundation Establishment level. Blending seamlessly into the crowd, she went completely unnoticed by both the guide and her own disciples. By the time she snapped to her senses, Fairy Maogui found herself standing dazed and alone amidst the surging crowd, surrounded on all sides by complete strangers.

However, Fairy Maogui had stood dazed in place for less than the time it took to brew a cup of tea when someone stepped forward to address her:

[—"Excuse me, Fairy, have you encountered some trouble?"](# "Speaker: Mei Shiyiniang")

Maogui looked up and found a pretty young girl. Unfortunately, her breathing was loose and her meridians feeble; she was actually an ordinary mortal.

Yet this mortal maiden was draped in a red-and-gold patterned outer robe typically worn by Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples—only later did Fairy Maogui learn that this robe was not the formal uniform of official disciples, but rather the specialized livery of tour guides. The girl held up the nameplate hanging from her chest for her to see:

—"I am a guide for the Immortal Market. If you have any instructions, I might be able to lend a hand,"-she offered.

Fairy Maogui glanced indifferently at the nameplate inscribed with 'Mei Shiyiniang'. In her heart, she merely wondered why the Heavenly Spirit Sect would recruit even mortals as disciples. Unaware of the distinction between an 'Immortal Market guide' and a 'Heavenly Spirit Sect guide', she announced her affiliation:

—"I am a cultivator from Moon-Welcoming Valley. I wish to rendezvous with my disciples."

Mei Shiyiniang was a mortal from the Central Domain. It was only because her final exam scores at the White Jade Capital Academy were outstanding that she had been selected, as she had hoped, to serve as event staff for the Immortal Market. Her training had focused strictly on market operations without covering outside cultivation sects, so she naturally had no clue what Moon-Welcoming Valley was.

Unflustered, Mei Shiyiniang asked with a beaming smile:

—"Did you get separated? Would you like me to make a public broadcast announcement to find them?"

—"Ah, no, no need!"-Maogui said in a flurry of panic. She didn't know what a 'broadcast' was, but from the literal meaning, it appeared to be a method of 'broad-area audio paging'. While Maogui could cast such a minor spell herself, for a Mahayana powerhouse to get lost in a marketplace seemed utterly humiliating. Maogui shrank back, desperately wanting to avoid public embarrassment:-"Perhaps... I can just search on my own."

—"Why don't I lead the way? I'm quite familiar with this area,"-Mei Shiyiniang offered patiently.-"Do you know where they might have gone?"

—"An alchemy chamber, probably?"-Maogui guessed.

—"An alchemy chamber? Which alchemy chamber, and what is its name?"-Mei Shiyiniang asked.

—"Uh, Moon-Welcoming Valley's alchemy chamber..."-Maogui stammered.

—"...How about this: let's start by checking the nearest alchemy chambers first?"-Mei Shiyiniang suggested with a warm smile.

[There's really no need for that.]

Maogui wanted to decline, but seeing the mortal girl already leading her toward a relatively quiet alleyway—Mei Shiyiniang had noticed Maogui's unease around dense crowds and deliberately picked a less congested route—and looking at the bustling throngs all around, she ended up following closely behind the young maiden's heels.

Fortunately, Maogui was a Mahayana cultivator; few people in this world possessed the ability to take advantage of her. Otherwise, a temperament like hers would have been a genuine cause for concern.

Mei Shiyiniang thought much the same.

Though she had no idea what Fairy Maogui's cultivation realm was, this internship as a guide had thoroughly shattered her rose-tinted glasses regarding immortal cultivators. She had discovered that these cultivators were far from the omnipotent beings of legend. Especially in social interactions—perhaps due to spending decades in secluded meditation that turned them into recluses—many of these cultivators were hopelessly clumsy when conducting transactions, and more than a few ended up getting swindled or overcharged, something even the most comprehensive regulatory framework couldn't entirely prevent.

And upon bringing this lost fairy to the "Pill & Elixir District", Mei Shiyiniang became all the more convinced of her assessment.

—"This one, that one, and this one over here—I want all of them! Ah, they can even be custom-ordered...?"-Fairy Maogui marveled.

—"Um, Fairy Mao? These might not be very suitable for you,"-Mei Shiyiniang intervened, braving the shopkeeper's murderous glare.

Yet Mei Shiyiniang felt it was her duty.

They were currently standing in the plaza adjoining this neighborhood. Because several major pill shops lined this street, making it the central elixir distribution hub of the Immortal Market, a colossal cauldron had been erected in the square.

Normally, this cauldron was used for refining pills. But recently, with the opening of the Immortal Arts Assembly, a bakery had actually rented the cauldron to expand their customer base and advertise. They were using the pill cauldron to bake cakes, setting up an endless queue of tasting counters to showcase all manner of newly developed pastries!

For a time, the sweet, rich fragrance of cake actually overpowered the herbal aroma of medicinal pills.

At first, Fairy Maogui had assumed they were genuinely refining pills, pausing to take extra glances because she had never smelled such a 'pill fragrance' before. But upon strolling closer and discovering it was culinary baking rather than pill refinement, Fairy Maogui became even more excited than if they had been refining real elixirs.

—"When I was a child, I once tried to use my master's cauldron to refine sugar beads, but ended up with nothing but charcoal. How on earth did they accomplish this?!"-Fairy Maogui said enthusiastically to Mei Shiyiniang, peering curiously at the pastries:-"And they're all so pretty! I'll buy every single one!"

Mei Shiyiniang was instantly shocked by Maogui's sheer extravagance.

She painstakingly advised:

—"I don't know what cultivation level you possess, but even at Qi Condensation, it's ill-advised to overeat. Although they contain a bit of spiritual energy, they are terribly fattening,"-she said. (She had personally gained five pounds lately because of them.)

—"It doesn't matter; I'll take them back to use as room decorations,"-Fairy Maogui said.

At her level of cultivation, the spiritual energy provided by such low-grade spiritual sugar and spiritual wheat was utterly negligible, nor did the texture suit her refined palate. It was simply that her own cauldron had only ever produced round, hard medicinal pills. Seeing people use a cauldron today to produce something so soft, fluffy, and fragrant, Maogui felt they would make wonderful room ornaments.

To that end, she custom-ordered a whole mountain of exquisite little pastries, and even commissioned a massive nine-tiered cream cake pagoda.

Mei Shiyiniang simply could not fathom Maogui's thought process.

Helplessly, she accompanied Maogui to watch the baker bake the cakes. The artisan treated the baking process like a theatrical performance.

Truth be told, it was indeed quite fascinating to watch.

Right as the cake sponge was slid into the cauldron to bake, Fairy Maogui suddenly murmured, glancing left and right:

—"It's burnt,"-Fairy Maogui murmured.

—"Burnt?"-Mei Shiyiniang echoed, taking a beat before realizing what she meant.-"That shouldn't be possible, right? Didn't they just put it into the cauldron?"

—"Exactly, Fairy, you can't go throwing around baseless slander! My family's craft is an ancestral heritage—how could it burn?!"-protested the baker, who had transitioned to the trade just one year prior.

Maogui hurriedly clarified:

—"No! Master baker, your craft is splendid! I was referring to pill refinement..."

[—"Pill refinement?"](# "Speakers: Mei Shiyiniang & Baker")

Both the baker and Mei Shiyiniang froze in confusion.

Then, realization dawned upon the baker:

—"Ah, you mean that bunch of greenhorns preparing for the exam next door?"-the baker asked.

[—"Exam?"](# "Speaker: Fairy Maogui")

Mei Shiyiniang immediately recalled the activity schedule for shops in this district, explaining to Fairy Maogui:

—"Ah, that must be the preliminary mock trials for the qualification exams!"

—"Qualification exams? I seem to have heard of them,"-Fairy Maogui said.-"However, isn't it an Artifact Refiner Qualification Examination?"

—"Yes, for this year it's temporarily just artifact refiners and formation masters,"-Mei Shiyiniang said.-"But word has it that alchemists will have to take exams sooner or later as well. So the Heavenly Spirit Sect's Alchemy Workshop organized a mock competition to build hype and liven things up."

[The Heavenly Spirit Sect's idea of 'livening things up' is truly bizarre.]

Fairy Maogui thought to herself.

Still, since waiting for the cake to bake would take time anyway, she and Mei Shiyiniang strolled toward the examination grounds, taking the opportunity to check if the nearby apothecary belonged to her sect.

The examination ground for the Alchemy Workshop lay at the opposite end of the plaza.

Over there, instead of a single colossal cauldron rented by the bakery, over a hundred miniature cauldrons were neatly aligned atop parallel long tables. Several rows of examinee cultivators were scrambling in frantic disarray, tossing all manner of ingredients into their cauldrons. The clatter of colliding utensils and the hissing steam from the cauldrons blended together, resembling chaotic kitchen cooking even more than the cake-baking cauldron did.

Seeing this spectacle, Mei Shiyiniang grew somewhat skeptical that Fairy Maogui's 'burnt' smell had come from this direction at all.

Never mind the fact that so many people were refining pills simultaneously—the most damning part was that after toiling away for half the day, the aroma of their pills still couldn't overpower the fragrance of cake. Standing here only made one feel hungrier, making it utterly impossible to discern which cauldron was burning.

Fairy Maogui was oblivious to Mei Shiyiniang's skepticism. As a seasoned senior and mentor, she simply couldn't bear to watch this pack of fools stumble through such moronic techniques. In fact, she hardly needed to look—her nose alone told the whole story:

—"Row three, number seven; and row five, number six... Their pills have already burnt to a crisp. Especially the one in the third row—it would be wise to take safety precautions—"

*BANG!*

Just as Fairy Maogui finished speaking, the very cauldron she had singled out exploded in magnificent fashion.

Fortunately, such mishaps were commonplace. Several proctoring cultivators rushed over immediately to contain the blast, preventing the disaster from spreading.

Over at the examiner's dais, however, a proctor took note of Fairy Maogui's remarks and waved cheerfully:

—"Haha! Senior, you seem to possess quite a profound grasp of pill refinement!"-the proctor called out.

*Senior?*

Mei Shiyiniang glanced at the examiner. She didn't know the proctor's cultivation base either, but anyone qualified to serve as an examiner had to be at least at the Nascent Soul stage or above, right? In that case, what about the cultivation level of the guest beside her...?

Mei Shiyiniang stole what she assumed was a subtle, covert glance at Maogui.

Maogui, for her part, was astonished by the mortal girl's composure.

Within the Heavenly Spirit Sect, it seemed that whether one was a mortal or a Body Integration stage powerhouse, nobody paid much mind to disparities in cultivation realm!

—Ah, that was right: the examiner was a Body Integration powerhouse.

Fairy Maogui answered the examiner:

—"I am indeed an alchemist."

—"Then are you interested in the qualification examinations?"-the examiner asked Maogui.

—"No, an examination like this..."-Maogui began.

—"Not taking the exam here, of course, but joining as an exam proctor!"-the examiner chuckled.-"Truth be told, I'm a body cultivator; I don't know the first thing about alchemy. I was only roped in because they were short on personnel, and I've got no clue whether I can even carry out my duties properly."

[With the abysmal standard of these examinees, parking a cat on the proctor's chair wouldn't make the slightest difference.]

Maogui thought to herself.

Still, she intended to decline.

Fairy Maogui hadn't forgotten why she had come to the Immortal Market in the first place. Before reuniting with her disciples, she had no wish to squander time on such bizarre side-tracks.

Yet before she could voice her refusal, an examinee approached, holding a box of newly refined pills with both hands:

—"Lord Examiner, I have completed my submission,"-the examinee presented his work.

—"Let me have a taste?"-Turning around, the examiner took a pill from the youth's hands and tossed it straight into her mouth, chewing thoughtfully.-"Not sweet enough. A bit too hard. Fifty points."

—"Wh-What kind of grading rubric is that?!"-The examinee's face flushed crimson on the spot.-"Your requirement for us to refine a 'sweet Heart-Clearing Pill' was bizarre enough! Since when do medicinal pills require specific flavor profiles?!"

The examiner was rendered momentarily speechless by the retort.

Mei Shiyiniang, however, felt the examinee had a point:

Letting a body cultivator who knew nothing of alchemy devise test questions and grade alchemy exams was utterly absurd, even if it was just a mock trial for fun.

—"Why shouldn't it be required to be sweet?!"-Fairy Maogui interjected, glaring icily at the examinee.-"The flavor profile of a medicinal pill is of paramount importance! The slightest discrepancy in taste signifies a world of difference in chemical composition! If you cannot even make adjustments at this basic level, how could you ever hope to refine a proper elixir?!"

She was, after all, the Grand Elder of Moon-Welcoming Valley. Scolding incompetent juniors was the one time Fairy Maogui was completely immune to [social anxiety](# "Literal CN: 社恐 (shèkǒng) — short for 社交恐惧症; social anxiety disorder / extreme social awkwardness").

She proceeded to deliver a blistering, point-by-point critique of the examinee's pill. Before she had even finished dressing him down, the youth broke into sobs and bolted away in tears—while down below, two more cauldrons belonging to terrified examinees exploded in sheer panic.

Once the reprimand concluded, however, Fairy Maogui found herself naturally taking a seat at the examiners' table.

—"Care for a taste?"-The original examiner offered the pill left behind by the weeping examinee to Fairy Maogui.

Fairy Maogui took a bite:

—"Indeed, it is not sweet enough,"-Fairy Maogui remarked.

—"In that case, let's just eat cake instead, shall we?"-The examiner produced another treat.

This time, it was a bona fide slice of cake.

Furthermore, unlike the promotional goods sold on the street, the cake produced by a Body Integration examiner was naturally high-grade spiritual food matched to their cultivation realm.

Fairy Maogui's eyes lit up:

—"So this thing isn't just pretty—it's actually edible!"

—"It was always meant to be eaten, wasn't it?"-Unaware that Fairy Maogui had just purchased a whole haul of cakes to use as room decorations, the examiner chuckled and asked:-"By the way, I have yet to ask for Senior's esteemed name!"

[—"Maogui of Moon-Welcoming Valley."](# "Speaker: Fairy Maogui")

[—"I am Shen Qingqian of Blood Demon Hall."](# "Speaker: Shen Qingqian")

The two cultivators engaged in friendly chatter.

Watching them, Mei Shiyiniang let out a quiet sigh of relief, mentally reproaching herself for her earlier casualness:

[They truly are top-tier powerhouses!]

No matter how eccentric they appeared on the surface, when it came to their professional craft, they were genuinely terrifying masters!

And the way she had dressed down that alchemist just now had been truly spine-chilling!

Still... why did it feel like events were hurtling in a completely unforeseen direction?

*

And just as Mei Shiyiniang had anticipated, several days later, on the day of the Artifact Refiner Qualification Examination:

The handful of Moon-Welcoming Valley disciples who had signed up for the artifact refiner exam out of curiosity and amusement walked into the examination hall, looked up, and [nearly wet themselves on the spot](# "Literal CN: 当场吓尿了 (dāngchǎng xià niào le) — internet/colloquial hyperbole for being scared witless"):

[Why on earth is our Grandmaster sitting at the commentary desk?!]

[Wait, no! Why does a qualification exam have a commentary desk in the first place?!]
