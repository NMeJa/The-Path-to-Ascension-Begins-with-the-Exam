# Chapter 123: Beneath the Abyssal Ocean (第123章 渊海下)

The Ascension Stairway.

As the name implied: a flight of stairs by which one could ascend to the immortal realm.

Its existence could be traced back to the primeval wild era preceding the ancient cultivation realm—a primordial epoch when cultivators did not yet exist, and there were only "shamans."

Through the Ascension Stairway, the Great Shamans traveled back and forth to the upper realm, conveying the divine decrees of gods and True Immortals. In those days, mortals had no need to laboriously cultivate; so long as they possessed virtue, talent, made outstanding contributions, or simply possessed exceptional courage to step onto the Ascension Stairway and brave the trial of heavenly lightning, it was sufficient to become an immortal.

Alas, this Ascension Stairway was ultimately destroyed in the cataclysm of the Demon-God War.

With the severing of the Ascension Stairway, every avenue of ascending to the upper realm by means other than rigorous cultivation vanished into thin air. Cultivators of the present day could only search for traces of that bygone era amidst crumbling ancient scrolls and forgotten ruins.

As for the origins of the Ascension Stairway, diverse theories naturally arose over the ages.

The most widely circulated theory today went as follows:

—*In the primeval wild era when all things were freshly formed, a True Immortal, traversing the myriad realms, was accidentally injured and plummeted down into this mortal world. Becoming the land's very first king yet perpetually longing for his native realm, he gathered all manner of rare materials and painstakingly erected the Ascension Stairway. From that moment forward, this world drew the attention of the upper realm. The primeval barbarians received sacred knowledge imparted from above, coming to understand etiquette, honor, and shame, learning the methods of channeling spiritual energy—and from this foundation, all manner of cultivation techniques were born.*

Yet there existed an alternative theory:

—*This world was once an integral piece of the upper realm itself. In that even earlier epoch, the Ascension Stairway was merely an ordinary bridge connecting to the immortal world. However, struck by a horrifying celestial catastrophe, this realm was tragically severed and torn away, leaving only the Ascension Stairway to tenuously sustain a passage between the two realms. But in the aftermath of the Demon-God War, even this passage was utterly shattered.*

[—"Mr. Untiring."](<# "Speaker: Young Ran Deng">)

—"What is it, A-Ran?"-Zhou Hui asked.

—"In which book is that latter theory recorded?"-a very young Ran Deng, who had not yet formally become a disciple, asked.-"Although I heard a similar account from my [Ah-Ma](<# "Literal CN: 阿嫲 (ā'má) — Grandmother / maternal elder; dialectal term of endearment">), I haven't seen such a record anywhere, whether in my clan's private collection or inside your Library Pavilion."

—"Because it is a hypothesis that lacks verifiable evidentiary support,"-Zhou Hui answered.-"I merely heard it from the mouths of a few ancient elders who managed to survive from the aftermath of the Demon-God War to the present day."

—"Still, for Mr. Untiring to bring up this particular theory specifically, it must be because the latter holds far greater credibility, doesn't it?"-she pressed.

—"That is by no means certain,"-Zhou Hui replied, shaking his head with a faint smile at little Ran Deng.-"Perhaps my mood is simply exceptionally foul today, and I felt like talking complete nonsense?"

[—"Ngh..."](<# "Speaker: Young Ran Deng">)

—"If you truly wish to uncover the truth of something, rather than relying on hearsay, it is far better to investigate the matter yourself. But more importantly—"-Zhou Hui paused, glaring out the door.-"Yuwen Xuan! I see you! Get in here and apologize to A-Ran!"

[—"Baa!"](<# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan">)

***

—"Back then, I really never imagined that one day I would personally walk upon the Ascension Stairway! Wouldn't you say so, Eldest Senior Brother?"

Standing atop the white jade steps, Ran Deng gazed down into the distance as she questioned the little black lamb beside her.

The little black lamb, who had been laboriously hopping up the steps one by one, instantly froze solid in place.

It widened its beady eyes, staring at Ran Deng:

—"Maa?!"-the little black lamb bleated in shock.

Ran Deng's smile deepened:

—"Have you forgotten? When we were little, the day you hacked my hair into wild weeds, Master told me the legend of the Ascension Stairway just to comfort me—though he delivered it exactly like an academic lecture. Come to think of it, precisely because you were always bullying me back then, I kept putting off my formal discipleship ceremony, letting 'Second Senior Brother' snatch the prime opportunity... What is with that look on your face? Even though I've seen through your true identity, I won't seize this chance to take petty revenge and shave your wool off."

[No! The very fact that you had that thought means you definitely intend to do it!]

Glares fixed upon Ran Deng, the little black lamb took slow, deliberate steps backward, desperately attempting to put some distance between itself and his junior sister.

Right at that moment, their Master's voice drifted down from higher up the stairway:

—"A-Ran?"-Zhou Hui called.

—"Master, we're coming right up!"-Ran Deng called back.

Ran Deng hoisted the little black lamb by the scruff of its neck, smoothed its wool, and, thoroughly satisfied to see it go as stiff as a stone carving, tucked it securely under her arm before striding up the stairs.

As for how Ran Deng and the others had set foot on the Ascension Stairway—

And how Zhou Hui had managed to unearth the Ascension Stairway in the first place—

That all traced back to several months ago.

***

On the very day that Fairy Ruopei's fiery article was published in *White Jade Capital Weekly*, igniting domain-wide flames across the entire cultivation realm.

Although the Untiring Immortal Venerable kept an eye on the development, unlike other bored cultivators who merely wished to [watch the world burn](<# "Literal CN: 看血流成河 (kàn xuèliú-chénghé) — watch blood flow like a river; idiom for gleefully watching catastrophic conflict / bloodbath">), Zhou Hui had far too many pressing matters on his plate.

For instance, the Immortal Arts Assembly; the peer-review vetting of academic journal submissions; the curriculum and textbook adjustments for the Southern Domain; and the demonic aberrations beneath the Abyssal Ocean...

Ah, the demonic aberrations beneath the Abyssal Ocean currently held the absolute highest priority.

After all, as an elder of the Heavenly Dao Alliance's Rites Academy, Lord Siwu could not remain indefinitely at the Heavenly Spirit Sect. What was more, several of his disciples had gone missing deep within the Abyssal Ocean. Everything about the situation gave off the unmistakable urgency of a time-limited quest.

In truth, had it not been for Patriarch Jiuzhai's sudden visit, followed immediately by Shui Huang'er's arrival, and then the Southern Domain's trial livestream that inadvertently summoned heavenly lightning, Zhou Hui would have traveled to the Abyssal Ocean that very day to investigate the origin of the demonic creatures and resolve the matter once and for all.

Yet precisely because it had been delayed by those few days, Zhou Hui discovered that this affair was far more tangled than initially assumed.

—"Has Eastern Fence Garden still not contacted you today?"-Zhou Hui asked as he stepped into the guest quarters, addressing Lord Siwu.

Ran Deng cast an irritated glance at Lord Siwu.

Throughout this period, Lord Siwu had been lodging continuously in the guest chamber of Zhou Hui's cave abode. This displeased all the direct disciples to no end, and was one of the prime reasons Ran Deng publicly slandered him as a "[shameless pervert squatting at our Heavenly Spirit Sect](<# "Literal CN: 死皮赖脸待在天灵派的变态基佬 (sǐpí-làilǎn dāi zài Tiānlíng-pài de biàntài jīlǎo) — shameless perverted gay guy mooching at Heavenly Spirit Sect; Ran Deng's colorful gossip smear">)."

Yet Lord Siwu was utterly powerless to do otherwise!

He was currently wrapped from head to toe in rings upon rings of runic talismans, looking for all the world like a wrapped mummy, with Patriarch Jiuzhai personally standing watch beside him. Lord Siwu had never suffered such profound indignity in his entire life; he was the one who wanted to flee this place most of all!

But he did not dare.

—"Still no news from Eastern Fence Garden,"-Lord Siwu said. The moment his eyes fell upon the Untiring Immortal Venerable, they lit up as though gazing upon his sole savior.-"However, the 'True Immortal' Lord has issued me fresh divine guidance, directing me to advance into the lower strata of the Abyssal Ocean."

As he spoke, he cast a dread-filled glance toward the "projection" hovering before him.

It was a real-time spiritual illusion mirror showing the landscape within the Abyssal Ocean ruins. Sitting right within the projection was a scarecrow dressed in Lord Siwu's own robes.

The scarecrow appeared grievously mangled and battered, frayed straw scattering all over the ground.

It was crouched in miserable concealment within a rocky fissure, while nearby, a colossal demonic beast roared as it thundered past.

Lord Siwu felt his scalp go completely numb.

This scarecrow was his scapegoat substitute; whatever befell the scarecrow mirrored the fate destined for him.

In other words, had Zhou Hui not rescued him, even if he had miraculously escaped the demonic creatures' clutches that day, he would have ultimately been reduced to the wretched state of this scarecrow substitute right now.

Just watching his proxy, Lord Siwu found the horror unbearable.

—"Lord Immortal Venerable, should I reject the directive and 'rest' here for another day?"-Lord Siwu asked.

—"I told you just moments ago, you had best not,"-Patriarch Jiuzhai said wearily, appearing noticeably more decrepit than before.-"Even for someone of my standing, shielding secrets from the Heavenly Dao is excruciatingly arduous. Every passing day shaves off a substantial chunk of my lifespan."

Indeed, this scarecrow substitute had been crafted by Patriarch Jiuzhai himself.

Only a Tribulation Transcendence divination master of his caliber could forge a surrogate capable of temporarily pulling the wool over the eyes of both the upper realm and the Heavenly Dao.

Thus, that day, Zhou Hui had entrusted Patriarch Jiuzhai to conceal Lord Siwu's existence from the Heavenly Dao, leading the upper realm to believe Lord Siwu was still lost deep beneath the Abyssal Ocean, continuing his fruitless search for Eastern Fence Garden.

Speaking of this, Jiuzhai felt utterly perplexed:

—"To be frank, I've wanted to ask this for some time: in Si Wu's situation, clearly something went wrong with the deity he worships. In that case, shouldn't concealing it from the upper realm be more than enough?! Why must you conceal it from the Heavenly Dao as well?!"

—"Compared to the Heavenly Dao, shouldn't deceiving the upper realm be far more difficult?"-Lord Siwu asked in discontent upon hearing this.

Having served as a high priest communicating with the upper realm for countless centuries, Lord Siwu mentally identified himself as no different from the Great Shamans of the primeval era. Even now, hearing someone talk about the upper realm like this grated on his nerves.

Jiuzhai scoffed dismissively.

In his eyes, someone of Si Wu's ilk did not walk the same path as him at all.

Too lazy to explain in detail, Jiuzhai merely grunted:

—"It's the difference between [the emperor far away and the local magistrate right in front of you](<# "Literal CN: 县官和现管的区别 (xiànguān hé xiànguǎn de qūbié) — the difference between the distant magistrate and the immediate overseer; an adage highlighting that immediate authorities hold immediate sway">)... Untiring, I'm asking you!"

—"...Jiuzhai, haven't you noticed that the Heavenly Dao has been acting rather peculiar?"-Zhou Hui countered.-"That script of its defies common logic entirely."

—"If somebody scrubbed me clean six times in a row, I would target him with a vengeance too!"-Patriarch Jiuzhai snapped.

—"Yet it was obsessively targeting me from the very beginning,"-Zhou Hui noted calmly.-"Had it not insisted on murdering my disciples, I would never have done any of those things."

[Yet it never anticipated that over a minor disagreement, someone would turn around and wash it clean like a dirty rag!]

Jiuzhai complained bitterly in his heart.

—"So, you suspect that this matter involving Si Wu is just like your situation—deliberately targeted by the Heavenly Dao?"-Patriarch Jiuzhai asked.

—"I cannot be certain, but I have no desire to let it find out,"-Zhou Hui replied.

Jiuzhai let out a heavy sigh upon hearing this:

—"Did I come all the way here just to serve as your slave laborer? Before coming, I really should have scrutinized the future properly."

—"...Lord Jiuzhai, didn't you 'look' before you came?"-Ran Deng couldn't resist asking.

Jiuzhai gave no verbal answer; he merely fixed his gaze on Ran Deng and offered a distinctly eerie, chilling grin.

Ran Deng instinctively shrank back behind Zhou Hui.

—"Cheer up,"-Zhou Hui said.-"I know you must have already 'foreseen' it. Today is the final day."

—"Lord Untiring?"-Lord Siwu asked, far more anxiously than Jiuzhai.-"What do you mean?"

—"Thanks to your diligent efforts over these past days, I have a clear understanding of the situation beneath the Abyssal Ocean,"-Zhou Hui answered.

As he spoke, he flicked a casual hand gesture toward the projection hovering before Lord Siwu. The illusion instantly expanded outward, transforming into a three-dimensional topographic sandbox map. Although vast swathes of it remained shrouded in black [fog of war](<# "Literal CN: 黑色 (hēisè) — black/unexplored; classic gaming trope of fog of war on undiscovered maps">), along with the movement trajectory of Lord Siwu's scarecrow substitute, substantial regions had been fully illuminated, allowing one to extrapolate the surrounding terrain.

—"Along this entire path lurk demonic aberrations, vicious beasts, remnant vengeful souls, chaotic demonic miasma, and raging spiritual storms. To drive a Mahayana cultivator into such a desperate corner is indeed no small feat. Yet by the same token, it revealed far more intelligence than before,"-Zhou Hui noted.-"Had I descended to explore on the very first day, it would never have been this effortless. To put it bluntly, had there been demonic nests in the vicinity, my presence might have scared them off entirely. To be able to grasp the situation of this sector today is entirely thanks to your contributions."

—"Ah, please, it was nothing!"-Lord Siwu stammered, his face flushing crimson.

A profound sense of pride and validation welled up within him; he felt that his days of frantically steering that scarecrow through harrowing death traps had truly not been in vain.

Meanwhile, Jiuzhai merely rolled his eyes to the heavens:

[He's using you as expendable bait, you fool!]

Where on earth did this absurd double standard come from? Whenever Jiuzhai merely hitched a harmless ride, everyone suspected him of hatching some insidious, world-ending conspiracy. Yet this fellow Untiring shamelessly and openly exploited everyone under heaven, and people still walked away feeling utterly honored to be used!

Jiuzhai remained thoroughly, hopelessly baffled.

The Untiring Immortal Venerable now addressed Lord Siwu, whom he had dispatched as a [scout to uncover the map](<# "Literal CN: 开地图 (kāi dìtú) — uncovering/revealing the map; modern gaming terminology">):

—"Rest assured. With this map in hand, I shall now proceed to scour and purge that territory, ensuring no further threat lingers."

—"I shall accompany you!"-Lord Siwu offered with fierce excitement.

—"No need. If the upper realm discovers your 'treachery,' your end will not be pleasant."

—"Lord Immortal Venerable—"-Lord Siwu gasped, moved almost to tears.

—"As for you, Lord Jiuzhai..."-Zhou Hui turned toward Jiuzhai.

—"I'm not going,"-Jiuzhai refused with absolute finality.-"Whatever you intend to do down there, it is neither proper for me to 'see' nor proper for me to be present."

—"Very well, I shall go myself,"-Zhou Hui nodded.

***

And so, having settled the affairs of the two 'guests,' Zhou Hui finally brought Ran Deng to the Abyssal Ocean.

To the very spot where he had previously picked up Lord Siwu.

The corpses of the demonic aberrations were already gone, but the entire space remained thoroughly corrupted, radiating a deeply unsettling miasma.

Yet for Zhou Hui to dare bring Ran Deng along, he was naturally certain his disciple would never succumb to demonic corruption of this meager degree.

And indeed, that held true.

Although Ran Deng was hardly an outstanding combatant on the battlefield, when confronted with bizarre, hazardous environments, she proved far more adaptable and alert than her martial siblings. Without needing Zhou Hui's instructions, she could instantly devise countermeasures on the fly.

For example, before descending into the Abyssal Ocean, merely glancing at the scenery within the projection had prompted her to ignite a shroud of pale-orange spirit flames around her body, sizzling the approaching demonic miasma with sharp crackling pops.

Ran Deng surveyed her surroundings with palpable disgust:

—"The air down here is dreadful."

She turned a puzzled gaze toward Zhou Hui:

—"Still, Master, why did you bring *that* along?"

She was referring to the little black lamb nestled in her Master's arms.

For reasons unknown, before departing for the Abyssal Ocean, Master had made a special detour to the mountain gate just to retrieve this black lamb.

—"Oh, this little fellow? It serves a rather unique purpose,"-Zhou Hui said, looking down at the black lamb in his grasp.-"It will come in handy in just a moment."

—"Maa, maa!"-the little black lamb bleated smugly.

Ran Deng fixed a deep, piercing stare upon the creature.

Back when she first saw Master 'pick up' this black lamb from the Blood Demon Hall, she hadn't paid it much mind. Yet for this sheep to survive inside the Heavenly Spirit Sect for so long without being roasted and devoured by Eldest Senior Brother was suspicious in itself.

Furthermore, Second Senior Brother's attitude toward this sheep was conspicuously ambiguous, which deepened Ran Deng's suspicions considerably.

—"Do not stray far from me; this place is treacherous,"-Zhou Hui instructed.-"According to Si Wu's travel route, there are at least three demonic nests and over a dozen beast dens in the immediate vicinity."

Ran Deng's eyes widened in disbelief:

—"That's impossible?! From an [ecological standpoint](<# "Literal CN: 生态学的角度 (shēngtàixué de jiǎodù) — ecological perspective; Zhou Hui's modern scientific framework taught to disciples">), demonic nests and beast dens could never exist in such close proximity, let alone in such sheer quantities! These aren't minor critters!"

Those were colossal monstrosities capable of ambushing a Mahayana titan!

Any single one ought to have been the supreme apex predator of an entire domain.

—"Indeed. That is precisely why it is so bizarre,"-Zhou Hui sighed.-"However, I suspect that had I explored this area on day one, perhaps not a single demonic creature would have appeared at all; everything would have appeared perfectly 'normal.'"

Ran Deng grew anxious upon hearing this:

—"Yet if those creatures were deliberately hiding from you, Master, wouldn't exploring now yield the exact same outcome? They'll still flee the moment they sense you."

—"Then we simply deal with them all in one fell swoop,"-Zhou Hui replied. Naturally, he had already devised the complete solution before ever stepping foot down here.-"Si Wu's—or rather, his substitute's—recent 'explorations' have already allowed me to pinpoint the precise ranges and exact coordinates of every single 'nest.' All that remains is to purge them simultaneously before they can scatter or burrow into hiding."

Along with those words, Zhou Hui raised his hand.

A sphere of concentrated lightning materialized within his palm.

It was precisely one of the heavenly thunder orbs he had harvested during his last heavenly tribulation!

By the intrinsic nature of heavenly lightning, no matter where a cultivator hid—be it deep underground or beneath the abyssal ocean floor—none could ever evade the heavenly trial. Naturally, it possessed the penetrating power to pierce straight through the Abyssal Ocean ruins.

And so, in the span of a single breath, dazzling, incandescent thunder surged forth, blanketing every coordinate in the sector and illuminating a radius of a thousand li as bright as day.

—"...Truly, Second Senior Brother is indeed Master's disciple,"-Ran Deng sighed in awe.

***

—"Ah, the weather today is dreadful; I have a terrible feeling about this."

That morning, Ah Huang grumbled under his breath.

Hearing those words, his colleague burst into boisterous laughter:

—"This is the Abyssal Ocean ruins! You can't even see the sky down here; how on earth could you tell whether the weather is good or bad?!"

—"It's just... a premonition?"-Ah Huang said.-"It feels as though my luck is going to be rotten today."

—"Doesn't that give you even less reason to worry? I heard you were reassigned here precisely because of your miraculous luck!"-his colleague said.-"Granted, the brass upstairs are all bastards, but when it comes to squeezing every ounce of labor out of us, they never waste a thing! If they say you're lucky, it must be the verdict of rigorous evaluation. So if even you are second-guessing your luck, what hope is there for someone like me?!"

[—"..."](<# "Speaker: Ah Huang">)

Ah Huang offered no reply.

He had never dared confess to his colleague what specific event had caused his superiors to deem him "lucky":

Years ago, back when he still worked on the surface, he hadn't even been an official employee of the Carefree Palace headquarters. He had merely been a lowly doorkeeper thug for a subordinate branch, the Fallen Immortal Palace—a nobody who didn't even know what the Fallen Immortal Palace actually did. (To be frank, even down here in the Abyssal Ocean, he still hadn't the foggiest clue what he was actually doing.)

Until that fateful day that altered his destiny forever.

Ah Huang remembered with terrifying clarity that the weather on that day had been dreadful as well, leaving him feeling breathless and stifled. Consequently, barely a short while into his shift, he had slipped away into an obscure corner to slack off.

Little did he anticipate that at that very moment, countless bolts of celestial lightning plunged from the heavens, blasting the entire Fallen Immortal Palace into pulverized dust! What was more, that apocalyptic lightning had refused to relent, plowing the grounds of the Fallen Immortal Palace several times over before finally departing.

Only later did Ah Huang learn that the Fallen Immortal Palace had somehow offended a Tribulation Transcendence titan of the Central Domain—the Untiring Immortal Venerable—incurring that devastating catastrophe. And because Ah Huang had been huddled in that obscure corner, whether because his cultivation was too pitiful to register on the titan's radar or because the grandmaster couldn't be bothered to squish an insignificant ant like him, he had miraculously escaped with his life.

As a consequence of surviving, he received the official accreditation of having "extraordinary luck," which promptly earned him an exile reassignment to the Abyssal Ocean ruins!

Before this, Ah Huang had possessed no clue whatsoever that Carefree Palace maintained such a subterranean facility so deep beneath the Abyssal Ocean. Moreover, the suffocating atmosphere here felt as though once you arrived, even if you broke through to the Void Refinement stage, you would never be permitted to leave.

And so: first encountering a Tribulation titan plowing the earth three feet deep, followed by being banished to the bottom of the Abyssal Ocean... could that truly be called "good luck"?!

Ah Huang brooded in bitter misery.

—"Alright, stop woolgathering; it's about time for our shift,"-his colleague said, clapping Ah Huang on the shoulder.

—"Right,"-Ah Huang nodded.

Together with his colleague, he donned the uniform armor, secured the protective face mask, proceeded to the storehouse, retrieved a large basin, and placed it before a grotesque, massive vat that incessantly seeped fresh blood and emitted muffled, agonizing groans from within.

Ah Huang had no clue what was inside the vat, nor did he possess the slightest desire to find out.

Exactly as he did every day, he pulled on his gloves and unplugged the wooden bung on the side of the vat. A thick black liquid gushed out, accompanied by an overpowering, heady aroma of aged wine that pierced right through his protective mask.

Inhaling the fragrance, his colleague marveled for what felt like the hundredth time:

—"It smells so heavenly... I really want to sneak a taste."

—"Don't! Have you already forgotten Number Three? His flesh rotted away until his entire stomach was gone!"-Ah Huang warned in panic.

He could not comprehend why, with such a horrifying gruesome precedent right before their eyes, his colleagues always harbored an irresistible urge to [court death](<# "Literal CN: 作死 (zuòsǐ) — seek death / play with fire; modern slang for recklessly inviting fatal disaster">). In truth, this was the fifth colleague Ah Huang had rotated through in just a few short years. For a job categorized as "non-hazardous," its casualty turnover rate was staggering.

—"I was only joking,"-his colleague mumbled.

Since the other party said so, Ah Huang did not press the matter further to avoid breeding animosity. In the suffocating depths of the Abyssal Ocean, the last thing one wanted was to offend a coworker—after all, you never knew when someone might snap and go mad, and when that happened, the target of their resentment was invariably the first to suffer.

Ah Huang and his colleague hauled the basins of black water toward a long row of feeding troughs and dumped the liquid inside.

Behind the feeding troughs stood a row of barred, grated windows. As the heady aroma of fine wine wafted inward, pale hands reached out through the grates to scoop up the black water. Immediately following were the eerie giggles of young children, intermingled with the soft, alluring voices of seductive maidens.

Much like the intoxicating wine, the sounds made one's very bones itch.

Ah Huang acted as though he had heard nothing at all. Having poured the black water, he turned on his heel to leave.

This was his sole "job."

Four times a day, pouring black water into the feeding troughs.

Seeing Ah Huang turn to leave, his colleague called out:

—"Leaving already? Aren't you going inside to have a little fun?"

Fun? Finding "fun" with whatever unknown abominations were trapped inside?!

Every single worker brought down here had been sternly warned never to peek inside!

—"Well, if we don't wash out this vat, it will cause trouble later,"-Ah Huang offered with feigned consideration.-"Why don't you head inside first? I'll come join you after I finish scouring the vat."

—"Then you must definitely come!"-his colleague insisted.

At that split second, his colleague's pupils dilated until his eyes were as pitch-black and devoid of light as the cursed black water.

A sudden chill of cold sweat broke out across Ah Huang's back. Under his colleague's unblinking stare, he struggled to push the heavy vat toward the washroom.

However, his luck today was truly abysmal. Halfway there, he caught his foot on something and took a violent tumble. The colossal vat rolled several times across the flagstones, and in a stroke of sheer absurdity, ended up wedged squarely over his head.

And at that exact microsecond—thunder roared.

Dense, relentless, deafening thunderclaps rumbled without pause, as though blanketing the entirety of the Abyssal Ocean.

Through the narrow gap beneath the rim of the vat, Ah Huang saw an abnormal, blinding white glare erupt.

[—"...This situation feels awfully familiar!"](<# "Speaker: Ah Huang">)

Ah Huang thought to himself. Halting his initial movement to scramble out, he curled up obediently beneath the shelter of the massive vat.

Right at that moment, an icy chill prickled the nape of his neck—something like a droplet of liquid had landed on his skin.

*Oh gods, no! A stray droplet of residual black water from inside the vat!*

Seized by stark terror, Ah Huang gripped the hem of his robe, not daring to touch his neck. According to the mandatory safety briefing on day one, low-level laborers were strictly forbidden from ever coming into direct physical contact with the black water.

[Is it over? Am I going to rot away into a puddle too?!]

As he wallowed in sheer despair, he noticed crackling blue electrical sparks dancing across the inner walls of the vat. The residual black water clinging to the wood let out ear-splitting shrieks before vaporizing into wisps of gray ash!

The exact same shrill shriek echoed from the back of his neck, accompanied by a sharp, tingling numbness.

Yet that electrical current did not truly harm Ah Huang; it merely jolted him until his limbs trembled like leaves in the wind.

Even so, Ah Huang remained huddled with his arms wrapped tightly around his knees, not daring to twitch a single muscle. He naturally lacked the audacity to lift the vat and peek at the carnage outside; he simply chanted the sacred title of the Untiring Immortal Venerable under his breath over and over, praying for the apocalypse to end swiftly.

It was hard to say this was not the correct decision; his relentless caution had always spared him endless grief.

Yet on this occasion, it caused him to miss the chance to witness an utterly bizarre spectacle:

Had he dared lift the vat and steal a glance outside, he would have seen that heavenly thunder—precisely like the day it obliterated the Fallen Immortal Palace years ago—smash the entire facility to absolute smithereens. From within the shattered vats and the cavernous chambers behind the feeding troughs, countless swarms of dense, shrieking wraiths surged outward. Some disintegrated into flying ash midair under the heavenly lightning; others, shielded by their fellows, fled alongside demonic aberrations and monstrous beasts pouring out from other quarters, scrambling in a desperate frenzy toward the sole opening spared within the thunder net.

And waiting at that lone opening... was a colossal, cavernous maw, wide open and waiting to swallow them whole.
