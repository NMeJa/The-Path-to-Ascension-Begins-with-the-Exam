# Chapter 231: Tian Yan

[Did something happen over at the banquet as well?]

Tian Yan's footsteps paused.

No one had reported anything to him, yet an ominous premonition rose unbidden within his chest.

"An ominous premonition felt by Tian Yan"—such a sensation itself meant that whatever loomed could never be a trivial matter.

Unlike the vast majority of sinecures in the Heavenly Court, after all, the office of Chief of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny was no cushy post handed out for nothing. Not only did it demand substantive governance, it directly concerned the Heavenly Dao of the Celestial Realm.

To this end, when the "previous" Chief of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny sensed his impending demise, he had scoured the three thousand worlds through astromantic divination to handpick the two most brilliant hexagram cultivators of that era. He had petitioned the Celestial Emperor to issue an imperial decree forcing them into wedlock, lavished them with heavenly treasures to nourish their physiques, and only upon confirming that they had conceived a fetus inheriting the pinnacle talents of both parents did he reincarnate into the unborn child.

Such was the origin of "Tian Yan."

Tian Yan had entered the world versed in civil governance and military strategy, nearly omniscient from birth.

From the heights of the Heavenly Court down to the past, present, and future of the three thousand worlds, Tian Yan could deduce virtually everything under the heavens.

Yet "nearly omniscient" was not true omniscience after all.

Tian Yan was no heavenly deity. Nay, even an omniscient god, when confronted with the sprawling and indeterminate permutations of future trajectories, could suffer cognitive dissonance. Far less could be expected of an immortal official like Tian Yan, whose operating interface remained fundamentally human. Inevitably, there were moments when he simply "did not know." Such occasions were exceedingly rare, yet even when he truly knew nothing, a distinct "feeling" would take root.

Such as the ominous premonition gripping him right now.

A calamity unknown even to the "omniscient" Lord Tian Yan was, by definition, a monumental catastrophe—with a high probability of spelling disaster for both the Heavenly Court and Tian Yan himself.

Yet though Tian Yan cast a glance in the direction of the banquet hall, he ultimately chose not to investigate in person.

For most of his existence, Tian Yan had dealt solely with "things already known." When it came to an "unknown omen," he believed that no degree of caution could ever be deemed excessive.

Moreover, the banquet was far from the only source radiating an ill omen at this moment. He sensed dark portents from several other quarters simultaneously:

At the front lines of the God-Demon Battlefield, his perception of the combat outcomes had abruptly severed;

Within his own Ministry of Celestial Destiny, the official seal bound to his avatar had lost all spiritual resonance, and the locations of the vast majority of his subordinates were suddenly impossible to detect;

And worst of all, naturally, was the Sky-Tour Sacrificial Rite that had yielded zero divine response.

The so-called Sky-Tour, in the eyes of ignorant outsiders, was merely an ostentatious royal progress wherein the Celestial Emperor toured a select few worlds favored by the Heavenly Court, culminating in a ceremonial prayer at Xuanpu to flaunt celestial majesty.

In reality, the Sky-Tour was anything but hollow pageantry; it was a paramount and indispensable mechanism.

During the procession, the Celestial Emperor did far more than perform ceremonial inspections. With every world he entered, acting as the eyes of the heavenly god, he assimilated the entirety of that realm's information. As these vast archives accumulated, by the time the concluding sacrificial rite commenced, the heavenly god would bestow divine power in accordance with the Celestial Emperor's wishes, recalibrating ambient spiritual energy and the karmic fortune of every world across the cosmos.

Thus, it was no exaggeration to say that each Sky-Tour decided the future of every realm under heaven.

For that very reason, according to the ancient calendar, the original circuit was supposed to encompass every single world and cosmic expanse touched by celestial power. Yet as the domain governed by the Heavenly Court sprawled ever wider and the governed realms multiplied exponentially, adhering to the complete protocol would demand over ten thousand years per tour. Even an emperor could not stomach such an eternity, prompting the circuit to be pruned down to only the few most pivotal realms.

Though this revision meant certain minor worlds went without the heavenly god's divine grace or suffered oversights, it had long been treated as an acceptable compromise, and thus no "trouble" had ever arisen.

Until now. This was the first time in recorded history that a sacrifice following the Sky-Tour had received no response whatsoever.

In truth, even before the sacrifice—before the Sky-Tour had even commenced—Tian Yan had harbored an inexplicable foreboding. Yet whenever he attempted to probe deeper, his mind drew a complete blank.

To someone who had been "nearly omniscient from birth," such an "unknown" was more horrifying than any known disaster.

It was akin to a sighted man suddenly struck blind, or a hearing man abruptly struck deaf; every familiar fixture of daily existence seemed to transmute into a lethal trap.

Consequently, just as he would never personally set foot in the banquet hall shrouded in bad omens, he refused to venture blindly into any other unknown territory—including his own Ministry of Celestial Destiny. Even though his official seal had lost its resonance "without rhyme or reason," that very lack of reason was the most terrifying element of all.

[Resolving minor details accomplishes nothing; what matters is resolving the root cause behind everything.]

[Indeed, misfortunes do not swarm together without cause. There must be a singular source behind it all.]

[Only by locating that source can the unknown be transformed into the known.]

So Tian Yan pondered.

He paced a slow circle around the outer perimeter of the Xuanpu altar. Then, once the gathered dignitaries had all dispersed, he stepped back atop the sacrificial terrace.

Tian Yan was not deliberately procrastinating on the Celestial Emperor's orders, nor was he fleeing from those "ominous premonitions." Rather, before setting out, he had to locate the "source of the omen" precisely as he had reasoned.

To put it plainly: he needed to cast a divination.

As the Heavenly Court's court diviner, Tian Yan was nearly omniscient even without actively divining. When he did cast a hexagram, he obtained computational throughput on par with a heavenly god, attaining absolute, thorough "omniscience."

Yet Tian Yan rarely cast divinations—in fact, he almost never went out of his way to do so.

Aside from the fact that a single divination severely overextended his celestial power and karmic fortune, there was a far graver consequence: the moment he calculated a hexagram, regardless of whether the omen was auspicious or grim, that particular "future" became irrevocably fixed.

Indeed, the Heavenly Dao of the Celestial Realm possessed no pre-written script, nor could anyone draft a script for it. The Heavenly Court's future was inherently indeterminate, fluidly shifting according to countless variables. Yet the moment someone peered into that future, the timeline collapsed into a fixed state. And the greater the power of the observer, the more unalterable that outcome became—a truth that applied above all to Tian Yan.

A future witnessed by Tian Yan was virtually impossible to change.

Even for the Heavenly Court itself, a future with room for maneuver was infinitely preferable to one locked in stone. For that very reason, not even the Celestial Emperor would compel him to divine against his will.

Yet to Tian Yan, the "unknown" was far more terrifying than a "certain bad ending."

Furthermore, with so many ill omens whose specifics eluded even him, the present "future" was already heavily tilted toward some disastrous trajectory. Remaining in total ignorance would be far more lethal.

Tian Yan deemed it essential to ascertain the truth.

At the same time, based on all the factors above, this divination could not be conducted carelessly.

First, the target of the inquiry could not be too monumental. Questions like "What will become of the Heavenly Court?" or "Why did the heavenly god fail to respond to the sacrifice?" were strictly out of the question. The moment a result was calculated, the status of the Heavenly Court and the heavenly god would be locked in. If he were unlucky enough to divine catastrophic results such as "The Heavenly Court faces destruction" or "The heavenly god is compromised," not even Tian Yan would possess the power to reverse it.

By the same token, the query could not be too direct. Asking point-blank where the "ominous premonitions" originated would force what was previously a mere "premonition" to crystallize into immutable reality.

Second, the temporal focus could not be set on the future, yet neither could it fixate on the past. In the worst-case scenario, manipulating time—rewinding into the past or traversing into the future—might be required to achieve a desired outcome. One could not solidify the "past" simply because events had already transpired; both past and future had to retain the elastic malleability required for temporal intervention.

Lastly, the subject of the divination could naturally not be Tian Yan himself or the "vital figures" by his side, lest he lock in an unalterable tragedy. Yet neither could the subject be an utterly detached, irrelevant outsider, as whatever was deduced would offer Tian Yan no practical utility.

Having weighed all these constraints, the question to be divined became exceedingly simple.

—"I need to know the fortunes and misfortunes of my subordinates—the immortal officials of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny,"-Tian Yan intoned.

This was the "safest" possible query.

As the department under Tian Yan's personal governance, the status of the ministry's officials was intimately bound to him; their trajectories would clearly highlight where the crux of the crisis lay. At the same time, because they were "merely" subordinates, even if some grim fate were divined upon them, it was of little consequence to Tian Yan.

If anything, the very existence of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny had been designed precisely for contingencies such as this.

Tian Yan closed his eyes. Raising both hands toward the sacrificial altar laden with cold, aroma-less offerings, he formed the mudra of divination.

In tandem with his movements, a gust of wind swept up the mist enveloping the altar, lifting Tian Yan's long hair as the vapor condensed into a dense canopy of clouds. From somewhere beyond perception, celestial music faintly resonated—

Yet—

A [funeral dirge](# "Literal CN: 哀乐 (āiyuè) — mourning music / funeral dirge")?!

Tian Yan's eyes flew wide.

It was too late. The altar, bathed in brilliant sunlight only moments prior, was now entirely enveloped in suffocating storm clouds. A desolate wind howled across the stone, echoing like the mournful weeping of a grieving soul.

Without question, this was an omen of [supreme calamity](# "Literal CN: 大凶 (dàxiōng) — great ill fortune / supreme calamity").

And not merely ordinary calamity—at a glance, it presaged the absolute worst variety of ruin.

—"...Depleted troops, exhausted rations; [surrounded on all sides](# "Literal CN: 四面楚歌 (sìmiàn chǔgē) — hearing Songs of Chu on four sides; completely besieged with no way out"), [total annihilation](# "Literal CN: 全军覆没 (quánjūn fùmò) — the entire army wiped out")?"-Tian Yan muttered the hexagram's verdict aloud in disbelief.

As a diviner, this was the first time in his life he had ever witnessed a verdict so brutally direct, completely devoid of any redeeming silver lining or turnaround. It was practically poking him right between the eyes and barking: *Every single one of you can go die!*

—"Impossible. One or two might be understandable, but how could the entire Ministry of Celestial Destiny be..."-Tian Yan uttered in utter incredulity.

In that instant, he threw all prior reservations and cautious calculations to the wind, caring nothing for the staggering expenditure of celestial power and karmic luck. Pulling the official roster of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny from his sleeve, he smoothed the scroll flat across the altar to divine precisely how they had fallen into such "supreme calamity."

The process was far from smooth.

Tian Yan could palpably sense something actively interfering with his divination. And what terrified him most was that this disruptive force inexplicably seemed to emanate from the depths of the altar itself—from the "heavenly god"!

Fortunately, the interference was not overpowering—or rather, the entity harbored no deliberate intent to obstruct him. It was simply that the power once bestowed upon the Heavenly Court was no longer flowing with its customary smoothness. Moreover, the fates of every official in the Ministry of Celestial Destiny were far too uniform and tragically "abrupt"—akin to divining the destiny of men on death's door. It did not demand the massive exertion of power usually required, and Tian Yan ultimately brought the divination to completion.

Pressing his palm against the ministry's roster to read their fates, flickering images began projecting into Tian Yan's mind like a cinematic reel.

First, Tian Yan "witnessed" a gore-drenched battlefield.

Endless hordes of demons seemed to "sprout" from spatial fissures, from the ocean, from the earth, and from the sky, churning with black-crimson demonic miasma as they rampaged across the world.

—"The God-Demon Battlefield?"-Tian Yan muttered instinctively, though he caught himself almost immediately:—"No. These coordinates belong to Langhuan. Did the demons launch an advance assault... No, that's not right either. Are these demons merely phantoms of the past? This entire space... is it a resonant phantom illusion of the God-Demon Battlefield from ten thousand years ago?"

Tian Yan had seen through the essence of the apparition in a single glance. Yet because he had, his apprehension only deepened.

—"What could possibly sustain a phantom illusion of such colossal scale... No, that doesn't matter! A genuine demon invasion would actually be preferable; after all, 'immortals' counter 'demons' under the immutable laws of Heavenly Rule. But inside an illusion, the 'rules' turn treacherous in order to conform with 'history.' Even True Immortals of our Ministry of Celestial Destiny would be reduced to mere playthings of whatever script is executing within—"

As if in answer to Tian Yan's dread, a bloodcurdling shriek rang out "not far away."

Without needing to turn his head, Tian Yan could "see" the subjects of his divination: the immortal officials of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny.

When swept into this illusion, the officials had been scattered like dust. The group Tian Yan currently "beheld" was a four-man party. Yet gone were the opulent, magically fortified vestments of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny; in their place, they wore the faded, obsolete uniforms of the lowest-ranking celestial soldiers. Disheveled and frantic, they fled before a ravenous pack of demons, their formidable immortal cultivation completely paralyzed.

—"Just as I feared: the worst-case scenario,"-Tian Yan muttered.

Having been dragged into the phantom illusion, they were bound to abide by its rules. In the historical God-Demon War, no officials from the Ministry of Celestial Destiny had participated; in the early stages, not even high-ranking True Immortals had been present. Consequently, when the ministry's officials tumbled into the illusion, they were mapped directly onto the celestial soldiers originally stationed in Langhuan for "reconnaissance."

The catastrophe was that Tian Yan remembered: that particular garrison of celestial soldiers had been "sacrificed."

Or more accurately, the Heavenly Court back then had already resolved to use Langhuan as a killing ground to annihilate the main demonic host. They had deliberately ignored the early distress calls dispatched by White Jade Capital in Langhuan, waiting until the demons had completely overrun the realm before entering with their primary legions under the pretext of answering the world's desperate summons—turning the entire realm into a chessboard to wage war against the demons.

That strategic decision had naturally been part of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny's "distinguished record."

When the decree was handed down, of course, not a soul cared what gruesome fate awaited the celestial soldiers stationed in old Langhuan.

Now, however, they were being granted the privilege of experiencing it "firsthand."

Their celestial vestments replaced, their cultivation suppressed, and having spent millennia cooped up in bureaucratic [desk jobs](# "Literal CN: 办公室 (bàngōngshì) — modern office / desk job humorously applied to celestial civil servants") without fighting a single real battle, the officials were chased into pathetic disarray.

Fortunately, their luck was decent(?); their drop point happened to be the closest to White Jade Capital. Quite naturally, the officials bolted toward White Jade Capital as fast as their legs and rudimentary spells could carry them.

Even so, halfway through their sprint, one official was snagged from behind and ripped limb from limb while still drawing breath.

[—"RUN!"](# "Speaker: Immortal Official")

The remaining three officials did not pause for a fraction of a second over their colleague's demise.

It was not strictly because they lacked camaraderie (though in truth they possessed precious little to begin with); rather, as True Immortals, a "death" of this nature did not mean true obliteration. They had myriad methods of resurrection at their disposal, making it utterly pointless to throw away their own lives on a rescue. Furthermore, inside an illusion teeming with peril, not knowing how many times one might die, it was prudent to avoid dying carelessly. Above all, the agonizing pain and sheer terror of that final instant were sensations that even an ascended True Immortal would rather not experience repeatedly.

And so, the trio flew all the faster.

Watching them scurry toward White Jade Capital, Tian Yan's brow furrowed tightly.

—"Don't go there,"-he attempted to warn them.

Yet he swiftly found himself hitting an impenetrable barrier.

Though Tian Yan could "see" the scene, his true body remained atop the terrace of Xuanpu in the Celestial Realm, merely observing his subordinates' status through astromantic divination.

Under normal circumstances, of course, this posed no obstacle. Even across multiple worlds, Tian Yan could transmit his voice directly to anyone he wished. But this time, against this particular illusion, his voice could not pierce the boundary.

And even if it had, it was already too late.

The three officials reached the vicinity of White Jade Capital's grand gate. Just as expressions of wild relief bloomed across their faces and they made to plunge through, the demonic pack lingering at a distance suddenly surged forward with explosive velocity!

In a single coordinated pounce, two of the officials were ensnared. Swarmed like men engulfed by an angry cloud of hornets, they could not break free in the slightest—unable to squeeze out so much as a dying shriek.

Moments later, nothing fell to the stone except a few gnawed remnants of bone.

To suffer such gruesome deaths paired with repeated "resurrections"—after just a few rounds, even a True Immortal would be driven utterly insane, perhaps even begging for the sweet mercy of soul obliteration. Trapped within the confines of this "illusion," however, the nightmare was destined to replay endlessly.

What was more, this dramatic trope of dying at the final millimeter of apparent escape looked far from natural; it reeked of calculated manipulation.

—"Like a clumsy, amateur script,"-Tian Yan critiqued in displeasure.

As he uttered the words, he seemed completely oblivious to the fact that his Ministry of Celestial Destiny spent every waking day toying with mortals and cultivators using identical theatrical scripts. In truth, this very death trope was a common staple in the tragedies penned by his own subordinates.

Nor did Tian Yan genuinely care about the "deaths" of his minions.

Gazing coldly as the sole "surviving" official dashed headlong into White Jade Capital, he harbored a distinct feeling that if a director truly presided over this illusion, given the sadistic tastes displayed thus far, this last survivor would meet no happy end either.

Reality matched Tian Yan's deduction to the letter.

Some grand ceremony seemed to be underway in the central plaza of White Jade Capital. The instant the official sprinted inside, a colossal spatial rift tore open right on cue, and a gargantuan entity plunged through, crushing the sprinting official into a cloud of fine powder!

Yet the moment Tian Yan caught a clear glimpse of what had emerged from the spatial fissure, he froze in place:

—"Lu Shi?!"-Tian Yan gasped.

[Ah, a purely visceral, reflexive jolt of fright...]

[That wretched dragon had perished long ago. Indeed, didn't it die right here on the God-Demon Battlefield ten thousand years ago? To suppress its skeleton—and later to reclaim it—immense effort had been expended, and rumor had it the retrieval hadn't even succeeded.]

Tian Yan contemplated the apparition with a stony face.

For the "omniscient" Lord Tian Yan to react with such shock was only natural, for this dragon bore an unmistakable resemblance to the one that had originally guarded the Ministry of Celestial Destiny.

To frame it that way made the beast sound like a glorified doorman, yet much like the Pixiu guarding the Celestial Emperor's private treasury, a True Dragon capable of overseeing the Ministry of Celestial Destiny was no ordinary creature. Such divine beasts inherently carried "divinity." Depending on what they guarded, they could even command a substantial degree of divine authority, empowering them to adjust—and protect—the Heavenly Dao of the Celestial Realm.

It could be said that as long as that dragon stood watch, the Heavenly Court's Heavenly Dao would never falter.

Under those circumstances, however, not even the Chief of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny wielded total authority to command the beast; in fact, he was obliged to heed the dragon's "counsel" to a considerable degree.

In every conceivable sense, the immortal and the dragon had harbored massive disagreements.

Especially regarding the question of Langhuan.

When Langhuan originally fell and its denizens clashed with the Heavenly Court, Tian Yan had advocated abandoning Langhuan entirely. The dragon, however, maintained that Langhuan was an indispensable part of the Heavenly Realm. When the God-Demon War subsequently erupted, the beast had proactively descended to Langhuan in an attempt to avert the conflict.

Tian Yan had seized that golden opportunity to sabotage the dragon behind the scenes, ensuring the beast was trapped and buried there forever.

Naturally, this was a secret.

A secret known to neither immortal nor god.

After all, if word leaked that the Chief of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny had assassinated his department's own guardian divine beast and sealed its remains beneath the earth, it would make for a rather ugly scandal. Thus, Tian Yan had smothered all records.

Perhaps the Celestial Emperor was aware, yet he had never uttered a word, so Tian Yan simply treated him as oblivious.

—"Haha! Who could have dreamed we would reunite like this one day?"-Tian Yan sneered, though a flicker of doubt soon crept across his features:—"Yet... why does something feel slightly off?"

Tian Yan moved to scrutinize the beast more closely when the jade pendant at his waist began chiming insistently.

Clicking his tongue in irritation, Tian Yan did not immediately withdraw his sight from the illusion. Tapping the jade pendant, a frantic voice instantly barked from within:

—"Tian Yan, what in the blazes is happening?! Didn't you swear that 'he' wouldn't show up on the battlefield?! Why is 'he' standing right here?!"

—"'He'?"-Tian Yan asked.

—"Don't play dumb with me! Your nephew! That androgynous, pretty-boy face of his—one look and anybody could tell!"

[Who are you calling androgynous...]

Yet Tian Yan had no leisure to take offense at the derogatory slur. His eyes widened, and he finally brought the jade pendant to eye level:

—"Nephew?!"-Tian Yan demanded.

The pendant immediately projected a radiant holographic illusion. The scene bore an eerie resemblance to the vista before him—another grand battlefield of immortals and demons. At the very nexus of the demonic vanguard stood a slender, statuesque "Demon Sovereign."

He was breathtakingly gorgeous. Amidst the grotesque, churning demon horde, he stood out like a crimson water lily blooming upon a stagnant swamp, commanding the gaze of any onlooker even from afar, making it impossible to tear one's eyes away.

Yet as Tian Yan stared at that face bearing a subtle, undeniable resemblance to his own, his gaze turned black as pitch.

—"I see... so that ill omen was him all along?"-Tian Yan muttered in a glacial whisper.-"I clearly cast him down into Langhuan..."

Ah. Present-day Langhuan was evidently harboring an enormous problem.

Tian Yan cast a glance around his surroundings, his eyes lingering particularly upon that colossal dragon.

The voice inside the jade pendant continued blabbering in panic:

—"What do we do?! I can't actually fight him, can I?! I mean, I don't give a damn about slaughtering someone who shares your bloodline! But right at the critical juncture when the Celestial Emperor is scouring the realms for an heir, he's strictly off-limits, isn't he?! If memory serves, isn't he the son of your younger sister and the Celestial Em—"

—"Shut your mouth!"-Tian Yan barked.

Perhaps shocked to hear the perpetually suave, scholarly Tian Yan speak in such a raw, menacing tone, the voice abruptly swallowed its words into dead silence.

Gazing out across the illusion at the True Dragon, and then glancing back toward the "Demon Sovereign" commanding the opposing battlefield, a chilling smile curled upon Tian Yan's lips.

—"Though I know not by what means he managed to appear there, now that he stands upon that field, the possibility of that prophecy coming to fruition can no longer be dismissed,"-Tian Yan murmured.
