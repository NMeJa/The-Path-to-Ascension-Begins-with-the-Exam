# Chapter 51: Yuwen Xuan's Emergency Food Rations (第51章 宇文轩的储备粮)

In the end, Yuwen Xuan wasn't able to come down immediately.

It wasn't because Zhou Hui also felt that hanging for merely five days was too short; rather, Yuwen Xuan had brought it entirely upon himself by [courting disaster](# "Literal CN: 作 (zuò) — courting death / drama-queen antics / bringing trouble upon oneself").

He too felt that five days was far too short(X). Relying on his rich accumulated experience of being strung up in the past, the quick-witted Eldest Senior Brother guessed that his revered Master likely had some task lined up for him to handle, and thus actually had the audacity to put on airs.

—"I won't come down!"-Yuwen Xuan declared in a [tsundere](# "Literal CN: 傲娇 (àojiāo) — putting on a coy, obstinate, arrogant front despite secretly wanting the opposite") tone, delivering lines of self-reflection that held not a single shred of sincerity:—"On the Double Tenth festival, I slaved away cooking for the entire sect all day long! I only played around for a tiny bit after dinner, barely even gambled, yet Master strung me right up here! Truly, I am in dire need of deep reflection!"

[Eldest Senior Brother, do you want to take a look at what you look like right now before you start courting disaster?]

Mo Xi roasted him inwardly.

Tucked safely behind his Master, he had no desire whatsoever to lay eyes on Yuwen Xuan hanging from the tree like an enormous fleshy caterpillar.

Even the several monsters strung up alongside him found it utterly unbearable:

[—"Brother Yuwen, do you call that reflection?!"](# "Speaker: Monster Gambling Buddy")

[—"Apologize to your Master properly! Quick, tell him you won't dare do it next time!"](# "Speaker: Monster Gambling Buddy")

[You might be happy hanging here, but if we hang any longer, we're going to turn into [air-dried jerky](# "Literal CN: 风干 (fēnggān) — wind-dried meat; monsters lamenting they'll become dried jerky if left hanging on the tree")!]

There were also those who pleaded directly to Zhou Hui for mercy:

—"Lord Immortal Venerable, this little demon knows its wrongs! Next time I will never, ever play mahjong with Brother Yuwen... err, play cards, or engage in any other activities involving gambling! This little one swears upon its life!"

—"Immortal Venerable, I have been wronged! I am merely an ordinary rat demon from the back mountain! I only came because I was coerced; it wasn't that I genuinely wanted to gamble!"

[—"Yes, exactly, we were forced~~!"](# "Speaker: Monster Gambling Buddies")

Instantly, the tree canopy echoed with a chaotic chorus of desperate pleas for mercy, leaving only Yuwen Xuan raising his chin "defiantly," stealing glances at Zhou Hui exclusively from the corners of his eyes.

Zhou Hui was provoked into a laugh.

—"Fair enough. Since you desire reflection so deeply, don't bother coming down,"-Zhou Hui said, flicking his sleeve.

*Crack!*

Yuwen Xuan heard his already-pulverized lumbar vertebrae creak and groan as his shattered spine was somehow twisted into yet another knot, securing him to the tree even more firmly than before!

Standing to the side, Mo Xi grimaced so hard his teeth felt numb with phantom pain.

Zhou Hui didn't subject the other monsters to the same treatment.

After all—apart from snake demons and dragonkind, what other monster could possibly endure being tied into knots like that?!

Those unlucky monsters strung up as collateral damage not only escaped being tied into knots, but fell from the branches like overripe peaches—*thud, thud, thud*—slamming straight onto the ground.

Fortunately, these creatures possessed thick hides and coarse flesh, hardly minding a few impacts against the turf.

Even so, none dared to clamber back to their feet right away; instead, they remained in full [five-point prostration](# "Literal CN: 五体投地 (wǔ tǐ tóu dì) — casting five points of the body (knees, elbows, head) to the ground in total submission") before Zhou Hui, saying with devout honesty:

[—"Thank you, Immortal Venerable, for sparing our humble lives!"](# "Speaker: Monster Gambling Buddies")

Zhou Hui didn't tell them to dispense with formalities either, merely arching a brow and asking:

—"Speaking of which, since you regularly gamble with Yuwen Xuan, you ought to frequent the [Rare Beast Enclosure](# "Literal CN: 珍兽苑 (Zhēnshòuyuàn) — Spirit Beast Pens / Rare Beast Enclosure in the back mountain where exotic beasts are reared"), correct? Do you happen to know which disciples are regularly dragged over there to do chores?"

Speaking of this matter, Zhou Hui was also somewhat annoyed.

According to sect protocol, all disciples assigned to work across various divisions were meant to be formally registered. Given Qu Qianxing's meticulous nature, this area was ordinarily managed with sterling precision: every disciple's post, duty shift, and transfer were recorded with crystal clarity, accompanied by regular surveys auditing disciple job satisfaction.

The sole exception was the Rare Beast Enclosure, where the rosters were a murky, tangled mess.

Chiefly, no one wanted to go to the Rare Beast Enclosure in the first place, meaning zero candidates voluntarily signed up for it. Yuwen Xuan couldn't care less; whenever he found himself short of hands, he would simply dash over to the Palace of Silence or other departments to grab random disciples at will. Whether their wages were ever properly settled was anyone's guess. The disciples themselves were equally unwilling to register—their thought process was presumably: [Being snatched away for temp labor is rotten luck as it is; if we actually get registered under the Rare Beast Enclosure, wouldn't that trap us in that horrific nightmare factory for the rest of our lives?!]

Therefore, to get a clear picture of the Rare Beast Enclosure's actual status, Yuwen Xuan himself would naturally know best.

Now that Zhou Hui was asking Yuwen Xuan's gambling buddies, the monsters merely shook their heads [in a daze](# "Literal CN: 懵懂 (měngdǒng) — muddle-headed / bewildered / in a daze").

—"D-Don't know!"-one large, lumbering simpleton of a monster stammered. It was a black bear demon:—"I'm not that close with Yuwen Xuan, just play cards together once in a blue moon! Whatever he does, I don't know a thing!"

Another comparatively shrewder monster shot the black bear demon a glare, yet spoke up all the same:

—"Immortal Venerable, though we frequently fool around with Brother Yuwen, it's mostly at the Delicacy Pavilion. A place like the Rare Beast Enclosure... how could little demons of our meager cultivation ever dare draw near?"

—"Oh?"-Zhou Hui narrowed his eyes dangerously.

Sensing the faint spiritual pressure radiating from the Immortal Venerable, the smallest among the monsters—the one who had introduced itself as a rat demon—instantly shrieked:

—"Lord Immortal Venerable! This little one knows!"

—"Short Tail, you—"-Yuwen Xuan barked from above.

Without even sparing him a glance, Zhou Hui raised a hand. A soft *splat* sounded from above, and Yuwen Xuan fell instantly silent.

Instead, a fine spray of bloody foam rained down, spattering the monsters from head to foot.

The demons immediately froze stiff on the spot, not even daring to tremble.

Zhou Hui smiled warmly at the rat demon:

—"Continue."

—"Al-Although this little one and the others played cards at the Delicacy Pavilion, Lord Yuwen's servant beasts—no, disciples!—frequently came to the Delicacy Pavilion to fetch fodder, so we remembered who they were,"-the rat demon reported hurriedly.

—"Very good,"-Zhou Hui said, finally satisfied:—"Go summon them here."

***

The rat demon proved remarkably efficient. A mere quarter-hour later, three or four disciples were escorted before Zhou Hui—supposedly the serv... err, disciples whom Yuwen Xuan favored most.

For the second time today, Zhou Hui was provoked into a rueful laugh.

The old adage 'birds of a feather flock together' was truly not without merit.

[Top students](# "Literal CN: 学霸 (xuébà) — academic prodigies / straight-A honor students") usually hung out with top students, [academic failures](# "Literal CN: 学渣 (xuézhā) — academic underachievers / failing students") hung out with academic failures, and the few disciples Yuwen Xuan had rounded up to labor in the Rare Beast Enclosure were [pure, unadulterated trash](# "Literal CN: 渣渣 (zhāzhā) — dregs / worthless scrap / utter failures").

As a 'teacher,' passing such an evaluation on disciples of his own sect was admittedly poor form, but the objective facts spoke for themselves.

These few were unlike Qin Xian or Yu Cong, whose lackluster scores on the inner sect assessment or sect-wide general exams stemmed from pursuing intense specializations that caused [severe subject imbalance](# "Literal CN: 偏科 (piānkē) — skewed academic performance; excelling in one specialty while failing all general subjects"). Nor were they like Guan Ye or Zhou Hui, afflicted with deficient spirit roots or congenital handicaps. Rather, they were the exceptionally well-rounded variety of academic failures—[trash of the most comprehensive order](# "Literal CN: 渣得非常全面 — well-rounded failures who fail equally and consistently across every single category"). Even though Zhou Hui's exam papers were dynamically tailored to spoon-feed questions matching each examinee's individual aptitudes, not a single strong point could be found across their entire profile.

And this was on a purely written examination!

One had to understand that cultivating immortality inherently augmented one's physiological and mental faculties!

Barring those who entered the Dao through raw body-tempering, the vast majority of cultivators who stepped into Qi Condensation and Foundation Establishment received initial stat-boosts to 'memory' and 'comprehension' to facilitate their cultivation. This meant that cultivators learned with far greater ease than ordinary mortals.

—This was precisely why many illiterate cultivators could quickly learn to read and write and perform mental arithmetic shortly after joining a sect.

To still bomb a written exam under such conditions made one genuinely question whether they had a brain inside their skulls.

Not to mention, Zhou Hui had not yet conducted practical exams—according to his curriculum syllabus, those were slated for next spring. If practical assessments were factored in, their marks would surely be even more horrifyingly unwatchable, outright declaring to the teacher: *Despair! In this world, there really do exist people who cannot learn a single damn thing!*

Just how on earth had Yuwen Xuan managed to dig these individuals out?

Pressing a palm to his forehead, Zhou Hui sighed:

—"Fine. Lead the way to the Rare Beast Enclosure."

—"Yes, Immortal Venerable,"-the disciples responded with utmost deference.

Fortunately, these academic underachievers had decent attitudes... No, scratch that, that wasn't good at all! If a student was simply an unmotivated slacker who refused to study, it wouldn't drive an educator to such depths of despair; it was precisely when a student possessed a wonderfully sincere attitude, worked hard, and *still* couldn't learn a thing that a teacher felt true, bottomless despair.

The group set off toward the Rare Beast Enclosure.

Mo Xi followed carefully behind Zhou Hui.

He was exceedingly curious as well.

The Rare Beast Enclosure was situated in the Heavenly Spirit Sect's back mountains, leaning toward the direction of Heaven-Cleaving Chasm. Though referred to as the 'back mountain,' it was barely a collection of gentle knolls compared to the towering Heaven-Cleaving Chasm, or even the main peaks of the Mount Tianling range ahead. Consequently, spiritual energy was walled off by the front peaks, pooling in the valleys before them. The back mountain had exceedingly sparse spiritual qi, while the noxious miasma blocked by Heaven-Cleaving Chasm settled downward, turning the area into a dead zone completely unsuited for cultivation.

Having grown accustomed to spiritual energy, cultivators who entered such an energy-starved environment felt as though a chunk of their six senses had been sealed off, inducing deep unease. Under ordinary circumstances, nobody was willing to venture near such a place.

In truth, it was equally unsuited for rearing spirit beasts.

Yet Yuwen Xuan had selected this exact location as his 'Rare Beast Enclosure,' even plopping his own immortal cave right here. Just like *'Why doesn't Eldest Senior Brother die when hung from a tree with a broken neck?'* and *'Is that guy even human?'*, this had long remained one of the great unsolved mysteries in Mo Xi's heart.

This bewilderment had reached its peak in recent days.

Ever since Mo Bei's 'A Grief of Tea' opened for business, it required colossal supplies of rare beast milk. Furthermore, because the cafeteria food was so abysmal, Eldest Senior Brother had provided beast milk to the entire sect to shut the disciples' mouths, creating staggering demand. Although Yuwen Xuan seemed to have established some pipeline with Thousand Beasts Hall, their influence within the sect was clearly limited, meaning any shortfall was supplied directly by the Spirit Beast Pens.

Yet the Spirit Beast Pens didn't even have a fixed headcount of handyman disciples! How on earth was it supplying all that?

Mo Xi had been dying of curiosity for a long time.

Then again, curious as he was, Mo Xi didn't harbor such a suicidal urge to investigate on his own.

Without Zhou Hui personally leading the way, Mo Xi, as the 'most favored youngest disciple,' would never have casually approached Yuwen Xuan's cave abode, let alone his Rare Beast Enclosure.

In fact, even Zhou Hui didn't casually visit the Rare Beast Enclosure.

The reason, naturally, differed from Mo Xi's.

Primarily, back when Zhou Hui forcibly confiscated the egg that Yuwen Xuan had been preparing to eat—the very egg that later hatched into Lu Shulong—Yuwen Xuan had thrown a weeping, hysterical tantrum, howling that Master didn't respect his 'privacy.'

In reality, Yuwen Xuan had no privacy to speak of. In his childhood, because he was far too unruly, Zhou Hui had worried he might run off and get lost, making him wear enchanted clothing and accessories equipped with twenty-four-hour location tracking and surveillance for all twelve two-hour periods of the day; Yuwen Xuan hadn't displayed the slightest dissatisfaction with that. Yet when Zhou Hui merely swapped out the bowl he ate from, Yuwen Xuan had thrown a fit for three straight days.

As for being forbidden from eating that dragon egg, it had provoked Yuwen Xuan to throw a tantrum for three solid months without stopping.

Zhou Hui had been given such a headache by the racket that he finally agreed never again to touch his food bowl, nor even look at what was inside. That preserved 'respect' had evolved over the centuries into what was now the Rare Beast Enclosure.

In other words: that was Yuwen Xuan's giant feeding trough.

Zhou Hui had largely adopted an 'out of sight, out of mind' mentality toward whatever lurked inside.

This time, however, he deduced that what he required was likely to be found inside Yuwen Xuan's food bowl(X)—the Rare Beast Enclosure—and thus made a deliberate trip.

Of course, regardless of what objections Yuwen Xuan might harbor, whenever Zhou Hui set his mind to doing something, he did it.

And so, the party stepped through the entrance of the Rare Beast Enclosure—which, in truth, was also the front door to Yuwen Xuan's immortal cave.

—"Huh? It's much cleaner(ordinary) than I imagined,"-Mo Xi muttered with a hint of disappointment.

Actually, calling it ordinary wasn't quite accurate either. The architecture of this cave abode was an unmistakable 1:1 replica of Zhou Hui's immortal cave, right down to the styling and arrangement of the furniture.

Yet Zhou Hui's abode was pristine and minimalist to the point of austerity; apart from slightly packed bookshelves, every corner radiated a detached aura free of mortal desires. Yuwen Xuan was the exact opposite.

Here, the floors, low tables, and stone walls were draped in all manner of soft furnishings: vibrant, multi-colored rugs, plush silk cushions, and gorgeous embroidered tablecloths, radiating an overwhelming aura of 'love for life'(X).

Mo Xi even found this place cozier than his own cave abode—which was stuffed with costly, ostentatious treasures—and it evoked a strange sense of familiarity.

*Wait, familiarity?*

Finding it comfortable was one thing, but why familiar? He had never actually stepped foot inside this place before!

[...It must be because the layout is identical to Master's cave abode that gave me this illusion!]

[Speaking of which, what kind of sickness does Eldest Senior Brother have?! Why did he choose the exact same floor plan and furniture as Master's?! Judging by these cushions and tablecloths, he clearly despises this aesthetic!]

Mo Xi's mind was bursting with complaints.

Zhou Hui swept an uninterested glance across the surroundings before asking the disciples:

—"Where are the animals kept?"

—"This way."

The disciples led the way down a side passageway.

Mo Xi's snark got stuck in his throat. His eyes widened:

—"The study?!"

If this truly was laid out identically to Zhou Hui's immortal cave, what lay beyond that corridor was unquestionably the study, right?

As it turned out, it (ostensibly) was indeed a study.

Furthermore, perhaps because it was packed with bookshelves brimming with scrolls and tomes, rendering the soft furnishings unable to drastically alter the vibe, this chamber resembled Zhou Hui's immortal cave far more than any other room.

Yet the instant he stepped past the passageway and laid eyes on the study—particularly the desk directly facing the doorway—Mo Xi suddenly saw "Master" sitting behind the desk with silver hair cascading down, his wrists bound in golden chains, glaring at him with an unprecedented expression of vicious spite and fury, snarling:

—"Mo Xi! Where is the ____ I ordered you to find?!"

Instantly, an uncontrollable eruption of murderous intent surged within Mo Xi's soul, an urge to slaughter...

—"Xi'er?"

At that moment, a warm palm gently stroked the crown of his head, snapping Mo Xi back to reality in an instant.

Casting a glance to his side with eyes that hadn't yet quelled their bloodlust, he beheld the genuine Master gazing gently upon him. The faint, placid smile at the corners of Zhou Hui's lips looked as though it wouldn't waver even if the heavens caved in, causing the turbulence in Mo Xi's heart to settle into instant calm.

He looked toward the desk once more: it was completely empty, with nothing there at all.

—"N-No... I seemed to have experienced some hallucinations just now..."-Mo Xi confessed hesitatingly.

He didn't know how to explain it. After all, that scene was far too bizarre. Mo Xi could swear on his life that he had never harbored the slightest shred of disrespect toward Master, let alone murderous intent!

—"Hallucinations?"-Zhou Hui harbored no suspicion, remarking placidly:—"It is likely the effect of hallucinogenic incense. Your cultivation base is relatively low, making you susceptible to its influence."

—"Hallucinogenic incense?"

It had been fine before Zhou Hui pointed it out; the moment he mentioned it, Mo Xi indeed detected a fragrance so overpoweringly pungent it bordered on foul, causing him to sneeze several times in rapid succession.

—"What kind of atrocious taste does Eldest Senior Brother have?!"-Mo Xi complained, rubbing his nose.

—"It is likely to mask the scent of the beasts he rears,"-Zhou Hui explained:—"Lest ferocious beasts catch one another's scent and trigger bloody clashes."

—"Ah? But where are those animals kept?"-Mo Xi asked in astonishment:—"Like the beast milk we've been drinking?"

No matter how one looked at it, a study hardly seemed like a place that could raise livestock.

—"It is [Kui Bull](# "Literal CN: 夔牛 (Kuí Niú) — mythical one-legged thunder-bovine from the Classic of Mountains and Seas whose roar sounds like thunder; source of sect beast milk") milk,"-one disciple volunteered:—"Junior Brother Mo, please look this way."

The disciple guided Mo Xi before a folding screen.

It was a plain white folding screen resembling the one in Zhou Hui's study. Yet when a gentle breeze wafted across, undulating rippling patterns characteristic of vast grasslands materialized upon its surface. Within those ripples, the shadowy silhouettes of several thousand cattle faintly emerged. Listening closely, one could even hear their bellowing roars echoing like rolling thunder.

—"They weren't originally kept here,"-the disciple explained:—"However, milk has been in constant demand lately, so Senior Brother Yuwen instructed us to relocate the herd to a more convenient spot."

The disciple asked:

—"Junior Brother Mo, would you like to go inside and take a look?"

—"You can even go inside?!"-Mo Xi gasped.

He immediately realized what a foolish question that was—if one couldn't enter, how could they harvest Kui Bull milk? They could hardly herd them out one by one into the hallway to milk them!

Mo Xi truly wanted to step inside for a peek, so he turned to look at Zhou Hui, hoping to secure Master's permission first.

Instead, he discovered Master standing beside the bookshelves behind the folding screen, slowly unrolling scrolls to inspect them as though searching for something.

*Wait, paintings on the bookshelf?*

Mo Xi glanced at the screen, then at the scrolls that clearly depicted various scenes, and a terrifying realization struck him:

—"Could it be that all of those are where Eldest Senior Brother rears beasts?!"

—"Correct. Everything on the shelves is Senior Brother Yuwen's livestock ranches,"-the guiding disciple said in awe.

Yet when their eyes fell upon the bookshelves, their complexions turned slightly pale. Clearly, these '[scroll-world ranches](# "Literal CN: 画卷养殖场 (Huàjuàn Yǎngzhíchǎng) — pocket dimensions painted onto scrolls serving as pocket ranches for spirit beasts")' had not left these disciples with particularly pleasant memories.

Mo Xi thought nothing of it.

Seeing Zhou Hui rummaging through that disorderly mountain of scrolls, Mo Xi abandoned any notion of touring the Kui Bulls, trotting up to his Master's side and asking sweetly:

—"Master, what are you looking for? Let me help you find it!"

Saying so, he picked up a scroll. Finding it blank white like the screen, with nothing discernable to the naked eye—and seemingly lacking the wind-activated mechanism of the Kui Bull grassland—Mo Xi lacked the patience to decipher it manually and swept his divine sense directly inside.

—"Wait—"

Spotting Mo Xi's move, Zhou Hui immediately snatched Mo Xi by the scruff of his neck, yanking his divine sense forcefully back out.

And thank heavens Zhou Hui acted with lightning speed.

For in that split second, Mo Xi caught the foul reek of a bloody gale; before he could even register what was happening, a colossal claw swept violently across his face.

By the time Zhou Hui dragged him back, three bloody gashes had already been carved across Mo Xi's cheek.

Mo Xi stood frozen in a daze, barely comprehending what had just transpired.

The nearby disciples, on the other hand, were thoroughly accustomed to such scenes, leaping into action with practiced routine:

[—"Medicine, quick, bring the medicine!"](# "Speaker: Disciple")

[—"I recall scratches from a [Zhu Yan](# "Literal CN: 朱厌 (Zhū Yàn) — ancient ferocious ape-beast whose appearance foretells warfare; notorious for lethal claws")... Right, the ointment is over here!"](# "Speaker: Disciple")

—"Junior Brother Mo, rub some on. Your face will heal in no time; it won't leave a scar,"-a disciple offered.

Zhou Hui showed no intention of personally treating Mo Xi's superficial wound, merely watching the disciples' practiced motions as an ominous suspicion took root:

—"Yuwen Xuan hasn't been having you feed ferocious beasts of that caliber as well, has he?"

—"Yes, Immortal Venerable,"-the disciples answered, hanging their heads.

[How on earth are you people still alive?!]

Zhou Hui almost blurted the question out loud. Yet a sudden thought struck him; dredging his memory, he recalled these disciples' scores on the [Fortuitous Encounter Questions](# "Literal CN: 机缘题 (Jīyuántí) — exam questions with nonsensical premises and dice-rolled answers designed purely to test a student's innate luck stat")—

The so-called Fortuitous Encounter Questions were problems where the premise was completely nonsensical, the answers were equally absurd, and no logical correlation existed between prompt and solution. Such questions could never be solved through rational contemplation; the 'correct answer' was literally determined on the spot by Zhou Hui rolling a set of dice.

That was correct: it was a question type designed purely to assess raw 'luck.'

To prevent disciples from cheating via sheer 'serendipity,' Zhou Hui had enchanted the multiple-choice sections of his exam papers such that blind guessing on questions a student didn't know would invariably land on the wrong choice; only the 'Fortuitous Encounter Questions' truly gauged a disciple's innate luck stat.

And the disciples before his eyes had all scored above eighty percent on the Fortuitous Encounter Questions!

Then again, it made sense: possessing zero discernible talent yet still managing to step onto the immortal path was an immense stroke of serendipity in itself.

—"...You have done very well. In the future, I will instruct Yuwen Xuan to overhaul the feeding protocols to ensure your safety,"-Zhou Hui encouraged the disciples gently.

[Wait, Lord Immortal Venerable!]

[In such a lethal working environment, aren't you going to rescue us from this place?!]

The disciples lamented with crestfallen faces.

Zhou Hui, however, merely wanted to capitalize on this squad of [European Kings](# "Literal CN: 欧皇 (Ōuhuáng) — gacha/gaming slang for players with god-tier luck and blessed RNG"):

—"Since you are so familiar with the collection, help me find where the 'Giant Turtle' is stored."

Yuwen Xuan's scrolls were shoved into shelves haphazardly without a single label; searching for anything specific was like fishing for a needle in the vast ocean.

—"Understood, Immortal Venerable,"-the disciples replied with weeping grimaces.

—"Search this section of the shelf,"-Zhou Hui said. Naturally, he wouldn't be as utterly irresponsible as Yuwen Xuan; he pulled out the scrolls devoid of malevolent, ferocious auras and placed them onto a separate shelf:—"These are safe. As for the hazardous ones... I shall have Yuwen Xuan sanitize them before dealing with them further!"

—"Yes! Immortal Venerable!"-the disciples breathed a collective sigh of relief.

Watching from the side, Mo Xi couldn't resist extending his little paw once more, grabbing a scroll and casting his divine sense inside:

[Master said this side is safe, so taking a peek shouldn't be a problem... Whoa!]

Mo Xi's feline pupils dilated in astonishment. He beheld an azure ocean, and isolated islands drifting upon the waves—no, those weren't islands, but colossal tortoises floating on the sea!

—"Master! I think I found the 'Giant Turtle'!"

Mo Xi promptly retracted his divine sense, calling out to Zhou Hui in delighted surprise.

Zhou Hui, however, was staring at another scroll in sheer shock:

—"How on earth did he get his hands on this?!"

—"What is it?"-Mo Xi stood on tiptoe, leaning in to look.

Looking at the scroll with the naked eye revealed nothing obvious; no concrete beast was depicted upon it, only an entire expanse of abstract texture that looked somewhat like plumage, yet also resembled fish scales.

Having suffered a painful lesson moments ago, Mo Xi naturally didn't dare recklessly project his divine sense inside again.

—"What is this, Master?"-Mo Xi asked, brimming with curiosity.

—"A magnificent treasure!"-Zhou Hui gazed upon the abstract scroll, his tone carrying a rare note of excitement:—"I originally intended merely to employ the Giant Turtle. But with this, gathering all living beings across the Five Domains and Six Abysses for a grand banquet wouldn't pose the slightest issue!"

—"That said, just how did Yuwen Xuan get hold of this?"

Zhou Hui couldn't help feeling a touch bewildered.

***

High upon the ancient peach tree, a twisted human figure hung suspended.

Not only was he contorted at impossible angles, but trails of fresh blood trickled down his cheeks. His eyes were tightly shut, his breathing faint and shallow, looking for all the world as though his final breath was about to give out—that is, until he suddenly sneezed:

—"Achoo!"

—"Did the wind pick up?"

Accompanying that sneeze, Yuwen Xuan blinked his eyes, glanced at the swaying branches nearby, and yawned. He was just about to close his eyes and drift back to sleep when he caught a scent in the wind that he had drooled over for centuries.

Yuwen Xuan snapped wide awake! Flapping like a landed carp against the trunk, sickening *crunch-creak* sounds echoed from within his frame as his fractured body somehow straightened out slightly.

Unfortunately, the butterfly bowknot Zhou Hui tied this time was far too tight; for the moment, Yuwen Xuan found it impossible to untie the knots and leap down.

Helpless, Yuwen Xuan could only crane his neck around with all his might, screaming into the depths of the sect:

—"Master! I was wrong! Don't touch my [emergency rations](# "Literal CN: 储备粮 (chǔbèi liáng) — emergency food reserves / stockpiled rations; Yuwen Xuan's secret beast collection saved to eat for the rest of his life")—!"

—"Those are the rations I was saving to eat for the second half of my life—!"

The wind grew ever fiercer, carrying his sorrowful wails far, far into the distance.

***

—"The wind has picked up,"-Zhang Hongjing murmured to himself, gazing out the window.

At this moment, their cloud carriages had roughly reached the perimeter of the Mount Tianling range. Yet a vast expanse of white fog obstructed their vision; even when Wind Spirit Root cultivators cast spells, they couldn't blow the mist away. Only just now had a gust wafted across from the direction of Mount Tianling, though it still failed to disperse the fog.

[Could the Immortal Venerable be displeased?]

Although Zhang Hongjing didn't believe the Untiring Immortal Venerable would be so petty, his guilty conscience was an undeniable fact.

On the night he was attacked, just as Mo Xi had promised, he had received the Heavenly Spirit Sect's reply granting him an audience the following day. To show proper courtesy, their party ought to have arrived at Mount Tianling by noon.

What he hadn't anticipated was that the merchants in his delegation became completely intoxicated with the secret realm—no, not just the merchants; it would be more accurate to say that the vast majority of the 'Persuasion Delegation' members hadn't slept a wink, [pulling an all-nighter](# "Literal CN: 待了通宵 / 修仙 — staying up all night; cultivators spending all night running trials in the secret realm") inside the secret realm! After all, tourist status carried no time limits. Some had even purchased long-term membership passes in an attempt to challenge the secret realm trials as ordinary cultivators. As a result, gathering everyone back together had cost Zhang Hongjing a considerable amount of time.

Even then, they had departed with reluctant longing, looking as though they wished they could camp in the secret realm for ten days to a half-month to conduct 'extended field research.'

However, with so many people undergoing drastic shifts in mindset, the remaining members were hardly fools.

Zhang Hongjing had convened this crowd under the pretext of 'persuading the Untiring Immortal Venerable,' yet it had turned into something resembling an investment symposium. Naturally, certain individuals began questioning his true motives.

Such as the eldest disciple of Blood Demon Hall standing before him.

—"Don't change the subject, Sect Leader Zhang!"-he demanded in a loud voice:—"You wouldn't have dragged us here under the guise of 'persuading' them just so you could ally with the Heavenly Spirit Sect yourself, would you?!"

—"How could that be, Fellow Daoist Bai?"-Zhang Hongjing replied. Although that was indeed his exact plan, he couldn't possibly say it out loud; otherwise, the Thousand Harmony Gang would erupt into internal strife before anything else.

To appease the 'internal moles,' Zhang Hongjing still had to feign a neutral and coerced stance.

He lamented aggrievedly:

—"You all know my stance! How could I possibly form an alliance with the Heavenly Spirit Sect?"

—"Oh? Yet I've heard you have stubbornly dragged your feet on severing diplomatic ties with the Heavenly Spirit Sect!"-that 'Fellow Daoist Bai' sneered. Without waiting for Zhang Hongjing to explain, he snorted coldly:—"Regardless, once we reach the Heavenly Spirit Sect, let our Blood Demon Hall negotiate with them first."

—"That would be..."

—"You're unwilling?!"-Fellow Daoist Bai barked loudly.

Zhang Hongjing was naturally unwilling. After all, Blood Demon Hall's stance was fundamentally aligned with the Palace of Carefree Wandering.

This wasn't to say that Blood Demon Hall was Carefree Palace's lackey—had they been, Zhang Hongjing wouldn't have deliberately invited them along. Rather, while the sect's name sounded terrifying, it was actually an orthodox sect primarily composed of body cultivators. However, to temper their physical vessels, body cultivators consumed astronomical amounts of resources—much like the animals in Thousand Beasts Hall(X)—and thus relied heavily on Carefree Palace's supplies. Furthermore, because their sect was situated in the remote southwest corner of the Central Domain, furthest from the Heavenly Spirit Sect, they harbored few qualms about offending the Untiring Immortal Venerable; they merely wished to reopen trade routes as soon as possible, replenish supplies, and sell off their annual hoard of 'local specialties' at the auction to secure spirit stones for the coming year.

Conversely, as long as they could replenish provisions and sell their goods, Blood Demon Hall didn't particularly care who their trading partner was.

That was why Zhang Hongjing had decided to bring them along.

Faced with their interrogation now, Zhang Hongjing didn't panic, smiling warmly:

—"Of course I am willing. It is merely that..."

Before he could finish his sentence, cries of horror erupted outside the cloud carriages:

[—"Wh-What is that?!"](# "Speaker: Cultivator")

Accompanying the shouts, the cloud carriage lurched violently, executing a sharp evasive turn as though dodging an obstacle.

The cabin immediately descended into chaos:

[—"What happened?!"](# "Speaker: Cultivator")

[—"Could we be under attack?!"](# "Speaker: Cultivator")

[—"Could it be that the Immortal Venerable refuses to let us approach Mount Tianling?!"](# "Speaker: Cultivator")

Some were genuinely panicked, while others deliberately fanned the flames of disorder. Amidst the chaos, Tao Zhu suddenly roared:

—"What are you screaming about?! It's merely a Kunpeng... A KUNPENG?!"

Even as he spoke, Tao Zhu shoved open the carriage door and peered outside.

The others crowded behind him.

Everyone looked up to see an immense mass of something sweeping overhead across their fleet. It was so colossal that it could easily envelop the entire convoy, blotting out the celestial canopy.

Yet this mammoth entity was remarkably light and airy; with merely a gentle breeze, it was swept into the higher stratosphere.

And once the object ascended to a sufficient altitude, the onlookers finally discerned clearly what it was:

It was merely a single feather!

—"It truly is a [Kunpeng's Feather](# "Literal CN: 鲲鹏之羽 (Kūnpéng zhī Yǔ) — a single feather of the mythical beast Kunpeng, large enough to envelop entire fleets and blot out the sky")!"-Tao Zhu exclaimed:—"Yet how could a Kunpeng be here?!"

As he spoke, he looked toward the direction from which the feather had drifted—the direction of Mount Tianling.

At that moment, driven by the gale that carried the Kunpeng's feather, the thick fog finally parted. Yet the cultivators across the convoy were once again seized by a disorienting sensation of having no idea where in the universe they were:

They saw vast earth stretching across the high heavens above, and vast earth resting beneath the thick cloud banks below. The [Golden Crow](# "Literal CN: 金乌 (Jīnwū) — mythological three-legged solar crow representing the sun") was sandwiched between the two landmasses like a mere chicken egg! Only the eternal, unchanging silhouette of Heaven-Cleaving Chasm on the distant horizon barely indicated which of the two worlds was real.

Yet the spectacle unfolding behind Heaven-Cleaving Chasm was far more surreal.

It was an immense fish—a [Kun](# "Literal CN: 鲲 (Kūn) — gargantuan primordial fish from Zhuangzi that transforms into the giant sky-covering bird Peng")—swaying its tail as it slowly submerged into the void beneath Heaven-Cleaving Chasm. It was so unfathomably gargantuan that despite descending for what felt like ages, more than half of its colossal body remained exposed against the sky, looking as though it couldn't possibly submerge completely even by nightfall.

Sitting inside the cloud carriages and gazing upon that giant fish was like tiny beings the size of water fleas sitting inside a fishbowl, peering up at a cat outside the glass.

Colossal, and beyond human comprehension.

[—"This is the Heavenly Spirit Sect...?"](# "Speaker: Cultivator")

[—"This is the power of the Untiring Immortal Venerable...?"](# "Speaker: Cultivator")

Someone asked in a numb murmur.

Even though wonders abounded throughout the cultivation world, reshaping a sect to such an extent through individual power alone was staggering to contemplate. Not to mention, the Heavenly Spirit Sect actually harbored a Kunpeng!

All at once, everyone understood where the Heavenly Spirit Sect drew the confidence to host the Central Domain Winter Solstice Auction.

With a Kunpeng at their disposal, moving the Palace of Carefree Wandering from the Eastern Domain here within a single day wouldn't pose the slightest difficulty!

—"By the way, Fellow Daoist Bai, where was I just now?"-Zhang Hongjing asked, turning around:—"You wanted to negotiate with the Immortal Venerable personally?"

Fellow Daoist Bai of Blood Demon Hall had long scurried back to his seat. And not just him—everyone else who had spent the entire journey aggressively cross-examining Zhang Hongjing had done the same.

As though everyone had suddenly remembered what courtesy and decorum meant, they sat upright and orderly in their places.

Utterly, delightfully docile.
