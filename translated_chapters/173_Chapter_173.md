# Chapter 173: A Hair's Breadth

Tao Zhu was knocked flat on his back with exasperation, yet a wave of relief washed over his heart.

If Untiring took this attitude, it indicated there was no danger—at least for Untiring, he possessed the absolute power to ensure everyone departed safely, perhaps even coming away with gains.

As for Untiring abruptly losing himself in research, that was the most normal thing in the world.

Or rather, this was the Untiring that Tao Zhu had always known.

Over the past several hundred or thousands of years, without requiring the slightest prompting of desire, Untiring could enter a state of cultivation and scholarly study anytime, anywhere. Sometimes it was upon discovering cultivation notes left behind by ancient predecessors; other times, it was merely walking along the road and suddenly comprehending a minor clause of a mental technique he had previously failed to grasp, whereupon he would sit down cross-legged and meditate right in the middle of the street.

In this regard, Tao Zhu got off lightly; the greatest direct witness (and victim) was almost certainly City Lord Jian Haoge of Sky City.

Back in the day, that arrogant young master Jian had initially wanted nothing more than to compete with Untiring to see who was superior, yet somehow it evolved into picking up the oblivious Untiring from market streets, demon caves in desolate mountains, underwater dragon palaces, ghost domains across the Great Wilderness... and similar locales, hauling him back home.

The most outrageous incident occurred when Jian Haoge dug Untiring out from the stomach of an ancient beast deep within the Abyssal Ocean. Tao Zhu had been present as well, and vividly remembered City Lord Jian, his face crimson with rage as he unleashed a torrent of furious curses, rolling up his sleeves and scrubbing Untiring with a stiff brush for three full days! Untiring, meanwhile, only realized three months after being scrubbed clean that he had 'somehow ended up in Sky City,' and even wanted to turn right back to locate the beast's carcass to verify his enlightenment—infuriating City Lord Jian half to death all over again.

Therefore, during the time Boss Tao had been staying at the Heavenly Spirit Sect recently, seeing Untiring lecture his disciple—probably the daughter of the Ran clan—saying, "One must not get too lost in reading; at least maintain an orderly appearance," Boss Tao had nearly lost his composure on the spot. He had desperately wanted to record that scene and deliver it to City Lord Jian; it would surely infuriate City Lord Jian to the point of storming over to the Heavenly Spirit Sect in person to challenge Untiring to a duel!

[Wait a moment! Untiring isn't planning to sit and meditate for months or years this time, is he?!]

Boss Tao shuddered in alarm.

Yet looking at Mo Bei and Mo Xi before Zhou Hui, he breathed a sigh of relief.

With his disciples present, it shouldn't come to that.

After all, if one spoke of Untiring transforming into his current worldly, socially engaged state, it had all started when he began raising cubs.

Although Tao Zhu had not squatted at the Heavenly Spirit Sect to witness firsthand how Untiring raised cubs, thinking about how the first cub he took in was a Taotie, one knew that was hardly a creature that could be free-ranged. The moment one looked away, danger would strike—danger would strike existences other than the Taotie! The bitter complaints of Master Peilan, that quack fox doctor, bore witness to this truth as well; during that period, Peilan had practically become Untiring's private physician, dispatched exclusively to rescue unlucky wretches from the Taotie's maw before they could be completely devoured.

Tao Zhu even suspected that Untiring's sudden inspiration over the past year or two to promote pedagogical methods and reshape the path to ascension was likely because he realized that so long as he continued babysitting this pack of little cubs, he would never have time to cultivate in peace—a textbook case of [adversity breeding innovation](# "Chinese Idiom: 穷则思变 (qióng zé sī biàn) — when conditions become intolerable, one must find new ways to adapt and reform").

Yet to drive a stubborn blockhead like Untiring to such lengths, just what monumental chaos had this pack of disciples wrought?!

Amidst all these wild, rambling thoughts, a sudden doubt struck Tao Zhu:

[—"Why haven't those two unlucky kids been disqualified yet?"](# "Speaker: Tao Zhu")

[—"Huh?"](# "Speaker: Shui Huang'er")

—"Untiring's two disciples!"-Tao Zhu pointed at Mo Bei and Mo Xi.-"Even I was disqualified, so why are they still in there?"

—"How could you possibly compare to Little Sister Mo Bei?"-Shui Huang'er said disdainfully.

[I, a Mahayana powerhouse, can't compare to two juniors? Were all those lightning tribulations blasted on me for nothing?!]

Tao Zhu glared, but looking at the bird perch she sat upon and recalling her identity, he swallowed his retort.

The demon race's values differed from humans to begin with, to say nothing of a Saintess consecrated to the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree. Though this phoenix maiden's personal cultivation was not high, so long as she remained within the Sacred Tree's Sun Wheel Palace, forget a single Mahayana cultivator, even if a host of Mahayana cultivators besieged the Sacred Tree, she possessed sufficient demonic power to protect it.

Saintesses like her were usually severely out of touch with the outside world; who knew how Untiring had swindled her.

—"If Untiring really takes away your secret treasure, you'll be weeping your eyes out,"-Tao Zhu muttered under his breath, sulking internally.

—"I actually hope the Lord Immortal Venerable claims the secret treasure sooner!"-Shui Huang'er replied.

Yet the gaze she directed toward Mo Bei and Mo Xi was actually somewhat grave.

No matter how partial Shui Huang'er was, no matter how much she believed Mo Bei's future was boundless, she could not genuinely believe the current Mo Bei could truly surpass the Mahayana-stage Tao Zhu; she too felt they ought to have "come out" long ago.

Besides, like Tao Zhu, calmly facing one's desire from the outset and getting disqualified was no bad thing; the spiritual energy here was so abundant that sitting down to focus on cultivation was of immense benefit.

Remaining trapped like this for so long was actually an ill omen. It indicated that Mo Bei and Mo Xi harbored even deeper obsessions toward the "secret treasure"—most likely something ominous.

Furthermore, empowered by the Sacred Tree, this "desire" was no ordinary "desire"; it could force out the subject's "truth," and even manifest that "truth" into reality!

What bewildered Shui Huang'er most was that a portion of this obsession had even tainted her own person, such that as the "examiner," she had been forced to pluck a feather to take her place, exiting early to guarantee her safety.

[Little Sister Mo Bei, please don't let me down!]

Though Shui Huang'er did not know what it meant for an [idol's house to collapse](# "Modern slang: 塌房 (tā fáng) — lit. 'house collapse'; the shattering of a fan's idealized image of their idol due to scandal or ugly reality"), she still prayed inwardly.

She looked toward Mo Bei's direction.

Reflected in the phoenix's eyes, the maiden was not sitting obediently in place like a doll as Tao Zhu saw; rather, she stood drenched in fresh blood in the very center of a mountain of corpses and a sea of blood.

Shui Huang'er froze:

[—"What is going on here?"](# "Speaker: Shui Huang'er")

[—"Little Sister Mo Bei's desire is..."](# "Speaker: Shui Huang'er")

[—"...slaughter?"](# "Speaker: Shui Huang'er")

* * *

[—"...Hah... hah..."](# "Speaker: Mo Bei")

Mo Bei panted heavily, trying to regulate her breathing.

The hand gripping her sword trembled uncontrollably.

All around her lay corpses strewn in chaotic disarray, ranging from demon beasts no different from ordinary wild animals to grotesque, terrifying demons, piled high into a literal hill.

Mo Bei had come under attack seventy-nine days ago.

To be precise, calculating by Mo Bei's perception, she had been trapped in this place for a full seventy-nine days.

Mo Bei still remembered that seventy-nine days ago, she had been following Master to inspect the demon race's secret treasure. Whether her mind had wandered on the road or she had suddenly become overly engrossed in circulating the spiritual energy within her body, by the time she snapped back to reality, she was already standing all alone on tree boughs that twisted like a labyrinth.

[Did I get lost?]

[Or is this a demon conspiracy?]

Mo Bei did not dwell on it—using one's head was usually Mo Xi's job.

Mo Bei merely stood in place, calling out hesitantly:

[—"Master? Mo Xi... hey, Mo Xi! Master?"](# "Speaker: Mo Bei")

She received no response. Rather, her shouts startled something; she heard rustling sounds emanating from the white tree shadows, followed by a surge of killing intent riding a sharp gust of wind that struck straight toward the back of her head.

Mo Bei did not even turn around.

Reflexively drawing the soft sword at her waist, she slashed right across.

Foul, reeking blood splattered across the ground.

Only then did Mo Bei see that the creature attacking her was a demon beast resembling an ape yet also like a rabbit.

Mo Bei had no idea what kind of demon beast this was, nor whether killing it would offend the demons, but none of that seemed to matter, because the moment she felled the first beast, an endless cascade of rustling sounds echoed from the tree shadows. Accompanied by this commotion, all manner of hideous demon monsters poked out their heads.

What followed was [unrestrained slaughter](# "Chinese Idiom: 大开杀戒 (dà kāi shā jiè) — to embark on a killing spree; to slaughter without restraint").

For these entire seventy-nine days, Mo Bei had been fighting without pause.

Fortunately, the opponents were not very strong, all within the range Mo Bei could handle—or rather, this was what even Mo Bei found bizarre. After all, if the demons truly wanted to deal with her, in a place like the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, finding a few Golden Core or Nascent Soul level monsters would easily kill her. Or were the demons toying with her for sport?

Yet Mo Bei was soon unable to use her empty brain to engage in the thinking she was so unsuited for.

After all, this was not the preliminary arena of the Immortal Arts Assembly; there was no Master to erase cultivation realm disparities for her, nor any space to rest. Because she had not yet formed a core, Mo Bei still needed occasional sustenance. Harshest of all, Mo Bei's opponents were continuously growing stronger: from being slain in a single strike, to taking a full day and night without dying, their cultivation gradually surpassed Mo Bei's own realm.

Under these conditions, Mo Bei had exhausted all physical stamina. Even her [fasting pills](# "Pinyin: Bìgǔ Dān (辟谷丹) — inedia pills consumed by low-realm cultivators to sustain physical bodily functions without eating food") had been completely finished eight days ago; now, she was essentially overdrawing her spiritual energy to barely hold on.

Yet in this near-desperate plight, Mo Bei's eyes grew brighter and brighter.

She could feel her sword technique becoming increasingly fluid, seeming to finally comprehend the profoundly esoteric realms Master spoke of during their daily lessons. She could also feel the spiritual energy in her body, having been depleted, replenishing and circulating in an endless, cyclical cycle, finally approaching the brink of breakthrough.

Just a tiny bit more—just a hairline!

Mo Bei tightened her grip on her sword, looking at the only surviving demon monster before her.

It was a giant lizard demon over two meters tall, covered in wounds as well. It glared at Mo Bei with icy vertical pupils, gathering power for its final strike.

However, through the preceding battle, Mo Bei had already realized that this lizard demon would only expose its fatal weakness at the exact moment of its attack. To kill it, there was only that split second.

At the same time, slaying it would grant her the opportunity for a breakthrough!

With trembling hands, Mo Bei gripped the hilt of her sword tightly.

She had no intention of retreating, nor did she stop to think that breaking through in such a perilous place was a terrible idea—such an unsafe environment would only cause her advancement to fail.

Conversely, if Mo Bei were capable of overthinking, she would have long run out of energy to battle until now.

[I can do it!] Mo Bei firmly believed.

[Ah, I've been waiting for this moment.]

[Sure enough, seeing that fellow Mo Xi breakthrough a step ahead made me wildly jealous deep down!]

Mo Bei suddenly had an epiphany.

It was at that exact instant that the lizard demon violently whipped its thick tail, opened a maw bristling with tiered, razor-sharp teeth, and lunged to bite Mo Bei.

Mo Bei's eyes burned with astonishing brilliance. Burning every shred of spiritual energy in her body, commanding limbs so fatigued they could barely stand, she prepared to unleash her final sword strike—

—"Watch out!"-in a [hair's-breadth crisis](# "Chinese Idiom: 千钧一发 (qiān jūn yī fà) — a thousand jun hanging by a single hair; an imminent, critical juncture"), a shout rang out from high above.

Accompanying this voice, a pillar of light plunged from the sky, piercing straight through the lizard demon Mo Bei was about to slay.

The lizard demon's blood splattered across Mo Bei's face.

Mo Bei froze on the spot, maintaining her sword-swinging posture as she stared blankly at this scene.

She saw the lizard demon she had wrestled with for an entire day punched through with a gaping hole; she saw the lizard demon she was about to slay crash down with a thunderous thud; she saw a white-robed young man standing atop the lizard demon, an unremarkable face bearing a pair of eyes brighter than the sun, flashing a sparkling, radiant smile at her:

[—"Are you alright, miss?"](# "Speaker: White-Robed Youth")

In that split second, Mo Bei heard the sound of her own heart skipping a beat.

In the very next instant, Mo Bei thrust her sharp sword straight into the young man's chest.

The young man wore a look of utter disbelief, parting his lips to say something, but Mo Bei's soft sword flicked upward, cleanly slicing him into two symmetrical halves.

Only after bisecting him did Mo Bei come to her senses:

[—"Ah, that seemed to be a human..."](# "Speaker: Mo Bei")

—"Of course he was a human—and your fated lover to boot!"-a snickering voice chimed in from behind Mo Bei.

Mo Bei nearly slashed back with her sword, but hearing that familiar tone, she finally breathed a sigh of relief.

Turning her head, Mo Bei saw a familiar face.

Her own face.

—"You actually killed him so decisively? Is that really wise? You've always wanted your fated lover, haven't you?!"-"Mo Bei" asked with an odd expression, sporting that face as he sized up the young man's corpse on the ground.

—"A fated lover who interrupts my breakthrough—who needs him?"-Mo Bei said coldly.

Thinking about how she had struggled desperately for so many days, standing a mere hairline away from breaking through, only for that hairline opportunity to be ruined by such an idiot, she was so furious her chest ached.

From this angle, that young man was indeed one of the rare individuals capable of stirring Mo Bei's emotions.

Mo Bei wished she could hack that idiot a few more times to mince him into meat paste.

[Still, someone would probably snitch to Master, wouldn't they?]

With her last shred of rationality, Mo Bei mused, before glaring toward the other "herself":

—"Speaking of which, Mo Xi, why are you disguising yourself as me again? It wasn't easy for you to finally differentiate into a male,"-she demanded.

—"Who differentiated into a male?! I was male to begin with, alright?!"-Mo Bei... no, Mo Xi jumped up in outrage.
