# Chapter 54: Don't Forget the Main Task (第54章 别忘了正事)

Qiao Linggen had procured this coral bead at the Rogue Cultivator Guild of Fengqing Town, a branch subordinate to the Wanhe Gang's Fengqing City division.

He was one of the anonymous, unregistered members of this guild under the Wanhe Gang.

Naturally, he was a rogue cultivator.

As a "half-demon" possessing a [seven percent demon bloodline](# "Literal CN: 百分之七妖族血统 — a precise bureaucratic/genetic fraction reflecting Wanhe Gang's modern demographic census"), his father genuinely wished that his child would never experience the ancestral throwback of the demon race, much less the agonizing torment of demon core backlash. For this reason, Father Qiao named him "Linggen"—Spiritual Root. According to a demographic survey conducted by the Wanhe Gang in [the fifth year of the Chi Dynasty of the Immortal Calendar](# "Literal CN: 仙历赤朝五年 — an administrative epoch dating system used in the cultivation world"), "Linggen" (Spiritual Root), "Xianyuan" (Immortal Fate), and "Chengxian" (Ascension to Immortality) ranked as the most popular names among mortals, expressing their rustic wishes and earnest yearning for a better life. The Heavens answered his father's prayer—and answered it with overwhelming generosity, bestowing upon Qiao Linggen all five spiritual roots!

These spiritual roots were simply too trash. Not a single cultivation sect was willing to take him in. Consequently, Qiao Linggen spent two years studying under a veteran rogue cultivator from the nearby Wanhe Gang Rogue Cultivator Guild, becoming a rogue cultivator himself. In doing so, he formally joined the Wanhe Gang, becoming one of its peripheral members.

The peripheral fringe of the already loose and decentralized Wanhe Gang was truly about as peripheral as one could possibly get.

Essentially, paying an annual fee of two spirit stones made one a member. There were no rigid gang obligations, and one could enjoy "services" such as posting commissions, selling gathered materials, and purchasing medicinal pills at Wanhe Gang guilds across the land. Thus, the vast majority of rogue cultivators registered their names, treating it almost as a kind of "Rogue Cultivator ID card."

Yet even such a bottom-tier rogue cultivator was enough to be revered as a [loose immortal](# "Literal CN: 散仙 (sǎnxiān) — in orthodox Daoist taxonomy, an unranked/earthly immortal who has attained transcendence without a formal celestial post; here used ironically by rustic mortals to describe low-tier rogue cultivators who live a century without sickness") in the eyes of rural countryside mortals.

—To ordinary mortals, a Qi Condensation third- or fourth-layer rogue cultivator could live a century free of disease or ache; what reason was there not to call him an "immortal"?! Besides, those supreme figures at the very pinnacle of the realm hadn't ascended either, yet weren't they still addressed as "Immortal Venerable" or "Immortal Monarch"?

Of course, for a loose immortal with such an abysmally low "immortality quotient" like Qiao Linggen, he was naturally incapable of taking on standard cultivator work such as foraging rare herbs, hunting for treasures, or slaying fiends. Instead, he simply served as the village chief of his ancestral village.

However, in recent years, this Chief Qiao had run into a massive crisis:

The Zheng Clan, a cultivation clan of Fengqing City, demanded that their village uproot their traditional crops and switch to planting [Solomon's Seal](# "Literal CN: 黄精 (huángjīng) — Polygonatum sibiricum, a classic Daoist tonic herb revered in xianxia for replenishing qi, aiding bigu (fasting), and serving as the primary ingredient for low-tier cultivation pills").

Naturally, Qiao Family Village was unwilling.

They were not tenant sharecroppers of the Zheng Clan, and the land belonged to their own ancestors—why should they obey the Zheng Clan?! Furthermore, Solomon's Seal couldn't fill their bellies. Although the Zheng Clan promised to purchase the harvest with grain, what if they went back on their word?—Don't say that "immortal clans would never break a promise"; the cultivation clans of Fengqing City had pulled such underhanded stunts far too many times!

Therefore, Qiao Family Village refused to comply with the Zheng Clan's demands.

But following their refusal, fewer and fewer itinerant merchants were willing to purchase Qiao Family Village's crops over the years, and the purchase prices were squeezed lower and lower, pushing the village to the brink of collapse. To make matters worse, the merchants who had still been willing to buy Qiao Family Village's produce in recent years primarily sold it through teleportation arrays to the Southern Domain; now that the Palace of Carefree Wandering had severed the trade route, their crops became completely unsellable.

Left with no other options, Qiao Linggen sought to compromise with the Zheng Clan. At this point, however, the Zheng Clan put on haughty airs, demanding that Qiao Family Village sell all their land to the Zheng Clan before they would even consider buying their harvest!

—Was that not equivalent to cutting off Qiao Family Village's very lifeline?!

Qiao Linggen was worried out of his mind.

He wanted to petition the Rogue Cultivator Guild to uphold justice, but given his status—peripheral among the peripheral—there was no chance the Wanhe Gang would stick its neck out for such a tiny, insignificant village.

It was precisely at this juncture that Qiao Linggen caught wind of the Winter Solstice Auction at the Rogue Cultivator Guild.

The Winter Solstice Auction was something Qiao Linggen knew about—a grand assembly of immortals held annually in Fengqing City. However, though it was right on their doorstep within walking distance for mortals, a "loose immortal" like Qiao Linggen naturally lacked the qualifications to enter. He could only listen to the senior predecessors in the guild brag and boast, counting that as having seen the world.

What filled Qiao Linggen with pride was that in earlier years, their village's specialty could be taken to the market to be sold—rounding up, it was practically as if their village had visited the immortals' bazaar as well.

However, with the decline in merchant traffic, that past "glory" had faded into history. To rogue cultivators like Qiao Linggen, the Winter Solstice Auction had become a distant memory.

This year was different. Word spread that the auction had moved to a new venue—a place called "Mount Tianling."

Qiao Linggen had never even heard of the place. But the organizers seemed to anticipate this, and unlike in previous years, proactively provided a means of transportation:

A coral bead.

Legend had it that as long as one held this bead, one could reach the immortals' bazaar.

After verifying it repeatedly and receiving assurances from the Rogue Cultivator Guild that it was no scam, Qiao Linggen gritted his teeth, fished out his village's final nest egg of a dozen spirit stones, and bought the coral bead—the other party seemed somewhat reluctant and had only parted with it out of old acquaintance.

Clutching the bead, he didn't dare linger. Terrified of being mugged, he sprinted straight back to Qiao Family Village.

—What he didn't know was that after his departure, dozens of rogue cultivators just like him hurriedly left the Rogue Cultivator Guild, causing the guild hall—normally buzzing with noise at any hour of the day—to suddenly become quieter than ever before.

Returning to the village, Qiao Linggen assembled all the villagers and displayed the bead.

Upon hearing the news, the villagers were both thrilled and anxious:

[—"That's an assembly of immortals! Would they even spare a glance at our goods?"](# "Speaker: Villager")

[—"The village chief might be fine, but we're just ordinary mortals! If we go to an immortals' assembly, won't we get driven out?"](# "Speaker: Villager")

[—"Didn't the peddlers who came to collect our harvest say our stuff was just hauled along as a side-cargo and barely fetched anything? What if we make this trip only to lose money?"](# "Speaker: Villager")

[—"How is this bead supposed to take us there?"](# "Speaker: Villager")

Listening to their barrage of doubts, Qiao Linggen felt fury rise in his chest. He swept his arm:

—"Stop being so wishy-washy!"-Qiao Linggen barked, glaring around at them.-"Do we have any other choice?! Are we really going to sell our land to the Zheng Clan?!"

—"At least selling it gets us a bit of coin. If we had obediently planted Solomon's Seal from the start, we might not have fallen to this state..."-someone in the crowd grumbled, whether short-sighted or having secretly pocketed kickbacks from the Zheng Clan.

However, Qiao Linggen held considerable authority in Qiao Family Village—if not for that, they couldn't have held out under the pressure of cultivation clans until today.

He glared with wide eyes, and the cynics instantly swallowed their tongues.

Following past custom, the villagers crated and bundled the crops they wanted to sell. Qiao Linggen selected several sharp, clever young lads to accompany him. Then, following the instructions from the Wanhe Gang, they planted the coral bead at the [Zi hour of the thirteenth day of the tenth month](# "Literal CN: 十月十三日子时 — the hour of the Rat (11:00 PM to 1:00 AM), midnight").

Just as promised, the coral bead grew the moment it met the wind. Within minutes, it flourished into a massive clump of yellow coral, magnificent and beautiful. But Qiao Linggen had no time to admire it. The moment the coral reached full maturity, a dizzying whirl flipped the world upside down for him and the young men he had chosen. In the next breath, just like the disciples of the Blood Demon Hall, they found themselves standing upon the seabed of the Abyss Sea.

Yet Qiao Linggen had no time to admire the underwater scenery of the Abyss Sea. Before he could even steady his footing, he watched giant bubbles congeal around them, lifting them and their cargo upward.

Just then, a colossal dark shadow swam past overhead, and the bubble settled smoothly onto the shadow's broad back.

Everything unfolded so rapidly that by the time the bubble around Qiao Linggen dissolved, he still hadn't processed what had happened.

Of course, even if he had processed it, he wouldn't have understood—or rather, the Wanhe Gang hadn't bothered to explain anything to mortals who didn't even qualify as cultivators in their eyes. When distributing the seeds, they hadn't explained a single thing; they had simply and brutally decreed a fixed time, specified that planting the coral tree would trigger direct teleportation, and omitted all operational steps.

The poor men from Qiao Family Village had been tossed around like ragdolls, left thoroughly dizzy and disoriented.

Standing atop that dark shadow for several minutes, they finally came to their senses and looked around.

The men discovered they were standing at what appeared to be an island ferry dock. Above the "island," fluorescent coral trees grew in abundance, intermingled with magnificent pavilions and graceful terraces, with faint celestial melodies drifting through the air.

For country villagers who had never set foot outside their hometown in their entire lives, this was already a spectacle of breathtaking splendor.

—"Uncle Gen, is this the immortals' bazaar?"-a village youth asked excitedly.

—"No, we're on the back of a Kunpeng,"-Qiao Linggen replied. Fortunately, before buying the coral bead, he had gathered some basic intel, preventing an utter embarrassment.-"To truly reach the immortals' bazaar, it'll take another half-day."

The youth nodded, half-comprehending.

Beside them, someone even more confused than Qiao Linggen's group overheard and asked curiously:

—"Fellow countryman, what is a Kunpeng?"-the man asked.

Only then did Qiao Linggen notice that at the dock, there were many others just like them. Among them, quite a few, like the villagers he had brought, possessed not a shred of spiritual energy—they were pure mortals.

[Wait, wasn't this supposed to be an immortals' bazaar?]

Qiao Linggen thought anxiously, only letting out a breath of relief when he caught sight of the pavilions nestled among the distant coral groves on the island.

—He figured nobody would construct such an elaborate spectacle just to swindle folks like them.

—"A Kunpeng is a gargantuan divine beast that is both a fish and a bird!"-Qiao Linggen explained, showing off the trivia he had just picked up at the Rogue Cultivator Guild.-"I heard the immortals' bazaar specifically arranged them to ferry guests. We are currently riding on this fish-bird's back."

—"Whoa! Then this fish-bird must be enormous!"-the other man gasped.

—"I heard that to this day, no one has ever measured the full size of a Kunpeng,"-Qiao Linggen said.

He had originally intended to boast a bit more with these folks, but noticed schools of fish swimming outside the perimeter. Remembering that they were deep in the ocean, though knowing immortal arts protected them from getting soaked, Qiao Linggen still reflexively glanced at his cargo with worry.

—"Let's head up there to chat!"-Qiao Linggen said to the others.-"Standing at the dock might block others coming aboard."

His suggestion met with general agreement.

Led by Qiao Linggen, the group stepped onto the "island."

However, looking at the exquisite pavilions nestled among the coral groves, the villagers were terrified of soiling them with their feet. Instead, they all sat down near the outer coral forest, finally feeling at ease.

After brief introductions, Qiao Linggen learned that the man's address of "fellow countryman" hadn't been mistaken—after all, since they had boarded at the same dock, this entire group hailed from the vicinity of Fengqing City.

The one who had called him "fellow countryman" actually came from the furthest reaches, no longer within the jurisdiction of Fengqing City, but near the edge of the God-Demon Ruins—a mortal village scraping by on its precarious geographic location.

—"We bought our coral bead from the Wanhe Gang,"-that fellow countryman said.-"In past years, our goods were also sold to peddlers from Fengqing City—I heard they belong to their cultivation clans? But not only did they drive prices down, they also invented all sorts of excuses to buy on credit. This year, we heard the auction moved to a new venue and ordinary folk could go too, so we pooled our means to get a bead and come see the bazaar ourselves."

—"Here, look, this is our village specialty. Are you folks interested?"-he asked, taking out his village's "specialty" to show everyone.

It was an ashen, drab gray bone that looked completely worthless.

—"Huh? That can be sold for money?"-the Qiao Family Village youth exclaimed upon seeing it.-"Uncle Laohei in our village had one! A few years back, when he fell into debt to the Zheng Clan, they seized his house and took that bone along with it, even sneering that it was worthless garbage!"

—"He was definitely cheated,"-the man said.-"This thing lies at the very bottom of the deepest lake behind our village. Every time we harvest one, it costs a life! It sells for thirty spirit stones!"

—"Those damn Zheng bastards..."-the youth gritted his teeth.

Qiao Linggen patted the youth's shoulder and let out a silent sigh.

Of course, such an "expensive" specialty was beyond the means of anyone present—and even if they could afford it, they wouldn't know what to do with it. Thus, the man sighed in disappointment.

Next, the others present exchanged notes on their respective goods, only to discover that the strange bone of unknown utility was actually the most valuable item among them; the rest were all worthless agricultural produce.

—"Will any immortal actually want our stuff?"-someone muttered under their breath.

With that question, nobody had the heart to marvel at the miraculous scenery anymore; the atmosphere grew heavy and solemn.

***

Fortunately, their dejection didn't last long.

The Kunpeng's speed was indeed terrifyingly fast—crossing an entire continent took only an instant. What actually ate up "half a day" was the time spent boarding passengers at various stops along the way.

Soon, the travelers noticed that the glowing jellyfish around them were growing denser, like streetlights illuminating the abyssal depths. And along with the gathering lights, a colossal coral reef—massive as a mountain, majestic as an imperial palace—loomed before their eyes.

[—"Even the Dragon King's Palace could hardly surpass this!"](# "Speaker: Qiao Linggen")

Qiao Linggen blurted out in rustic awe.

Many years later, he would still vividly recall this sight. Even when he had traveled across countless lands in the cultivation world and witnessed far more wondrous marvels, he would still maintain that the Coral Convention Center in the Abyss Sea below the Heavenly Spirit Sect was the most breathtaking vista he had ever beheld.

As they arrived at their destination, bubbles congealed once more at the dock to transport everyone toward the convention center.

Qiao Linggen and his companions chose a bubble and drifted toward the halls.

He looked back over his shoulder. Alas, the Kunpeng was so unimaginably massive that even to the very last moment, Qiao Linggen never managed to see what the beast looked like in its entirety.

However, this time Qiao Linggen finally noticed:

The "islands" and docks upon the Kunpeng's back were not limited to just theirs—there were hundreds, thousands of them, arranged in orderly tiers across the gargantuan shadow!

Gazing from afar, it was unmistakably a mobile metropolis traversing the ocean.

—"So this is a Kunpeng!"-Qiao Linggen marveled. He felt that just this single ride on a Kunpeng had already made the entire trip worthwhile.

Yet that thought was merely a passing sentiment; the heavy burdens of mortal livelihood still weighed upon his shoulders.

He pulled his gaze away from the Kunpeng and looked toward the Coral Convention Center ahead.

Nor could he fail to notice the other bubbles.

Because everyone was disembarking from the Kunpeng simultaneously, the bubbles grew densely packed. With Qiao Linggen's rock-bottom cultivation base, he could sense that the vast majority of these bubbles radiated terrifying spiritual pressure—it felt as though any random passenger inside could crush him like an ant with a casual flick.

If Qiao Linggen felt this way, the mortals around him felt it even more intensely.

Yet mortals had no concept of spiritual pressure.

They shivered uncontrollably, like [summer insects fluttering snow-frosted wings in a blizzard](# "Literal CN: 夏虫 (xiàchóng) — summer bugs; an allusion to the Zhuangzi idiom 'summer insects cannot speak of ice' (夏虫不可语冰), here describing beings facing an existential cold they cannot comprehend")—because they had no idea what "snow" was, they had no idea what was threatening their lives.

[It really is an 'immortals' bazaar' after all!]

Another layer of apprehension weighed upon Qiao Linggen's heart.

Fortunately, the masters of this establishment seemed to have anticipated this long in advance. The oppressive discomfort of the spiritual pressure lasted only for a fleeting instant; soon, the bubbles diverged into different routes into the convention center, without interfering with one another.

Qiao Linggen was quickly reunited with the companions from his dock, arriving at the same entrance.

Through this experience, Qiao Linggen came to understand:

The journey had likely segregated their "seats" and sectors according to cultivation realm.

Far from feeling insulted, Qiao Linggen only felt immense relief.

He couldn't even afford to offend high-tier Qi Condensation cultivators, let alone Foundation Establishment "immortals" who possessed genuine divine abilities. Being thrust into the same room with them was by no means a good thing.

Besides, their entrance was plenty bustling as well!

Although the Heavenly Spirit Sect separated cultivators by cultivation realm, they did not treat them with prejudice. Everyone wishing to enter the market first registered their identity at the entrance, including name, age, cultivation base, origin, and so forth.

Once registration was complete, those who came solely to shop could head straight into the bazaar!

Merchants, however, faced a bit more paperwork.

Fortunately, the procedures were crystal clear, easily understood even by country folk who had never entered a city:

—"If you are here to sell goods, you must first proceed to the Commodity Registration Office,"-the registrar directed. Qiao Linggen assumed they were officials, for they remarkably possessed a Qi Condensation seventh-layer cultivation base—thankfully, they didn't release spiritual pressure to intimidate anyone:—"Follow this path straight ahead and enter that door. Over there, your commodities will be registered and appraised. If needed, an authentication can be conducted. If there are no prohibited items, you will be issued a vendor permit."

—"D-Does it cost money?"-Qiao Linggen asked nervously.

—"That depends on the type and quantity of goods you intend to sell,"-the official replied with a patient smile.-"All transactions under three thousand spirit stones are entirely tax-free."

Hearing this, Qiao Linggen instantly let out a long breath of relief.

He never harbored any wild hopes of his goods reaching three thousand spirit stones—he had never even seen that many spirit stones in his life! If they could sell for five hundred spirit stones, it would be enough for the entire village to celebrate a lavish New Year.

[The Heavenly Spirit Sect are truly good people! They don't even collect taxes!]

Qiao Linggen thought with profound gratitude.

Of course, he didn't drop his guard entirely. He had heard itinerant peddlers say that at such grand assemblies, even if taxes weren't collected up front, organizers would strip several layers of skin off you under various pretexts.

Filled with a mixture of joy and trepidation, the group proceeded to the Commodity Registration Office.

Perhaps due to timing, Qiao Linggen and his party were among the earliest arrivals. The registration office was practically empty, with only managers stationed behind the counters. Remarkably, they were in the Foundation Establishment realm, looking exceedingly stern and formidable, making Qiao Linggen's knees tremble slightly.

—"Then I'll go first!"-at last, the bold fellow countryman spoke up.

Carrying his village's unassuming specialty, he walked up to the counter:

—"We want to register this!"-he declared.

—"...Blood Wolf Bone?"-One of the managers turned the bone over, shaved off a small sliver to inspect it closely, and only spoke after a long pause:—"Quite rare. Unfortunately, the quality isn't great—it's been soaked until it's brittle."

It sounded like the standard preamble to drive down the price.

[Is it really worthless after all?]

Cold sweat beaded on the countryman's forehead, yet he stubbornly insisted:

—"We won't sell for less than thirty spirit stones! Old Zhang's family lost half a leg harvesting this..."

—"Thirty spirit stones?!"-the manager exclaimed in disbelief, speaking in a 'you've got to be kidding me' tone:—"If you're willing to sell to me for thirty spirit stones, I'll take as many as you have—"

—"Hey!"-the neighboring manager shouted in warning.

The first manager raised his hands and sighed:

—"Fine, fine. This bone can fetch five hundred spirit stones. However, I advise you not to set up a stall. It's much more worthwhile to put it up for the small street auction—it should fetch an even higher price."

The moment the countryman heard "five hundred spirit stones," he looked as though he couldn't breathe. Hearing that it could fetch even more, his mind went completely blank, and he could only stammer:

—"A-Auction? This can go to the Winter Solstice Auction?!"

—"Well, the main auction hall is out of the question,"-the manager said.

He then patiently explained to the mortals, just like the registrars at the entrance.

As it turned out, the Winter Solstice Auction was held on the final day of the immortals' assembly, serving as the grand finale of the entire gathering. Prior to that, auction items naturally had to be registered in advance, and interested bidders and merchants like them would arrive early. The Heavenly Spirit Sect would select the most valuable items to feature in the main Winter Solstice Auction. However, there were many items like the countryman's Blood Wolf Bone—rare and possessing considerable value, yet not grand enough for the main stage. Therefore, small daily auctions would be held across all the market streets, ensuring these precious goods reached those who needed them most at fair market prices.

—"Precious goods..."

The countryman wore an expression that was half-crying, half-laughing. Finally, remembering something, a surge of righteous indignation washed over his face.

Qiao Linggen understood his feelings completely. Patting the shoulders of the equally dumbfounded youths beside him, he warned:

—"Once we get back, not a word of this to Old Hei."

—"I know,"-the youth replied in a heavy, muffled tone.

The Blood Wolf Bone that the Zheng Clan had swindled from Uncle Laohei would never be recovered.

After all, they had zero leverage to confront a cultivation clan.

After the countryman, Qiao Linggen had the youths carry their village's harvest forward. He didn't expect his goods to fetch a price anywhere near the bone, but earning a little blessing for the village should be possible, right?

Inspecting the cargo, the manager wore a peculiar expression:

—"Spirit Frosting?"-the manager asked in surprise.

—"T-This isn't ordinary sugar, it was grown in spirit fields,"-Qiao Linggen explained anxiously.-"Its spiritual energy can satisfy at least what a Qi Condensation fifth-layer cultivator needs—"

—"Even if you say that, very few people would want this,"-the manager shook his head.-"At most, it can sell for four spirit stones per jin."

[That's plenty!]

Joy broke across the faces of Qiao Linggen and his village youths.

Ordinary peddlers wouldn't even offer this price, and they preferred paying in mortal gold and silver.

Before the manager could finish, however, the adjacent manager tugged at his sleeve:

—"Hold on. Ordinary cultivators might not need it, but think about *A Cup of Sorrow*—"

—"Ah!"-the manager let out a sharp cry. For some reason, an urgent look flashed across his face. Turning to Qiao Linggen and the others, he said:—"Wait here a moment. This appraisal might not be accurate."

As he spoke, he swiftly pulled a sheet of paper from beneath the counter, folded an origami crane, and sent it fluttering erratically into the air.

With no other choice, Qiao Linggen and the others stood aside and waited.

Initially, they were fraught with worry that the manager might renege. But as they watched the transactions around them, an inexplicable sense of security settled over their hearts.

They noticed that the appraisals here were genuinely fair and impartial, devoid of selfish greed. Just as the registration officials had stated, commodities under three thousand spirit stones were completely tax-free, with no hidden fees or extortion. Only renting a shop stall required a modest rental fee—which was only natural. Furthermore, while there were numerous peculiar rules and regulations, adhering to them didn't seem difficult.

Qiao Linggen's initial anxiety gradually transformed into fascinated interest as he listened to appraisals and registrations around him.

However, he didn't have much time to listen. Barely fifteen minutes later, a commotion arose outside the registration office. An ethereal fairy of unprecedented beauty barged in, breathlessly asking:

—"Someone is selling sugar?!"

***

When Yu Feifei received the paper crane from the mortal market, Zhou Hui happened to be at the Market Management Department. Watching Yu Feifei dash off in a flurry, he couldn't help but ask Mo Bei in surprise:

—"Sugar? You're actually lacking sugar?"

—"Yes, Master,"-Mo Bei nodded earnestly.-"It was fine at first, but as sales volume exploded, who would have thought that compared to beast milk and tea leaves, white sugar became the hardest commodity to procure."

—"...Now that you mention it, that does make sense,"-Zhou Hui mused.

This was an issue he hadn't paid attention to previously, but upon closer thought, it wasn't surprising for the cultivation world.

After all, high-grade spiritual beasts could always provide spirit milk; and spirit tea was universally beloved by high-tier cultivators like Zhou Hui, offering countless varieties even up to the Tribulation Transcending realm. Only sugar—specifically sugar endowed with spiritual energy, not the common kind eaten by ordinary mortals—was extraordinarily scarce in the cultivation world.

In the final analysis, sugar was not a necessity.

Perhaps for mortals it was an indispensable comfort, but for cultivators who had achieved [bigu](# "Literal CN: 辟谷 (bìgǔ) — grain abstinence; the Daoist practice of fasting from mortal food and subsisting entirely on qi") and no longer ate regular food, why would they eat sugar? Even if there were oddities like Yuwen Xuan or snack addicts like Ran Deng, their cravings could be easily satisfied with a bit of honey—which could be top-grade spiritual bee honey, offering genuine nourishing spiritual energy.

However, for the sheer volume demanded by *A Cup of Sorrow*, spiritual honey was naturally far from enough. Even Eldest Senior Brother didn't keep that many bees! They had to use white sugar. Yet sourcing white sugar containing spiritual energy proved agonizingly difficult.

After all, white sugar... or rather, crops like sugar cane and sugar beets, were predominantly mortal flora. Like rice and wheat, they had to be cultivated in specialized spirit fields to produce spiritual energy. But since they weren't essential necessities, they were naturally shunned.

Yet *A Cup of Sorrow* was crafted specifically for cultivators. Spirit milk and spirit tea were already used; if the sugar alone were a mortal commodity, it would severely degrade the taste and mouthfeel. Thus, sugar endowed with spiritual energy became their greatest bottleneck.

—"Still, I assumed the Southern Domain produced sugar... Ah, is it because of the trade route?"-Zhou Hui asked. Only now did he feel the inconvenience the Palace of Carefree Wandering had caused him, slight though it was.

This assumption was also promptly refuted.

—"No, it has nothing to do with Carefree Palace. The Southern Domain barely produces sugar anymore either,"-Tao Zhu chimed in from the side. Although his primary sphere of activity was the Abyss Sea, as a merchant he kept a keen eye on developments across all regions:—"Nowadays, most spirit fields are actually used to grow medicinal herbs."

—"Medicinal herbs?"-Zhou Hui echoed.

—"Exactly. Things like Solomon's Seal and the like,"-Tao Zhu said, patting his round belly.-"Sugar can't be used to refine pills, so what's the point of hogging spirit fields? But medicinal herbs are different. Just think how many Qi Condensation cultivators there are, and how massive their demand is! This rakes in far more spirit stones than sugar."

—"What kind of logic is that? The Solomon's Seal Pills consumed by Qi Condensation cultivators only require thirty-year herbs, don't they? Spiritual plants at that level haven't even grown enough to give back to heaven and earth! Eating them is a sheer waste of natural spiritual energy! Besides, it harms their cultivation foundations..."-Zhou Hui said with displeasure.

—"Go tell that to those Qi Condensation cultivators! Tell them to delay their Foundation Establishment for the sake of heaven and earth's spiritual energy—do you think they'd agree?"-Tao Zhu asked.

Zhou Hui frowned, unable to refute the point.

—"In short, popping pills is the grand trend nowadays. Cultivating slowly step-by-step is an outdated mindset,"-Tao Zhu concluded.

—"Master..."-Mo Xi called softly, looking worriedly at Zhou Hui.

Seeing Master lost in contemplation, he feared Tao Zhu's blunt words might have upset him.

—"This is an excellent research topic,"-at that moment, Zhou Hui suddenly nodded:—"Conveniently enough, let this serve as the topic for your midterm examination: [*On the Impact of Supply and Demand Relationships on Crop Cultivation in the Cultivation World*](# "Literal CN: 《论供需关系对修仙界农作物种植的影响》 — Zhou Hui weaponizing modern neoclassical economics and Marxist agricultural critique as a 30,000-word midterm paper for sword cultivators and Daoists")—"

Hearing this declaration, everyone present felt as though struck by lightning.

—"M-Midterm examination?!"-Mo Xi stammered, his eyes bulging:—"B-But Master, we're supposed to be hosting the Central Domain's largest auction—"

[How can you derail from the main plot to do something else?!]

—"And what does that have to do with you disciples of the Heavenly Spirit Sect?"-Zhou Hui replied as if it were the most natural thing in the world:—"The investor is Boss Tao; the ones handling the guests are the Wanhe Gang. You are merely lending a hand for your [social practicum](# "Literal CN: 社会实践 (shèhuì shíjiàn) — the modern Chinese university/high-school requirement where students must do field work or internships and write exhaustive reports"). Since it is a social practicum, how could you not submit a report? Bundling it into your midterm examination is already showing leniency."

—"In short, by mid-November, every single one of you will submit a thirty-thousand-word research report,"-Zhou Hui instructed, before murmuring under his breath:—"Come to think of it, it's about time I prepared the exam papers for the Beginner Class as well."

[You're actually a Demon Venerable, aren't you?!]
