# Chapter 234: The Script Deliberation

Neither Ran Deng nor Mo Xi noticed Qu Qianxing's profound bewilderment.

Ran Deng simply hadn't dared inspect Qu Qianxing's face too closely, whereas Mo Xi, who was usually a virtuoso at reading the room and sizing up facial nuances, failed to notice purely because he was entirely too busy.

Yes, Mo Xi was swamped.

He was arguably the single busiest person alive right at this exact moment in time.

Mo Xi couldn't even fathom how he had degenerated into such a pathetic state.

As far as Mo Xi recalled, he was supposed to be the most relaxed slacker in the entire Heavenly Spirit Sect. After all, Master had stationed him at the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree solely to maintain the "Study-Encouraging System." His sole point of contact with their sect's Immortal Arts Assembly was just running the "livestream broadcast." Furthermore, this system had been audited and battle-tested by technical titans like Master and Fourth Senior Brother in succession. Barring unforeseen accidents, he should have had virtually nothing to do!

Perhaps out of that exact consideration, Master had entrusted him with the task of authoring the God-Demon War narrative script inside the trial system.

Yet what Master (presumably) didn't know was that prior to this, Mo Xi had already taken on a moonlighting gig on the side: using the Study-Encouraging System to recreate in the Immortal Arts Assembly trials the prophetic forms of his senior brothers and sisters falling to the demonic path. Trying to graft a grand narrative storyline on top of that pre-existing mess was brutally, agonizingly hard!

To draw an analogy: if the current Immortal Arts Assembly trial system were a video game, Mo Xi, due to a momentary lapse in judgment, had found himself concurrently acting as narrative designer, systems and balance designer, level designer, and lead programmer all rolled into one. Then, through his own self-inflicted meddling, he had injected redundant, hacky operations into the low-level source code early on, making post-launch maintenance a living nightmare.

Worst of all, this wasn't a single-player game—it was an MMO! Consequently, it suffered from every single toxic pathology endemic to online multiplayer games.

For instance: chaotic players running amok at all hours pulling off deranged shenanigans; for instance: endless bugs cropping up anywhere and everywhere like a hydra; for instance: deeply alarming "server" stability; for instance: a sudden influx of colossal data volume actively overwriting the backend stats of every player on the shard, driving the mathematical balance to the brink of collapse; and now, to top it all off, there was even a full-blown "hacker intrusion" happening in real time...!

No, it was far more severe than that. To put it more accurately: Master had acquired some unidentified server from who-knew-where, and it was currently executing a baffling, spontaneous [server merge](# "Literal CN: 合服 (hé fú) — combining multiple game servers into one") with an entirely different game server!

Naturally, Mo Xi possessed no formal terminology like "MMO" or "servers." But confronted by this cascading mountain of chaotic crises, he felt as though he was on the verge of dropping dead from sheer overwork.

—"Why is it always me?!"-Mo Xi groaned, clutching his temples.

The Immortal Arts Assembly had practically nothing to do with him in the first place, yet here he was, shouldering the weight of the entire world! Just like 'last time'—Eldest Senior Brother and Second Senior Brother were clearly the ones stirring up trouble, so why was he the only one running around ragged...? Wait a second. *Last time?*

Mo Xi massaged his aching forehead in disoriented confusion, feeling as though his brain had been pureed into mush.

—"Wherefore dost thou fret?"-Ruwang's projection inquired.

Ever since he manifested, Ruwang had remained hovering near Mo Xi. His primary body, however, was perpetually seated in cross-legged meditation with closed eyes; only the theatrical mask atop his head spun round and round like a second face, restlessly surveying their surroundings.

Seeing Mo Xi's expression of someone ready to drop dead on the spot, the mask drew out its voice in an operatic lilt:

—"[With sufficient brute force, one can shatter myriad arts](# "Literal CN: 一力破万法 (yī lì pò wàn fǎ) — classical cultivation adage stating that absolute, overwhelming physical strength overcomes all intricate techniques and spells"). [Beat the living shit out of them and be done with it](# "Literal CN: 干他娘的就完了 (gàn tā niáng de jiù wán le) — crude colloquial vulgarity contrasting sharply with Ruwang's formal operatic register")."

[What kind of ghostly nonsense is that?!]

For that matter, were the words uttered by this theatrical mask actually Ruwang's intent, or did the mask have ideas of its own?

Mo Xi stared dazedly at Ruwang's face—which was an exact, identical replica of Master's—for a few moments, and decided that the mask definitely had a mind of its own. In any case, Master's mouth would never utter such coarse language.

Even if it wasn't Master himself, but merely an impostor wearing the exact same face, it felt thoroughly wrong.

Even so, this "impostor" was an absolute eyesore.

Mo Xi fumed in silent resentment, precisely like a game developer who had pulled ten consecutive days of grueling overtime, forced to face an investor who might pitch preposterous, game-breaking requirements at any second—his stomach churning with bitter grievances.

Naturally, his resentment was not without cause.

Mo Xi had originally assumed that after His Majesty Ghost Emperor Ruwang arrived to relay Master's instructions, he would promptly take his leave. Who could have guessed that the fellow would first audit the narrative script he authored, then supply the spiritual energy required to run said script, and finally micromanage and supervise how Mo Xi allocated every scrap of spiritual power—refusing to leave entirely! It forced Mo Xi to sneak around like a thief whenever he wanted to do anything. Was this not the exact spitting image of a meddling investor who brought along their own in-house accountant?!

—Even if Mo Xi didn't know the corporate term 'investor'—why on earth did Master station such an overwhelming titan here? Even before Master himself, Mo Xi wouldn't feel this suffocatingly constrained!

Yet shrewd as Mo Xi was, he would never let a single trace of such thoughts leak onto his face. Instead, he adopted an expression even more deferential and docile than the one he wore before Master, piping up obediently:

—"Shattering myriad arts with sheer brute force is a feat reserved solely for supreme powerhouses of Your Majesty's caliber. This junior has only studied for a short time and has yet to master such peerless might. However, I have at least ascertained the origins of the intruder."

[—"Oh?"](# "Speaker: Ruwang")

—"It is... the Heavenly Court,"-Mo Xi said.

Upon hearing this, Ruwang finally ceased speaking through the mask. Opening a single eye, he cast a glance toward Mo Xi. It was a blood-red eye—and though set into a face identical to Master's, matching in size and contour, it belonged unmistakably to an entirely different entity. Merely being grazed by that gaze sent an icy chill straight through Mo Xi's hands and feet.

Yet Mo Xi braced himself and met that gaze, pressing on:

—"I just received intelligence that the spatial coordinates of a certain frontline in the God-Demon War have shifted. Calculating the trajectory of that spatial shift, it aligns perfectly with our sector. To pull off something on this scale exceeds the capabilities of even a True Immortal; only the deities seated at the very pinnacle of the Heavenly Court could manage it."

—"Dost thou fear?"-Ruwang asked.

—"No, I simply don't know what to do..."-Mo Xi replied, his eyes darting.

Inwardly, he breathed a phantom sigh of relief.

Hearing that not even the mention of the Heavenly Court caused him to flinch, it seemed this Lord Ghost Emperor was truly on Master's side. Indeed, Mo Xi had never been entirely at ease around Ruwang. After all, his past-life memories had never returned, nor did he intend to let them return.

Little did Mo Xi know, his petty inner calculations could never slip past the Ghost Emperor.

Ruwang's greatest specialty was precisely mental and spirit-control arts.

Fortunately, Ruwang couldn't care less about Mo Xi's little schemes. After all, having been father and son three reincarnations ago, Ruwang felt that even without employing spells, he knew exactly what this brat was thinking.

—"Is this not precisely the hour to wield power?"-Ruwang still did not speak directly with his mouth, merely fixing his blood-red eye on Mo Xi. Yet the mask covering the other half of his face urged in an immensely cheerful cadence:—"So long as thy script is coherent and possesses sufficient power to defend against foreign assault, thou canst halt the convergence of the two spaces."

—"Power... you say?"-Mo Xi inquired, studying Ruwang's expression.—"Is this power identical to the energy currently being disseminated to everyone through the Mountain-Protecting Grand Array?"

Naturally, Mo Xi had felt the influx of power surging through the Mountain-Protecting Grand Array as well.

While the speed at which he felt the power wasn't quite as acute as Qu Qianxing and Ran Deng, who were directly interfaced with the grand array, he possessed an additional "data source": prior to this surge, in order to realize the God-Demon War "script" inside the trial system, Mo Xi had already come into contact with the power of the Genesis Machine. Thus, he immediately sensed the intrinsic correlation between the two.

The reason Mo Xi hadn't immediately reported this to Qu Qianxing and Ran Deng was because Ruwang was squatting right here.

He wasn't sure whether this eminent being would permit him to inform his senior brother and sister.

(Though there was also a component of Mo Xi's petty vanity: *Master allowed me to tap into this power first! As expected, I truly am his most doted-on disciple.*)

Mo Xi harbored an inexplicable dread toward Ruwang. Not daring to ask bluntly, he could only probe in roundabout circles.

Ruwang seemed oblivious to Mo Xi's anxiety; his theatrical mask, however, was unfailingly prompt to answer:

—"That power... one could say it originates from the same source,"-Ruwang's mask explained succinctly.—"Except the power thou hast now received belongs to a variant refined and processed by thy master—far more harmless and infinitely easier to control."

Although he had not accompanied Zhou Hui into the depths, thanks to their ancient master-servant contract, so long as Zhou Hui consented or fell unconscious, Ruwang could perceive the other's status in real time and even synchronize with Zhou Hui's requirements. This was one of the primary reasons Zhou Hui had left Ruwang stationed beside the Genesis Machine.

Having nearly become a god, Zhou Hui had resolved to parcel out his divine power, which naturally meant he wouldn't do so without leaving instructions. It was just that in his present condition, he could not even move to rescue Ran Zhen, let alone personally brief his disciples on his status. Thus, he had delegated this task to Ruwang—who was already in communication with Mo Xi—alongside a mountain of directives.

Regrettably, Ruwang was never cut out to be a good teacher.

There was a reason Ruwang had always regarded Guan Ye as his proudest pupil.

Because only someone of Guan Ye's temperament would obsessively drill concepts he couldn't comprehend over and over again, even conducting experiments upon his own mortal flesh until he mastered them to perfection.

And because he was so pathologically earnest, a "mere" seven or eight cycles of reincarnation and seven or eight bouts of amnesia were insufficient to make Guan Ye truly forget the knowledge he had acquired. Give him half a chance, and nine times out of ten he would rapidly re-master everything from scratch.

It was true of ghost-taming arts; it was equally true of artifact refinement.

By contrast, Mo Xi was the textbook definition of a negative example.

In terms of raw innate talent alone, even disregarding his Extreme Yin Constitution, Mo Xi was undeniably several times more gifted than Guan Ye. In particular, due to his yin-spirit hybrid heritage (though originally nobody knew of it), his affinity with Ruwang's techniques was a match made in heaven. Yet Mo Xi had never possessed the slightest interest in learning knowledge for knowledge's sake. If Master taught something, he would half-ass his way through just enough to scrape a passing grade. Consequently, across multiple reincarnations, neither the Heavenly Spirit Sect's orthodox arts, nor ghost-taming, nor even demonic degeneration had left the slightest lasting imprint upon him.

Fortunately, thanks to this very apathy, succumbing to demonic corruption hadn't left any lingering psychological corruption on Mo Xi either.

One hardly knew whether to classify this as a "virtue" or a flaw.

Ah, the train of thought had derailed.

In any event, as Mo Xi's "foster father" during the third cycle, Ruwang possessed an intimate understanding of the yin-spirit twins' temperaments. He believed that explaining too much to Mo Xi was a complete waste of breath. Frankly speaking, Ruwang felt that even Former Master Untiring explaining a massive thesis on divine power permissions and operational states when giving him orders was also a waste of time. Foundational "theoretical mechanics" simply slid right off the smooth brains of most mortals without leaving a wrinkle—just like Mo Xi.

An impatient teacher meeting an impatient student: this pair of (former) foster father and son was undeniably the worst pedagogical combination imaginable.

Yet a Ruwang of this sort excelled at seizing human psychological vulnerabilities. He directly had his mask pitch a proposal tailored to Mo Xi's tastes:

—"However, this power presents an opportune windfall for thee. So long as thou draftest a script of appropriate content and mobilizest the power to its maximum extent, thou canst ensure others receive a smaller share while thou harvestest more for thyself, simultaneously thwarting the Heavenly Court's schemes. Is that not killing two birds with one stone?"

—"...Is that Master's recommendation?"-Mo Xi asked suspiciously.

—"Master Untiring cares not how much power any individual absorbs,"-Ruwang spoke in his own voice this time.—"If anything, as his disciple, the more power thou harvestest, the more it proves that he is the true mandate of Heaven."

[That sounds like pure sophistry, doesn't it?!]

[Every single sentence sounds related to Master, yet not a single word addresses what Master actually intended!]

Mo Xi thought with a bizarre squint.

In truth, Ruwang's "temptation" of Mo Xi was indeed driven by personal bias.

Much like Peilan, he felt it was an absolute tragedy that Untiring had abandoned the opportunity of "apotheosis" so casually. However, Ruwang would never question any of Untiring's decisions; whatever Master Untiring wished to do naturally carried his profound reasoning. It was just that gifting away all this power to random strangers was simply too wasteful—even if the recipients included Ruwang himself. To prevent such waste, bestowing it upon Untiring's personal disciples was the optimal choice.

Ruwang found it a slight pity that Guan Ye was not present. Then again, even if Guan Ye were here, whatever Ruwang said would fall on deaf ears; that stubbornly earnest child would only complete his assigned homework to the letter, never taking extraneous actions just because someone egged him on. Mo Xi, on the other hand, was someone whose nature could never refuse such power.

And as luck would have it, the mission Untiring had assigned Mo Xi previously carried this exact authority.

Ruwang figured that Master Untiring wouldn't mind his youngest disciple hoarding the lion's share of divine power. If Mo Xi was clever enough, he could even ascend to True Immortality in a single step at the cost of merely a "tiny price."

Right now, Mo Xi had clearly picked up on his underlying subtext. Ruwang did not believe Mo Xi could possibly decline.

Indeed, Mo Xi sank into deep contemplation.

Only after quite a while did he ask:

—"Must we stop it?"

[—"Eh?"](# "Speaker: Ruwang")

—"I mean, must we really halt the convergence of the two spaces?"-Mo Xi asked, breaking into a seemingly calm analysis:—"Although we suspect the Heavenly Court is behind it, we have no idea who the specific adversary is, what kind of 'person' they are, or how much power they can wield. Under these circumstances, if I draft a script to block the collision of the two spaces, no matter how I frame it, it boils down to clashing brute force against brute force. We don't know the opponent's trump cards, nor how much energy we'll waste..."

—"Dost thou fear defeat?"-Ruwang countered. Assuming Mo Xi was frightened, he offered what he believed was a calming pill:—"Rest easy. No matter who the mastermind orchestrating this may be, even if it were the Celestial Emperor in person, they cannot prevail against this."

Saying this, Ruwang opened both blood-red eyes.

Yet he looked not at Mo Xi, but toward that "Genesis Machine" where his true body was stationed.

Ever since a short while ago—perhaps precisely when Untiring achieved 'apotheosis'—this machine had been popping open myriad holographic projection screens and emitting bizarre hums. Ruwang figured that had Master Untiring been here, he would have been overjoyed to the point of oblivion, diving into closed-door research for several years or several centuries on end. Regrettably, the one stationed here was Ruwang. He had zero interest in researching this contraption, nor would he ever bother. According to the Lord Ghost Emperor's "philosophy of life": mortals are born solely to march toward death. Since all are doomed to perish anyway, how one is born matters not a whit.

Possessing such a "ghostly philosophy," Ruwang was contradictory solely in matters concerning Untiring.

His singular wish was that Untiring might exist forever—or at least endure until Ruwang himself perished. Otherwise, this world... no, the entire cosmos governed by the Heavenly Court was truly beyond redemption; it might as well burn to ashes! This affection even extended, by association, to Untiring's disciples.

Thus, despite harboring minor grievances regarding Mo Xi, Ruwang genuinely wished for him to intercept the divine power Untiring was giving away.

To this end, he did not hesitate to reveal the existence of the Genesis Machine:

—"For the adversary thou facest now confronts not merely the primordial genesis power that fashioned all worlds, but a power that is moreover held in the grasp of thy master."

—"Genesis power? Master?!"-Mo Xi cried out in shock.

[How long has Master been gone?! Why does it feel like he's pulled off another world-shattering catastrophe?!]

At this moment, Mo Xi had yet to realize that he was currently brushing shoulders with the miraculous reality of: *Master and Senior Brothers worked so hard that he accidentally became a god along the way.*

Mo Xi himself even brushed aside such a prospective future:

—"If we possess such power, then relying on crude brute force is an even greater waste!"

[—"A waste?"](# "Speaker: Ruwang")

—"Exactly! I thought through it carefully: because we don't even know who the enemy is, countless narrative details cannot be scripted,"-Mo Xi explained.—"Take our current predicament. At best, I could only scribble hastily: 'Harnessing the genesis power bestowed by Master, Mo Xi locked onto the nexus between both spaces. Meeting force with force, he wielded that invincible primordial might to steer both realms back onto their proper tracks, causing the mastermind to suffer lethal karmic backlash and perish body and soul.' But in reality, setting aside whatever price I might pay to invoke such power, simply not knowing the opponent's true capabilities means we could run into: 'Regrettably, the adversary excelled in Secret Art X, resurrecting shortly thereafter and resolving to exact bloody vengeance upon Mo Xi,' or 'What Mo Xi did not know was that the adversary had prepared Contingency Y, reviving in short order, whereupon...'."

Mo Xi's grievance came from hard-won, empirical experience.

Through the trial system of the Immortal Arts Assembly, he had thoroughly learned just how terrifyingly potent human (player) subjective agency truly was. Expecting them to behave as obedient actors and perform tamely according to the script was an utter impossibility.

Thus, believing that one could unilaterally dictate another's destiny purely through raw force was the height of arrogant delusion.

This was the conclusion reached by Mo Xi, whose future had been puppeteered by others across multiple cycles of reincarnation.

His Majesty the Ghost Emperor, having spectated all those cycles from afar, neither agreed nor disagreed. However, his theatrical face-paint mask shifted into a weeping visage:

—"That is merely because thou grossly underestimatest the authority of this power. Taking thy hypothetical plot just now, for instance: thou needst only append the clause, 'The mastermind died thoroughly and completely, incapable of resurrection, without so much as a soul fragment remaining,' and the deed is accomplished."

Having reached this point, as though worried Mo Xi still didn't comprehend, Ruwang's mask added:

—"Thou couldst even pen directly: 'Because the gravitational shearing force was too immense, the Heavenly Court was torn asunder, and the Celestial Emperor perished as a consequence.' That, too, is entirely within thy power."

What Ruwang left unsaid was that obliterating the Heavenly Court naturally carried a "price." Wielding such cataclysmic authority to annihilate the reigning cosmic regime would inevitably drive Mo Xi insane, degenerating him into the most terrifying fiendgod imaginable—an implicit "equivalent exchange" encoded within the Genesis Machine.

Fortunately, Mo Xi lacked the suicidal gall to do any such thing.

—"How is that any different from writing: 'The Heavenly Court and all our troubles were eaten by Eldest Senior Brother!'?!"-Mo Xi roasted him furiously.—"Only a ninth-rate hack writer who's so blocked they can't squeeze out a single word in days would ever resolve a plot like that! That would never pass a pitch review in game design! And besides! What good does destroying the Heavenly Court do me?! It's not like I can immediately assemble a new Heavenly Court to replace it! Who's going to clean up the collateral fallout and bureaucratic aftermath?! Master?! ...Actually, it feels like Master genuinely might go and clean it up..."

—"Which is why I did not truly command thee to pen the script thus!"-Ruwang's mask retorted.—"I merely inform thee that slaying one or two 'masterminds'—even if it be the Celestial Emperor himself—requires no fear of consequences."

Yet Mo Xi stubbornly shook his head:

—"Come off it! 'Resurrection' was merely a metaphor! When someone dies, it inevitably breeds new hatred, sparking desires for revenge and dragging in a chain of collateral complications! Besides, if the mastermind orchestrating the convergence of two spaces and quietly plotting to eradicate our world dies silently in some forgotten corner, does that truly benefit me at all? Can that [uproot the weed by its roots](# "Literal CN: 斩草除根 (zhǎncǎo chúgēn) — eliminate an evil completely to leave no future trouble")?!"

His final sentence was dripping with venomous spite, a visceral embodiment of "hatred." Even if his past memories were gone, certain searing emotions remained etched into the marrow of his bones.

Ruwang finally realized that Mo Xi was not cowering in fear, but had an entirely different scheme in mind.

—"What wouldst thou do?"-Ruwang asked.

—"[Beat them at their own game](# "Literal CN: 将计就计 (jiàngjì-jiùjì) — counter an opponent's scheme by turning their own stratagem against them"),"-Mo Xi declared.

He paused, gathering his thoughts, before elaborating:

—"Crudely blocking the spatial merger and assassinating the mastermind is an egregious waste of power. Moreover, since we can't immediately overthrow the Heavenly Court anyway, the return on investment is abysmal. Why not let a portion of events play out according to their script and allow the spaces to merge? That way, they'll keep authoring their narrative, delivering even more script pages and incriminating leverage right into our hands while sinking deeper into the quagmire. Not only can we catch the mastermind red-handed, but we'll also secure a mountain of blackmail receipts to 'have a nice chat' with the Heavenly Court down the line!"

—"Worst-case scenario, even if the scheme collapses, I can always rewrite the script at the eleventh hour. At most, we're just sacrificing a single trial system; I'm sure Master will forgive me,"-Mo Xi added, ever the master of leaving himself an escape route.

Ruwang had always disliked Mo Xi's opportunistic cunning:

—"Just a trial system? Are not thy senior brothers and sisters inside as well?"

—"A necessary sacrifice!"-Mo Xi sold them out with zero hesitation:—"Besides, it's precisely because they're all inside that I dare let the enemy barge right in! Whoever that bastard is, daring to use a script to manipulate my senior brothers and sisters—they must have a death wish! Let them witness firsthand what happens to their precious script when they throw these uncontrollable 'variables' into the mix!"

—"Dost thou trust thy senior siblings so deeply?"-Ruwang asked.

—"Of course! I'm the weakest direct disciple in our entire Heavenly Spirit Sect!"-Mo Xi proclaimed proudly.

[There is literally nothing to be proud of about that!]

Even Ruwang felt an overwhelming urge to roast him. Yet reflecting on the reincarnation cycles he had observed, it seemed that was literally true. Even during the cycle where he had adopted Mo Xi, this poor child had been bossed around by his senior brothers like a spinning top; it was genuinely a bit pathetic.

Yet Mo Xi clearly didn't consider himself pathetic in the slightest, chortling smugly:

—"Furthermore, everyone inside the trial system has already performed so brilliantly. Now that they're receiving that 'genesis power,' their performance will surely be even more spectacular! I'd love to see how that interloper deals with the game-breaking bugs that I couldn't fix."

He was rejoicing like a screenwriter who had just found an unsuspecting sucker to take over his botched, abandoned script—which somehow made him look even more pathetic.

Ruwang blinked, caught off guard:

—"Wait. Dost thou mean to say thou art willing to disperse this power among the others inside the trial system?"

He had explicitly shown Mo Xi the method to monopolize the power for himself.

—"Wasn't that how Master arranged it from the start?"

Mo Xi asked in genuine bewilderment, completely oblivious to how his current choice differed so profoundly from his past incarnations across previous cycles.
