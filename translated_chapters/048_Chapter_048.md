# Chapter 48: Tradition (第48章 传统)

With Thousand Beasts Hall gone, the grand gathering of the [Winter Clothes Festival](# "Literal CN: 十月朝 (Shíyuèzhāo) — the 1st day of the 10th lunar month, traditional festival marking the onset of winter") naturally continued unabated.

Missing the evening feast was truly a crying shame. Today's banquet had been prepared by Yuwen Xuan under Zhou Hui's direct orders, and it was so delicious that the disciples who had participated in the presentations nearly swallowed their own tongues. In their delirious bliss, some even harbored the utterly foolish notion of jumping ship to transfer to the Spirit Beast Enclosure—a madness they could only snap out of by fixating unblinkingly upon Yuwen Xuan's terrifying face.

Even so, they resolved in their hearts to redouble their efforts from now on. Only by doing so could they ensure their department heads would be summoned back to the annual presentation next year—which was their sole opportunity to mooch a bite of the celestial delicacies personally prepared by Yuwen Xuan's own hands!

The ordinary disciples were overjoyed as well; at the very least, today they weren't forced to eat slop fit for swine.

Although Yuwen Xuan had no interest in laying out an open-ended flowing banquet for the junior disciples, he had prepped thousands of catties of spirit beast meat and massive vats of seasoning, personally portioning and marinating every ounce. He had also gathered heaps of spiritual fruits, purchased spiritual milk ahead of time, and brewed colossal batches of fruit tea and boba milk tea. (Rumor had it he had also casually distilled a batch of fruit wine, though ordinary disciples wouldn't get a taste this Winter Clothes Festival—Master's table was obviously an exception.) Furthermore, he bought various vegetables that were easy to prepare. Disciples could freely claim whatever ingredients they fancied, then either use communal roasting grills or the Artifact Refinement Hall's newly minted [air fryers](# "Literal CN: 空气炸锅 (kōngqì zhàguō) — magical convection artifact engineered by the Artifact Refinement Hall") to roast and fry their own meals. While the resulting flavors couldn't rival a formal feast, compared to the dark culinary abominations served up at Delicacy Pavilion, it was more than enough to move the young disciples to grateful tears.

Yet toward the pleasant close of the evening banquet, Zhou Hui noticed that one of his direct disciples was missing.

He rose soundlessly, intending to slip away without disturbing the festive disciples.

—"Master?"-Qu Qianxing was nonetheless the first to notice Zhou Hui's movement.

—"Keep having fun,"-Zhou Hui said with a mild smile.-"I have had a little wine, and feel like taking a walk."

—"...Yes,"-Qu Qianxing nodded.

He didn't believe a few sips of spiritual wine could go to Master's head, but Master seemed never particularly fond of raucous social gatherings(?). Alas, as the chief coordinator of the evening banquet, Qu Qianxing had upcoming program schedules to oversee and all manner of cleanup logistics once the feast concluded; he simply could not accompany Master on his departure.

Mo Xi, on the other hand, immediately wanted to scurry over:

—"Master, I will accompany you—"

—"Little Xi'er, you haven't even had a few cups yet, and you're already trying to sneak off?"-A pair of jade-white arms hooked around Mo Xi's shoulders. Lu Shulong clambered completely over his junior brother like a sprawling dragon. His cheeks were flushed crimson, and his normally limpid, autumnal eyes were misty with alcohol. Even knowing full well that he was male, one could not help feeling one's heart skip a beat at such ethereal allure.

Mo Xi, however, felt only utter disgust. He shoved with all his might against that breathtaking face:

—"Get lost, you drunkard!"

—"[Jie-jie-jie](# "Literal CN: 桀桀 (jiéjié) — classic cackling trope of sinister or unhinged cultivators"), Little Xi'er is so heartless,"-another body, seemingly lean yet rock-solid as an iron mountain, plopped down on Mo Xi's other side. Guan Ye leaned his head in close. His complexion looked completely normal, yet his glassy, unfocused eyes stared blankly as he poured wine into the cup before Mo Xi:—"Your sword formation today... *hic*... stole so much of the limelight, it made my presentation afterward so difficult!"

—"The sword formation was Mo Bei! I was clearly helping you!"-Mo Xi shrieked, desperately dodging the brimming wine cup Guan Ye was shoving into his face.

Across the table from Mo Xi, Mo Bei sat completely expressionless. Cup after cup, she poured wine for herself, drinking in an endless, rhythmic loop as if it would never stop.

Zhou Hui looked upon this [chaotic scene of dancing demons](# "Literal CN: 群魔乱舞 (qúnmó luànwǔ) — demons dancing in wild revelry; unruly, hilarious bedlam") with suppressed amusement.

—"Keep an eye on Bei'er. Don't let her drink herself into a stupor again,"-Zhou Hui instructed Qu Qianxing.

—"Indeed. Just how much has she had?"-Qu Qianxing sighed, stepping over to his younger junior sister:—"Mo Bei, stop drinking. Drinking any more will harm your body."

He confiscated Mo Bei's wine flagon.

Who could have guessed that Mo Bei would haul a full-sized ceramic wine jar from beneath the table, tilting her tear-rimmed face upward:

—"No! This tastes so good! I only want this, I don't want anything else—"

On the other side, Mo Xi shrieked:

—"Get away from me, both of you! *Aaaaargh*, I'm going to murder you two sooner or later!"

Qu Qianxing felt a thumping migraine coming on.

Zhou Hui gave a quiet chuckle. Most irresponsibly dumping these unruly fledglings onto his most dependable second disciple, he mounted a wisp of rosy cloud and flew back toward the Heavenly Spirit Sect.

***

However, Zhou Hui did not merely take a stroll as he had claimed, nor did he return directly to his immortal cave. Instead, he flew straight to the very roof of the Library Pavilion.

With the entire sect's disciples off ascending the heights at Heaven-Cleaving Chasm, the Library Pavilion was naturally deserted and quiet.

Zhou Hui didn't venture inside. Scanning the highest floor, nestled beneath an inconspicuous little skylight in the corner where the roof and the staircase intersected, he found a familiar silhouette.

After all, this discreet skylight—practically impossible for an outsider to spot—had been designed by the girl exclusively for her own private use.

—"Ah-Ran, reading here again?"

Zhou Hui rested a hand against the ledge of the skylight, leaning down to ask.

Tucked beneath the window sill was a recessed niche. Inside sat a sky lantern, a towering stack of books, a snack box, a tiny stove used to warm beverages, a cozy little blanket, two soft cushions, and a plush body pillow.

And Ran Deng was nestled right in the midst of it all.

She wasn't reading at the moment, however. Wrapped in the blanket and hugging her body pillow, she was gazing out into the distant void, lost in thought.

The silhouette that suddenly materialized startled her:

—"...Master."

Ran Deng instinctively reached for her magical artifact to launch a defensive strike, but upon recognizing who had arrived, she let out a sigh of relief. Her body subconsciously shrank inward, seemingly trying to make room.

Yet the space beneath the skylight was only so large; no matter how much she curled up, there was simply no room to spare.

Seeing this, Zhou Hui smiled faintly. Without making any ostentatious gestures, by the time he sat down, the space beside Ran Deng had seamlessly expanded to accommodate an adult man.

Once seated, Zhou Hui asked gently:

—"What is it? Angry again?"

—"The way you say that, Master, makes it sound like I'm constantly angry,"-Ran Deng mumbled, burying half her face into the body pillow.

She fell silent for a beat, then let out a muffled snicker:

—"Then again, the reason for my anger is always the exact same one... Master, you knew, didn't you? Today's formation wasn't prepared by me at all!"

—"I rather hoped you had put in some effort,"-Zhou Hui said with a wry smile.

—"As expected... Master also thinks it would be better if I strove to become an array master?"-Ran Deng asked, her voice tinged with dejection.

—"Of course I hope so,"-Zhou Hui leaned against the adjoining wall, resting his chin on one hand while placing the other gently atop Ran Deng's head, smiling warmly:—"I hope you become an array master; I hope you become the greatest array master in this entire world; I hope you can ascend to immortality by virtue of your formations... But tell me: are you the kind of person who would live your life for my expectations?"

—"...If it were Master's wish, I might actually try to work hard!"

—"'Might'?"

—"Probably..."

—"'Probably'?"

—"Roughly..."-Speaking up to here, Ran Deng buried her face entirely into the pillow. Her shoulders began to quiver—as if laughing, yet also as if sobbing. Only after a good while did she lift her head, her rimmed eyes slightly reddened:—"Fine! Even if it were Master's earnest hope, I would at most work hard for three days. Exactly three days, not a single day more! After that, I would still study it, but as I studied, I would harbor bitter resentment toward Master—just like how I resent my father—cursing why you all always force me to do things I despise."

Her voice choked up slightly as she continued:

—"Just like this time... This time it wasn't even my father, but Ah-Jiu and the others. Deep down, everyone thinks that as my father's only daughter, having flawlessly inherited the talent required of an array master, refusing to inherit the family legacy is a grotesque waste, an act of sheer unfilial treason!"

Zhou Hui looked quietly upon Ran Deng, offering no interruption.

Ran Deng pressed on:

—"Besides, I don't even have a principled reason to rebel, nor do I have anything I desperately yearn to do. Whether I study formations or not makes zero difference to me. I simply despise... being coerced... *Aaaargh*, honestly, I'm not even certain about that! In the end, I just hate it! Or perhaps I'm simply running away—evading hard work, dodging responsibility, fleeing everything I dislike!"

At this point, Ran Deng let out another self-deprecating laugh, staring up at Zhou Hui with reddened eyes:

—"You see? Every piece of logic that should be known, I know. Every principle that should be understood, I understand... A disciple like me, who understands everything perfectly yet refuses to strive and only wants to slack off—I must be utterly detestable, right, Master?"

—"On that note, whenever I look at you, I often find myself thinking how wonderful it would be if I had your talent,"-Zhou Hui confessed candidly.-"After all, it wasn't until I reached Void Refinement that I expended immense effort to patch up my formation studies."

The corner of Ran Deng's lips twitched, attempting a smile, but failing to form one.

—"However, setting it aside for the time being is by no means a bad approach,"-Zhou Hui continued.-"When I was young, I realized I lacked natural talent in formations. My learning efficiency was abysmal, so I did not stubbornly grind myself to dust or wager my lifespan on it. Instead, I sought out paths better suited to myself. If you feel the pressure is too overwhelming, putting it on pause is perfectly fine... By the way, the source of your distress this time stems from those newly arrived clan children, correct? Would you like me to reprimand them? For taking matters into their own hands and defying your arrangements?"

—"Mmh."

—"Or should I simply pack them all up and ship them right back to your father?"

—"Mmh!"

—"Then why not take it a step further? I shall publicly announce a severance of diplomatic ties with your father, declaring that my disciple is solely under my authority, allowing you to completely sever your father-daughter relationship. That way, you will never again be plagued by the trouble of inheriting the family business, free to do whatever your heart desires in the future..."

—"T-that's going too far! It hasn't reached that point!"-Ran Deng stammered in a fluster.

She looked up, meeting Zhou Hui's smiling eyes, and said with flushed cheeks:

—"Deep down, I probably still can't bring myself to abandon them... just as I couldn't bear to leave Master."

—"Hah. Since you cannot sever ties, and you cannot continue running away, then only one final approach remains,"-Zhou Hui sighed:—"[Face it head-on](# "Literal CN: 迎上去 (yíng shàngqu) — meet it directly; advance toward it")."

—"Master means... I should compromise and do as my father wishes?"-Ran Deng asked warily.

—"Facing it head-on is by no means a compromise; it is about [seizing the initiative](# "Literal CN: 掌握主动权 (zhǎngwò zhǔdòngquán) — taking control of the active initiative and leverage"),"-Zhou Hui explained:—"Before I explain what 'seizing the initiative' entails, you must first ponder carefully: aside from generally telling you to inherit the family trade and become an array master, what is the deepest, truest wish held by your father and your kinsmen?"

—"...To become the premier formation clan?"

—"You can think with far greater granularity. Put yourself in your father's shoes; project yourself into your clansmen's mindset. Why do they harbor such an unyielding obsession with becoming the foremost array masters?"-Zhou Hui prompted.

—"Just competitive vanity, clan glory, and other messy nonsense like that, right?"

—"No, think even more closely. Didn't you say you understand all the reasoning? Then don't let your mind idle. Think deeply: why would they possess such a sense of clan honor—"

—"Because of ancestral legacy, inertia..."-Ran Deng grumbled with superficial impatience, yet she nonetheless followed Zhou Hui's prompting and thought with utmost care. An image suddenly flashed across her mind: her childhood self, standing beside her father as he gazed with immense pride upon an ancient formation designed by their ancestors:—"...Or perhaps, because our clan's average lifespan is so tragically short, he yearns to leave behind something enduring? Just like that defensive barrier our ancestors erected over the Abyss Sea, something people will still speak of thousands, tens of thousands of years later?"

...Yet thinking along this line only made her feel even more unfilial!

Whether it was her father or her clansmen, all carried such heavy, profound convictions, desperate to ensure their family craftsmanship was handed down across eternity. Only she herself spent all her time pondering how to evade and slack off...

But did Master believe that realizing this would suddenly make her wake up and turn over a new leaf?

Impossible!

Ran Deng knew her own "laziness" all too well.

She knew with absolute certainty that guilt would drive her to read formation manuals for two days, after which she would promptly return to [lying flat](# "Literal CN: 摆烂 (bǎilàn) — letting things rot; giving up and lying flat"). She was just that utterly useless!

Besides, was the ambition of her father and clan even something that could ever be fulfilled? Today they wanted her to become the supreme array master and return to take over the family business. Tomorrow, considering that Ran Deng was also a member of the clan and cursed with a short lifespan, they would pressure her into marriage to bear descendants. And once those descendants grew up, the cycle would begin anew.

Just thinking up to this step made Ran Deng want to collapse flat onto the floor.

—Yet in Zhou Hui's eyes, this was precisely why Ran Deng was truly tricky to handle:

She was far too honest, and far too intelligent.

Compared to Yuwen Xuan and Mo Xi, Ran Deng was naturally an obedient child. Tell her to draft architectural blueprints, and she would draft them properly; tell her to measure survey coordinates, and she would measure them diligently; tell her to act as Eldest Senior Sister, and she would fulfill the role conscientiously. She completed every task meekly—an exceedingly honest student.

Yet she differed fundamentally from genuinely honest, stubborn souls like Guan Ye and Mo Bei.

Guan Ye and Mo Bei were also obedient, but they completed their homework with straightforward simplicity without overthinking. Driven by a single-track focus, they had neither stray anxieties nor the slightest inclination to lie flat.

Ran Deng's tragedy was that she was far too brilliant, seeing through everything with blinding clarity.

While [thinking ten moves ahead for every step](# "Literal CN: 走一步看十步 (zǒu yí bù kàn shí bù) — seeing ten steps ahead; grandmaster-level foresight") was an indispensable trait for an array master, the trouble was:

Ask her to draw a single blueprint for the Library Pavilion, and her mind immediately foresaw the gargantuan engineering project of remodeling the entire sect; ask her to measure a few survey coordinates, and she had already calculated the full renovation of every single disciple's cave abode; ask her now to reflect upon her father's plight, and she instantly extrapolated all the way to taking over the family clan for ten thousand generations. When such a terrifyingly sharp mind was paired with a conscientious work ethic, how could she not want to lie flat?

Because with her intellect, she could see crystal clear: the road ahead was pitch-black despair!

Precisely because Zhou Hui understood this about Ran Deng, he deliberately refrained from holding her to strict demands, knowing harsh pressure would only drive her further into retreat.

For a girl like Ran Deng, Zhou Hui offered this counsel:

—"Look, you understand it perfectly! Since you know what your father hopes for, why not turn the tables and [paint a visionary cake](# "Literal CN: 画饼 (huàbǐng) — drawing a pancake to appease hunger; corporate slang for pitching grand, alluring visions to manage expectations and buy time") for him?"

—"In the end, that still means fulfilling his wish... Wait, paint a cake?"

—"Indeed. Since you know your father is preoccupied with clan prestige and desperate to leave something behind, why not play along with his ideals and show a modest interest in his undertakings? For instance, inquiring what he is doing in the Abyss Sea, what formations he is currently deploying... Ah, no, that assignment is still too advanced for you right now,"-Zhou Hui paused, musing for a moment:—"Let us start with those newly arrived youngsters instead. What are their ambitions?"

—"Hmph. They want to push me into becoming Master's most outstanding disciple, making the Array Hall the most valued department under your gaze,"-Ran Deng sneered coldly. Her clansmen's little schemes could be deduced with her knees.

—"Then grant their wishes! Rather than letting them run amok and drag unnecessary workloads onto your plate, why not take the initiative to bring them under your control?"-Zhou Hui said.-"Tell them that the project in your hands is what Master values above all else. Paint them grander, brighter career prospects. Pile onto them the maximum workload they can possibly endure so they have zero spare time to overthink—yet never snuff out their hope. Let them learn more techniques, fulfill their petty desires at the right moments, and feed them a few carrots."

Listening to Zhou Hui lay this out, Ran Deng's eyes widened—

Zhou Hui looked upon Ran Deng:

—"You see? In doing so, you will be walking the very 'righteous path' they desire, becoming the ideal leader of the Array Hall and the worthy heir expected by the Ran Clan. Simultaneously, you will have delegated your own workload, diverted their meddling attention, and secured breathing room—or should we say, slacking room—for yourself. Is that not having the best of both worlds?"

At this point, Zhou Hui suddenly quizzed her:

—"Following this metric, do you understand how to handle your father now?"

—"...I should write frequent letters to him, discussing array theory and putting on the appearance of striving diligently. I can probe and map out his project milestones, the clan's affairs, and other variables, anticipating what his next demand of me will be. What can be accomplished effortlessly, I fulfill; what I refuse to do, I preemptively counteract, or execute superior alternative choices in advance, or distract him with other developments to alter his mind, thus carving out space for myself..."-Ran Deng rattled off at lightning speed.

This was what Zhou Hui loved most about Ran Deng: she grasped ten points from a single cue, exceptionally easy to teach.

As Ran Deng spoke, she suddenly realized something, bursting into a snicker.

—"What is it?"-Zhou Hui asked.

—"No... it's just that I never imagined there would come a day when Master would be teaching me how to slack off,"-Ran Deng truly dissolved into helpless laughter, her shoulders shaking:—"When you are literally the 'Untiring' Immortal Venerable!"

—"Caught you off guard, didn't it?"-Zhou Hui laughed along.

***

The master and disciple laughed together for a good while until Ran Deng said:

—"I can't... I've laughed myself out of breath."

She habitually reached toward her snack box.

In truth, Ran Deng had undergone inedia long ago. Yet while she could skip regular meals, she could never part with snacks.

Under normal circumstances, preserved fruits and jerky were never out of reach. Lately, she was often seen sipping boba milk tea, and when drafting blueprints, she always had a hard candy tucked in her cheek.

Zhou Hui had never quite understood where Ran Deng's snacking obsession came from. As an elder, he felt that even if one didn't practice inedia, one ought to eat proper meals.

—"Speaking of which, you didn't seem to eat much at dinner?"-Zhou Hui recalled that Ran Deng had spent the first half of the banquet daydreaming, and disappeared altogether during the second half.

—"I did eat,"-Ran Deng immediately caught his drift, but didn't wish to trouble her master:—"Besides, the banquet is already over, right? There is no need to trouble..."

—"It is no trouble at all. You don't honestly believe that bottomless pit of an Eldest Senior Brother could ever be satisfied with a single dinner, do you?"-Zhou Hui winked at Ran Deng:—"Come along. I'll take you to raid your Eldest Senior Brother's secret pantry."

Saying this, Zhou Hui rose to his feet.

Ran Deng remained seated in her cozy nook, staring up at Zhou Hui's back in a momentary daze. Then, springing up, she hugged Zhou Hui's arm, just as she had done countless times in her childhood.

—"Okay!"-Ran Deng beamed.

***

Leading Ran Deng, Zhou Hui departed the Library Pavilion and flew toward Delicacy Pavilion.

This place could loosely be considered Yuwen Xuan's private turf, as among the direct disciples, only he ever utilized the kitchens to cook meals for Zhou Hui—and conveniently whip up midnight snacks for himself.

Theoretically, the kitchens should have been completely empty today.

The banquet had taken place at Heaven-Cleaving Chasm. For convenience's sake, everyone—including Chef Old Huang, whose culinary skills left so much to be desired—should have brought all cookware up the mountain. Consequently, Delicacy Pavilion ought to have been just like the Library Pavilion: pitch-black and deserted.

Yet just as they approached Delicacy Pavilion, Zhou Hui and Ran Deng saw the second-floor dining hall ablaze with lights. The warm yellow glow cast grotesque, distinctly non-human silhouettes onto the latticed windows, and muffled shouts drifted down:

[—"Red Dragon!"](# "Speaker: Monster Gambler")

[—"Eight of Dots."](# "Speaker: Monster Gambler")

[—"Pong!"](# "Speaker: Monster Gambler")

—"Tsk! My luck tonight is insanely hot!"

[—"Yuwen Xuan, I'd have to be a ghost to believe you!"](# "Speaker: Monster Gambler")

—"Then how about we raise the stakes and bet bigger?"

...

If it were merely pure monsters having fun, it wouldn't have mattered much. But mingled among them was a fellow who had no business being there.

—"Eldest—"-Ran Deng opened her mouth, only for Zhou Hui's swift hand to seal her with a Silence Art.

After casting the spell, Zhou Hui placed a finger to his lips in a shushing gesture. He then layered concealment arts over both himself and Ran Deng to erase their presence and aura before narrowing his eyes at the wildly flailing shadows on the window:

—"Heh. Heh."

[...This is not my fault!]

[It is definitely not because I skipped dinner and Master brought me to the kitchen out of concern for my empty stomach that we stumbled upon this scene!]

Ran Deng desperately pleaded innocent in her heart, quietly loosening her grip on Zhou Hui's arm and taking two prudent steps back.

Zhou Hui had already drifted silently upward, hovering right outside the second-floor window.

Inside the hall, dense smoke swirled amidst a foul stench of alcohol. A motley crew of monsters, completely unable to maintain human guise, had reverted to their colossal true forms, crowding around a mahjong table. Among them, only Yuwen Xuan—sitting with his back to the window—retained any semblance of human shape.

A monster beside him jeered:

—"You dare bet bigger? Aren't you afraid of getting punished?"

—"Master won't notice,"-Yuwen Xuan curled his lip:—"Today he has to babysit that whole flock of fledglings; whether he even realizes I'm gone is debatable. If it weren't so dreadfully dull up there, why would I bother sneaking down here to play?"

—"Heh-heh. Are you jealous? Just how old are you, still clinging to your master like that?"

—"You don't understand,"-Yuwen Xuan pinched a tile between two fingers, letting out a world-weary sigh:—"Life is like a circle. Once you pass this stage, you can never get it back even if you want to. One must cherish the present! When it's time to cling, you should—"

Yuwen Xuan never finished his sentence, noticing the expressions of the monsters across from him turning bizarre.

The moment he realized what was happening, a calm voice rang out right behind his back:

—"Is this how you cherish the present?"

The instant the first syllable reached their ears, every monster—including Yuwen Xuan—exploded with a *bang* into billowing mist, scrambling to flee in all directions!

Regrettably, how could any of them escape the grasp of a Tribulation Transcendence titan?

By the time Zhou Hui's final word landed, those plumes of smoke were rewound like a reversed cassette tape, sucked back into their respective seats and condensing into their original shapes.

Yuwen Xuan in particular felt a gentle pair of hands rest tenderly upon the crown of his head:

—"Xuan'er, a moment ago you wished that I would focus solely on you, did you not?"

Yuwen Xuan froze stiff, terrified to turn his head and daring not twitch a muscle, only managing a dry, squeaking laugh:

—"N-no, Master... I, I think being alone is actually quite wonderful... *Aaaaaargh!*"

***

—"*Aaaargh!*"

The next morning, Jing Chenjun was jolted awake by his roommate's bloodcurdling scream. He had still been savoring the wonderful holiday of the Winter Clothes Festival, filled with bright hopes for his future, only to be greeted by such bizarre shrieking.

—"W-what is it?"-he asked in a panic.

—"The... the mountain gate!"-Huang Xiao stood on the balcony, clutching his terrified, distorted ears, pointing in sheer horror toward the distance.

Jing Chenjun initially found Huang Xiao's reaction strange, but once he saw the distant spectacle clearly, all decorum vanished:

—"People? There are people hanging from that tree?!"

—"And monsters..."-Huang Xiao added with a trembling voice.

The freshmen dormitory faced right toward the mountain gate—specifically toward the ancient peach tree blooming in resplendent pink even in the heart of autumn. Normally a scenic panorama, the ambiance took quite a different turn when three or four blood-dripping creatures dangled from its boughs.

The humanoid figure in particular was twisted into a grotesque knot, resembling a bizarre [butterfly bow](# "Literal CN: 蝴蝶结 (húdiéjié) — contorted into an impossible bowknot by Zhou Hui's punitive arts"). One could only wonder how human anatomy could be contorted into such an unnatural configuration.

Naturally, Huang Xiao and Jing Chenjun were not the only ones traumatized. Other early-rising disciples were similarly frightened out of their wits, using their own screams to jolt awake their roommates, who rushed out only to be subjected to the exact same horror.

Thus, in the crisp light of dawn, every freshman disciple underwent a sect-wide [SAN Value Check](# "Literal CN: SAN值检测 (sānzhí jiǎncè) — sanity check; Call of Cthulhu gaming meme for cosmic mental trauma").

The freshmen's chaotic shrieks finally alarmed the dormitory proctors on each floor—senior disciples of the Heavenly Spirit Sect appointed to supervise the rookies' daily lives.

They bellowed:

—"What are you all screaming about?!"

—"B-but Senior Brother! A c-c-corpse..."-a young disciple pointed a trembling finger toward the mountain gate.

—"Oh, that? That's Eldest Senior Brother,"-the senior disciples remarked with utter nonchalance, long accustomed to the spectacle:—"And probably his gambling buddies... Pfft, looks like Lord Immortal Venerable caught them all in one fell swoop this time!"

—"E-Eldest Senior Brother?!"-The freshmen were utterly bewildered. They looked at that twisted "corpse," then looked at the monster hanging alongside him, arriving at a conclusion:—"C-could it be that Eldest Senior Brother betrayed the sect and got captured?!"

Instantly, their minds conjured an over-the-top melodramatic epic combining treason, tragic romance, and unspeakable taboos.

—"What on earth are you imagining?!"-The senior disciples scoffed:—"How could Eldest Senior Brother do something like that? He's dangling up there for nothing more than gambling or playing mahjong... Then again, it's not impossible that he blew yesterday's festival presentation and got strung up for poor academic grades."

—"YOU GET HUNG FROM THE TREE FOR POOR ACADEMIC GRADES?!"-The freshmen turned pale with sheer terror.

This was ten thousand times more terrifying than hearing Eldest Senior Brother had betrayed his master!

Initially, the senior disciples had planned to educate these rookies on "Eldest Senior Brother dangling from the peach tree" as a commonplace scenic fixture of Mount Tianling. But seeing the rookies' traumatized reactions, a deliciously wicked scheme spontaneously sparked in all their minds.

They sneered darkly at the youngsters:

—"Of course! If your academic marks are poor, naturally you get hung up. Did you honestly think our Sect Grand Examinations were just for fun? As a matter of fact, after every annual grand exam, that ancient peach tree is covered with a whole canopy of dangling students! If your mastery isn't sufficient, you'd better pick out a comfortable branch early."

`"!!"`

From that day onward, the Instructors noticed that the disciples in their classes suddenly spiked in diligence. When they eventually discovered the cause, they tacitly refrained from correcting it; a few Instructors even deliberately amplified the rumor to boost their own performance metrics! As a result, the horrific rumor was not debunked until the end-of-year Sect Grand Examination concluded.

As for how, in the following year, disgruntled freshmen seized upon another Eldest Senior Brother dangling from the bough to prank the next intake of rookies—gradually crystallizing into a notorious [Heavenly Spirit Sect tradition](# "Literal CN: 天灵派恶劣传统 (Tiānlíng pài èliè chuántǒng) — the hazing tradition where seniors scare freshmen into studying by claiming poor exam scores get you tied to the peach tree")—that was a tale for another time.

***

While the Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples were quaking in dread over Eldest Senior Brother dangling from the tree, the cultivation world of the Central Domain—nay, not merely the cultivation world, but even mortal kingdoms and demonic beast tribes—was plunged into a colossal tempest.

Speaking of which, the speed of information propagation in the cultivation world was an absolute enigma.

Thanks to primitive communication infrastructure, certain news traveled at a snail's pace. For instance, remote, secluded sects still remained oblivious to the recent feud between the Untiring Immortal Venerable and Carefree Palace, knowing nothing of the trade route blockade. Yet because cultivators possessed myriad transmission arts, certain pieces of intelligence spread at blinding velocity:
