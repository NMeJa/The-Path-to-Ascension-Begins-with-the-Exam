# Chapter 2: Your Master Is Still Your Master (第2章 你师尊还是你师尊)

What does one do after [lying flat](# "Literal CN: 躺平 (tǎngpíng) — modern Chinese youth philosophy of opting out of the hyper-competitive rat race")?

For an [Involution King](# "Literal CN: 卷王 (juǎnwáng) — champion of grinding / hyper-competitive overachiever"), this was something Zhou Hui had never once considered.

After all, when it came to 'resting'—who didn't know how to do that?

Or so Zhou Hui had originally believed.

Yesterday, after sending his disciples away from the Ascension Platform, he had returned to his immortal's cave intending to change his robes and 'rest.' Yet while undressing, he noticed traces of heavenly lightning lingering upon the shredded fabric. Without a second thought, he immediately preserved the sample and began conducting comparative research against lightning specimens accumulated across his previous reincarnations.

Not until darkness descended over the immortal's cave did Zhou Hui realize several [shichen](# "A shichen (时辰) is a traditional Chinese two-hour time unit; several shichen equals 4–6+ hours") had slipped by. He hadn't even finished changing; his eldest disciple's outer robe was still draped over his shoulders.

Only then did Zhou Hui hurriedly slip into his inner garments and seat himself upon the prayer mat. Out of sheer muscle memory, his body assumed the meditative lotus posture, nearly plunging him into deep cultivation trance on the spot.

Fortunately, he caught himself in time:

—"Good grief, habits are truly terrifying,"-Zhou Hui muttered to himself.-"Going without a plan will never do. I must properly schedule a relaxing retirement."

With that resolve, he pulled a blank scroll from the bookshelf. Immediately, a diminutive [Brush Monkey](# "A tiny spiritual beast whose shed fur is prized for crafting talisman brushes") clambered out from the brush pot beside him, skillfully grinding ink and dampening the brush for him.

Sitting upright before his low table, Zhou Hui penned several large, elegant characters onto the scroll: *Retirement Schedule Plan*.

Evidently, the Untiring Immortal Venerable had no grasp of what 'relaxing' actually meant.

In fact, his entire concept of 'retirement' was strictly limited to a brief interlude in that other world.

In that 'dream world,' Zhou Hui had indeed 'normally' retired for a brief period. Due to his extraordinary contributions to the nation, he had been admitted to the country's most prestigious convalescent retreat. Yet as a top-tier scientist, even during that time, he was bombarded with invitations to deliver keynote lectures and attend academic conferences, appointed as a visiting professor by multiple universities, and officially re-hired out of retirement almost immediately.

Yet Zhou Hui genuinely believed *that* was what 'ordinary retired life' looked like.

Still, even Zhou Hui understood that the two worlds were worlds apart; transplanting that exact lifestyle here was out of the question. Thus, he could only tweak and adapt according to his memories, concocting a cultivation-world version of a convalescent home's daily schedule.

Gazing at the blank scroll, Zhou Hui murmured in deep thought:

—"Three meals, bathing, entertainment... it feels like the amenities here are severely lacking,"-Zhou Hui murmured, stroking his chin.-"Right, I also promised to give those kids remedial lessons. Should I structure them like academic lecture series? Come to think of it, the teaching methods in this world need serious improvement as well..."

[Improvement? They need a radical overhaul!]

Zhou Hui thought.

It wasn't that his previous instruction had been particularly poor, but rather that the educational paradigm of the Heavenly Spirit Sect—and indeed, of nearly every sect throughout the cultivation world—was based on 'karmic destiny.'

Which sect you entered, which master you bowed before: depended on 'karmic destiny.'

Which cultivation technique you practiced, which spells you learned: depended on 'karmic destiny.'

Which medicinal pills you required, which magical treasures you sought: depended on 'karmic destiny.'

What heights of cultivation you could attain, how difficult your breakthroughs were... still depended on 'karmic destiny.'

In the past, Zhou Hui hadn't seen any issue with this setup; after all, that was how he had clawed his way up.

Zhou Hui had once trudged thousands of li only to be turned away from an immortal's lecture because he 'lacked karmic destiny.' He had scoured every celestial market across the realm, unable to find a suitable cultivation manual because his 'karmic destiny had not yet ripened.' Even so, relying purely on his own tenacity, he had still reached the Tribulation Transcendence Stage. Consequently, he had taken it for granted that 'karmic destiny' was simply an inherent component of learning.

Yet after experiencing that other world, Zhou Hui realized that this so-called 'destiny-based education' was utter poppycock.

[This doesn't even qualify as education!]

[This so-called learning method has zero efficiency! Think of how much precious time has been squandered!]

[Furthermore, their cultivation foundations are paper-thin. Those without karmic destiny can't even find proper techniques to begin with, while those 'blessed with karmic destiny' easily trap themselves in information silos and develop [severely lopsided grades](# "Literal CN: 偏科严重 (piānkē yánzhòng) — extreme academic imbalance, excelling in one subject while failing others")!]

[The standardization of education must be placed on the agenda immediately!]

Zhou Hui's brush flew across the paper, swiftly producing another thick stack of proposals:

—"First, academic disciplines must be rigorously categorized, compulsory education implemented, and standardized textbooks compiled for each subject... But prior to all of that, the old pedagogical chaos leaves it impossible to gauge how well these children have mastered the basics. A [baseline assessment exam](# "Literal CN: 摸底考试 (mōdǐ kǎoshì) — diagnostic / baseline placement test used in Chinese schools at the start of a term to gauge students' true levels") is an absolute necessity."

Even with just a cursory assessment, the list of tasks ahead was already immense.

Unfortunately, this had all been decided on the spur of the moment. Zhou Hui had already promised to lecture his disciples tomorrow morning; forget compiling comprehensive textbooks—even for a simple examination, there was barely enough time to draft the test papers.

Fortunately, for a Tribulation Transcendence heavyweight, this posed no problem at all.

Zhou Hui immediately produced a mustard-seed spatial dimension.

It was an artifact he had personally refined: ten days inside equated to a single day in the outside world. Zhou Hui normally used it for closed-door cultivation, but this time, he carried an armful of foundational cultivation manuals inside, bracing himself to pull an all-nighter drafting exam questions.

—What? [Lying flat](# "Literal CN: 躺平 (tǎngpíng) — opting out of competitive grinding and relaxing")?

This *was* lying flat!

For one, he wasn't cultivating; for another, he wasn't doing anything to elevate his personal combat prowess. He was merely drafting an examination for his disciples and organizing some cultivation scriptures along the way—how could that possibly count as [grinding](# "Literal CN: 卷 (juǎn) — hyper-competitive involution")? It was pure, leisurely recreation.

Zhou Hui's conscience was entirely clear.

And so, the following morning, the disciples of the Heavenly Spirit Sect were handed a steaming-hot, hefty stack of examination papers.

***

Zhou Hui's second disciple, Qu Qianxing, gingerly unfolded the examination paper.

Like his fellow junior brothers, the moment his eyes landed on that hefty stack of papers, all suspicion that their master had been possessed evaporated into thin air—what kind of Extraterrestrial Demon would possess the deranged patience to draft this many exam questions?!

Yet Qu Qianxing's confusion remained unresolved; if anything, his anxiety deepened:

[Since Master is still Master, why did he abruptly abandon his ascension?]

[Could it be that he suffered grievous injuries during the heavenly tribulation, rendering ascension impossible? No! Even if that were true, knowing Master's unyielding character, he would never throw in the towel so easily. He would have attempted breakthrough after breakthrough.]

[Which means this sudden examination holds some deeper significance. Perhaps Master's true intentions are hidden within these questions.]

Analyzing the situation with keen focus, Qu Qianxing cast an inquisitive gaze over the problems on the page.

The questions, however, left him blinking in utter bewilderment.

[—"Question formats I've never seen before."](# "Speaker: Qu Qianxing")

It wasn't that Qu Qianxing lacked worldly experience.

In truth, before Zhou Hui took him under his wing, Qu Qianxing had once been a mortal scholar. He had sat for the imperial civil service examinations and had nearly clinched the title of *Zhuangyuan*—the imperial top scholar—had his face not been so outrageously handsome that the Emperor personally designated him as the *Tanhua* [instead](# "The Tanhua (探花) was the Imperial Third Scholar in the imperial civil service exam, traditionally awarded to the candidate who combined exceptional academic brilliance with striking physical beauty"). Having walked that path, Qu Qianxing had naturally tackled mountains of examination papers in his life.

Yet Qu Qianxing had never laid eyes on an exam like this.

Fully half of this paper consisted of multiple-choice questions: presenting a premise and requiring the examinee to select one or more correct answers from A, B, C, and D. Of the remaining half, three-quarters were fill-in-the-blank and true/false problems. Only at the very end were there a handful of essay questions, the most difficult of which were designated as bonus questions.

Such a layout led the disciples, who had never encountered such a format in their lives, to think—

—"This is so easy!"-one of the junior brothers blurted out in excitement.

Easy?

Qu Qianxing pressed his thin lips together.

At first glance, multiple-choice questions did appear far simpler than essays.

There was no need to compose ornate prose, no need to fret over literary polish, and the answers were literally printed right on the page—all one had to do was pick the correct option out of four.

Qu Qianxing started off smoothly enough. With just a few sweeping glances, he cracked the first few problems without breaking a sweat. But when his eyes reached Question Nine, he ground to a dead halt:

> **Question 9**: Which formation most efficiently enhances the spiritual energy quality of a spirit herb field?
>
> A) Spring Returning Formation
>
> B) Spirit Gathering Formation
>
> C) Wood Spirit Formation
>
> D) Spirit Replenishing Formation

This question was not inherently difficult.

The formations in all four options could be mastered during the Qi Condensation Stage. To Qu Qianxing, however, the practical functions of these four arrays seemed virtually interchangeable. Since every one of them gathered spiritual energy, he had always deployed whichever was most convenient to set up at the time. He had never meticulously measured or compared the exact volume of spiritual energy each drew in, let alone analyzed their nuanced biological interactions with a spirit herb field.

After wavering repeatedly, Qu Qianxing selected the Spring Returning Formation, the one he was most accustomed to utilizing.

He looked to the next question. It was once again a technique accessible at Qi Condensation, but it was a Water-attribute spell. Qu Qianxing possessed a Gold Heavenly Spirit Root and was completely unfamiliar with the Water element.

Qu Qianxing had no choice but to guess an uncertain answer once more. He moved on to the subsequent problem, but that one fell entirely outside his realm of knowledge, stripping away even the luxury of a 'vague educated guess' and forcing him to pick an option blindly.

—And the crowning stroke of cruelty: Qu Qianxing discovered that his master had inscribed an arcane spell over the paper. One could not rely on divination or luck to 'cheat,' nor could one fudge an answer with flowery, evasive rhetoric as in an essay. You either knew it, or you didn't.

The moment this realization struck him, cold sweat broke out across Qu Qianxing's forehead:

[Who on earth claimed this exam was simple?!]

[This question format! This syllabus breadth! This surgical precision! It unquestionably tests an examinee's true mastery of every granular knowledge point to the absolute limit! If the imperial civil service examinations had used questions like this back then, those useless, [wine-sack rice-bag](# "Literal CN: 酒囊饭袋 (jiǔnáng fàndài) — useless gluttons / incompetent bureaucrats") officials would have been exposed in the first round!]

[As expected of Master!]

Qu Qianxing looked toward Zhou Hui in breathless awe and dread.

The Untiring Immortal Venerable, however, appeared utterly nonchalant about the proceedings in the examination hall. He leaned against his low desk in a languid, relaxed posture entirely uncharacteristic of his usual bearing, a bamboo scroll held in his hands as he read with tranquil focus.

Yet everyone present was acutely aware that with Zhou Hui's terrifying cultivation, not even the flutter of a butterfly's wing within a thousand li could escape his perception. Attempting to cheat was pure suicide.

Indeed, the instant Qu Qianxing lifted his eyes, Zhou Hui seemed to register it and spoke in a leisurely drawl:

—"No need to be nervous,"-Zhou Hui said placidly.-"I merely wish to see where everyone's foundation stands."

—"If you encounter questions you cannot solve, you may skip them for now and tackle the ones you know first—ah, I nearly forgot. This exam has a strict time limit. Finished or not, I will collect the papers in four... *cough*, two shichen."

As he spoke, Zhou Hui retrieved a ticking magical treasure from his wide sleeve.

The artifact was clearly a chronometer; every crisp *tick-tock* made the relentless drain of time palpable in their bones.

Only then did Qu Qianxing remember that he, too, was sitting here as an examinee. If he dallied any longer, he would become one of the very 'useless bureaucrats stripped bare by the exam.'

Qu Qianxing hastily lowered his head, stripped of any remaining bandwidth for wandering thoughts.

Qu Qianxing was far from the only one.

The moment Zhou Hui announced the time limit, a synchronized rustling swept through the hall—the sound of brushes gliding rapidly across Xuan paper, reminiscent of the first snowfall on a late autumn night: quiet, yet stirring.

Regrettably, this early snow was unmistakably a southern snowfall. It did not hold out for long before tapering off into a sluggish, disjointed drizzle.

[So soon?]

Zhou Hui's expression remained placid, but his divine sense swept across his disciples' examination papers one by one.

In truth, even without doing so, as a veteran educator, Zhou Hui could already form an accurate baseline judgment of their scores simply by observing his students' postures and answering speed.

The best performer, predictably, was his second disciple, Qu Qianxing.

Qu Qianxing had a deep theoretical grasp of cultivation methods and maintained an understanding of spells suited to every type of spirit root. Evidently, aside from his own rigorous cultivation, he had spent no small amount of effort tutoring his junior brothers and sisters; he was an exceptionally conscientious second senior brother.

The pity was that he had, after all, hailed from the mortal realm. His broader understanding of the cultivation world lacked depth, and the sheer width of his knowledge was correspondingly starved.

Setting aside niche, obscure lore, he was barely acquainted with certain fundamental common senses of the cultivation world. Moreover, he possessed an inherently 'utilitarian' streak—indifferent to any knowledge outside of direct cultivation. Perhaps this very flaw had sown the seeds for him being blindsided and manipulated after assuming the mantle of Sect Master in the future.

In contrast to Qu Qianxing, when it came to sheer breadth of knowledge, the standout performers were surprisingly his third disciple, Ran Deng, and fourth disciple, Guan Ye.

Although Ran Deng was ranked third among the direct disciples, she simultaneously held the title of Eldest Senior Sister for the entire Heavenly Spirit Sect. As a role model, her foundation in cultivation methods and spells was quite respectable.

The only hitch was that Ran Deng was chronically lazy by nature. She lacked Qu Qianxing's rigorous scholarly drive, so her comprehension of techniques never penetrated beneath the surface.

Conversely, Ran Deng was free of Qu Qianxing's blind spots. Born into an established cultivation clan, she did not lack worldly exposure. Furthermore, she loved devouring wild folklore, rumors, and miscellaneous hearsay; she knew a little about everything and could write something down for every question, yet she refused to study a single subject in depth. She was the textbook embodiment of a [jack-of-all-trades and master of none](# "Literal CN: 样样通，样样松 (yàngyàng tōng, yàngyàng sōng) — knows a little of everything, but master of nothing").

His fourth disciple, Guan Ye, was far more grounded than Ran Deng.

Guan Ye likewise possessed wide-ranging interests, but he also possessed ample patience, an investigative spirit, and a deep appreciation for hands-on application. Whether it came to spellcraft or other disciplines, it was clear he had studied with genuine rigor; as long as a question fell within the boundary of his cultivation realm, there was nothing he couldn't answer.

Regrettably, due to his peculiar constitution, Guan Ye's cultivation progress was agonizingly slow.

When he had first entered the sect, his cultivation had clearly been higher than everyone else's. Now, however, he was stuck at the middle stage of Foundation Establishment, on the verge of being overtaken by the youngest junior brother and sister who had apprenticed latest.

Of course, cultivation realm did not account for everything.

Among Zhou Hui's direct disciples, the one with the highest cultivation was undoubtedly his eldest disciple, Yuwen Xuan.

This fellow had cultivated for less than two centuries, yet already stood at the Peak of the Soul Formation Stage; breaking through to the Void Refinement Stage was just around the corner.

Yet his brain suffered from an aristocratic malady: a dignified Soul Formation powerhouse who couldn't even answer basic [freebie questions](# "Literal CN: 送分题 (sòngfēntí) — warm-up questions meant to give students free points") from the Qi Condensation syllabus! Today's rock-bottom score belonged exclusively to him. It made Zhou Hui itch to crack open his skull and inspect what kind of cranial wiring had managed to cultivate to Soul Formation.

And to Zhou Hui's dismay, Yuwen Xuan was not the only problem child in the family.

His fifth disciple, Lu Shulong, was in the exact same boat.

Still, Lu Shulong's predicament was slightly better than Yuwen Xuan's. Beyond having raw talent, he possessed extraordinary aptitude for spiritual plants and medicinal alchemy. This lent his examination paper a considerably more respectable sheen, preventing it from inciting the volcanic rage inspired by his eldest senior brother's blank disasters.

And with senior brothers and sisters like these, one could hardly pin great hopes on the youngest two either.

Zhou Hui's sixth disciple, Mo Bei, and seventh disciple, Mo Xi, were a pair of fraternal twins. Having apprenticed latest, they had cultivated for the shortest time; consequently, their cultivation was modest. Their exam answers were middling—marginally better than inner sect disciples of comparable cultivation, but not by much. Passable at best; perhaps only with further cultivation would they achieve anything notable.

Yet compared to their grades, their true danger lay in the ideological pathologies written into the Heavenly Dao's script.

According to that script, the Heavenly Spirit Sect's youngest junior sister, Mo Bei, would devolve into an incurable [love-brain](# "Literal CN: 恋爱脑 (liàn'àinǎo) — infatuation so extreme that someone abandons all loyalty, rationality, and ethics for their lover"), obsessively throwing herself at the protagonist, showering the enemy with sect resources, and ultimately backstabbing her own martial brothers for his sake—serving as the direct fuse for the sect's annihilation.

Meanwhile, the youngest junior brother, Mo Xi, would secretly collude with the Demon race from early on, disseminating demonic techniques within the sect and 'gifting' the protagonist the moral high ground in public opinion, framing the total slaughter of their sect as a righteous act of justice.

Zhou Hui had no wish to view his own disciples through tainted lenses merely because of the 'Heavenly Dao's script.' Yet looking at their examination papers now, he could see the ominous warning signs—

If events had unfolded according to the cycles of his previous lifetimes and Zhou Hui's original temperament, he would have entered seclusion to nurse his wounds after this heavenly tribulation, abandoning his disciples' education entirely. Qu Qianxing would have been forced to prematurely assume the mantle of Sect Master with inadequate cultivation, leaving him with zero time or energy to guide the youngest twins. Their eventual degeneration into those tragic archetypes was entirely plausible.

Expanding the scope even further: if his direct disciples and inner sect disciples turned out this wretchedly incompetent, what hope was there for the outer sect disciples?

Wasn't the annihilation of the Heavenly Spirit Sect practically an inevitability?

Zhou Hui concluded with bitter exasperation, [despairing at their inability to live up to their potential](# "Literal CN: 恨铁不成钢 (hèntiě bùchénggāng) — resent iron for not turning into steel; exasperated at someone's failure to meet expectations").

It wasn't that Zhou Hui expected his disciples to defy the Heavenly Dao.

Zhou Hui had always led by example. Since he himself was [lying flat](# "Literal CN: 躺平 (tǎngpíng) — refusing to participate in competitive grinding"), he didn't demand that his disciples continue [grinding](# "Literal CN: 卷 (juǎn) — hyper-competitive involution"), particularly when such grinding was utterly futile—the Heavenly Spirit Sect was fated for extermination; even disbanding the sect would accomplish nothing.

In fact, across his six reincarnations, there had been one lifetime where Zhou Hui hadn't even founded the Heavenly Spirit Sect. Yet his disciples had still joined neighboring sects, crossed paths with the protagonist, and while fleeing for their lives, 'coincidentally' led the protagonist straight to the hidden grotto where Zhou Hui was quietly cultivating. One could only sigh: 'the will of Heaven cannot be defied!'

Fortunately, this did not mean the disciples were beyond salvation.

After all, the only one whose escape routes had been exhaustively paved by the Heavenly Dao was Zhou Hui himself. The others were mere cannon fodder at most; once they completed their roles in the 'Annihilation of the Heavenly Spirit Sect' plotline, they would be free of the Heavenly Dao's script.

Even Qu Qianxing, who had been singled out by name, was 'merely' reduced to ashes by the protagonist; the script never decreed that he could not reincarnate.

The only reason Zhou Hui was offering them 'remedial lessons' was his hope that before his departure, he could impart a bit more knowledge to these children. Once they gained their 'freedom' in the future, whether they chose to lie flat or strive upward would be their own prerogative.

Until now.

With grades like these, what right did they have to lie flat? What right did they have to ascend?

In this state, they wouldn't even be able to run for their lives, let alone possess the luxury of choice!

A cold, furious smirk curled at the corner of Zhou Hui's lips:

Retake the curriculum!

They needed to retake everything, starting from the most rudimentary basics!
