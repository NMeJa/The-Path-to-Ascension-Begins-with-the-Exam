# Chapter 138: The Name List (第138章 名单)

[← Previous Chapter](137_Chapter_137.md) | [Table of Contents](INDEX.md) | [Next Chapter →](139_Chapter_139.md)

---

Jian Haoge was not nearly as unconcerned about this matter as he pretended to be.

Consequently, Zhou Hui received a jade slip. Inside was an exhaustive record of rumors concerning the clandestine secrets of the God-Demon War, speculations regarding the whereabouts of the descendants of White Jade Capital, certain suspicious under-the-table maneuvers orchestrated by the Ceremony Court and the Palace of Carefree Wandering following True Immortal decrees after the cultivation realm migrated to Sky City, and even the diary of Sky City's builder—Jian Haoge's grandfather, the Ancestor of the Jian Clan.

—"This isn't specially meant for you, mind you!"-Jian Haoge said, following the [standard tsundere script](# "Literal CN: 标准格式 (biāozhǔn géshì) — standard boilerplate format; textbook tsundere excuse: 'It's not like I'm doing this for you!'").-"It's only because my family's Nuo'er caused you trouble that I'm providing these materials for your reference, so you don't let Nuo'er meddle in things he shouldn't touch... Furthermore, because these are merely our Jian clan's 'internal prejudices' without a shred of concrete proof—airing them publicly would only make us a laughingstock—no outsider is permitted to know. Only you may look at them. Understood?"

—"Understood,"-Zhou Hui answered with utmost gravity.-"I will not disclose it to a single soul. I swear."

After all, this effectively counted as "Upper Realm classified intelligence."

This much was self-evident from the fact that even Zhou Hui had never heard the faintest whisper of it.

One had to bear in mind that back in the day, Zhou Hui had spent centuries alongside East Hedge Garden excavating the ruins of the Abyssal Ocean to unearth the truth behind that ancient war! Yet whether it was Zhou Hui himself, the grandmasters of East Hedge Garden, or veteran antique peddlers like Tao Zhu, not a single one had ever caught wind of a "Curse of the True Immortals." That alone spoke volumes about how extraordinarily sensitive the True Immortals were regarding this subject.

Then again, this was likely the very reason why the cultivation world—which ironically relied on "archaeology for technological progress"—cared so remarkably little about history.

For "history" was merely the retrospective summation compiled by posterity regarding a past that was already dead and buried.

The cultivation realm, however, was fundamentally different. Leaving aside immortal gods and ancient demons whose natural lifespans were near-eternal, even mortal cultivators possessed unnervingly prolonged lives once they reached the upper realms. They were part of "history," yet they were simultaneously the destroyers of "history."

Take the God-Demon War: to some, it was ten-thousand-year-old ancient history, discernible only in scattered fragments through archaeological digs; yet to others, it was an ordeal they had personally lived through. They could twist the truth at whim, rewrite records they found displeasing, and even scheme to murder the descendants of those with whom they had held grudges millennia ago.

Under such circumstances, excavating ancient ruins in the Abyssal Ocean—which might well have been doctored by someone—yielded far less than studying certain "living artifacts."

Worst of all, the longer someone lived, the more formidable they usually were.

Whether the Jian clan had truly been "cursed by the True Immortals for knowing too much" remained uncertain; but Jian Haoge's present conduct was certainly more than sufficient to earn him a curse from the True Immortals.

Zhou Hui naturally appreciated the sentiment. He said to Jian Haoge:

—"This time, I will resolve this matter flawlessly and ensure everyone receives a good ending."

—"A good ending?! Why does your tone sound like you're sending me off to die?!"-Jian Haoge bristled, every hair standing on end.

In the end, this transmission concluded once again amidst Jian Haoge's flared temper.

Yet after severing the connection, Jian Haoge flew down toward the snowy mountain ranges beneath Sky City in surprisingly high spirits.

Upon those snowy peaks lay his secluded cave abode, used for closed-door cultivation—because the City Spirit of Sky City was simply far too noisy; attempting focused, meditative cultivation up in the city was an absolute impossibility.

Only, Jian Haoge had no intention of entering seclusion this time. He merely intended to conduct a rigorous, comprehensive examination of both himself and his own "lifespan and destiny," lest some disastrous misfortune truly occur as Untiring had warned, finding himself already marked as an "object of the True Immortals' curse."

After all, it wasn't just that Untiring's [crow's beak](# "Literal CN: 乌鸦嘴 (wūyāzuǐ) — someone whose ominous predictions always come true; a jinx") never uttered a single comforting word—his ominous predictions were also terrifyingly accurate.

[—"I'm just exercising due caution, it's not like I'm scared!"](# "Speaker: Jian Haoge")

---

After terminating the communication, Zhou Hui pressed the jade slip Jian Haoge had given him against his forehead, examining the contents within.

Following a broad overview, Zhou Hui understood why Jian Haoge had characterized it as having "no evidence."

Because what the Jian clan knew was far from comprehensive.

According to the diary of the Jian Ancestor, during the era of White Jade Capital, the Jian clan had been highly sought-after sound cultivators. The ancestor had possessed an aloof, tranquil temperament that detested conflict; thus, long before the God-Demon War erupted, he could no longer bear the increasingly suffocating tension within the Central Domain and resolved to lead his kin and friends north to take refuge in the then-sparsely populated Northern Domain.

However, the Jian clan at that time had been a fairly large cultivation family with substantial assets; even in the cultivation realm, relocating such an enormous clan was no simple task.

At that juncture, a young master of the Qingyang clan—who happened to be an ardent admirer of the Jian Ancestor's zither artistry—learned of his intentions. He provided an exhaustive map of the Northern Domain as a reference for relocation sites, and even sponsored a massive sum of spirit stones to assist the Jian clan's migration.

In exchange, the young master of the Qingyang clan requested that the Jian clan escort certain cultivators unsuited for combat, along with a group of mortal refugees, to the Northern Domain for sanctuary—the mortal dynasties existing in the Northern Domain today had been established by those very people.

From the Qingyang clan's request, the Jian Ancestor had sensed something extraordinarily amiss: why would an ordinary immortal realm war necessitate the deliberate evacuation of mortals?

Yet when he inquired, he received no explanation whatsoever.

Through careful observation, the Jian Ancestor recorded various conditions regarding White Jade Capital; however, these could only serve as circumstantial references to gauge the veracity of rumors. Just as Jian Haoge had noted, they could not be taken as definitive fact.

On the other hand, during the construction of the Jian clan's flying vessel, the Jian Ancestor had recorded an incident that had deeply unsettled him:

Owing to the Jian clan's own wealth combined with the Qingyang clan's "investment," the Jian clan had hired the finest artifact-refining grandmaster in White Jade Capital at the time to construct their flying vessel. Their initial interactions had been relatively pleasant—though the Jian clan's specifications had been somewhat demanding, causing the artisan's hair to fall out in alarming clumps; nevertheless, in consideration of the astronomical compensation, the artisan had harbored little real resentment.

It was precisely during this period that a True Immortal had been drawn to the scene. Claiming to admire the artisan's craftsmanship, this True Immortal offered to assist the Jian clan free of charge in building a colossal vessel capable of accommodating an entire White Jade Capital. However, for the vessel's primary core material, it required the "Dao Bone" of an artisan.

—"What is a Dao Bone?"-the Jian Ancestor had asked curiously.

—"Certain mortals are born with supreme, peerless talent—that is a 'Dao Bone,'"-the artisan had answered him.-"If your flying vessel incorporates a Dao Bone, it will remain a spiritual artifact while simultaneously becoming a part of the artisan himself. It will upgrade and grow on its own, becoming an eternal creation."

—"Hahaha, I only wanted to move house!"-the Jian Ancestor had laughed.

Because the Jian Ancestor refused, the matter seemed to have been dropped. However, the artisan subsequently became brooding and taciturn, repeatedly displaying bizarre, erratic behaviors. Yet seeing that the artisan's craftsmanship had grown even more consummate than before, the Jian Ancestor paid it no mind—until the day the flying vessel was completed, when the artisan leapt into the forging furnace of his own accord.

The resulting flying vessel was countless times more magnificent and flawless than the original blueprints.

Yet using it was an utter [heart-stopper](# "Literal CN: 心梗 (xīngěng) — myocardial infarction; slang for being deeply stifled, traumatized, or sick to one's stomach with distress").

> *"Had I not lacked the spirit stones and time to undertake such a colossal project a second time, I nearly would have abandoned this flying vessel."*

So recorded the Jian Ancestor at the very end.

Reading up to this point, Zhou Hui felt a faint stir in his heart.

He knew that the Jian clan's flying vessel was in fact the prototype of "Sky City." The present Sky City was built upon the foundation of that very vessel; consequently, that vessel remained the pinnacle achievement in the hearts of countless artifact refiners.

In fact, it was said that the northern cultivators currently preparing to travel from the Northern Domain to the Central Domain had sought out the City Lord of Sky City specifically to request those blueprints.

—"A flying vessel, is it..."-Zhou Hui murmured, an interconnected thought springing to mind.

However, regarding that matter, he would need to make certain preparations first. Fortunately, there was still plenty of time before the construction of Sky City's new flying vessels would be completed; thus, he placed a mental bookmark on this point and resolved to address it later.

He then closed the diary and turned to examine the remaining materials.

In contrast to the Jian Ancestor's vague records regarding the state of White Jade Capital, the intelligence Jian Haoge had gathered from various elder figures and the "True Immortal Decrees" relayed by the Ceremony Court proved considerably more substantial.

Zhou Hui sorted through the findings:

First, the Qingyang clan was an absolute certainty.

Although they were the Saint Lords of White Jade Capital and had entered into an alliance covenant with the True Immortals, just as no emperor relishes having a retired emperor looming over his head, the Qingyang clan had long harbored deep dissatisfaction toward the True Immortals. The Jian Ancestor's diary had likewise noted that the Qingyang clan and the True Immortals had been ostensibly united while secretly estranged; thus, the destruction of the Ascension Stairway leading to the Upper Realm was not the least bit surprising.

Conversely, precisely because they had signed that alliance covenant, the True Immortals could not directly harm the Qingyang clan—unless they could reclaim that single drop of True Immortal blood bestowed upon the Qingyang clan during the covenant's signing.

Next was the True Dragon "Lu."

According to the Jian clan's records, that True Dragon was one of the bloodline ancestors of the Eastern Domain's oceanic races—a [philandering scumbag dragon](# "Literal CN: 渣渣龙 (zhāzhā lóng) — a playboy dragon that leaves a trail of romantic debts wherever it goes") who scattered his affections everywhere, yet remained passably responsible. For the sake of his progeny's interests, he had consistently maintained good relations with this realm and was willing to accept the constraints of White Jade Capital. Most crucially, because he refused to serve as a True Immortal's mount and held past grudges against them, standing on the Qingyang clan's side was entirely natural.

Yet that dragon's end had been thoroughly tragic.

During the God-Demon War, he had seduced another True Dragon—the mount of a certain True Immortal. Despised and cast out by that dragon's master, he was battered into grievous injury—Zhou Hui rather suspected he had fallen victim to a [badger game (physical edition)](# "Literal CN: 仙人跳（物理）(xiānrēntiào wùlǐ) — Chinese slang for a honey-trap extortion scheme, here literalized where the dragon seduced another's mate and was physically beaten half to death"). Suffering heavy wounds, he was corrupted by demonic qi and descended into madness, whereupon he was treated as a demon and slain by mortal cultivators.

It was said that a certain cultivation clan in the Western Domain still preserved his dragon horns, while other components of his anatomy continued to circulate through the mortal realm.

The gold crown Jian Nuo had used to test Lu Shulong had originated from that very dragon.

As for Lu Shulong, he was quite likely the fruit of that "badger game," left behind and somehow never brought to the Upper Realm.

Following that was the Demon "Bai."

This figure had no direct record within Jian Haoge's materials; however, the Jian Ancestor had recorded an individual whose identity was uncertain.

He mentioned that the Qingyang clan had been acquainted with a True Immortal who had lingered in the cultivation world for several centuries, purely out of infatuation with a female cultivator. Moreover, she was a mother who had slain her own unfaithful Daoist partner—a woman surnamed Shen.

> *"If that True Immortal carries on like this, he will fall into demonic ways sooner or later."*

Such had been the Jian Ancestor's assessment of that romance.

Reading this, Zhou Hui flipped back through the materials Jian Haoge had compiled.

This time, he found it.

Among those targeted by the True Immortals' curse were several high-stage cultivators who had opposed them back then. Among them was a female cultivator surnamed Shen; she and her husband had perished during the God-Demon War, but the whereabouts of her two children remained unknown, and certain individuals were still searching for them.

—"Shen and Bai,"-Zhou Hui murmured under his breath.-"Separately, these two surnames are unremarkable. But placed together..."

Blood Demon Hall.

Blood Demon Hall's Sect Leader Shen Qingqian, and her eldest disciple Bai Wubin.

Of course, Bai Wubin was a pure human; Zhou Hui was certain he possessed no True Immortal bloodline. Yet with the surname "Bai" in the equation, the entire affair suddenly took on a distinctly melodramatic flavor.

Most importantly, in the Heavenly Dao's script, Bai Wubin appeared to be the protagonist's sworn brother and sidekick, yet his inexplicably tragic mistreatment and subsequent fall into demonic ways bore an uncanny resemblance to the True Immortal recorded in the Jian Ancestor's diary.

[Could it be that after the True Immortal fell into demonic ways, he adopted one of the Shen woman's children, making Bai Wubin his descendant? And Shen Qingqian descended from the other child? So in the True Immortals' script, they engineered Bai to slaughter Shen's bloodline and fall into demonic corruption just like that traitor before him?]

Zhou Hui reasoned along the [melodramatic soap-opera logic](# "Literal CN: 狗血 (gǒuxuè) — dog-blood; absurd, clichéd, melodramatic tropes commonly found in trashy television dramas") of that novel—a level of simulation well within his capabilities.

Yet merely entertaining the thought gave Zhou Hui a full body of goosebumps.

—"Utter nonsense,"-Zhou Hui muttered.

He disliked wild guesses devoid of evidence:

—"Next time, I'll find a way to examine the bloodline origins of those two from Blood Demon Hall, and the matter will be clear."

To be frank, Zhou Hui wasn't particularly eager to know the answer to this speculation.

At this point, the three-person group from "ten thousand years ago" witnessed by Ran Deng all possessed concrete leads.

Naturally, the True Immortals' blacklist was by no means limited to these few.

A great many cultivators who had received immortal and demonic inheritances during the God-Demon War were listed, along with other entities Zhou Hui did not recognize at all. Yet the chaotic assortment of surnames and races looked vaguely familiar, seemingly flitting in and out of that original novel script.

Finally, there was one entry that genuinely caught Zhou Hui by surprise:

Array Masters, the Ran clan.

The Ran clan's circumstances were somewhat unique, prompting Jian Haoge to mark them out specifically.

It turned out that during the God-Demon War, a diviner had calculated that a maiden of the Ran clan would pose an obstacle to the True Immortals, even altering the ultimate outcome of the war. At that time, however, there had been only a single female cultivator in that generation of the Ran clan.

That female cultivator had been remarkably decisive. She declared that to prevent the prophecy from coming to pass, she was willing to die immediately; but as the price for "saving the cultivation world," and for the sake of the Ran clan's future, her cultivation had to be preserved and passed directly to the next clan head.

The True Immortals had agreed to her terms, bestowing a cultivation technique that permitted her to bequeath her cultivation directly to her descendants.

And so that female cultivator had perished, and the Ran clan's next clan head had inherited the entirety of her cultivation. Regrettably, whether due to an inherent flaw in the True Immortals' manual or because mortals were unsuited for divine techniques, that clan head failed to attain the lifespan commensurate with the inherited cultivation, forced to pass it hurriedly to the next successor.

Not only that, but ever since, the lifespans of the Ran clan had never been long.

—"They got scammed,"-Zhou Hui commented.

All along, he had assumed Ah-Ran's abbreviated lifespan was due to the nature of their inheritance technique! He had even contemplated researching it, only to be rebuffed every time by (different generations of) Ah-Ran.

Presumably, because it involved True Immortals, the Ran clan had found it ill-advised to speak ill of them!

Even after all this, the True Immortals seemed thoroughly dissatisfied. It was reported that because "the heavenly mandate did not change," they suspected the Ran clan had deceived them as well; consequently, the True Immortals' wrath toward the Ran clan had persisted unabated.

Seen in this light, in the original script, after Ran Deng died following the annihilation of the Heavenly Spirit Sect, the entire Ran clan had still been thoroughly exterminated due to their unyielding quest for vengeance.

But this record held a rather intriguing nuance:

"The heavenly mandate did not change." The death of the Ran clan's ancestor had brought about no change whatsoever; yet she had been the sole female meeting the conditions at the time. In other words, from the very beginning, the one destined to "hinder the True Immortals" had never been that Ran clan maiden forced into self-sacrifice.

Then the one who had altered the heavenly mandate was—

Zhou Hui looked toward the sky outside his cave abode.

Even from Mount Tianling, the distant presence of the Ascension Stairway remained distinctly visible.

—"Ran Deng, was it?"-Zhou Hui sighed quietly.

Indeed. The "Ran clan maiden" of the hexagram had to be Ran Deng.

In fact, during her recent trial, Ran Deng had traversed the rift in time to arrive ten thousand years ago, overwriting the rules of the Ascension Stairway and claiming it as Zhou Hui's possession. Consequently, following the God-Demon War, the True Immortals were barred from ever entering this world again.

This was good news, proving that everything in this current lifetime had become settled history, impossible to erase.

Yet it was also sorrowful news, for the Ascension Stairway incident had directly condemned the Ran clan to generations of tragically abbreviated lifespans.

—"I must lift the curse on the Ran clan,"-Zhou Hui murmured with a sigh.

At this point, among his direct disciples, two were confirmed to have vendettas with the True Immortals:

His Third Disciple Ran Deng, and his Fifth Disciple Lu Shulong.

In addition, Eldest Disciple Yuwen Xuan would almost certainly have to be counted among them.

That Taotie was overwhelmingly likely connected to him.

—"Strangely enough, there's no one surnamed Qu, nor anyone surnamed Mo,"-Zhou Hui muttered.

It wasn't that he wished for Qu Qianxing or Mo Xi and Mo Bei to be cursed by True Immortals; rather, in the Heavenly Dao's script, their participation had been excessively prominent. To find zero connection now left Zhou Hui feeling slightly uneasy.

Still, having nearly half his disciples on the True Immortals' blacklist was more than enough reason for the other side to strike.

Zhou Hui pondered this, turning the jade slip over in his hands for a good while.

Suddenly, a cold scoff escaped his lips.

—"This is actually a good thing! I was just thinking my 'karmic ties' weren't strong enough!"-Zhou Hui remarked.

He spread his hand, and the surrounding environment instantly shifted.

Had Lu Shulong been here, he would undoubtedly be trembling like a leaf, for Zhou Hui had once again recalibrated his surroundings into the "Heavenly Dao's observation perspective."

And that glaring "thread" was still here.

It still hung suspended from high "above," weaving the Heavenly Dao of this mortal realm.

Looking at this "thread," Zhou Hui first raised his hand, atop which a tiny flame ignited, taking the shape of a seal.

This was Ran Deng's soul flame.

She had determined the rules of the Ascension Stairway, and Zhou Hui had bestowed upon her the mark of "Passing"—this celestial seal was that very mark.

Naturally, much like exam scores, celestial seals had an archival record on Zhou Hui's end, so he could manifest it whenever he pleased.

Cradling this celestial seal, Zhou Hui exhaled a breath of golden mist from his mouth.

This was "dragon breath."

Previously, in order to draft an appropriate cultivation method for Lu Shulong, Zhou Hui had requested a drop of heart-blood from him. After refining it, he had returned it to Lu Shulong for continued assimilation; nevertheless, since that drop of heart-blood had once been entrusted to Zhou Hui—representing Lu Shulong's absolute faith—the aura of that heart-blood remained behind, allowing Zhou Hui to invoke Lu Shulong's aura at any time.

—This was precisely why living beings never surrendered their heart-blood lightly; giving it once was tantamount to surrendering perpetual control. By that same token, were Zhou Hui so inclined, he could invoke Yuwen Xuan's condition as well.

However, since Yuwen Xuan's true connection to the True Immortals remained unknown, it was ill-advised to do so for the time being.

Lu Shulong's "curse" was already ready at hand.

Zhou Hui blew gently, directing this mouthful of dragon breath onto the celestial seal. The flame of the celestial seal surged violently, igniting that very "thread" and blazing upward along its suspended trajectory in a single fiery rush.

Zhou Hui watched this spectacle with quiet satisfaction.

In the next instant, his own body transformed into a column of moon-white flame, blending into the upward-surging conflagration. It blazed furiously upward, on the verge of reaching the suspended "island" from which the silk thread dangled.

Regrettably, just a hairsbreadth away, the flames abruptly sputtered out.

Moon-white sparks drifted down from above, coalescing upon the ground to reform Zhou Hui's figure.

—"Still unable to ascend?"-Zhou Hui muttered under his breath.

In truth, the moment he attained godhood, he had sensed that even without thunder tribulations carving open a passage, he possessed the qualification to ascend to the Upper Realm. Whenever he wished, he could ascend and become a True Immortal... no, a member of the "gods."

The sole problem was the "firewall" separating the upper and lower realms.

This was likely the very "covenant" preventing immortals and demons from directly interfering in this realm again.

For Zhou Hui to breach that firewall meant he would have to relinquish his identity in this world, completely becoming one of their kind; moreover, once ascended, he would be unable to descend again. What difference would such a method have from standard tribulation ascension?

Worse still, were he to ascend via conventional means, the moment he breached the firewall, he would alert the True Immortals of the Upper Realm, exposing himself completely before their eyes.

Formidable though Zhou Hui was—and fully aware of his own might—to charge recklessly into the Upper Realm without having ever engaged a True Immortal in true combat, and with zero clarity on their situation, would be a foolish and pointless endeavor.

Zhou Hui naturally would not do that.

He decided to turn the tables and exploit the "thread" they had lowered.

While that "thread" existed here to alter and influence the local Heavenly Dao, it simultaneously created an exploit in the "firewall." As long as he could reinforce this "thread" and forge it into a 'Dao,' Zhou Hui could hack his way across to their side.

This was what Zhou Hui had mentioned to Lu Shulong previously regarding his desire to use karmic luck (qiyun) to refine the "thread."

Yet even for Zhou Hui, this was his first time attempting such a feat; he could not guarantee success.

By contrast, "karmic ties" were far more definitive.

Since the True Immortals harbored an obsessive vendetta, their thoughts themselves constituted "karma." Zhou Hui could follow the very karma of their own making and ascend along it, bypassing the refinement process entirely.

And this method was indeed viable—just moments ago, he had been merely a hairsbreadth away.

—"The karmic ties of Ah-Ran and Shulong alone are not enough; there must be others,"-Zhou Hui mused.

He contemplated the figures listed on that roster.

Fortunately, the others were not far from him either:

—"Right—the True Immortal blood refined by the Qingyang clan can also be put to use. Furthermore, just to be safe, should I summon those two from Blood Demon Hall as well?"

The Qingyang clan was simple enough; after all, Villa Master Qingyang and Qingyang Qingyu were currently right here at Mount Tianling. It was Blood Demon Hall that presented a slight logistical challenge.

He could not speak of this matter openly. After all, Jian Haoge had taken a tremendous risk in providing these materials, and Zhou Hui had sworn not to reveal it to anyone—which naturally included the parties involved.

The Heavenly Spirit Sect and Blood Demon Hall maintained a cooperative partnership, but Shen Qingqian was not Zhou Hui's subordinate; she was hardly on call at a moment's notice, let alone to verify bloodline connections between her and her disciple over an unverified hunch.

Zhou Hui disliked social pleasantries, but that did not mean he was ignorant of them. Toward cooperative partners, formal protocol had to be observed.

This was also to avoid arousing external suspicion.

Zhou Hui pondered for a moment, and quickly arrived at a decision:

—"Right—the final examinations of White Jade Capital Academy provide a ready-made excuse!"

After all, White Jade Capital Academy's Physical Education class (strike that, Body Refinement) had utilized Blood Demon Hall's curriculum.

And thus, the Sect Leader and Eldest Disciple of Blood Demon Hall found themselves receiving an utterly baffling invitation from the Untiring Immortal Venerable.

An invitation to inspect the academic outcomes achieved since the founding of White Jade Capital Academy together.
