# Chapter 80: Warming Up (第80章 预热)

Cloud City (Yunzhong City).

True to its name, this was a city situated amidst the clouds.

Not that it hovered aloft in the firmament; rather, the metropolis was perched atop the cliffs of a spur belonging to the towering Heaven's Chasm Mountain Range. Shrouded year-round by dense, billowing cloud banks, only sporadic upturned eaves and flying cornices peered through. Viewed from afar, it evoked the poetic elegance of ["clouds so dense that one knows not where he dwells."](<# "Literal CN: 云深不知处 (clouds are deep, knows not where) — quote from Tang Dynasty poet Jia Dao's (贾岛) famous poem 'Seeking the Recluse Without Finding Him' (寻隐者不遇)">)

At the same time, this was the largest immortal city in the Central Domain.

Whenever cultivators from other domains spoke of the Central Domain's immortal metropolises, only two names typically sprang to mind: Fengqing City and Cloud City.

Among them, Fengqing City owed its fame to the advantage of hosting a grand cross-domain teleportation array. Serving as the primary arrival station for the majority of outsiders visiting the Central Domain, Fengqing City naturally became one of the domain's flagship representatives.

Yet if one spoke of which city harbored the absolute greatest number and highest density of cultivators in the Central Domain today, none could hold a candle to Cloud City.

It was said that originally, this city had merely been a tiny village clinging to the cliffside. However, its geographical location was nothing short of extraordinary: not only was it sheltered far away from the massive beast tides that rampaged every few years, but it was also entirely insulated from raging spiritual storms. At the same time, it could absorb the rich, concentrated spiritual energy that accumulated within the sea of clouds, serving as an exceptional waystation for expeditions into the Abyss Sea ruins. Gradually, more and more wandering cultivators were drawn here to settle, causing the settlement to blossom into a colossal metropolis. Later, when Fairy Xia became City Lord, constructed the Cloud Pool, and inaugurated the annual New Year Assembly, this towering city among the clouds gained renown throughout the Five Domains and Six Abysses, hailed alongside the Northern Domain's Sky City and the Eastern Domain's No-Return Immortal Isle as the three foremost gathering sanctuaries for immortals across the cultivation world.

—Then again, some held that Cloud City was merely riding on the coattails of White Jade Capital.

After all, in terms of sheer urban scale and cultivator population, Cloud City was actually only on par with the Southern Domain's Divine Tree of the Fire Abyss; in fact, if one factored in subterranean territories and the monstrous beast populations, the Divine Tree easily eclipsed Cloud City in scale. Yet because the Central Domain had been the very core of the cultivation realm prior to the Great War of Gods and Demons, people inevitably projected their boundless nostalgia for White Jade Capital onto Cloud City, longing to catch a fleeting glimpse of White Jade Capital's ancient majesty through its soaring spires. Add to that Fairy Xia's formidable social standing among the upper echelons of the Five Domains and Six Abysses, and Cloud City acquired the dazzling prestige it enjoyed today.

Of course, petty rivalries over rank did nothing to diminish Cloud City's unchallenged status within the Central Domain. Someone had once calculated that at least half of all "Mid-Three Realms" cultivators in the Central Domain congregated in this single city.

Lu Zhen was one of them.

Lu Zhen hailed from the Xiafei Sect of the Northern Domain and was currently at the mid-stage of the Nascent Soul realm. Having hit a cultivation bottleneck with no path to advance, he had journeyed to the ruins of the God-Demon Battlefield to try his luck, hoping for some fortunate encounter to unearth useful resources that might spark a breakthrough. Cultivation could not be neglected in the meantime, so he had rented an immortal cave in Cloud City, living a monotonous existence where his days consisted solely of meditating and scouring ruins.

Such was the daily routine of most cultivators in the Mid-Three Realms.

After all, the Mid-Three Realms was an intensely awkward stage of cultivation.

A sect's resources were inherently finite. Even a major sect in the Northern Domain with thousands of disciples could at most sustain a dozen or so Nascent Soul and Soul Formation elders. Consequently, senior administrative posts like sect leaders and council elders were extraordinarily scarce. If the sect master's lineage of direct disciples were talented enough, they would monopolize these lucrative appointments entirely—and only they were privileged enough to receive personal guidance from powerhouses of the Upper-Three Realms.

Meanwhile, Mid-Three Realms disciples who were not direct disciples and failed to climb the managerial ladder—cultivators like Lu Zhen—were left stranded without resources, without masters, and without offices. Their only recourse was to roam the world in search of "karmic opportunities."

They either scurried frantically across the four corners of the world scrounging for scraps of resources, or sought out impoverished, declining sects to serve as hired guest elders, or attempted to establish their own modest sects to teach disciples. As a result, they became the most accessible "high-tier" immortals that low-level cultivators ever encountered.

Objectively speaking, the vast majority of cultivators were ruthlessly culled during the Mid-Three Realms, with numbers plummeting precipitously by the Void Refinement stage. This was precisely why low-level secret realms were so plentiful: legions of cultivators perished during the Mid-Three Realms, their remains condensing into "secret realms" for lower-tier practitioners to explore. Only a minuscule fraction ever crossed the threshold into the Upper-Three Realms.

Therefore, for a Mid-Three Realms cultivator like Lu Zhen, avoiding [mid-life career anxiety](<# "Literal CN: 中年焦虑症 (middle-aged anxiety syndrome) — modern buzzword applied comically to cultivators stuck at Nascent Soul/Soul Formation bottlenecks">) was practically an impossibility.

On this day, Lu Zhen concluded a secluded meditation that had lasted seventy-seven full years. Gazing upon his cultivation base, which had barely budged an inch, he let out a dejected sigh. He casually inspected the miniature spiritual herb plot within his abode; though lush and verdant, the spiritual plants grown over seventy-seven years amounted to virtually nothing in the eyes of a Nascent Soul cultivator. Lu Zhen sighed heavily once more and stepped out of his cave-dwelling.

Lu Zhen rented Unit 67-86 of the Cloud Tower.

The so-called Cloud Tower was a colossal ring-shaped complex erected in the very heart of Cloud City. Spanning ninety-nine tiers in total, each level maximized its exposure to the qi-rich cloud banks. The denser the surrounding clouds, the richer the spiritual energy within—at higher floors, the concentration even surpassed the main mountain peaks of most prominent sects. Fairy Xia had opened this complex to cultivators from the Soul Formation stage down to Void Refinement. Each cave abode was outfitted with a miniature herb garden, a beast-rearing chamber, and an alchemy room, along with puppet automatons to manage daily upkeep during secluded meditation. With every imaginable cultivation amenity provided, it was a prime sanctuary for Mid-Three Realms practitioners—provided one had enough spirit stones to foot the bill.

However, decades of zero cultivation progress had made Lu Zhen thoroughly impatient with wasting any more time cooped up here.

—"Perhaps I should venture into the ruins again? But I've already scoured the well-known ones,"-Lu Zhen mused.-"Should I risk my neck in more treacherous depths to try my luck? Or should I just pack up and leave the Central Domain altogether, heading for the Southern Domain or the desolate Western Domain?"

He felt like a headless fly buzzing about aimlessly. Yet as an ordinary cultivator devoid of high-level connections and sect patronage, he didn't even possess a general compass for locating "karmic opportunities."

Naturally, having just emerged from seclusion, Lu Zhen was in no position to embark immediately on a [spur-of-the-moment trip](<# "Literal CN: 说走就走的旅行 — modern Chinese travel meme for impulsively packing up and leaving on vacation">).

He first released several paper cranes, hoping to contact fellow cultivators of comparable rank. He needed to catch up on the latest trends and gossip circulating in the cultivation realm, check for intelligence regarding newly discovered secret realms, and only then decide his next move.

Lu Zhen harbored little hope, however.

Upon reaching the Mid-Three Realms, every prolonged seclusion essentially triggered a complete reshuffling of one's social circle.

A span of several decades was more than enough for a sweeping swath of peers to perish or vanish without a trace. Those fortunate enough to survive were either locked in deep seclusion themselves or wandering the world chasing elusive "karmic encounters," leaving little room for sustained contact.

Reality proved no exception. After waiting for the time it took an incense stick to burn, only two or three paper cranes fluttered back, and all of them were automated auto-replies indicating: *Target is currently unavailable. Please do not disturb.*

Lu Zhen was well accustomed to this.

He had zero interest in waiting for the remaining cranes that had an eighty-percent chance of never returning. Stepping onto his flying sword, he soared toward the immortal bazaar outside the Cloud Tower, intent on seeing if he could glean any rumors in the marketplace, or simply stock up on travel supplies for a journey.

In stark contrast to the quiet solitude of the Cloud Tower, the immortal bazaar of Cloud City was perpetually bustling with boundless vigor.

Countless storefronts lined both sides of the straight, wide avenues—some purveying spirit pills, others offering magical artifacts, encompassing every trade under heaven. Rather than merely hanging shop banners by their doors, grand establishments projected dazzling phantasms of their wares directly into the sky. Radiant, multicolored glows erupted from newly forged high-tier artifacts, while fragrant scents of freshly brewed pills wafted through the breeze. Among them, the most conspicuous landmark was unmistakably the soaring pagoda of Carefree Pavilion. Atop its swept eaves, radiant and captivating immortal maidens sang and danced vivaciously, entrancing newly arrived cultivators so thoroughly they could hardly peel their eyes away.

Even Lu Zhen experienced a momentary lapse of focus gazing upon those enchanting maidens, briefly contemplating whether he ought to pay a visit to Carefree Pavilion first.

Yet after patting his slender storage pouch, he ultimately touched down into the marketplace below.

He would certainly visit Carefree Pavilion eventually, but checking the bazaar for fresh gossip came first. Besides, after seventy-seven years of isolated meditation, he longed to bask in a bit of human hustle and bustle.

Calculating thus, Lu Zhen casually stepped into a tavern.

The establishment appeared to be newly opened.

The facade was vast and pristine, and the interior was exceptionally spacious, occupying half an entire immortal block.

Lu Zhen felt zero sentiment toward this.

He was well aware that those who opened shops in the immortal bazaar to earn spirit stones were mostly Foundation Establishment cultivators. If they lacked the backing of an established sect, by the time Lu Zhen completed a modest round of seclusion, their shops would almost certainly have gone bankrupt.

—Nascent Soul practitioners in the Mid-Three Realms like Lu Zhen never ran shops as a livelihood unless they were seeking worldly experience to polish their mental state. After all, their lifespans spanned millennia; spending a thousand years running a shop day in and day out was enough to drive anyone insane. Hiring managers to run it was equally out of the question: whenever they entered seclusion for a few decades, hired staff would have long absconded with the funds or usurped the shop outright, and even seizing it back yielded negligible profit.

At any rate, each time Lu Zhen emerged from seclusion, virtually every storefront in the bazaar—save for monolithic institutions like Carefree Pavilion—had been completely replaced.

Faced with such transience, Lu Zhen's heart remained as tranquil as still water.

Precisely because he knew that even his favorite shops would vanish before his next seclusion ended, he had long learned never to invest emotional attachment into anything.

That said, an expansive storefront had undeniable perks.

Whether newcomer or veteran, passersby could seldom resist ducking inside for a look.

Even if it was merely to look.

Lu Zhen walked into the tavern with serene detachment, completely ignoring a peculiar raised setup in the center that resembled an arena ring, currently swarming with throngs of onlookers.

That too fell under the category of "fleeting trends."

The pastimes of low-tier cultivators evolved every few decades—the last craze had been mahjong, the one before that beast fights, and during the seediest eras underground bare-knuckle bouts had enjoyed brief popularity. None of it warranted wasting a breath on.

Carrying himself like a detached outsider standing above mortal dust, Lu Zhen picked out a quiet seat and sat down.

—On that note, Lu Zhen felt compelled to offer the establishment some praise: its "gaming zone" and dining area were sharply delineated, partitioned by silencing formations that kept the atmosphere peaceful. The sole downside was that he couldn't eavesdrop on neighboring tables' gossip.

The interior decor was tasteful as well, and the table even offered a pot of passable spiritual tea.

Lu Zhen poured himself a cup. Before long, a clever waiter hurried over with a bright smile, presenting the menu.

Lu Zhen hadn't come for drinks, so he naturally was in no rush to order. Holding the menu, he simply asked:

—"Has anything major happened lately?"-Lu Zhen asked.

—"Major events?"-the waiter beamed, his eyes crinkling into crescents.-"Plenty of big things have happened recently! But if you're asking what everyone cares about most, it has to be the [academic symposium](<# "Literal CN: 学术报告会 — modern university research symposium terminology applied comically to ancient Daoist debates">) being hosted at the Heavenly Spirit Sect by two immortals of the Upper-Three Realms!"

Lu Zhen nearly sprayed a mouthful of tea straight across the table.

He almost reached out to grab the waiter by the collar! Fortunately, his Nascent Soul pedigree (or what passed for it) allowed him to maintain the measured, slightly aloof composure expected of a senior cultivator. Feigning nonchalance, he asked:

—"Do you even know what an Upper-Three Realms cultivator is? Where on earth did you hear such news?"-Lu Zhen asked.

One had to realize that even though he had achieved the Nascent Soul realm, because he had never been selected as a direct disciple back then, he had never possessed the standing to meet the Upper-Three Realms titans of his own former Xiafei Sect!

For the present Lu Zhen, any intelligence concerning Upper-Three Realms cultivators hinted at potential karmic opportunity—though whether it would prove a blessing or a curse remained to be seen.

The waiter took no offense at Lu Zhen's condescension; if anything, he was thoroughly accustomed to this reaction. Chuckling, he replied:

—"This humble one naturally knows! Who in the streets hasn't heard by now? Two Mahayana powerhouses—Manor Lord Qingyang of Eternal Villa, who carries the bloodline of the ancient sacred clan, and the Fairy of the Sacred Mausoleum, descendant of White Jade Capital's tomb guardians and master of primordial talismans—have locked horns over the doctrinal orthodoxy of the White Jade Capital ruins! To settle the matter, they are each preparing to deliver [academic lectures](<# "Literal CN: 学术讲座 — modern academic lecture terminology used comically for Daoist sermonizing">)!"-the waiter explained enthusiastically.

Hearing this, Lu Zhen believed at least eighty percent of it.

He had never heard of any "Fairy of the Sacred Mausoleum," but the illustrious name of Eternal Villa was certainly known to him.

As one of the most storied factions in the Central Domain, they would hardly permit rumors to slander their own Manor Lord unchecked. If such word was spreading like wildfire, there had to be solid truth behind it.

Lu Zhen's pulse quickened with excitement:

—"What exactly is this 'academic lecture'?"-Lu Zhen asked.

—"It's equivalent to setting up an altar to preach the Dao, though the format is slightly different,"-the waiter explained with uncanny fluency, sounding as if he had personally attended an academic symposium himself.

Lu Zhen paid no attention to such minor details.

At this very moment, he could hardly restrain the urge to fly straight to the Heavenly Spirit Sect!

A karmic opportunity!

This was indisputably a grand karmic opportunity!

When a Mahayana titan set up an altar to expound the Dao, their heavenly resonance was potent enough to grant sentience to mindless grass and trees! If a Mid-Three Realms cultivator like Lu Zhen could listen in, the benefits would be boundless!

However...

—"Do you happen to know where this Heavenly Spirit Sect is located?"-Lu Zhen asked.

As an ordinary cultivator from the Northern Domain who had merely journeyed south to scour ruins, Lu Zhen was only familiar with the Central Domain's few premier sects and had never paid heed to the chaotic local sect politics. When he entered secluded meditation seventy-seven years ago, he had naturally never heard of any "Heavenly Spirit Sect."

Reflecting on this, Lu Zhen felt a flicker of confusion:

—"Come to think of it, why would they preach the Dao at the Heavenly Spirit Sect? Is there something extraordinary about that sect?"-Lu Zhen asked.

—"It's an academic lecture,"-the waiter gently corrected him, before eyeing him curiously.—"You haven't heard of the Heavenly Spirit Sect? That is the sect of the Tribulation Transcendence powerhouse, the Untiring Immortal Venerable!"

[Ah, the Untiring Immortal Venerable!] The very legend who had overturned Carefree Palace's Treasure Fair! To think he actually had a sect of his own!

[Presumably, a debate between two Mahayana powerhouses requires a Tribulation Transcendence titan to arbitrate!]

Lu Zhen felt he finally understood.

His desire to go burned even hotter. He pressed again:

—"May I ask where the Heavenly Spirit Sect is situated?"-Lu Zhen inquired.

—"Immortal elder, you wish to attend?"-the waiter asked instead.-"If you want to attend the lectures, you must first obtain an invitation letter."

—"Just tell me where the Heavenly Spirit Sect is,"-Lu Zhen demanded for the third time, already preparing to release his spiritual pressure to coerce the waiter directly.

He didn't believe for a second that he could ever get his hands on an invitation letter.

For high-level sermons delivered by titans, attendance was either purely down to luck—if an eccentric powerhouse suddenly had an epiphany and started raving to the empty wind, passersby were blessed; with a sufficiently profound master, even roadside rocks could awaken spiritual intelligence—or, if it was a pre-arranged formal lecture, the prime seats were inevitably reserved for direct disciples, scions of sacred bloodlines, and core disciples of colossal sects who represented the vanguard of the cultivation world. An adrift existence like Lu Zhen, who was virtually equivalent to a rogue cultivator, would be lucky just to catch a whiff of immortal mist lingering outside the gate.

Yet despite such inherent unfairness, Mid-Three Realms cultivators still chased after such events like moths to a flame. Even on the flimsiest phantom rumor, they would trek tens of thousands of miles without hesitation.

Word had it that even the Untiring Immortal Venerable had endured similar tribulations in his youth.

Perhaps because the Untiring Immortal Venerable had tasted those bitter waters himself, he had no intention of toying with cultivators through elusive gossip.

—"Immortal elder, you misunderstand! The Heavenly Spirit Sect is guarded by the formations of a Tribulation Transcendence titan; without an invitation letter, finding the sect grounds is completely impossible,"-the waiter explained hurriedly, noticing the stormy scowl gathering on Lu Zhen's brow.—"Fortunately, every cultivator across the entire Central Domain has a fair chance to vie for an invitation! That's why the whole immortal city is locked in fierce competition over them right now!"

—"How does one compete? A martial tournament?"-Lu Zhen asked.-"Where and when?"

He was already prepared!

Prepared to sprint to Carefree Pavilion for emergency shopping!

—Naturally! If it was a tournament, how could one step into the ring without stocking up on healing pills and offensive artifacts? Historically, no matter what competition was announced, Carefree Pavilion made a killing.

Yet this time was different.

—"No, there is no martial combat this time. The two esteemed titans wish to find cultivators capable of comprehending their doctrinal philosophies. Thus, the selection of invitation recipients is conducted through a tactical formation simulation known as 'Immortal Chess,'"-the waiter said.

—"Immortal Chess?"-Lu Zhen repeated.

Playing chess, was it? As one of the traditional Four Arts—the zither, chess, calligraphy, and painting—it was cultured, elegant, and deeply academic.

He listened as the waiter explained the rules of the board. Upon hearing that it was a strategic simulation of secret realm exploration, he felt even more convinced that this was truly worthy of an Upper-Three Realms titan's selection method.

—"...Our tavern provides complimentary chessboards for immortal elders to compete,"-the waiter concluded with a polite bow.-"We can also provide one free starter chess piece."

As he spoke, he glanced toward the "gaming zone."

Only then did Lu Zhen realize that the bustling crowd he had witnessed upon entering—whom he had dismissed as rowdy gamblers playing games—were actually all engaged in Immortal Chess!

Now, however, he found it completely understandable. Given that it concerned an academic lecture by revered titans, who wouldn't want a piece of the action?

Nevertheless, Lu Zhen considered himself far above the rabble. Maintaining the reserved dignity of a Nascent Soul cultivator, he nodded:

—"Very well. I shall play a round to see what it's about."-Lu Zhen said.

—"Then please choose your starting piece,"-the waiter offered.

—"Can I not take them all?"-Lu Zhen asked.

—"That will not do. After all, this touches upon the philosophical dispute between the two Mahayana titans,"-the waiter explained patiently.—"You may acquire pieces aligned with their respective doctrines by solving tactical puzzles in Immortal Chess. Of course, some patrons choose to draw pieces directly using spirit stones, but we strongly discourage such crude and impatient methods; that would be a desecration of true scholarship."

—"Indeed,"-Lu Zhen agreed.

Lu Zhen nodded indifferently, completely unaware of the psychological seed the waiter's parting words had just planted in his subconscious.

Lu Zhen merely pondered earnestly which faction to align with:

Manor Lord Qingyang of Eternal Villa was undoubtedly more famous, but the Fairy of the Sacred Mausoleum appeared to be a talisman cultivator, aligning directly with his own specialization. If his goal was to secure an invitation, would siding with the Fairy of the Sacred Mausoleum yield better odds?

The waiter gazed at him with infinite patience, looking upon him exactly as one would look upon a fat coin purse on the verge of spilling wide open.

* * *

—"As expected of Master! Immortal Chess has completely conquered the major immortal bazaars!"-Mo Xi reported, bounding up to Zhou Hui in pure delight.

—"Before this, whether I projected holographic illusions... er, opening cinematic animations, or hired [planted shills](<# "Literal CN: 托儿 (tuo'er) — shills or plants hired to drum up hype or manipulate crowds">) to demonstrate the gameplay, it only attracted low-tier cultivators. It failed to draw high-tier patrons, let alone generate serious buzz!"-Mo Xi exclaimed.

Zhou Hui remained unsurprised by this outcome:

—"When dealing with cultivators suffering from [mid-life career anxiety](<# "Literal CN: 中年焦虑症 (middle-aged anxiety syndrome) — psychological term for mid-career panic, comically applied to bottlenecked cultivators">), pure entertainment marketing only produces the opposite effect,"-Zhou Hui noted mildly.-"They care far more about whether what they are doing yields tangible, practical benefits."

It was much the same in his previous world.

Even when people loved playing video games, consoles had to be rebranded as ["learning machines"](<# "Literal CN: 学习机 (learning machine) — classic Chinese reference to devices like Subor (小霸王) marketed to parents as educational computers but used almost exclusively as NES gaming consoles">) before anyone would open their wallets.

At least the cultivators of this world were trying to fly with their own wings, rather than laying an egg and relentlessly nagging the chick to fly in their stead.

—"It's just too [involuted](<# "Literal CN: 卷 (juǎn) — short for 内卷 (involution), hyper-competitive rat race with diminishing returns">),"-Zhou Hui sighed softly.-"Pointless involution is merely a waste of precious life. Why must they be so anxious? They ought to learn to appreciate the life right before their eyes."

`...`

[Heh. Hearing those words come out of Master's mouth carries zero persuasive power whatsoever,] Mo Xi thought dryly to himself.

He couldn't comprehend many of the esoteric terms Master tossed around, but he understood the final sentence perfectly well—which only made it feel stranger:

No matter how he looked at it, using an academic symposium to bait people into playing video games, while the ultimate prize of competitive gaming was an invitation to an academic symposium... felt profoundly twisted in every possible way.

Yet a method that worked was a good method. What's more, this was Master's personal suggestion; Mo Xi would naturally never object.

Not only did he not object, but he took the initiative to conduct self-criticism:

—"It was my oversight... failing to consider the demands of the target demographic,"-Mo Xi reflected solemnly.-"Moving forward, I will follow this exact strategy and ramp up promotion regarding the 'academic functions' of Immortal Chess."

Besides, it wasn't as if they were lying. Hadn't this tactical formation simulation helped Second Senior Brother enhance his actual combat prowess?

Even if the rules had been tweaked to be slightly [pay-to-win](<# "Literal CN: 氪金 (kèjīn) — gacha/gaming slang for spending real money/spirit stones to roll for items">), that was merely recovering operating costs ahead of time to fund future updates!

So reasoned Mo "[Scumbag Game Planner](<# "Literal CN: 狗策划 (dog planner) — gaming slang for predatory game developers/designers who engineer exploitative monetization">)" Xi.

Having concluded his briefing, Mo Xi was just turning to leave when he heard Zhou Hui inquire:

—"Speaking of which, have you seen your Eldest Senior Brother's... little black lamb?"-Zhou Hui asked.

—"No,"-Mo Xi paused in his tracks. He didn't care for that black sheep in the slightest, but since Master had inquired, he offered helpful counsel nonetheless:—"Master, do you wish to find it? How about I go ask Eldest Senior Brother? He should know where his own pet has run off to."

The current "Yuwen Xuan" was merely a puppet crafted by the real Yuwen Xuan; it would be a miracle if it knew where his true body was.

Zhou Hui shook his head:

—"I haven't seen it these past two days, which is why I found it somewhat unusual,"-Zhou Hui said, waving his hand dismissively.-"If another ten days pass without sign of it, we can look into where it went."

—"Understood?"-Mo Xi nodded, thoroughly bewildered.

He found it utterly baffling why Master paid so much heed to a mere lamb.

* * *

—"Gaghh..."

The little black lamb made a dry-heaving motion, yet stubbornly forced the pastry crumbs surging up its throat back down into its belly.

Tears dripped silently from its eyes, *pitter-patter*. Mixing the dry pastry with salty tears, it forced itself to take bite after agonizing bite.

[So bloated! My stomach feels like it's about to burst!]

[Who knew that being full could be such an agonizing torture?!]

[Yet if I don't finish this cake, I won't be able to return to human form!]

[And without a human form, how am I supposed to discipline those lawless junior brothers and sisters who keep trying to steal Master's favor?!]

[Although I've already beaten them into line for one round and they should behave for now, judging by Master's habits, he's definitely picked up some new junior brother or sister from who-knows-where!]

[The very instant that new junior brother (or sister) shows their face, I must give them a crushing display of authority!]

Vowing thus in its heart, the little black lamb forced itself to chomp down another mouthful of pastry.

With this bite, however, the wretched sensation of bursting fullness vanished. Instead, its body felt feather-light; something clicked around its neck, its field of vision suddenly elevated, and the hollow hunger in its stomach returned once more.

The little black lamb... no, Yuwen Xuan, looked down at himself. He found that he had transformed back into his human form, with several crumbly fragments of the pastry resting on the ground.

—"Aaargh, what on earth have I been doing all this time?!"-he groaned, clutching his forehead.

While Yuwen Xuan had never claimed to possess much of a sense of shame, thinking about it from a human perspective—transforming into a lamb that knew only how to eat and sleep, clinging to Master's lap and whining for affection—was utterly enviable... wait, no! Utterly humiliating!

—"I'd better avoid thinking with a sheep's brain whenever possible,"-Yuwen Xuan muttered. After transforming into a sheep (read: Taotie), he had clearly sensed his intelligence regressing, becoming easily contented just like a little child.

—"Still, it's rare for Master to miscalculate. I turned back without even finishing the whole cake... hm?"-Yuwen Xuan mumbled, furrowing his brow.

He picked up the pastry crumbs and inspected them. He discovered that by chewing on the pastry intermittently, he had consumed roughly one-sixth of it.

Back in that subterranean ruin, Yuwen Xuan had personally witnessed Master using six skeletal remains to reconstruct six "Yuwen Xuans."

—"I see. So I've only temporarily reverted?"-Yuwen Xuan realized.

He simply didn't know when he would turn back into a sheep.

However, upon regaining human form, Yuwen Xuan found his enthusiasm for "giving the new junior brother (or sister) a crushing display of authority" cooling significantly. He suddenly remembered that Master seemed to have retrieved a divine soul remarkably similar to his own; the intoxicating aroma he had caught that day was undoubtedly connected to that soul.

However... devouring it would be extremely bad news.

Thinking with a human brain: although that thing smelled extraordinarily delicious, the entire process of finding it looked like a colossal trap no matter how one sliced it.

Master hadn't fed that half of the soul to him, which clearly meant Master believed he wasn't ready to consume it yet.

Yuwen Xuan had zero interest in tracking down the mastermind behind the trap, however.

Master was already investigating it anyway, and whatever Master investigated, Master would resolve.

What he needed to do was clear:

—"As expected, I must find other things to eat, even if it bloats me half to death..."-Yuwen Xuan murmured. Because only by devouring could he grow stronger.

He had to become stronger and stronger! That way, even if he stumbled into a similar trap again and fell for their scheme, he could simply devour the enemy whole instead of waiting around for Master to rescue him.

Yuwen Xuan harbored no grand illusions about being indispensable to Master; he only wished never to drag Master down. And if he could shield the little brats Master cared about, all the better.

—"Where am I going to find food, though?"-Yuwen Xuan muttered to himself.

Suddenly, his eyes snapped wide as he looked toward a certain direction beyond Mount Tianling.

A predatory grin spread across his face:

—"My luck is truly exceptional! They've arrived at the perfect time!"-Yuwen Xuan chuckled.

* * *

—"Achoo!"

Aboard an opulent cloud carriage gliding through the skies outside the Heavenly Spirit Sect, Su Xin sneezed loudly.

—"Little Grand-Martial Uncle, are you alright?"-several Soul Formation cultivators seated opposite Su Xin inquired nervously.

—"I'm fine. Someone must be thinking of me,"-Su Xin waved his hand dismissively, before composing his expression into stern solemnity and glaring at the cultivators.—"Where were we just now? ...Right. Regarding this 'Central Domain Representative Tournament,' our Heavenly Dao Alliance has come strictly as a member of the organizing committee. We must evaluate contestants with absolute fairness and impartiality—zero favoritism permitted. Understood?"

—"Of course we understand,"-the cultivators chorused.

Yet their tone sounded even more relaxed than when they had witnessed Su Xin sneezing moments ago.

After all, these were merely a bunch of Central Domain (read: countryside bumpkin) cultivators whose skills were largely indistinguishable. Even the strongest among them couldn't hold a candle to the Northern Domain's elite. The Heavenly Dao Alliance had only consented to preside over this tournament out of deference to the Plum-Bamboo Couple's close friendship with the Untiring Immortal Venerable. What was there to be nervous about?
