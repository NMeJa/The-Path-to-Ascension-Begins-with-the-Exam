# Chapter 239: The Petty Man Is Forever Beset with Anxiety

[← Previous Chapter](238_Chapter_238.md) | [Table of Contents](INDEX.md) | [Next Chapter →](240_Chapter_240.md)

Qu Qianxing's worries were, of course, far from groundless.

The "mastermind behind the scenes" was indeed observing the state of the battlefield, attempting to discover a means to destroy everything—except, at this very moment, he was in quite a sorry state.

—"How could it... deplete so much power?"-Tian Yan choked out with difficulty.

One hand propped against the desk while the other clutched his abdomen, crimson blood trickled from the corner of his lips. His condition appeared roughly on par with Qu Qianxing's—perhaps even slightly worse:

Qu Qianxing was fully conscious of the toll overdrawing his power took on his vessel, and was even prepared to endure it. Tian Yan, however, possessed no such mental preparedness. Ever since attaining his current post, across countless millennia of unmeasured time, he had never suffered a genuine injury, nor had he ever experienced "pain." To Tian Yan, the sensation of agony was practically an abstract, theoretical concept.

Thus, this esteemed and aristocratic Chief of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny never in his wildest dreams anticipated that he was merely, as was his customary habit, folding a sheet of paper—

Yes, folding a sheet of paper.

Overlapping the battlefields of two distinct spatial dimensions into a single coordinate—even if such an act, depending on the circumstances, might spell apocalyptic annihilation for the living beings in both realms—was, to Tian Yan, as effortless as folding a sheet of paper.

The only truly troublesome aspect was locating the coordinates of the two spaces to align them together. That was a rather tedious calculation process, requiring not merely spatial measurements but also mass calculations—far more bothersome than the mechanical act of "folding" itself, such that Tian Yan rarely employed this technique.

In fact, most of the time, even when he did deploy this spell, Tian Yan never bothered calculating too meticulously—a rough approximation was more than good enough. Although an excessively crude calculation meant that the overlapping of two spaces would yield many unforeseen and disastrous consequences, thereby squandering even more celestial power, since matters had already deteriorated to the point where such a bothersome art was required, Tian Yan didn't mind a bit of minor "waste."

Just as he had not tasted pain in ages, Tian Yan, residing high in the Celestial Realm for an eternity, had long forgotten what it felt like to be drained of celestial power.

Tian Yan had assumed this particular "paper fold" would be no different.

Weighing heavily on his mind was the "greatly ominous" result of his divination. He had discovered that the immortal officials under his command in the Ministry of Celestial Destiny had inexplicably been dragged into the "Ten-Thousand-Year-Old Langhuan Forbidden Grounds (?)." Furthermore, he found that the "old acquaintances" he despised had surfaced respectively on two battlefields where they had no business being, leading him to suspect that this was the herald of the ominous omen coming to fruition.

Yet [a noble gentleman does not stand beneath a collapsing wall](# "Literal CN: 君子不立于危墙之下 — ancient Chinese proverb from Mencius: a wise and prudent person does not place themselves in obvious danger"), and Tian Yan naturally had no intention of rashly setting foot on an unfamiliar battlefield.

What Tian Yan had always excelled at was setting up the stage in advance, then dragging everyone—friend and foe alike—onto his stage, reducing them to marionettes capable only of moving in accordance with his script.

This time, events had unfolded somewhat outside expectations, leaving Tian Yan without the leisure to construct a "stage." But that did not prevent him from blending the two known scenes together to manufacture a brand-new makeshift stage. In this manner, whatever was scheming behind the scenes would have its original script completely derailed. At least, judging others by himself, Tian Yan found having a pre-written script deviate annoying enough, let alone having the entire stage swapped out—under such conditions, no drama could possibly keep running.

In truth, Tian Yan had executed similar interventions on the Immortal-Demon frontlines countlessly during critical junctures; one could say he was thoroughly adept at it.

Thus, on this occasion as well, after merely a cursory pinch of his fingers to divine the trajectory, he first reached out to "seize" the spatial dimension of the Immortal-Demon battlefield.

Here, "seizing" was a metaphor.

A physical hand could not grasp space. The spell Tian Yan employed involved blanketing a spatial domain with celestial power and hauling it along.

This spatial displacement was completely invisible to human eyesight; those caught within could only perceive a sudden shift in their orientation, making it easily confused with instant teleportation. Yet in the eyes of certain celestial and primordial ferocious beasts gifted with multidimensional visual organs—such as dragons and Taotie—one could see Tian Yan's celestial power stretching and sprawling across higher dimensions like monstrous tentacles, violently coiling around unanchored space, ripping it away along with all living creatures inside, and hurling them like garbage into an ill-suited adjacent dimension, bringing catastrophic ruin to both.

Only, this time, the moment Tian Yan's celestial "tentacles" coiled around the frontlines of the Immortal-Demon battlefield, he felt an extraordinary "weight" wrench back against him, nearly shaking his entire reserve of celestial power to the foundation.

Worst of all, Tian Yan felt pain.

He felt as though it was not he who had "seized" space, but space that had "seized" him. A colossal "weight" hauled his celestial tentacles plunging in one direction, dragging his physical flesh down with it, such that all his internal organs were violently twisted and wrenched, throbbing in dull agony.

Tian Yan was thoroughly alarmed. Having zero tolerance for pain, he reflexively moved to let go his "hand."

This was undeniably a catastrophic mistake.

The spatial "weight" was already abnormal, forcing him to expend celestial power far beyond his calculations. Abruptly terminating the spell halfway would trigger an immense spiritual backlash, squandering even more celestial power and severely overdrawing his vessel in the form of doubled agony. Yet paralyzed by this multiplied pain, Tian Yan halted his cancellation, causing his power to bleed away in vain while the technique ground forward anyway. This push-and-pull siphoned away even more energy, until Tian Yan felt as though his internal organs had been tossed into a washing machine—though he had no earthly idea what a washing machine was—churned, wrenched, and squeezed to the brink of rupture, causing him to spray out a mouthful of blood.

Thanks to his quasi-divine constitution, Tian Yan would not perish from such a "minor misstep," nor would he even suffer critical injury; his internal organs could regenerate in an instant. Yet the lingering agony still made him [hesitate to strike the rat for fear of smashing the vase](# "Literal CN: 投鼠忌器 (throw at the rat, fear the vase) — refraining from action out of fear of unintended collateral damage"). Even now, with the spell completed, he dared not rashly retract all his celestial power, terrified of agitating his organs anew.

Naturally, even without the pain, he rarely bothered to scrub away the traces of his spellcraft. Lord Tian Yan of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny had never once worried that anyone could trace his power back to its origin to mount a counterattack against him.

It was the agony alone that proved insufferable to him.

Worst of all, this suffering did not stem from physical flesh, but from the depletion of his celestial power. Even if his physical body was repaired, the pain would not dissipate so quickly; Tian Yan could only endure the agony.

This proved immensely difficult to bear, to the point where, for a fleeting moment, he even began to regret his actions.

Right as Tian Yan was swimming in regret, of all times, his jade pendant emitted a crisp chime.

As everyone well knew, across both the cultivation world and the Celestial Realm, ornaments such as jade pendants were never mere accessories; they were magical artifacts. Tian Yan's jade pendant was no exception. Beyond functioning as a defensive treasure, it also possessed communication capabilities. Earlier, the fact that Qu Qianxing had materialized on the frontlines of the Immortal-Demon battlefield had been relayed to him by his informant stationed there.

—Tian Yan's informants were everywhere, not merely within the Heavenly Court, but scattered across all three thousand minor worlds.

These informants took many forms: strategists officially and legitimately dispatched; immortal officials who had incurred debts of gratitude to the Ministry of Celestial Destiny by commissioning custom tribulation scripts for their mortal descents; chess pieces recruited through participation in the Ministry's scripts; and even clones forged from fragments of Tian Yan's own celestial power.

This was not simply because Tian Yan possessed an excessive desire for control—though his need for control was indeed formidable—but rather because he had lived for far too long, and the Ministry of Celestial Destiny had meddled in far too many affairs, leaving behind far too many discarded fragments.

These informants were so numerous and tangled that Tian Yan couldn't even be bothered to remember some of them.

For instance, even in what was once the Langhuan Forbidden Grounds—which had severed all ties with the Celestial Realm and into which immortals could not easily step—Tian Yan had left behind a strand of Celestial Spider Silk infused with the aura of Hundun. That object was exceedingly rare, for a primordial beast like Hundun was far more perilous than even a Taotie.

After all, no matter how ferocious a Taotie was, it still possessed a physical form—[so long as it had a health bar, it could be killed](# "Gamified metaphor: 只要有血条就可以杀"). But whether a creature like Hundun even qualified as a "beast" was hard to say. Its external appearance seemed to be a manifested illusion; its true form was akin to a formless, void gas. Let alone mortals, even True Immortals could not perceive it, much less slay it. Whatever it wished to devour, it swallowed in utter silence, until it had consumed everything in existence, bursting apart to birth a brand-new minor world. Hence, items tainted by the power of Hundun would inherit certain of its attributes—such as supreme concealment, imperceptibility, assimilation, and even spatial manipulation. Yet Hundun itself was almost impossible to find, and items imbued with its power were rarer still.

Tian Yan had obtained that strand of Celestial Spider Silk only through a stroke of serendipity, originally intending to refine it into a robe.

A Heavenly Robe capable of rendering even a True Immortal completely formless and undetectable while conferring spatial abilities. Such a garment would be ideal for concealing him completely behind the curtains, allowing him to step in and out of various stages and manipulate the progression of scripts at will.

Regrettably, before he could find the time to refine that Heavenly Robe, the self-sealing of Langhuan occurred.

Although the decision to abandon Langhuan had been made by Tian Yan himself, he had never anticipated the people there would be so resolute... No, in truth, he had calculated it; he simply hadn't cared. At the end of the day, they were merely fragile creatures already earmarked to be fed to the demons. What mattered were certain unique treasures within Langhuan that had not yet been transferred out in time—including that land itself, which had originally belonged to the Heavenly Court and was exceedingly unique. None of these were things Tian Yan was willing to surrender.

Thus, during the great cataclysm before Langhuan was sealed, Tian Yan had silently tethered that strand of Celestial Spider Silk infused with Hundun qi to Langhuan. He had even bestowed a sliver of opportunity upon the silk to awaken its sentience so it could gather intelligence—as for whether a spider silk tainted with primordial beast aura gaining sentience might cause devastation for the local inhabitants, that too fell completely outside the scope of Tian Yan's concerns.

Ah, but the subject had wandered too far.

At this moment, all Tian Yan cared about was his own agonizing pain. Let alone a spider silk tossed into Langhuan tens of thousands of years ago (by Langhuan time), even the jade pendant chiming incessantly right before his eyes was something he had no desire to heed.

Yet even as Tian Yan ignored it, the chiming of the jade pendant refused to cease.

Not only did it not stop, it chimed with increasing cheerfulness. The jade pendant clattered against other decorative jewels, producing a crisp, melodic cadence that gave off the imposing momentum of an orchestra preparing to perform an entire Celestial Music Grand Ceremony from start to finish.

Yet when one was in excruciating pain, even the most exquisite immortal melody sounded like grating noise—and gods were no exception to this rule.

Tian Yan raised his hand. A flash of white light streaked through the air, and that jade pendant—which had accompanied him for tens of thousands of years (in Celestial Realm time)—disintegrated into fine powder.

Tian Yan attained a fleeting moment of peace.

Yet before he could even inspect his own condition, that pile of jade powder unexpectedly began chiming once more! Just as when it had been whole, it continued producing the crisp clatter of colliding jade, only the melody played had warped entirely out of tune, grotesque and distorted, grating terribly on the ears.

Fortunately, this musical performance did not last long. Giving Tian Yan no opportunity to refuse, someone forcibly forced the connection open.

And the only figures capable of forcibly initiating communication against Tian Yan's will were a select few high-ranking immortal officials of special standing within the Heavenly Court. The supreme commander of the Immortal-Demon frontlines was evidently among them.

Sure enough, a certain voice that never bothered to read the room barked out from amidst the jade dust:

[—"Tian Yan, stop playing dead! Are you pulling some kind of stunt again?! A brat who looks just like you suddenly popped up on the demon army's side!"](# "Speaker: Tian Shou")

It was the Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King, Tian Shou.

This current Heavenly General was obstinate to a fault and loved nothing more than picking fights with Tian Yan; ignoring him would likely only result in endless harassment.

In ordinary times, Tian Yan wouldn't have minded, but being in a foul mood, his tone was naturally rather venomous:

—"You only noticed just now?"-Tian Yan sneered.

His own "informant" had noticed the instant the person arrived on the battlefield.

If anything, it was strange that Tian Shou had only noticed now.

Such a stunningly gorgeous face.

Back then, in order to manufacture such a suitable vessel, Tian Yan had racked his brains and scoured across diverse heavens and realms to gather peerless beauties of every kind. Yet in the end, the physical body produced still failed to acquire the desired "divine authority," leaving him no choice but to begrudgingly make do.

Yet [man proposes, but Heaven disposes](# "Literal CN: 人算不如天算 — human calculations fall short of Heaven's calculations"). The bastard child unexpectedly borne by this body's younger sister had not only inherited what he desired right from the start, but had refused to obediently submit to his bidding. What was more, according to the prognostications of "Celestial Destiny," the boy exhibited inclinations toward demonhood, forcing Tian Yan to cast him down into Langhuan to make the bastard learn [how to be a proper human](# "Literal CN: 做人 (zuò rén) — conduct oneself / behave decently").

Naturally, the mandate of Heaven could not be defied; Tian Yan had merely been doing what humanly possible. It seemed the fellow had indeed fallen into demonhood after all.

Tian Yan had never expected the boy to reverse destiny in the first place; his sole hope was that the "unique function" of the land of Langhuan—even just a fragmentary sliver of it—might record that "divine authority." Yet right now, the situation in Langhuan was exceedingly bizarre as well...

Tian Yan pondered with a darkened countenance, feeling all the more uncomfortable.

On the other end, Tian Shou was taken aback by Tian Yan's cynical, sarcastic tone.

This Lord of Celestial Destiny was customarily hypocritical and sanctimonious to an extreme. Across thousands of years of working together (in Celestial Realm time), even a disaster like the failure of the Sky-Tour Ceremony hadn't caused his expression to waver, let alone speak in such an acerbic tone.

Still, this wasn't any grand issue. Once Tian Shou's brain processed what Tian Yan had said, he flared up in fury:

—"So it really is related to you! What kind of [dog-blood melodrama](# "Slang: 狗血 (dog blood) — cliché, melodramatic, trashy tropes") script are you scheming now?! Didn't I tell you to at least give me a heads-up in advance?!"-Tian Shou roared.

—"This isn't a script..."-Tian Yan attempted to defend himself with obvious impatience.

Tian Shou was even more impatient than he was:

—"If it's not a script, then it's your clone! Don't tell me such an outrageous demon just sprouted out of thin air on his own?!"-Tian Shou barked.

[It isn't...]

Tian Yan had initially intended to rebut, but catching Tian Shou's tone, he abruptly realized something:

—"You suffered at his hands?"-Tian Yan asked.

As Tian Yan asked this, the image of this Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King getting battered by the "nameless Demon Sovereign's" lightning had already flashed before his mind's eye.

Witnessing this, Tian Yan felt no gloating satisfaction; instead, his brows furrowed tightly.

This was not born of fraternal affection for a colleague, but because this fell into the realm of "uncalculated variables."

Tian Yan was "all-knowing." Theoretically, such an outcome should have been "foreseen" the moment he stitched the two worlds together. Yet even during his rough divination, he had completely failed to calculate this outcome.

What was more, that "bastard" shouldn't possess this kind of power!

He had explicitly suppressed the boy's power with the fate star of "Wenqu"!

Even if the boy sought to break free from that fate's suppression, he should have suffered catastrophic trauma from soul to cultivation base!

Tian Yan thought in utter disarray.

To the "all-knowing" Tian Yan, the fear of the unknown was far more terrifying than the fear of physical pain. For the time being, he had completely forgotten the agony born of overdrawing his power.

Tian Shou, however, had no inkling of Tian Yan's terror—the Lord of Celestial Destiny rarely even lost his composure, so who could have imagined him falling prey to fear so easily? Tian Shou simply assumed Tian Yan was mocking him.

He barked in annoyance:

—"Suffered? *Pfft!* I could crush your true form with ease—how could I possibly be helpless against a mere clone?!"-Tian Shou sneered, seemingly convinced that the "Demon Sovereign" was Tian Yan's clone.

Tian Yan did not correct him.

He had no desire for his private affairs to be known by others, least of all a blabbermouth with no filter like Tian Shou.

Fortunately, though this Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King never knew when to keep his mouth shut, through bizarre twists of fate he always managed to miss critical intelligence. For instance, when Tian Yan had previously installed "Wenqu" within the Ministry of Celestial Destiny and even proclaimed him his heir, Tian Shou had been stationed with his strategist on another Immortal-Demon battlefield, conveniently missing those few Heavenly Court court assemblies. Consequently, he had never laid eyes on Qu Qianxing in person—otherwise, with Qu Qianxing's looks, anyone who had seen him could never fail to recognize him.

Oblivious to Tian Yan's inner thoughts, Tian Shou continued ranting to himself:

—"I'm just worried about your damn script, that's all! If I casually slaughtered your clone and ruined your script, you'd start whining again... Wait, no! In this situation, it's your trash script that ruined my battlefield! You must take full responsibility! Go clean up your own damn mess!"-Tian Shou bellowed.

—"You intend to hand the battlefield over to me?"-Tian Yan frowned.

—"That's your responsibility! Of course you have to take charge!"-Tian Shou insisted.

Tian Yan's furrow deepened.

Tian Yan wondered whether the other party had even noticed:

This mighty Lord Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King was dodging battle! He was terrified! He was attempting to pawn his responsibility off onto someone else!

Of course, [passing the buck](# "Literal CN: 甩锅 (shuaiguo) — passing the blame / buck-passing") was hardly novel in the Heavenly Court.

Every bloated and redundant bureaucracy was fundamentally the same. Nobody wanted to shoulder responsibility; everyone scrambled to shift the blame onto someone else. The Heavenly Court was no exception—in fact, because it had existed for too long and the lifespans of its "employees" were far too vast, its internal pathologies were all the more severe.

Especially the military officials: having secured their posts through nepotism without needing to work hard, and having gained immense power effortlessly simply by becoming immortal officials—or at worst, begging the Ministry of Celestial Destiny for a script to descend to the mortal realms with an overpowered [Long Aotian](# "Trope: 龙傲天 (Long Aotian) — cliché invincible, arrogant power-fantasy protagonist") script to hoover up the spiritual energy of an entire world—their superficial combat power was maxed out. Yet at the same time, they were nurtured into arrogant, tyrannical bullies who fancied themselves peerless under the heavens, completely oblivious to how the word "responsibility" was spelled.

Yet Tian Yan had always indulged these foolish and haughty military officers.

Because their egocentricity and dictatorial nature made them unwilling to surrender even a shred of their "power" to others, their movements on the stage remained remarkably "controllable," preventing them from doing anything outside his calculations.

Take Tian Shou, for instance: he constantly sneered at Tian Yan and refused to heed the directives of the strategist dispatched by the Ministry of Celestial Destiny precisely because he guarded against Tian Yan encroaching upon his military authority. In normal times, how could he ever surrender his battlefield?

Did getting struck by lightning scare him so badly he dared not fight?

Yet recalling the sheer ordeal of moving and overlapping spatial domains just now, and feeling the lingering agony across his own vessel, Tian Yan found it not entirely incomprehensible. When it came to having zero "pain tolerance," the pampered True Immortals who had lived in luxury for millennia were all fundamentally the same.

Tian Yan did not let his emotions show, merely letting out a light chuckle:

—"Fine, I shall take responsibility,"-Tian Yan said with a light chuckle.-"Hand over the Tiger Tally."

[—"The Tiger Tally?!"](# "Speaker: Tian Shou")

—"Since you want me to take charge of the crisis on the battlefield, you can hardly expect me to do so without command of the army, can you? After all, I am merely a civil official,"-Tian Yan said breezily.

Fortunately, because Tian Yan had shattered the jade pendant earlier, Tian Shou's forced connection was strictly "voice call" without "video chat." Unable to see Tian Yan's face, Tian Shou naturally could not witness his current disheveled state; judging by tone alone, he sounded relaxed and even rather eager.

Hearing that "eager" cadence, Tian Shou hesitated.

This Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King already felt that the Ministry of Celestial Destiny wielded far too much power, suspecting that the strategist sent over was scheming to control him and usurp his military authority, which was why he had always looked upon Tian Yan with hostility. Tian Yan's words now only amplified that suspicion; he couldn't help wondering whether the current crisis was a trap deliberately orchestrated by Tian Yan to seize his military tally.

With suspicion taking root, Tian Shou naturally refused to let Tian Yan "have his way."

—"No,"-Tian Shou rejected bluntly.-"Even so, is the Tiger Tally something that can be handed over on a whim?!"

Tian Shou paused, then unreasonably added:

—"Not just the Tiger Tally—you are forbidden from touching my army as well! War is no child's play; how could it be given over just because you ask for it?!"

Hearing this, Tian Yan actually breathed an internal sigh of relief.

It seemed this Lord Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King had developed cold feet, but had not yet lost his mind entirely. Likely, just like himself, he had merely suffered a "minor setback."

As Tian Yan thought this, he felt his internal organs throbbing in dull agony once more.

In truth, he was barely different from Tian Shou. Even if the other party had surrendered the Tiger Tally—or rather, if Tian Shou had actually handed it over, it would only prove matters were catastrophic beyond belief—Tian Yan would never risk stepping onto the battlefield in person.

Tian Yan sneered internally, yet continued to put on an affected front aloud:

—"This won't do, that won't do—just what do you want me to do?"-Tian Yan asked.

Tian Shou bluffed magnanimously, unaware that he was playing right into Tian Yan's desires by reclaiming full authority to court death himself:

—"I can overlook whatever script you were tinkering with before, and I can even overlook the battlefield turning into this bizarre mess with the enemy army suddenly doubling in size—I'll treat it as cleaning up your mess,"-Tian Shou demanded.-"Just take your 'clone' back!"

Tian Yan still didn't bother clarifying that Qu Qianxing was not his clone, speaking with feigned reluctance:

—"That's not so simple,"-Tian Yan said with a troubled sigh.-"Whatever else he may be, the other party is still a Demon Sovereign. It's hardly a matter of me saying 'come back' and having him obey. One must employ special measures to achieve it."

—"What measures?"-Tian Shou asked warily.-"In any case, the Tiger Tally is absolutely out of the question!"

—"But there's no way to make direct contact with him... Fine! If you insist. However, without the authority granted by you, I cannot personally venture to that battlefield,"-Tian Yan's tone sounded all the more conflicted.-"Fortunately, I possess a few methods to control him. You need only follow my instructions..."

—"You want me to act as your pawn? Not a chance..."-Tian Shou said sharply.

—"Then forget it,"-Tian Yan said coolly.

Tian Yan's tone chilled instantly. Deliberately offering no further explanation, he blew upon the fragments of the jade pendant, letting Tian Shou's subsequent words scatter amidst the swirling jade dust.

Yet he still did not sweep the jade dust away.

This tactic of [playing hard to get](# "Literal CN: 欲擒故纵 (yuqin guzhong) — letting someone off the hook to catch them more firmly later") was wildly successful. Before the jade dust could even settle, the voice of the Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King rang out again, only with an even more distorted timbre:

—"Don't run off! I didn't say I wouldn't do it! Just what do I have to do?!"-Tian Shou barked, his shouting laced with exasperated desperation.

Tian Yan felt no smugness at having manipulated the Heaven-Protecting Heavenly King. He stated coolly:

—"No rush. That 'clone' bears a mark I left behind; dealing with him is not particularly difficult. I shall make some preparations, and then, as long as you utilize the Human-Faced Sore I left upon your body, you can act in my stead..."

[—"Wait, what Human-Faced Sore?!"](# "Speaker: Tian Shou")

—"A Human-Faced Sore is a second face molded from your own soul, growing on the back of your head beneath your helmet and hair,"-Tian Yan explained calmly.-"Fearing issues might arise on the battlefield, I requested her to report intelligence the strategist failed to notice..."

Earlier, upon encountering Qu Qianxing, it was that very Human-Faced Sore that had reported to Tian Yan in the first instance.

Although that could technically be considered Tian Shou, Tian Yan found it far more reliable than the real Tian Shou. Through it, much intelligence unbeknownst to Tian Shou's true self had been revealed—such as Qu Qianxing's existence.

Unfortunately, the party involved felt zero gratitude. Instantly, Tian Shou's horrified roar nearly blasted the remaining jade dust into the stratosphere:

—"Tian Yan, you perverted control freak! I'll fuck your—"-Tian Shou roared in absolute horror.

Regrettably, such vulgar curses failed to faze Tian Yan in the slightest.

—If he cared about such things, he wouldn't have grown a Human-Faced Sore on a colleague's skull to spy on him in the first place.

Paying no heed to Tian Shou's reaction, Tian Yan commenced his "preparations."

Indeed, at least regarding the issue of the "Demon Sovereign," Tian Yan had not lied: he truly held the mark to control the other party. Nor was it exclusive to the demoted Wenqu star; all other sinners cast down to the mortal realm bore similar marks. Whether they were doomed to eternal damnation, possessed a chance to atone and return to the Heavenly Court, or were simply descending to gild their resumes with a bit of mortal tenure, all of them left marks in the hands of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny.

Furthermore, Tian Yan "happened" to be stationed upon the sacrificial altar where the Sky-Tour Ceremony was being held. Naturally, there was no shortage of materials—and top-grade materials at that. Thus, the "preparations" were exceedingly simple.

In mere moments, the altar—which had still been bathed in daylight—appeared as though night had fallen. Yet it was not dark; instead, tens of millions of stars coalesced into a glittering, boundless river of astral light, breathtakingly intoxicating in its beauty.

Yet Tian Yan was in no mood to appreciate the scenery. He merely reached out to grasp the starlight nearest to him, murmuring with icy finality:

—"In the end, no matter how much you struggle, you are all still within my grasp—"

***

[—"Found it! I found the anomalous data!"](# "Speaker: Ran Deng")

On the other side, Ran Deng's brisk, lively voice rang out inside Qu Qianxing's mind once more.

Qu Qianxing paused in surprise:

—"So fast?"

—"Rather than fast, it's more like the other guy didn't bother hiding at all, and is even continuing to channel his power, making him even easier to trace... *Tsk*, it couldn't be a trap, could it?"-Ran Deng fretted.

—"Even if it is a trap, it matters not. It suits me fine to [beat him at his own game](# "Literal CN: 将计就计 (jiangji jiuji) — turning the enemy's scheme to one's own advantage"),"-Qu Qianxing sneered coldly.
