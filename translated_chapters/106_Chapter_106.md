# Chapter 106: [Changing the Future](<# "Literal CN: 改变未来 (gǎibiàn wèilái) — Changing the Future; chapter title">) (第106章 改变未来)

Regardless of how mortified the reserve sword cultivators felt, Mo Bei had no intention of sending the female cultivators who had tagged along to hack sugarcane.

After all, while harvesting sugarcane might be pure manual labor for ordinary farmers, for the sword cultivators of the Heavenly Spirit Sect, it was a precise [technical craft](<# "Literal CN: 技术活 (jìshù huó) — technical work / skilled craft">). At what exact position to strike, how much length to leave behind, what geometric shape the cut should form, and what chemical treatment to administer immediately afterward—all of these had been personally directed by Zhou Hui. Lu Shulong had provided technical support for precipitation and medicinal formulas, and the sword cultivators had run countless rounds of empirical trials in the experimental fields to ultimately calibrate the standardized benchmark values.

No matter how the Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples brought by Mo Bei ended up scoring—even if someone bombed with a flat zero—they had still studied solidly and diligently for a year and a half. One didn't need to worry about them making any outrageously catastrophic blunders.

The female cultivators from Fengqing City, however, were an entirely different story. While they had all learned a bit of swordplay to varying degrees, they possessed zero practical experience in agronomy. If thrown into the fields to harvest sugarcane out of the blue, they would likely ruin far more stalks than they harvested.

Even Mo Bei was not so rigid as to mechanically copy the exam parameters.

She knew full well that in Master's eyes, the agricultural harvest itself was infinitely more important than a single exam score.

Thus, Mo Bei inquired about the core cultivation disciplines of these female cultivators, pondered the comprehensive exam questions for the practical curriculum, and announced:

—"Let them help boil sugar instead,"-Mo Bei decided.

—"Understood. I will make arrangements right away,"-replied [Qiao Shiyi'niang](<# "Literal CN: 乔十一娘 (Qiáo Shíyīniáng) — Eleventh Daughter Qiao / Eleventh Lady Qiao; village naming custom based on birth order">), the village girl responsible for hosting Mo Bei and a student of the [Second Southeast Academy of White Jade Capital](<# "Literal CN: 白玉京东南第二学堂 (Báiyùjīng Dōngnán Dì-èr Xuétáng) — Second Southeast Academy of White Jade Capital">).

This was not the first time Shiyi'niang had assisted with a Heavenly Spirit Sect "exam."

Long before this, Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples had assisted with sowing and fertilizing the sugarcane, so Qiao Family Village had already developed a well-rehearsed protocol for accommodating sect examinations.

However, Mo Bei added a rather peculiar condition:

—"...Wait a moment. They are not disciples of our Heavenly Spirit Sect, but customers of Yi Bei Tea. So... treat this as an exam conducted like hosting guests."

Having said this, even Mo Bei felt it sounded a bit strange.

She suspected that had she made such a request in front of Mo Xi, that idiot younger brother would have relentlessly roasted her.

Fortunately, standing before her was a bright, endearing girl. Hearing this, Qiao Shiyi'niang blinked in surprise, then broke into a knowing smile.

—"I understand, Teacher Mo Bei,"-she said.-"I know what to do."

[...She understood just like that?]
Mo Bei wondered, bewildered.

She didn't even fully understand it herself.

Still, since the other party "understood," Mo Bei instantly reverted to her default state of being too lazy to use her brain, giving a profound, inscrutable nod.

Having received her instructions, Qiao Shiyi'niang dashed off toward the village storehouse.

*

[—"Prepare a set of sugar-boiling implements... Not our everyday ones! Those are far too grimy."](<# "Speaker: Qiao Shiyi'niang">)

[—"We need something prettier."](<# "Speaker: Qiao Shiyi'niang">)

[—"Forget it, don't use the iron cauldron; none of them look presentable... How about a vat? Last time Uncle Village Chief went to the Immortals' Bazaar, didn't he buy that transparent [glass cylinder](<# "Literal CN: 琉璃缸 (liúli gāng) — colored glaze / glass vat or cylinder">)? Bring that one out."](<# "Speaker: Qiao Shiyi'niang">)

[—"Glass isn't heat-resistant enough, but we can reinforce it with talismans. I ran an experiment last time; sticking a [Fire-Averting Talisman](<# "Literal CN: 避火符 (bìhuǒ fú) — Fire-Averting Talisman; talisman that insulates or redirects excessive heat">) onto it lets it withstand open flame."](<# "Speaker: Qiao Shiyi'niang">)

[—"Oh, and carry the parasol over as well... Ah, not the one tied with red silk ribbons! The thatched reed one will do nicely."](<# "Speaker: Qiao Shiyi'niang">)

Inside the storehouse, Qiao Shiyi'niang had everyone running around in circles.

Previously in Qiao Family Village, only Village Chief Qiao Linggen held such authority. To the villagers, Qiao Shiyi'niang had merely been "a very capable girl." Yet after attending the academy, earning praise from the immortals, and even inventing a mechanical [sugar press](<# "Literal CN: 榨糖机 (zhàtáng jī) — sugarcane juicer / mechanical press">) with her own hands, she had attained an authority second only to Qiao Linggen in certain matters. In fact, some villagers had begun whispering that marrying her off to another village would be a tremendous waste.

Qiao Shiyi'niang was conscious of this shift, yet she did not fully grasp what consequences it would bring to her life.

[The more useful and valuable I am, the better!]
The young girl thought with simple, unadorned resolve. Today, as always, she poured every ounce of effort into proving her worth.

—"Bring out that table and chair set we took to the Immortals' Bazaar as well! Oh, and don't forget the sugar press,"-she directed.

—"Shiyi'niang, is all this really necessary?"-someone asked in bewilderment.-"The last few times the immortals came to help, they never asked for such fancy utensils!"

—"This time is different,"-she replied.-"Some of the people here today aren't from the Heavenly Spirit Sect, but from Fengqing City."

[—"Fengqing City?!"](<# "Speaker: Qiao Family Villagers">)-The villagers instantly blanched.

They harbored zero goodwill toward Fengqing City. After all, the cultivation clans that had incessantly harassed and extorted their village all hailed from Fengqing City.

—"Don't make that face,"-Qiao Shiyi'niang said with shrewd calculation.-"Even though they come from Fengqing City, they aren't like those aristocratic cultivation clans. Lately, the Heavenly Spirit Sect has been hosting the Immortal Arts Assembly to expand its influence, and these fairy maidens can likely be won over as allies. Besides, we are Fengqing locals too. We can't stay cooped up in this village forever, relying solely on the Immortals' Bazaar to survive, can we? If we can assist the Immortal Venerable while building good ties with local cultivators outside the aristocratic clans, what could possibly be bad about that?"

Enlightenment dawned on the villagers.

—"Then what about the exam apparatus...?"-someone asked.

—"Follow the standard metrics strictly. That cannot be altered,"-Qiao Shiyi'niang stated firmly.

*

While the villagers bustled about in a whirlwind, the sword cultivators' exam had already commenced.

Yet this examination did not turn out nearly as "humiliating" as the reserve sword cultivators had anticipated.

True, while Senior Sister Mo Bei could slice humans with lethal flair, their having to hack sugarcane looked somewhat foolish, and the female cultivators had indeed worn amused grins upon hearing the exam topic. But once the blades began swinging, they discovered that harvesting sugarcane was... actually pretty cool?

Chiefly, it was because the spectating female cultivators erupted into gasps of astonished admiration:

[—"Whoa, that's amazing!"](<# "Speaker: Fan Club Cultivator">)

[—"He sliced clean through so effortlessly!"](<# "Speaker: Fan Club Cultivator">)

[—"Look how neat that cut is—every single cross-section is identical!"](<# "Speaker: Fan Club Cultivator">)

[—"Do sword cultivators even have standardized measurements for mowing crops?"](<# "Speaker: Fan Club Cultivator">)

[—"As expected of the Heavenly Spirit Sect's sword cultivators! They are completely different from those other so-called sword cultivators."](<# "Speaker: Fan Club Cultivator">)

Such praise cascaded continuously. As expected of a professional cheer squad, their flattering compliments had several reserve sword cultivators floating on cloud nine.

[Turns out this is actually pretty cool!]
A candidate currently taking the test thought to himself.

Spurred on by the cheer squad's shrieks, he couldn't resist flourishing his blade with a fancy sword-flower. Stalks of sugarcane fell in a crisp *swish-swish-swish*, and he felt as though he had captured the glorious aura of Senior Sister Mo Bei harvesting enemy heads just days prior.

Right then, Mo Bei's chilling, sinister voice whispered directly beside his ear:

—"The height variance on these thirty stalks exceeds thirty percent. Minus three points,"-she noted coldly.

The disciple's skin tightened instantly. Tuning out all external distractions, he no longer dared to show off, methodically maneuvering the flexible sword in his hand.

Truth be told, watching sugarcane collapse swath after swath to reveal flawlessly uniform cross-sections induced a bizarrely [soothing sense of satisfaction](<# "Literal CN: 舒适了 (shūshì le) — 'soothed / feels so comfortable'; modern internet slang for the deep satisfaction of seeing perfectly aligned, symmetrical, or organized objects">). Blended with the joy of an abundant harvest, it doubled the sheer pleasure.

Thus, the admiration of the Fengqing City female cultivators was genuine.

They were convinced this was some specialized, secret cultivation regime unique to Heavenly Spirit Sect sword practitioners.

Especially when one of them tried swinging her own blade at a harvested stalk, only to produce a jagged, crooked cut of inaccurate length, she felt profoundly ashamed.

[Little Sister Mo Bei must have trained this way too, right?]

[She truly isn't bloodthirsty at all! She probably doesn't even kill beasts or animals in daily life, choosing instead to hone her sword arts on plants!]

The female cultivators were instantly moved to tears by their own wild rationalizations.

They cast frequent, reverent glances toward Mo Bei. While their imaginative leaps weren't entirely wrong, Mo Bei certainly hadn't chosen this method out of subjective pacifism—it was purely Master's pedagogical curriculum. Had Master decided that slaughtering people granted more experience points, Mo Bei would have hacked humans without batting an eye.

Yet there was also someone—or rather, a certain "being"—whose admiration was directed at an entirely different facet.

—"Humans really are extraordinarily gifted at agriculture!"-Shui Huang'er muttered in awe.

Discarding all pretense of graceful maidenhood, she took a freshly severed stalk of sugarcane and bit straight into it. Sweet, spiritual-energy-infused juice instantly flooded her palate.

Shui Huang'er released a contented sigh.

Unlike human cultivators, the concept of [inedia](<# "Literal CN: 辟谷 (bìgǔ) — grain avoidance / fasting from mortal food to sustain oneself on spiritual qi">) virtually did not exist among the demon race.

Even the phoenix lineage, which according to legend ['eats only the fruit of the bamboo tree and drinks only from sweet springs,'](<# "Literal CN: 非练实不食，非醴泉不饮 — classic allusion from Zhuangzi describing the noble, incorruptible finickiness of the phoenix">) was merely being exceptionally picky about its diet.

Demons inherently required food.

However, owing to their complex physiology and differing racial habits, very few demon clans possessed any talent for agriculture. More importantly, even auspicious clans associated with bountiful harvests—such as the [Dangkang](<# "Literal CN: 当康 (Dāngkāng) — mythical boar-like beast from the Classic of Mountains and Seas whose cry heralds a bumper harvest">) or the [Wenyao fish](<# "Literal CN: 文瑶鱼 (Wényáoyú) — mythical flying fish with bird wings that brings world-wide agricultural abundance">)—merely exerted a 'passive' influence, making the weather more hospitable for crops or boosting pollination rates. The crops themselves remained unchanged, incapable of surpassing their biological boundaries.

Humans were different.

They could invent new farming implements, breed improved crop strains, clear wastelands, transform barren soil into fertile fields, and produce staggering volumes of grain to satisfy their own needs as well as those of omnivorous demon clans.

Frankly speaking, from the perspective of the demon race, humans dedicating themselves to immortal cultivation was an absolute waste.

Humans who cultivated immortality no longer farmed. Not only did they abandon agriculture, they seized land, planted medicinal herbs for pill alchemy, and fought the demon race over wild spiritual flora—a colossal headache in every sense.

Most disastrously, this behavior actively robbed certain demon clans of any chance to 'turn benevolent.'

Of course, for demons, this was never a moral dilemma of good versus evil; it was purely a matter of survival.

After all, among demons, clans that strictly required humans as their staple diet were exceedingly rare—meat was meat, so what difference did the source make? But when humans seized territory to grow alchemy herbs and plundered natural spiritual flora from secret realms and blessed grounds, it starved out unawakened spirit beasts and low-tier fauna. As these bottom rungs of the ecological food chain rapidly collapsed, higher-tier demons were inevitably hit with severe food shortages.

In the absence of natural prey, carnivorous demons naturally set their sights on the easiest to catch, most rapidly reproducing source of fresh meat:

Humans.

Once that slaughter began, coexistence became an irreconcilable blood feud. Driven to desperation, certain demon clans naturally defected to the Demonic Realm.

Consequently, in the subconscious mind of humanity, ["demons and fiends"](<# "Literal CN: 妖魔 (yāomó) — demon-fiends; conflating natural animist demons (yao) with corrupt extraplanar fiends (mo)">) had become a single, inseparable compound term.

As a divergent branch of the phoenix, the Azure Phoenix Clan belonged to the benevolent avian order. They avoided killing, detested demonic corruption, and naturally leaned toward the human camp. They harbored no desire to see warfare between humans and demons.

If agricultural harvests could surge on a grand scale, multiplying low-tier spirit beasts so that mid-tier demons never starved and had no reason to raid human settlements, peace throughout the world would naturally follow—the most ideal outcome imaginable.

Thus, this thriving field of sugarcane stirred profound reflections within Shui Huang'er.

—"It is quite rare indeed,"-Elder Tong Ling noted.-"Humans are actually willing to part with spiritual fields to cultivate ordinary crops, and they've grown them remarkably well. Perhaps we can purchase some to bring back."

—"Indeed,"-Shui Huang'er nodded absently.

Her thoughts were preoccupied with how humans had managed to grow them so successfully. An urge to [illicitly learn the craft](<# "Literal CN: 偷师 (tōushī) — stealing the master's secrets / covertly picking up a trade without formal apprenticeship">) sprouted in her mind.

Watching the sword cultivators' harvest with rapt attention, she quickly picked up on their specific requirements regarding cutting length and incision angles. Moreover, hearing Mo Bei explain that these parameters weren't merely to test swordsmanship, but to optimize the regrowth of the next harvest, deepened her intrigue.

After observing for a while, Shui Huang'er soon realized that this numerical, standardized method was surprisingly easy to memorize.

Compared to traditional, ambiguous farming methods that relied entirely on an individual farmer's vague intuition and years of muscle memory, this quantitative approach could be replicated one-to-one by any demon under her command simply by following the specifications.

Furthermore, judging by Mo Bei's tone, the Heavenly Spirit Sect hadn't just quantified harvesting data—every other stage of cultivation seemed to possess a similarly complete set of standardized parameters!

Rather than buying sugarcane, Shui Huang'er naturally yearned to acquire their comprehensive agricultural methodology. Yet since the Heavenly Spirit Sect had meticulously codified this knowledge, it would undoubtedly come at a substantial price.

Shui Huang'er found herself torn with hesitation.

While she was currently a starry-eyed fan of Mo Bei, formally approaching the Heavenly Spirit Sect as an official representative of the Southern Realm was a massive step. Besides, she was still technically a runaway! Shui Huang'er had no desire to return to official diplomatic duties so prematurely.

While Shui Huang'er deliberated, the villagers had already brought out the sugar-boiling... that is, the examination apparatus.

To demonstrate, Qiao Shiyi'niang engaged the sugar press.

The very first step—extracting the juice—caused Shui Huang'er's eyes to widen in sheer shock.

Even for the demon race, juicing sugarcane was grueling physical labor. Moreover, while demons with brute physical strength were plentiful, those blessed with patience were rare, making their manual juicing clumsy and inefficient.

Yet now, the mortal maiden merely fed the stalks into the mechanical device, and pure cane juice was squeezed out completely, leaving behind bone-dry pulp.

Shui Huang'er reached out and touched the remnants, finding the crushed bagasse astonishingly desiccated.

—"Is this... magical artifact crafted by the Heavenly Spirit Sect?"-Shui Huang'er asked.

—"No, it was built by our Shiyi'niang!"-a Qiao Family villager proclaimed proudly.

This time, even the female cultivators turned to look upon this mortal maiden in awe.

—"It wasn't just me,"-Shiyi'niang explained, her face flushing pink; she dared not claim all the credit for herself.-"The overall mechanism was recorded in our textbooks, and I received guidance from the Artifact Refinement Hall. I merely learned how to assemble and operate the complete system."

Moreover, unlike the fully mechanical process described in the textbook, because these sugarcane stalks contained spiritual energy requiring delicate refinement, certain steps benefited from cultivators' assistance—and for those, the Heavenly Spirit Sect had distributed specialized talismans to replace manual labor.

For female cultivators like those from Fengqing City who had joined the examination on a whim, it wasn't so much an "exam" as an experiential "workshop." They observed the entire pipeline from the sidelines and lent a hand with measuring and purifying the sugarcane juice.

For instance, a cultivator specializing in medicine was tasked with evaporating the juice to thicken the syrup.

Everyone "tested" with immense joy.

[An exam where one doesn't have to worry about grades truly is bliss!]
Especially a hands-on practical lab.

Only Shui Huang'er stood distracted, her mind completely elsewhere after hearing Qiao Shiyi'niang's words.

She walked through the entire process, staring blankly at the glistening, snow-white sugar, until she finally couldn't hold back and sought out Mo Bei.

—"...I heard this entire process came straight from the Heavenly Spirit Sect's textbooks?"-Shui Huang'er asked.

"?"
Mo Bei looked at her with flat, emotionless eyes.

Shui Huang'er stammered hesitantly:

—"Then... what does it take to be allowed to read those textbooks?"

[Like that girl? By attending the Heavenly Spirit Sect's academy? Would that count as formally joining the Heavenly Spirit Sect? But as Saintess of the demon race, I cannot formally apprentice myself to a human sect. If I send someone else to do it and bring the knowledge back, that would be illicit theft of secret techniques—practically declaring war on the Untiring Immortal Venerable!]

[Yet, I really, truly want to bring this knowledge home!]

Sugar refinement techniques—especially such convenient, large-scale industrial methods—were immensely valuable to the demon race. After all, demons craved sugar and carbohydrates just as urgently.

Shui Huang'er was torn with agonizing dilemma.

Mo Bei stared at Shui Huang'er for a quiet moment. Just when Shui Huang'er panicked, thinking her petty schemes had been exposed, Mo Bei turned her head toward Qiao Shiyi'niang, who was assisting with the exam.

Qiao Shiyi'niang sprinted over briskly:

—"Teacher Mo Bei, do you have instructions?"

—"Give her a set of academy textbooks,"-Mo Bei instructed.

—"Right away,"-Qiao Shiyi'niang glanced at Shui Huang'er, nodded, and trotted back to fetch the books.

Shui Huang'er's eyes widened in sheer disbelief, staring at Mo Bei in bewilderment:

—"Your textbooks... is it really alright to just give them to me like this?"

—"They were compiled for mortals to learn anyway; there are no core secrets in them,"-Mo Bei replied nonchalantly.-"My Master uses them to [accumulate karmic merit](<# "Literal CN: 积累功德 (jīlěi gōngdé) — accumulating merit / karmic virtue; good deeds done to earn celestial favor">)."

In truth, even if they were core Heavenly Spirit cultivation manuals, handing them out wouldn't have mattered in the slightest.

Mo Bei knew that not only did Master not guard them jealously, he actively believed the wider they were disseminated, the better.

Mo Bei held no complicated thoughts about the matter. She simply believed that since Master thought that way, she would follow along.

However, Mo Bei's offhand indifference led Shui Huang'er to miscalculate. Pondering it, Shui Huang'er initially thought that knowledge taught to mortals couldn't possibly be that vital.

As for white sugar... it was merely a useful commodity.

That illusion shattered completely the moment Shui Huang'er held the textbooks of the White Jade Capital Academy in her hands.

Even Elder Tong Ling, the ancient wood spirit, drifted out from her consciousness, marveling alongside Shui Huang'er as they flipped through the pages:

—"Tsk, tsk! This is shockingly practical! I had assumed human books were nothing more than vacuous wordplay!"

As Shui Huang'er skimmed the textbooks, her expression turned increasingly solemn.

Finally, with a crisp *snap*, she shut the book.

—"It seems our journey must come to an early end,"-Shui Huang'er told Elder Tong Ling.

This time, Elder Tong Ling offered no objection, tacitly concurring with her judgment.

Shui Huang'er let out a long exhale. In an instant, her radiant azure feathered crest and tail plumes unfurled. Mo Bei observed the transformation impassively, as though nothing in heaven or earth could perturb her mind.

Seeing Mo Bei's composure, Shui Huang'er felt surprisingly soothed.

She suddenly felt that although this "runaway trial" had been brief, it had not been in vain at all; in fact, it had been exceptionally delightful—especially having met Mo Bei.

Surely an Immortal Venerable capable of raising a disciple like Mo Bei would never disappoint, right?

—"May I speak with your sect's Immortal Venerable?"-she inquired.

—"If our Southern Domain wishes to introduce this curriculum, what price must we pay?"

Speaking not as a wandering maiden, but in her true capacity as the Saintess of the Southern Realm, Shui Huang'er formally inquired of Mo Bei.

And in that very instant of her inquiry, something invisible even to the demon race fluttered azure wings and took flight from beside her.

*

Heavenly Spirit Sect.

Resting on a prayer mat, the little black lamb poked its head out furtively, peeking toward the direction of Zhou Hui's bedchamber.

[Excellent. Master seems to have fully entered his meditative state.]
Yuwen Xuan thought.

Although Zhou Hui had abandoned ascending, in order to consolidate his cultivation and out of ingrained habit, he spent two shichen every afternoon meditating, [come thunder or rain](<# "Literal CN: 雷打不动 (léi dǎ bù dòng) — unshakable even if struck by thunder; set in stone / inviolable routine">).

This was precisely what Yuwen Xuan had been waiting for.

Wriggling out from the prayer mat, it didn't dare set its hooves onto the polished floorboards lest it make clicking *tap-tap* noises. Instead, it slid across the ground on its chubby, round belly, crawling silently and gingerly toward the study without emitting the faintest rustle.

In the exact same undignified posture, it clambered inch by inch onto the low tea table, peering down at the miniature "model" of White Jade Capital.

The interior of the model was profoundly serene.

Naturally, as a metropolis belonging to the prehistoric past, White Jade Capital had always been tranquil. Especially since Mo Bei had recently mobilized a massive contingent of heroic spirit soldiers, even fewer guards patrolled the celestial avenues, beneath vast expanses of iridescent, seven-colored providential fate that drifted above like clouds.

The little black lamb gazed upon that providential fate and heroic spirit soldiers, swallowing a mouthful of drool.

[Must not eat them!]

[Cannot eat them!]

[If I eat them, Master will hang me from the peach tree to make smoked mutton—and someone else might actually eat me!]

Muttering frantically in its heart, it forcefully wrenched its gaze away.

Its eyes settled upon a seemingly unremarkable building near the edge of White Jade Capital, oriented toward the Fengqing City teleportation array.

The little black lamb carefully extended a tiny hoof toward the structure—

Right at that moment, an ethereal wisp of azure vapor surged forth from the teleportation array, smacking the little black lamb squarely in the face.

[Eee?! What on earth is that?!]

The little black lamb recoiled instinctively, tumbling backward off the tea table with a soft *thud* onto the floor.

As if alerted by the sound, the air before the desk rippled and warped. In the next instant, a figure draped in moonlight-bright flowing silver hair manifested silently within the study.

—"Master!"-The little black lamb was so terrified that every lock of its curly fleece puffed on end.-"It wasn't me! I didn't even get to do anything yet! It has nothing to do with me!"

It denied everything with desperate urgency, deathly afraid that hesitating for a single second would get it strung up from the mountain gate tree.

However, Zhou Hui had zero inclination to pay attention to it. Instead, he stared at the miniature "model" before him with an expression of gravest solemnity.

Vast swaths of azure clouds had enveloped the skies above Fengqing City, cascading across the spatial teleportation array directly into White Jade Capital.

No, this was not a cloud. This was...

[Providential fate?!]

Realizing this, Zhou Hui spared no more thought for the model. Just as abruptly as he had appeared, he vanished from before the little black lamb, materializing the very next second high up in the stratosphere.

Zhou Hui soared exceptionally high—several times higher than Mount Heavenly Chasm, the tallest peak in the Central Domain—nearly breaching the void of outer space.

From this vantage point, he could overlook the entirety of the continent, including Mount Heavenly Chasm and realms even further beyond.

Within this panoramic panorama, Zhou Hui saw titanic torrents of providential fate surging toward the Central Domain, converging upon Fengqing City. This providential energy was nothing like the corrupted vestiges found in the Abyss Sea; it was pristine, benevolent, and unblemished, ready for immediate use without requiring any purification.

It was precisely because this tide was so colossal that it had startled Zhou Hui out of his deep meditation.

[How did this come about?]
Zhou Hui wondered in bewilderment.

[Where did it originate?]

Simultaneously, as if answering his silent inquiry, a resonant voice droned like distant thunder:

—"Bujuan, what did you do?! You have stirred up the providential fate of the entire Southern Realm!"

—"...Patriarch Jiuzhai?"-Zhou Hui recognized the speaker.

It was a Tribulation Transcendence supreme powerhouse of the Southern Realm—a rather eccentric [divination cultivator](<# "Literal CN: 卦修 (guàxiū) — hexagram / divination practitioner; one who reads the cosmic trigrams, astromancy, and fate">). Because this patriarch could perceive far too many branching "futures," much like the former Zhou Hui, he almost never ventured outside his sanctuary.

Evidently, for such a massive shift in providential currents to occur without alerting this diviner was impossible, especially when he resided in the Southern Realm itself.

Yet Zhou Hui pondered carefully and remained completely at a loss:

—"I have done nothing of the sort. Recently, I merely hosted an Immortal Arts Assembly, and dispatched my disciple to Fengqing City to maintain public order... that is all."

Patriarch Jiuzhai was visibly dissatisfied with this explanation, grumbling in a muffled, booming cadence:

—"I know about your Immortal Arts Assembly, but this flux of providential fate has fundamentally [altered the future](<# "Literal CN: 改变了南境的未来 (gǎibiànle Nánjìng de wèilái) — altered the future of the Southern Realm; echoing the chapter title 'Changing the Future'">) of the Southern Realm—!"

—"...Never mind. Bujuan, wait right there. I am coming over to discuss this in person."
