# Chapter 215: Rare Beast Enclosure

Was Yuwen Xuan also going to participate in the Immortal Arts Assembly?

Naturally, he had to.

Among the disciples under Immortal Master Fujuan, when it came to physical combat rather than intellectual debate, there were three who excelled:

Mo Bei, Lu Shulong, and Yuwen Xuan.

Zhou Hui had therefore entered all three of them into the Immortal Arts Assembly. However, if entering Mo Bei and Lu Shulong carried an element of tempering and educating his disciples, then with Yuwen Xuan—aside from attracting cultivators from the Middle and Upper Realms to enter the tournament—Zhou Hui mostly just wanted to drain Yuwen Xuan's boundless energy.

His Eldest Disciple had been an excitable loose cannon ever since childhood. The livelier the occasion, the crazier he became. Merely hanging him from a tree could no longer contain him; he had to be given a proper mountain of tasks to burn off his steam.

Unfortunately, Yuwen Xuan had a rebellious bone protruding right behind his skull.

While his adoration for his master was limitless, getting him to obediently follow orders was even more out of the question.

Besides, participating in an Immortal Arts Assembly according to mundane tournament rules wasn't nearly as fun as hunting. Hunting even yielded fresh snacks to fill his belly.

Thus, Yuwen Xuan [conveniently](# "Literal CN: intentionally / deliberately") forgot that he was scheduled to compete in the assembly. He had attempted to convince his master to deploy him to the frontline of the Immortal-Demon Battlefield to "rescue" Qu Qianxing.

Even though his master hadn't granted permission yet, that didn't stop him from hatching a brand-new idea. He flew straight toward his own Rare Beast Enclosure, and after finding the grounds completely empty, dashed without a pause toward the seabed near the Immortal Market.

The disciples of the Rare Beast Enclosure had no inkling that impending doom was heading their way. At that moment, they were gathered near the giant jellyfish aquaculture field in the vicinity of the Immortal Market.

Calling it "in the vicinity" was relative—it was actually several dozen kilometers away from the market itself.

Furthermore, the jellyfish field spanned an astronomical area, covering tens of thousands of cubic miles. Only the ocean depths could provide the sheer volume required for such sprawling aquaculture.

This was the Rare Beast Enclosure's "new enterprise."

Ever since Yuwen Xuan's Kunpeng had been requisitioned to serve as the Heavenly Spirit Sect's public transit bus, the sect naturally took responsibility for feeding it. Originally, Yuwen Xuan hadn't fed it much at all—he had firmly intended to purge its digestive tract completely before tossing it straight into the hotpot. Releasing it to graze freely on the seabed was out of the question; given the Kunpeng's bottomless appetite, it would completely devastate the ecosystem across the entire sea. In the wild, Kunpeng would devour an entire maritime sector, then be forced to migrate to another ocean, or simply enter hibernation until the regional food chain completely recovered.

Initially, the Heavenly Spirit Sect fed the Kunpeng marine creatures corrupted by demonic miasma.

Since the Kunpeng possessed zero spiritual intelligence—and was practically incapable of developing any—its colossal bulk allowed it to consume mutated organisms without falling into demonic corruption. The only problem was that mutated organisms were too scarce, quickly getting wiped out by its gaping maw, while pure demons possessed flesh and blood fundamentally alien to normal biology and were strictly off the Kunpeng's menu.

Fortunately, before the Kunpeng faced a food shortage, the sect made an unexpected discovery while using the beast to survey the dispersal radius of demonic pollution in the Abyssal Ocean. In the miasma-corrupted zones, one particular creature not only suffered zero ill effects, but was actively proliferating in massive swarms:

Jellyfish.

As cnidarians, jellyfish possessed no bones, brain, blood, or heart. They were composed almost entirely of water and collagenous mesoglea, fundamentally lacking the biological criteria required for demonic possession. In fact, in another world, they were likewise among the rare organisms that thrived rather than perished amidst industrial chemical pollution and ocean plastics.

Of course, they weren't entirely unaffected by demonic miasma. Under the miasma's influence, many jellyfish exhibited tendencies toward gigantism and hyper-toxicity; some disciples had even discovered bioluminescent pelagic jellyfish measuring ten meters across. Yet overall, their fundamental anatomical structure remained unchanged, their behavior remained identical, and whatever they had been doing before the mutation was exactly what they continued to do afterward.

These jellyfish thus became the ideal fodder for the Kunpeng.

Furthermore, they doubled as living bio-lanterns, illuminating the pitch-black ocean depths with mesmerizing glows.

However, relying solely on harvesting wild specimens proved insufficient. They had to transition to artificial aquaculture.

When it came to his "professional field," Yuwen Xuan was truly irreproachable.

Whether rearing docile spirit beasts or vicious demonic monsters, he was a master of his craft, let alone handling a bunch of mindless cnidarians that thrived in polluted environments. After requesting comprehensive marine environmental data on various jellyfish habitats from Lu Shulong, Yuwen Xuan conducted rigorous research on the ambient demonic miasma and ecological conditions, designing an entire state-of-the-art jellyfish farming system based on the findings.

Regrettably, right after finishing the aquaculture blueprints—no, even before he could finish—he infuriated his master and was strung up by his ankles from the mountain gate peach tree.

Afterward, Yuwen Xuan was either dangling upside down from the tree or roaming around transformed into a sheep, leaving him virtually zero time to visit the Rare Beast Enclosure.

Fortunately, there was Gan Tang.

Gan Tang was a former outer disciple prior to the Heavenly Spirit Sect's educational reform. Relying solely on the exam papers distributed to inner disciples, she had successfully broken through to the Foundation Establishment stage. Later, in the sect's very first comprehensive Diagnostic Assessment Examination, she achieved stellar marks, earned an appointment as a teaching instructor, and even received personal guidance from Immortal Master Fujuan.

With such an impressive resume, Gan Tang had more than enough credentials to enter the Tool Refining Hall or the Alchemy Chamber—the two most coveted departments among ordinary cultivators. Yet for reasons unknown, she had chosen the Rare Beast Enclosure, a place that practically everyone else in the sect avoided like the plague.

Consequently, the monumental task of constructing the jellyfish field fell squarely on Gan Tang's shoulders.

Of course, had Gan Tang failed to pull it off, no one would have blamed her. In truth, had she simply reported the difficulty upward, Qu Qianxing would have reassigned someone else to oversee it.

Yet Gan Tang shouldered the burden without hesitation.

She wasn't being reckless; she genuinely believed she was capable of executing it.

First, jellyfish weren't spirit beasts or demonic beasts; they were ordinary primitive creatures. In theory, even mortals could manage them.

Second, Yuwen Xuan's farming schematics were exceptionally clear and practical (the man's literary talent was atrocious, but his functional drafting was remarkably straightforward), engineered toward maximum efficiency and convenience. This was evident from how Yuwen Xuan had decided that expanding the sect's Kui Cow pastures would pointlessly drain manpower, leading him to casually outsource the enterprise to the Ten Thousand Beasts Hall. He never intended to squander internal disciples' cultivation time on menial labor; his blueprints had planned from the very beginning to hire mortals from the Immortal Market for subsequent operational maintenance.

Finally, the Rare Beast Enclosure already met every material prerequisite necessary to execute the plan.

As it turned out, Gan Tang did indeed pull it off.

Although her early Foundation Establishment cultivation was somewhat modest, whenever she encountered a spell she hadn't mastered, she actively sought out other personal disciples or even the little sprites and monster spirits who gathered to gamble near the enclosure's courtyard.

When manpower fell short, Gan Tang mobilized her peers and the students from her own classes.

When a suitable seabed location couldn't be found, Gan Tang combed through every oceanic trench near the Immortal Market until she located marine sectors matching the blueprint parameters.

Through such persistent stumbling and perseverance, the jellyfish field was erected right on schedule, and its operational scale turned out even grander than originally projected.

An unexpected bonus was that throughout this process, the Rare Beast Enclosure recruited a sizeable batch of enthusiastic apprentices.

Unlike the miserable wretches who had previously been coerced by Yuwen Xuan, these new disciples had joined completely voluntarily.

After all, gazing out into the pitch-black abyss and watching specks of starlight kindle, growing from delicate dustings of bioluminescence into an ocean of celestial starlight beneath the waves, was an immensely satisfying endeavor. Almost no one could resist its romantic beauty, making the Rare Beast Enclosure one of the most popular elective courses among incoming Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples over the past half-year.

With the advent of the Immortal Arts Assembly, every department across the Heavenly Spirit Sect was swamped with labor. Under such severe manpower shortages, following the sect's (newly established last year) tradition, it was the prime season for new disciples to undertake hands-on practical internships.

On this day, the newly admitted disciples of the Rare Beast Enclosure stood inside a temporary staging facility adjacent to the jellyfish field, practically trembling with anticipation.

—"Before descending into the waters, inspect the artifacts on your persons one final time to verify nothing is missing! Double-check everything, especially your Water-Repelling Talismans!"-Gan Tang declared sternly.—"Affix your talisman first, then cast your personal Water-Repelling Art on yourselves! While submerged, continuously monitor the integrity of the talisman so you don't fail to notice if your spell falters! Adhere strictly to the safety protocol standards!"

—"Understood—"-the disciples chorused in unison.

Though slightly restless, they dutifully complied with every instruction Gan Tang laid out.

After all, with their current cultivation levels, losing both their Water-Repelling Art and protective talismans amidst the Abyssal Ocean meant instant, irreversible death.

In truth, it wasn't merely disciples of the Lower Three Realms who faced peril; even cultivators of the Middle Three Realms could only linger in the depths of the Abyssal Ocean for extended durations once they attained the Soul Formation stage.

Because physical resilience diverged so drastically between stages, in the cultivation world, the realms accessible to mortals, low-tier cultivators, high-tier cultivators, and True Immortals were segregated by boundaries as distinct as clear river water meeting the sea.

Zhou Hui believed this was precisely the fundamental reason why scientific and technological development had stagnated in this world for millennia:

When a seemingly viable path—with "successful precedents"—lay illuminated right before people's eyes, the vast majority would swarm like moths to a flame down the singular road of "immortality cultivation," blind to every other possibility.

Zhou Hui's utilization of the Abyssal Ocean was an active effort to break through this "invisible barrier."

Naturally, while attempting this, he never neglected to drill the lethal hazards into his disciples' minds.

As a primary-level instructor, Gan Tang had etched these warnings into her very marrow, prompting her to reiterate them over and over again.

However, her tense fixation on safety protocols elicited chuckles from the other "people" present:

[—"You've repeated that dozens of times already. Even the fish have memorized it by now."](# "Speaker: Carp Demon Coach")

[—"Exactly, little instructor, do you really have so little faith in our Water-Repelling Arts?"](# "Speaker: Carp Demon Coach")

—"It isn't everyone here that I distrust, but the Abyssal Ocean,"-Gan Tang retorted without pulling punches.—"If you two possess such boundless confidence in your Water-Repelling Arts, why don't you dive in and swim two laps yourselves?"

[—"Oh my, we were born in fresh rivers, not saltwater seas,"-the two coaches instructing the disciples in aquatic arts replied coyly.](# "Speaker: Carp Demon Coaches")

They were two voluptuous, enchanting beauties boasting slender waists and long legs. Sleek, shimmering form-fitting gowns clung tightly to their rounded hips, draping down their legs before fanning out around their calves like elegant fish tails. In fact, it wasn't merely "like" a tail—the gown and legs were literally transformed from fish tails.

These two "coaches" were carp demon spirits residing in the ponds near the Heavenly Spirit Sect's Rare Beast Enclosure, and were among the little sprites who routinely gathered to gamble with Yuwen Xuan's wandering soul.

Only after entering the Rare Beast Enclosure did Gan Tang discover that the Heavenly Spirit Sect housed an astonishing number of minor spirits and monsters.

Since Yuwen Xuan was perpetually absent, leaving these spirits to loiter in the courtyard—they dared not step inside his actual immortal cavern—gambling away their days was unbecoming. Coincidentally, the enclosure lacked instructors for practical aquatic skills, so Gan Tang requisitioned these sprites to teach the disciples useful survival arts.

Yet to be frank, watching the demon spirits' flippant attitude, Gan Tang couldn't help but sigh:

—"When it comes to the Water-Repelling Art and related divine techniques, the lessons should rightfully be taught by a personal disciple—namely, Eldest Senior Brother Yuwen himself—just like how the Formation Hall, Tool Refining Hall, and Alchemy Chamber operate,"-Gan Tang lamented.—"Unfortunately, he's never around..."

In reality, he was around—he was just perpetually dangling upside down from the mountain gate.

Yet the moment she voiced this regret, several "veteran" disciples in the Rare Beast Enclosure bristled in instant terror, their hairs standing on end.

These few veterans had inhabited the Rare Beast Enclosure long before Gan Tang arrived.

Because Yuwen Xuan maintained a menagerie of man-eating and non-man-eating aberrations purely to satisfy his sporadic cravings, feeding them was an exercise in risking one's mortal life. Back then, the Rare Beast Enclosure had been a forbidden dead zone dreaded by every soul in the Heavenly Spirit Sect. Yuwen Xuan had routinely "randomly" seized a few unfortunate souls for forced labor without recording their names. However, these specific veterans had achieved "meritorious distinction" during the *Immortal Master Fujuan Confiscates the Kunpeng* incident. Afterward, Yuwen Xuan officially registered them as staff of the Rare Beast Enclosure, forcing them to accept their monthly sect stipends through bitter tears.

Regarding these poor souls, the entire sect agreed they were exceptionally cursed, and the veterans themselves were convinced Eldest Senior Brother would settle the Kunpeng score with them sooner or later.

To their shock, ever since that incident, Eldest Senior Brother had either been "missing" or strung up from the mountain gate, rarely setting foot in the enclosure. Then Gan Tang joined, bringing along a flock of lovely junior sisters (junior brothers were hardly counted as people). The jellyfish aquaculture field proved to be a lucrative business that dramatically swelled departmental revenue, not to mention that the enclosure enjoyed its own private kitchen with an abundance of fresh, high-grade ingredients. It had practically transformed into one of the most comfortable departments in the entire Heavenly Spirit Sect.

Although they still had to feed Yuwen Xuan's terrifying monsters, only after working in the enclosure did they realize: what were monsters compared to Eldest Senior Brother? Eldest Senior Brother was the true nightmare! They would rather serve as monster chow than ever have to face Yuwen Xuan in the flesh!

Consequently, the sole factor that kept them awake at night was the dreadful prospect of Yuwen Xuan returning.

Hearing Gan Tang's wistful sigh, one of the veterans hurriedly grabbed her sleeve:

—"Junior Sister Gan Tang, one can eat food recklessly, but one must never speak carelessly! What if *that creature* actually comes back?"

—"Indeed! We're living peaceful lives right now, why go out of our way to ramp up the difficulty level?"-another veteran chimed in hastily.

Before Gan Tang could respond to their panic, the newly joined disciples broke into amused giggles:

[—"Senior brothers, why are you all so terrified of Eldest Senior Brother?"](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

The one asking this was undeniably a new disciple.

A disciple enrolled after the Heavenly Spirit Sect's educational reform.

Before the reform, the Heavenly Spirit Sect followed the standard orthodox model: one master mentoring a handful of personal disciples, surrounded by an inner circle of inner disciples, supplemented by a peripheral crew of outer disciples handling chores. Because class sizes were small, everyone was intimately familiar with the personal disciples' temperaments, and the older disciples naturally knew what caliber of primordial chaos fiend Yuwen Xuan truly was.

Now, however, the sect had transitioned to large-scale lecture classes. Disciples had zero opportunity to interact with personal disciples before selecting their elective tracks. Compounded by the merciless examination system, the primary classes feared Qu Qianxing—who drafted the rigorous curricula—above all else, while the intermediate classes trembled before Guan Ye, who enforced the most brutal admission and graduation standards in the Tool Refining Hall. The other personal disciples each commanded respect and awe for various reasons, whereas Eldest Senior Brother Yuwen Xuan, perpetually hanging from the mountain gate peach tree, was widely suspected of being some sort of ornamental sect mascot or a cautionary landmark.

Even the new disciples of the Rare Beast Enclosure, having caught only rare glimpses of him, shared similar sentiments.

Ignorance bred boldness, and they piped up freely:

[—"Even if Eldest Senior Brother is strict, could he possibly be more terrifying than Senior Brother Qu or Senior Brother Guan?"](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

[—"Honestly, judging by how he designed the jellyfish field, Eldest Senior Brother's beast-taming mastery might even surpass the Ten Thousand Beasts Hall next door. I wouldn't mind if things got a bit more competitive!"](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

[—"And the jellyfish field is so breathtakingly beautiful! Maybe Eldest Senior Brother is actually a deeply romantic, gentle soul!"](# "Speaker: Romantic Disciple")

This particular youth appeared somewhat prone to romantic delusions.

Others took a more pragmatic view:

[—"Besides, it doesn't matter if Eldest Senior Brother is usually absent, but his absence during the Immortal Arts Assembly is inconvenient. I wonder if we can still secure our practicum credit points."](# "Speaker: Pragmatic Disciple")

[—"Our Rare Beast Enclosure already awards fewer elective credits than the other departments."](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

[—"Yeah, if we don't even get credit points during this assembly, I might have to enroll in a second elective track next year."](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

[—"Are you insane? Every department is fiercely competitive right now. Double-majoring will kill you!"](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

[—"But I don't want to fall behind..."](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

This was indeed the "sole" drawback of the Rare Beast Enclosure.

Because the sect now operated on a credit system, elective credits had to be signed off by department heads. With Yuwen Xuan missing around the clock and Gan Tang only at early Foundation Establishment, she lacked the administrative authority to independently authorize such crucial academic credits.

When Gan Tang escalated the issue, even Qu Qianxing lacked a clean solution:

Even Qu Qianxing refused to meddle with the Rare Beast Enclosure's academic credits, lest that primordial chaos fiend accuse someone of tampering with his feeding trough and unleash a catastrophic tantrum.

Indeed—Qu Qianxing regarded these disciples as dishes that had willingly delivered themselves into Eldest Senior Brother's food bowl.

The few veterans shared that exact awareness.

Looking at the naive new disciples, they let out cold, scornful sneers:

—"Reckless fools who don't know the taste of death. No wonder you had the guts to join the Rare Beast Enclosure! Once that plague god truly returns, you'll realize that insufficient course credits are nothing compared to facing that walking calamity!"

[—"Plague god?"](# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan")

—"That's right, a plague god!"-the veteran disciple's passion flared, eager to enlighten these doe-eyed juniors on the horrors he had endured.—"You have no idea what we suffered back then! No, forget back then—even today, if you took our place and delivered food into his cave dwelling just once, you'd witness what kind of unspeakable abominations are locked in there! And the demon king who breeds those horrors is naturally... Junior Sister Gan Tang, why are you pulling my, my..."

His sentence dissolved mid-breath as his arm was yanked repeatedly.

The disciple tried to shake off Gan Tang's grip, but the moment he raised his head, his eyes locked onto a familiar figure standing right behind him. Only then did he realize that the inquiring whisper of *"Plague god?"* had not come from any junior brother or sister, nor from anyone beside him, but from that terrifying, unforgettable voice capable of jolting him awake from his deepest nightmares:

—"E-Eldest Senior Brother!"

The veteran froze solid on the spot, and his companions fared no better, their complexions draining of all color.

Only the oblivious junior brothers and sisters, led by Gan Tang, bowed respectfully toward Yuwen Xuan:

—"Greetings, Eldest Senior Brother."

A few of the bolder ones couldn't resist sneaking extra glances:

[—"He doesn't look all that scary!"](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

[—"Isn't that smile quite amicable?"](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

[—"He's surprisingly handsome! Is he really the one who hangs from the mountain gate every day?"](# "Speaker: New Disciple")

Whispered murmurs broke out among the crowd.

It couldn't be helped. Although Yuwen Xuan dangled perpetually from the peach tree by the mountain gate, he looked like a swaying drowned ghost, and few had ever dared to study his facial features up close. Many junior disciples had never even seen their Eldest Senior Brother's true appearance.

Yet their idle chatter caused cold sweat to drench Gan Tang's back:

—"*Ahem!*"-Gan Tang coughed loudly, frantically trying to cut off their perilous dialogue.

Although she wasn't paralyzed with terror like the "veteran" disciples—having boldly transferred to the Rare Beast Enclosure of her own volition—as a former outer disciple, Gan Tang was intimately familiar with Yuwen Xuan's legendary "glorious exploits."

For instance, on the very day she arrived to seek entry into the Heavenly Spirit Sect, Yuwen Xuan had hunted down a colossal Giant Gu Diao from who-knows-where. Gloating with pride, he had flown directly over the heads of the crowd waiting at the mountain gate, dousing the unsuspecting supplicants below in a torrent of foul filth. Although Yuwen Xuan was subsequently strung up from the mountain gate for two whole weeks, and Gan Tang's batch was "blessed through misfortune" by being admitted into the sect as compensation, they were subsequently subjected to Lu Shulong's demonic physical training for six grueling months before finally purging the avian venom through their own cultivation.

Yet that was merely one of Eldest Senior Brother's most harmless escapades. There were other incidents, such as subjecting every disciple in the sect to relentless nightmares for an entire year just to fatten up a Dream Tapir to serve as a New Year's roast pig; or colluding with Mo Xi to sell gorgeous ornamental hair crowns in the Heavenly Robe Pavilion, which were secretly designed to sprout emotion-sapping spirit ganoderma upon the buyers' skulls. While exposing these "pranks" invariably landed Yuwen Xuan on the peach tree, dangling by his feet never hindered this venerable ancestor from brewing his next scheme.

Surviving long enough in the Heavenly Spirit Sect inevitably induced Eldest Senior Brother PTSD. Only these clueless newcomers would dare gossip about Yuwen Xuan right to his face!

To prevent these disciples from uttering any sacrilegious blasphemy before this living harbinger of ruin, Gan Tang bit the bullet, stepped forward, and forced a stiff, practiced smile:

—"Eldest Senior Brother, you have finally returned (finally climbed down from the tree)?"

In truth, Gan Tang was slightly apprehensive about whether Yuwen Xuan even knew who she was.

Since entering the Rare Beast Enclosure, she had barely seen him a handful of times, and she didn't even know who had authorized her departmental transfer. She suspected it had been Ran Deng, because when the jellyfish aquaculture initiative was first proposed, Ran Deng had sent someone to inquire. Under Ran Deng's guidance, Gan Tang had unearthed the jellyfish farming blueprints from Yuwen Xuan's scroll rack (or rather, breeding cages), and the subsequent apprentice quotas had been requisitioned from Qu Qianxing by Ran Deng. Gan Tang had no idea whether Eldest Senior Brother would be pleased to find his subordinates multiplying so rapidly; if he took offense, things would turn ugly fast.

Fortunately, Yuwen Xuan didn't seem to care—or rather, he was completely indifferent.

His gaze swept casually across Gan Tang and the new disciples before asking nonchalantly:

—"Is everyone here? Perfect. I have a task for all of you."

—"B-But..."-Gan Tang subconsciously glanced outside at the jellyfish field, shimmering like a submerged sea of stars.

Their original schedule was for the new disciples to acclimate to underwater conditions while harvesting a batch of jellyfish.

However, locking eyes with Yuwen Xuan's seemingly smiling gaze, Gan Tang felt a chill in her heart. She reminded herself: this man was the true master of the Rare Beast Enclosure, and she was merely his subordinate.

Gan Tang swiftly adjusted her posture, asking submissively:

—"What are your orders, Senior Brother?"

—"An examination,"-Yuwen Xuan replied. He drew a thick stack of examination papers from his storage pouch and tossed them into Gan Tang's arms.

Scrambling to catch the papers, Gan Tang stammered in utter shock:

—"An examination? Right now?"

—"Correct,"-Yuwen Xuan nodded, continuing to rummage through his storage pouch.

Gan Tang assumed he was retrieving examination seals or writing materials, but to her astonishment, he hauled out a massive, ornate high-backed wooden armchair. With a heavy *CLANG*, he slammed it down onto the ground before them, striding forward to seat himself with an air of absolute bandit swagger.

Slumping back into the chair, Yuwen Xuan crossed his legs, looked down his nose at the gathered disciples, and sneered:

—"In our Heavenly Spirit Sect, when is an examination ever abnormal?"

[No, we definitely do not want this kind of 'normal!']

The majority of disciples thought in unison, their faces turning completely green.

Yet most of these youths were already conditioned by sect life. They discovered they weren't particularly surprised; in fact, a bizarre sense of *"this is how it ought to be"* welled up within them. They finally felt like genuine members of the Heavenly Spirit Sect—after all, they had heard that disciples in every other department were subjected to nonstop tests.

Gan Tang recovered swiftly as well, asking reflexively:

—"Does this count toward course credits?"

—"Hm,"-Yuwen Xuan tilted his head back against the wooden chair, pondering for a moment before answering:—"It won't count toward your practicum credit points for this Immortal Arts Assembly—you can settle that with Ran Deng later based on your jellyfish harvest quotas. As for this specific 'project,' we'll calculate the final grades once the semester concludes."

Hearing this, the disciples immediately erupted into a collective gasp of delight. That meant not only were their practicum credits guaranteed, but their elective course credits for the end of the term were secured as well! Their initial grievance over the pop quiz evaporated in an instant.

Even the veteran disciples stared at Yuwen Xuan in sheer disbelief, unable to fathom that he had suddenly decided to perform an act of human decency.

[—"We don't have desks or chairs here, how are we supposed to write...?"](# "Speaker: Diligent Disciple")

Another disciple eagerly piped up:

[—"Are you stupid? Can't we just use the crystal holding crates for the jellyfish?"](# "Speaker: Clever Disciple")

[—"Oh, right!"](# "Speaker: Excited Disciple")

Without prompting, the disciples spontaneously arranged the crystal crates into makeshift desks, sitting down behind them in perfect order to take the test—a level of practiced discipline that was almost heartbreaking to behold.

It felt as though if they were subjected to a few more surprise tests, they would develop the permanent reflex of carrying foldable desks and stools wherever they went.

Yet the moment these well-behaved youths laid eyes on the examination papers, their relaxed expressions froze solid once again. Even Gan Tang was no exception.

Difficult.

Excruciatingly difficult.

In the Heavenly Spirit Sect, if the final examination taken by all disciples served as the baseline difficulty benchmark, the difficulty of elective exams fluctuated depending on the department. Swordsmanship was the easiest—even simpler than the general finals. While Mo Bei held strict standards for his own academic performance, he never tormented the sword cultivators under his wing; if a disciple's sword technique was exceptional, elective credits could even be used to offset failing grades in other subjects. The Water Mirror Pavilion came next; as long as one passed arithmetic, demands in other subjects were relatively lenient.

The electives exceeding the general finals in difficulty were Spells, Tool Refining, Formations, and Alchemy (ranked in ascending order). Disciples aspiring to pursue advanced spell theory in Qu Qianxing's Academic Hall faced the highest theoretical demands; their test papers were completely indecipherable to ordinary peers.

Yet even the Academic Hall's monstrous papers had never made Gan Tang feel as deeply nauseated as today's test.

Yes, nauseated.

When one couldn't understand an Academic Hall exam, it was simply incomprehensible. Gan Tang understood her own limits and accepted that she couldn't solve them; on the rare occasion she managed to crack a problem or two, she felt like a genius and celebrated with joy.

Yet today's test paper was composed entirely of material Gan Tang had studied—topics she genuinely believed she excelled at! Yet the questions approached the concepts from angles so bizarre, twisted, and tortuous that formulating an answer was agony. Even after painstakingly penning a response, she felt entirely uncertain of its correctness. To use an unrefined analogy, it felt like acute intellectual constipation.

What was truly fatal was that—whether by design or sheer coincidence—whenever she got stuck on a question, pondering over it longer and feeling increasingly uncomfortable, similar question types began appearing with greater frequency further down the paper!

After grinding through three or four problems, the entire exam transformed into Gan Tang's absolute worst theoretical nightmare. Having barely completed a dozen questions, cold sweat coated the back of her neck, soaking through her collar.

Gan Tang wasn't the only one suffering this ordeal.

Shortly after the test began, the room filled with heavy sighs, the frantic grinding of inkstones, and ragged, trembling breaths. Finally, after ten excruciating minutes that felt like centuries, someone shakily raised their hand, asking with a tearful quiver:

[—"Does this test really have to count toward our credits?"](# "Speaker: Weeping Disciple")

However ecstatic they had been moments ago, that was how utterly despairing they felt now.

In this suffocating atmosphere, only the veteran disciples maintained slightly relaxed expressions. They knew it! How could Yuwen Xuan ever be so generous? But they were already certified academic slackers; even if they failed miserably, it was nothing new...

The veterans looked up, only to find Yuwen Xuan staring down at them unblinkingly. In sheer terror, they hastily dropped their heads, pretending to scribble furiously on their papers.

Yuwen Xuan chuckled softly:

—"Eh? When did I ever say this test counted toward your credits?"

[—"Eh? But Senior Brother, you just said it would count toward our semester credits..."](# "Speaker: Confused Disciple")

—"I said the *project* counts, not the test. This test is merely a preliminary part of the project,"-Yuwen Xuan clarified.

Indeed, Eldest Senior Brother had phrased it as a "project," not an exam!

Gan Tang pressed:

—"Does the project consist entirely of examinations?"

—"Of course not. We aren't the Academic Hall. Tests aren't the end goal; they're merely a diagnostic tool,"-Yuwen Xuan replied breezily.

Practically every disciple breathed a collective sigh of relief.

As long as this demonic paper didn't determine their final grade, and the core assessment was a hands-on "project," testing wouldn't be the primary metric!

Thinking thus, everyone pricked up their ears, eager to learn what the project entailed.

Yuwen Xuan didn't elaborate immediately. Instead, he cast a glance at Gan Tang:

—"Right, you're Gan Tang, correct? I recall your grades are quite solid?"

Gan Tang was caught off guard. She hadn't expected Yuwen Xuan to know her name, let alone recall her academic standing:

—"They are acceptable. My midterm examination scores this semester were..."-Gan Tang began, hoping to report her exact marks to leave a favorable impression on her superior.

Yet Yuwen Xuan maintained his characteristically careless demeanor:

—"Hmph, a top student's 'acceptable.' Fine then. Hand over your identity token. I'll unlock a section of the question bank for you. Going forward, compile the specific questions that everyone struggles with most, and administer a targeted diagnostic test like this once every half-month."

—"Y-Yes, understood,"-Gan Tang complied.

So these questions really had been tailored specifically to target everyone's theoretical weak spots!

No wonder the problems felt so excruciating.

Still, identifying gaps and rectifying deficiencies was fundamentally beneficial for one's cultivation studies.

At this point, Gan Tang was still harboring optimistic thoughts.

Yuwen Xuan continued:

—"Next, each of you will adopt an octopus."

—"Huh?!"

—"No, jellyfish won't do. They're entirely water and collagen—not enough to fill the gaps between my teeth... Ahem, what I mean is, they lack the intelligence required to perceive emotions like a [Heart Demon](# "Cultivation heart demon / inner demon"),"-Yuwen Xuan muttered as if conversing with himself.—"We need something with a semblance of intellect. Ah, right, octopuses. Octopuses will do nicely."

Having settled the matter, Yuwen Xuan addressed his bewildered subordinates:

—"Here is the plan. Each of you will raise an octopus, link with it using an empathetic resonance artifact, and spend two hours every day lecturing the octopus."

—"Eh?"

—"You will teach it the exact exam questions that caused you the greatest suffering, until the octopus comprehends the material."

—"Eh-eh?!"

—"And your final elective credit for this project will be graded based on how delicious your octopus... *cough*, based on your octopus's level of cerebral activity."

—"Eh-eh-eh?!"

A chorus of agonizing wails erupted throughout the hall. This absurd assignment made them feel that taking written tests was preferable!

—"What is the objective of this project?"-Gan Tang asked, bracing herself.

—"Think of it as studying the impact of human emotional fluctuations on the texture and flavor of biological meat... *ahem*, on biological emotions,"-Yuwen Xuan declared smoothly.

What in the world did *"think of it as"* mean?

Gan Tang deeply disliked such ambiguous phrasing.

Yuwen Xuan seemed to sense her skepticism. Rolling his eyes slightly, he added with absolute gravity:

—"Human emotions have always been unique. Inwardly, they spawn Heart Demons; outwardly, they influence the growth of other living organisms. Therefore, I require your cooperation in this experiment to help you grasp the concept of empathetic resonance between biological entities. In the future, this will enable you to better tame and harmonize with your spiritual beasts."

Truth be told, when Yuwen Xuan dropped his smirk and spoke with solemn earnestness, he was remarkably persuasive.

Almost no one remembered that he was an unrepentant academic delinquent.

[—"I see!"](# "Speaker: Enlightened Disciples")

Enlightenment dawned upon the disciples. They looked at their Eldest Senior Brother with eyes full of profound gratitude, genuinely believing this was an exclusive, advanced training regimen designed by a personal disciple.

Naturally, once they actually began lecturing the octopuses, they would discover the true depths of this psychological torment.

Lecturing an octopus while linked through an emotional resonance artifact, no less.

That, however, was a story for later.

Having offered a carrot, Yuwen Xuan didn't forget to brandish the whip:

—"If you find teaching octopuses too easy, I don't mind having you lecture jellyfish instead."

—"No! Octopuses! Octopuses are perfect!"-the disciples shouted in terror.

Yuwen Xuan nodded in satisfaction, turning back to Gan Tang:

—"You handle the administrative arrangements. Right, earlier I heard you expressing concern regarding the Water-Repelling Art? The carp demon spirits' instruction is technically sufficient, but for safety's sake, pay a visit to Lu Shulong and ask him to assign a few safety marshals to your sector—I recall some of his Eastern Realm kin have arrived for the assembly. Additionally, drop by the Tool Refining Hall; I'll instruct Guan Ye to fabricate the emotional resonance artifacts required for this octopus education project."

Yuwen Xuan arranged every single logistical detail with such seamless thoroughness that he genuinely resembled an impeccable Eldest Senior Brother.

At this point, Gan Tang was thoroughly won over.

She wholeheartedly accepted his authority as the master of the Rare Beast Enclosure and her direct superior.

Straightening her posture, Gan Tang replied solemnly:

—"Understood. I will see it done."

She then asked for final confirmation:

—"Are you assigning this project to me because you'll be occupied with the Immortal Arts Assembly, Senior Brother Yuwen?"

She needed to know this so she would know where to find her superior when submitting future progress reports.

Yuwen Xuan's answer, however, left her thoroughly bewildered:

—"There is one particular match I plan to watch, but the one competing in the tournament will mostly be the version of me hanging from the tree."

—"Pardon?"-Gan Tang blinked, utterly lost.

This time, Yuwen Xuan offered no further explanation.

Instead, he stared deeply at Gan Tang, that respectful address of *"Senior Brother Yuwen"* seemingly stirring an old memory within his mind.

Yuwen Xuan reached into his storage pouch, pulled out a small object, and tossed it toward Gan Tang:

—"If you ever encounter mortal peril, just blow this."

—"Can this project encounter mortal peril?!"-Gan Tang cried out in shock.

She lowered her gaze to examine the object Yuwen Xuan had thrown to her.

It was a bizarre bone whistle.

Dark crimson.

As though carved from coagulated, rotten blood.

***

—"What is such a revolting whistle used for?"

At that exact moment, Rong Rong was staring with undisguised disgust at the bone whistle held in Xia Bing's hand.

Had Gan Tang been present, she would have been astonished, for the bone whistle in Xia Bing's grasp was identical in form to the one Yuwen Xuan had gifted her, save for its relatively normal, ash-gray coloration.

Xia Bing also gazed at the whistle with cold revulsion:

—"An artifact bestowed by the sect elders. Supposedly, it has the power to commandeer the opponent's spiritual beasts."

—"Ah, to secure victory in the arena?"-Rong Rong asked, wrinkling her nose.—"But aside from our Ten Thousand Beasts Hall, very few other sects rely on spiritual beasts in combat. Could it be intended to counter our own sect disciples?"

—"How could that be?"-Xia Bing gave an empty, mirthless smile.

Indeed, how could this possibly be intended for the tournament ring?

Such an artifact was most useful for dealing with their own Sect Master!

That was the true purpose the elders had in mind when placing this whistle into her hands!
