# Chapter 41: The Freshmen's Fantastic Adventures (第41章 新生们的奇遇记)

—"Whoa, incredible—"

The freshman trio craned their necks, gasping in awe as they stepped into the Library Pavilion.

Even Cheng Ziyi, wearing his customary look of haughty arrogance, was among them.

This Library Pavilion was situated on the grassy lawn beneath the main peak of Mount Tianling. From the outside, it appeared as nothing more than an exquisite three-story tower. Nestled amidst weeping willows and cradled by blooming blossoms, the surrounding scenery was quite charming—perfect for a sunny afternoon lounging lazily on the grass with a scroll in hand. Yet compared to the nearby Palace of Silence, which had already reached eleven stories and was still climbing higher, the Library Pavilion looked like little more than an ornate toy model.

Yet stepping inside felt like stepping into an entirely different realm.

First, the grand hall of the Library Pavilion was a circular plaza spacious enough to accommodate several hundred people. But the surrounding walls towered sheer and straight, like canyon cliffs soaring toward a thin slit of sky. What formed these walls, however, was not mountain rock, but densely packed bookshelves, every single shelf crammed to the brim with volumes.

Walking into such a colossal library, the three reasonably tall youths felt like denizens of a miniature kingdom.

Moreover, these books were alive with motion. One could see certain volumes fluttering off the shelves, circling high above like birds before returning to their designated roosts. Some disciples wrote out book titles directly at the checkout desk, whereupon several tomes swooped down from on high with a whoosh of fluttering pages, wheeling around them as if urging them to pick one. As for books disciples wished to return, they placed them upon the book-return dais; the books then either unfolded their pages or stretched out their bamboo slips, soaring upward of their own accord.

Disciples could also ascend personally to make their selections.

Every bookshelf along the walls clearly displayed its classification, detailed down to the minutest subcategories, making retrieval exceptionally straightforward.

On the first floor of the grand hall, beside the temporary shelves, stood colossal jade stelae inscribed with the [Four Traditional Categories](# "Literal CN: 经史子集 (Jīng, Shǐ, Zǐ, Jí) — the traditional Chinese bibliographic taxonomy: Classics, Histories, Masters/Philosophers, and Anthologies/Collectanea"). Across the jade surfaces, ink strokes branched upward like living tree boughs, dividing into different categories. Jing Chenjun, for instance, noticed that the bookshelves directly above the jade stele marked "Histories" were not only divided into the traditional fifteen broad categories—such as Official Histories, Miscellaneous Histories, and Geography—but within Geography's ten subcategories (General Gazetteers, Palaces, Waterways, Mountains and Rivers, etc.), the Mountains and Rivers subcategory was detailed right down to shelf labels organized by cardinal direction (East, West, South, North, Central), specifying which exact mountain in which specific region. Finding whatever reference material one desired was crystal clear.

Of course, the Library Pavilion's designer had also thoughtfully considered how books were to be retrieved.

Although sword-kinesis and personal flying artifacts were strictly forbidden inside the pavilion, any disciple wishing to find a book needed only to write the desired category and keywords upon the "Retrieval Platform." Ink would then rise into the air: part of it coalescing into small black ink birds that took flight toward the shelves to serve as guides, while another part condensed into stepped brushstrokes, forming an ink staircase to carry the disciple directly to the target shelf. The only drawback was that the stability of these ink stairs was tied directly to the caller's calligraphy. Consequently, the "ink stairs" beneath certain cultivators were so crooked and hideous they were an eyesore to behold, and climbing them was downright hazardous, looking as if they might collapse at any second.

—"Truly worthy of an immortal's library!"-Jing Chenjun marveled.

—"W-well, not all sect libraries are like this,"-Cheng Ziyi said, his face flushing red.

He mentally recalled the library pavilion of the Xiafei Sect and suddenly felt that place didn't even deserve to be called a library.

Attempting to salvage his pride, Cheng Ziyi added:

—"The Library Pavilion of the Heavenly Spirit Sect is renowned throughout the cultivation realm. The Untiring Immortal Venerable has a reputation for never tiring of learning and reading insatiably. It is said there is not a single book under heaven he has not read. What is even rarer is that he is willing to share the books he has read. Whomever it may be—cultivators or mortals, spirits, demons, or deities—he refuses no one who wishes to borrow them."

—"So that's how it is,"-Jing Chenjun and Huang Xiao murmured blankly.

Although Cheng Ziyi singing the praises of the Untiring Immortal Venerable was an everyday occurrence, standing before such a Library Pavilion gave his words sudden, overwhelming credibility, such that even Jing Chenjun and Huang Xiao absorbed it completely, welling with reverent admiration.

[Could it be that I came to the most right place after all?]

Jing Chenjun thought to himself.

The three youngsters wandered foolishly about the hall for over a quarter of an hour, their eyes dazzled by the sheer variety of books.

—"We really should have dragged Brother Ji along,"-Huang Xiao sighed.-"Even if just to broaden his horizons!"

—"Indeed!"-Jing Chenjun agreed with a sigh, then took the initiative to ask Huang Xiao:—"Fellow Daoist Huang, weren't you looking for recipes? Why don't you give it a try?"

—"Ah..."-Huang Xiao cast an uncertain glance upward.

Up there, a cultivator was currently sprawled across a second-story shelf searching for books. Unfortunately, it was painfully obvious that this person's calligraphy was an absolute disaster: a single short horizontal stroke was written like a wriggling caterpillar, so that its scribe, unable to stand firmly atop it, was also arched over like a caterpillar, butt in the air as he clung to the shelf.

Huang Xiao felt its own calligraphy was about on par with this unlucky fellow and wasn't sure if it wanted to humiliate itself before its new roommates.

Especially when both of these roommates looked like they knew how to write.

—"Don't mind me,"-Huang Xiao scratched its head.-"Looking for recipes can wait until the end, right? If you two want to read anything, why don't you look for yours first?"

—"Ah."

Jing Chenjun and Cheng Ziyi exchanged a glance.

Jing Chenjun was actually itching to try, yet he wasn't sure whether he could operate it successfully.

After all, he didn't even have a spirit root! He couldn't cast even the most rudimentary spells. If he walked up to the "Retrieval Platform," wrote down words, and nothing happened, what then?

Feeling disheartened was a minor issue; exposing his true identity before his roommates would be real trouble.

Jing Chenjun resolved to pick a time when his roommates weren't around and the Library Pavilion was quiet to sneak over and test it out.

Having made up his mind, Jing Chenjun offered an excuse with a smile:

—"Truth be told, there isn't any particular book I urgently wish to read. I only came to familiarize myself with the layout. I never expected this Library Pavilion to be so massive; now I don't even know where to begin. Starting with Fellow Daoist Huang's recipes sounds quite nice."

—"Very well,"-Cheng Ziyi nodded coolly.

And so, Huang Xiao was escorted to the Retrieval Platform by its two roommates.

Yet Huang Xiao still didn't want to expose its calligraphy. Looking at its two roommates, it simply said:

—"However, as you both know, my literacy is limited. I'm afraid I might search for the wrong thing. Would you two mind helping me?"

—"..."

Had Jing Chenjun been certain that his handwriting would work, why would he have demurred earlier? He immediately passed the buck:

—"Then let Fellow Daoist Cheng do it! I have never encountered a scene like this before and may not know how to cast such a spell. If it fails, I'll only embarrass myself. It's much safer to have Fellow Daoist Cheng, with his Heavenly Spirit Root, handle it."

Jing Chenjun craftily laid the groundwork in case his spellcasting failed.

Huang Xiao didn't see through Jing Chenjun's anxieties. As long as it didn't have to do it personally, anyone would do. Widening its sincere, beady little eyes, it turned to Cheng Ziyi as well:

—"Fellow Daoist Cheng, please! You hail from an orthodox clan, your calligraphy must be exceptional!"

For some reason, Cheng Ziyi stood frozen on the spot for two whole seconds. Only under the flattery of one human and one beast did he finally grip the brush:

—"Hmph! That's rogue cultivators from the countryside for you—incapable of doing anything right! In the end, it all comes down to me!"

So saying, he wielded the brush with grand momentum, splashing ink across the paper with sweeping majesty as he penned two characters—

—"Is this... 'Recipe'?"-Huang Xiao asked uncertainly upon seeing them.

Those two human characters, "Recipe" [食谱], were words it ought to have learned and known how to write. But why did these characters look nothing like the ones it recognized?

—"Fellow Daoist Cheng, are you... drawing talismans?"-Jing Chenjun asked gingerly. In his mind, he took stock of all the calligraphic scripts he knew and was certain none matched this style.

Yet who knew? Perhaps the cultivation realm had calligraphers unknown to mortals?

—"Cursive script!"-Unable to hold it in any longer, Cheng Ziyi's face flushed crimson as he hollered:—"This is cursive script! How uncultured can you two be, not even recognizing this?!"

—"..."

Huang Xiao and Jing Chenjun finally understood what was going on, staring speechlessly at Cheng Ziyi.

So this fellow wasn't just terrible at arithmetic?

With handwriting like this, wasn't landing in the beginner class completely natural?

It was a miracle that the Retrieval Platform could decipher such handwriting at all. From those two illegible [ghost-talisman scribbles](# "Literal CN: 鬼画符 (guǐ huà fú) — illegible scribbles looking like Taoist ghost-warding talismans; messy chicken scratch"), a dot rose and transformed into two blobby, misshapen ink birds. One flapped its stubby wings with great difficulty, heading toward the upper shelves of the "Masters" classification, while the other wobbled crookedly toward a small door on the left side of the grand hall.

—"How about it? Fellow Daoist Huang, do you want to head up and take a look?"-Jing Chenjun asked, looking up toward the high bookshelves first.

—"A bit difficult,"-Huang Xiao replied, staring at the ink ladder erected before the bookshelves by Cheng Ziyi's calligraphy.

That was no longer a caterpillar; it was a sheer vertical precipice with bizarre zigzags. Huang Xiao weighed it carefully and decided that climbing it in human form would be exceptionally challenging. To reach the designated shelf using this ink ladder, even if it didn't revert to its true form, it would at least have to pop its tail out for balance.

As a pure mortal, Jing Chenjun felt even less inclined to believe this ink ladder was usable.

He shook his head with a sigh:

—"In that case, let us head over there. Perhaps the shelves in the other rooms of the Library Pavilion are lower."

And so, Jing Chenjun and Huang Xiao followed the other ugly ink bird toward the small door on the left side of the grand hall.

Seeing their silent, noncommittal reactions, Cheng Ziyi blew his top:

—"Hey! What kind of reaction is that?!"

—"So what if my handwriting isn't great?! Let me tell you, once your cultivation rises, you'll know how to do everything!"

—"The entire point of cultivation is to make up for innate deficiencies!"

—"For instance, my great-grandfather's cousin's grandmother's second grandson couldn't even calculate one plus one! But after reaching Soul Formation, he could even solve [the Chicken and Rabbit in the Same Cage problem](# "Literal CN: 鸡兔同笼 (jī tù tóng lóng) — a classic ancient Chinese algebra problem calculating heads and feet of chickens and rabbits in a cage")!"

—"And my maternal aunt's great-grandfather's paternal cousin was said to be utterly incapable of studying in his youth, but once he reached the Void Refinement stage, he not only possessed photographic memory, but could recite texts backward!"

—"So as for mere calligraphy, once I cultivate to Body Integration or Mahayana, I'll master it sooner or later!"

—"..."

Why did hearing him describe it make the great powerhouses of cultivation sound so unimpressive?

So after slaving away for thousands, tens of thousands of years, all you achieved was the starting baseline of an ordinary mortal?

Is your cultivation purely meant to compensate for a sub-baseline IQ?!

Jing Chenjun was having an existential crisis.

Huang Xiao was not completely devoid of cultivation common sense like Jing Chenjun. Though it was a spirit beast—no, precisely *because* it was a spirit beast with instinctual hereditary memories—it understood the progressive cognitive and physical enhancements of each cultivation stage far better than humans.

Huang Xiao couldn't help but chime in:

—"Fellow Daoist Cheng, you shouldn't rely too heavily on cultivation. Certain knowledge truly cannot be absorbed without talent (such as advanced physics), but other skills can never be learned without practice (such as calligraphy). If you simply wait for cultivation to compensate, I fear you will remain completely clueless even by the time you ascend."

In Cheng Ziyi's current state, how could he possibly listen?

—"No way—*Mmph! Mmph!*"

Just as Cheng Ziyi was about to shout louder, the guiding ink bird suddenly detonated, splashing ink all over Cheng Ziyi's face and gagging his mouth shut!

Simultaneously, a stern female voice rang out:

> "No loud noises in the Library Pavilion!"

[—"That's Senior Sister Ran Deng, one of the direct disciples,"—](# "Speaker: Passing Disciple")a passing disciple muttered, shooting the trio a schadenfreude-filled glance upon hearing the voice.—"The Library Pavilion is essentially her domain, and she's also in charge of the renovation projects for our future immortal caves! If you offend her, be careful your cave doesn't get pushed back a thousand years."

[We're freshmen in the beginner class; we don't need to worry about immortal caves for now.]

Of course, only a fool would say that aloud.

Huang Xiao and Jing Chenjun immediately put on obedient smiles, while the hard-headed Cheng Ziyi, his mouth sealed by ink, would likely be unable to make a sound until the ink faded away.

—"Still, now that the bird is gone, what do we do next?"-Huang Xiao whispered.

—"It seems we've already arrived?"-Jing Chenjun murmured uncertainly, looking at the signboards reading "Shadow Cinema" and "Cookhouse."

He didn't quite understand what "Shadow Cinema" meant, but "Cookhouse" was clear enough.

Then again, didn't they say immortals practiced inedia? Why would there be a cookhouse?

Jing Chenjun cast Cheng Ziyi a puzzled glance, but unfortunately, the latter was currently muted and unable to offer an answer.

And so, the party stepped into the Cookhouse.

Unlike the common impression of dark, cramped, and filthy kitchen quarters, the Cookhouse inside the Library Pavilion was just as spacious and bright as the grand hall, with countertops so polished they reflected silhouettes like mirrors.

Before one stove, a chef clad in white was energetically wielding a wok spatula, surrounded by several drooling disciples watching intently.

As Huang Xiao and the others entered, one of the spectators twitched her delicate little nose and turned her head.

She called out happily to Huang Xiao:

—"Eldest Brother!"

—"Second Sister... Ah, keep your voice down,"-Huang Xiao, recalling the recent punishment, hurriedly made a shushing gesture to his sister.

The young girl quickly clapped a hand over her mouth. Leaving the cooking chef behind, she scampered over to Huang Xiao and the others with light, brisk steps.

—"This is my younger sister, Huang Xiaoxiao,"-Huang Xiao introduced to Jing Chenjun and the others.

He then introduced to his sister:

—"These are my roommates, Fellow Daoist Jing Chenjun, and Fellow Daoist Cheng Ziyi."

—"Greetings, older brothers,"-Huang Xiaoxiao chirped sweetly.

Huang Xiaoxiao truly lived up to her name: a dainty, adorable girl barely five feet tall. Yet one look at her round little cheeks and beady little eyes made it instantly obvious she came from the exact same mold as Huang Xiao.

Hearing Huang Xiaoxiao's name, however, Jing Chenjun's expression turned slightly subtle:

—"If you have a third sister, would her name be Huang Xiaoxiaoxiao?"

—"I don't have a third sister, only a third brother,"-Huang Xiao corrected upon hearing this:—"Besides, who on earth would give their child such a bizarre name?!"

—"I suppose so,"-Jing Chenjun chuckled.

Whereupon Huang Xiao continued:

—"My third brother is named Huang Sanxiao, my fourth sister is Huang Sixiao, my fifth sister... my seventh sister is Huang Qixiao, and my eighth brother is named Huang Moxiao."

[How about that? Doesn't that sound far simpler and clearer than human names?]

—"..."-Jing Chenjun's cheek twitched as he forced an awkward smile:—"Your family is truly a massive clan!"

[Even with all my imperial father's concubines, they never produced this many children!]

Huang Xiao puffed out his chest with pride:

—"Our family has always been like this. In the previous litter—"

—"Previous generation!"-Huang Xiaoxiao stomped on her brother's foot. She was far more street-smart:—"Our family has been like this for generations! Not just the previous generation—in my grandfather's generation, there were seventeen siblings (critters)!"

—"That truly is a large family,"-Jing Chenjun said in astonishment.

—"Not really. Whenever a spiritual energy storm sweeps through the Abyssal Ocean, almost all of them die off,"-Huang Xiaoxiao remarked.

—"..."-Once again, Jing Chenjun was at a loss for words.

The Huang siblings, on the other hand, were thoroughly accustomed to this everyday reality; there was nothing to grieve over. It wasn't that they didn't miss their kin, but if they held a memorial every single time someone was mentioned, given their clan's population, they would never get anything else done in this lifetime.

Speaking of their grandfather's generation, Huang Xiao leaned toward his sister and whispered:

—"Did you come here because of Seventh Grandpa too?"

—"Yes!"-Huang Xiaoxiao nodded.

The brother and sister exchanged a look, reaching an instant consensus:

They could not let Seventh Grandpa continue dragging the old Huang clan's reputation through the mud!

—"By the way, Little Sister Huang, is it alright for you to step away?"-Jing Chenjun asked curiously at this moment.—"That culinary tutorial over there... it is a tutorial, right? It seems to be wrapping up."

This was his first time seeing a culinary tutorial inside a Library Pavilion.

—"It's fine, you can watch it repeatedly,"-Huang Xiaoxiao turned to glance at it and waved to her companions.

—"Watch it repeatedly?"

Seeing their bewildered expressions, Huang Xiaoxiao, having arrived earlier and been guided by a senior sister, was relatively familiar with the setup. She took it upon herself to teach her brother and his roommates how to use the Shadow Cinema.

—"Yes, that gentleman is merely a phantom projection. You can try it out yourselves!"

Huang Xiaoxiao led her brother to an empty cooking station. Hanging here were small wooden plaques resembling menu order tags, which not only listed the dish name, but also the chef's full name.

For example:

> Egg Pancake — Chef Wang

Jing Chenjun casually took down this tag, and a crew-cut chef materialized before the stove.

—"Hello everyone, I am Chef Wang. In this video, I will share a home-cooked dish with you: Egg Pancake,"-the chef briskly began his demonstration.

—"So that's how it is—a holographic projection tutorial?"-Huang Xiao praised.

Jing Chenjun stared with wide eyes, observing intently.

It wasn't that he had any particular interest in this dish, but rather that he felt this was virtually indistinguishable from real cooking: not only was there sound, movement, and aroma, but you could even taste it!

Yes, from the seasonings to the finished dish, everything could be tasted!

One could even taste it using different tongues!

—"Don't just taste it with your own tongue,"-Huang Xiaoxiao introduced.

She picked up a chopstick holder and offered it to everyone. Inside, the chopsticks not only came in different colors, but were inscribed with tiny characters, such as "[Taotie's Tongue](# "Literal CN: 饕餮之舌 (Tāotiè zhī shé) — named after the mythological gluttonous beast Taotie")," "Divine Chef's Tongue," "Mortal's Tongue," "Mediocre Cook's Tongue," "Demon's Tongue," "True Dragon's Tongue," "True Immortal's Tongue," and so forth.

Huang Xiaoxiao specifically pulled out a pair of red chopsticks:

—"Taotie's Tongue is the most sensitive to taste... Ah, I was just about to say I don't recommend using that one!"

She spoke a second too late.

All three people present clapped their hands over their mouths.

How should one put it? The delicious egg pancake that had tasted so wonderful with their own tongues could now be dissected into the exact flavor of the oil, the egg, the cured ham—and then further broken down into the oil's temperature, the egg's freshness, the exact ratio of starch to meat in the ham... It wasn't that it tasted bad per se; it just didn't feel like eating a meal anymore.

—"Actually, using the Mortal's Tongue is the most enjoyable,"-Huang Xiaoxiao said.-"Everything tastes delicious."

—"Is that so?"-Jing Chenjun remarked with keen interest.-"I'll go sample some others."

He scampered off to other cooking stations to find dishes he liked.

Meanwhile, Cheng Ziyi, who had claimed that "cultivators must not covet mortal appetites," silently trailed behind Jing Chenjun, setting off to forage as well.

—"Don't 'eat' too much! Because you're only tasting the flavor in your mouth and nothing actually reaches your stomach, you'll only get hungrier and hungrier,"-Huang Xiaoxiao called out in warning from behind, though her words clearly fell on deaf ears.

Once the two nuisances had wandered off, Huang Xiao whispered to Huang Xiaoxiao:

—"Aren't there any simpler dishes? I feel like even with this, Seventh Grandpa could never learn it."

If Seventh Grandpa's affliction (cough) were so easily cured, why would he still be concocting dark culinary abominations by the time they arrived here?

Before Huang Xiaoxiao could reply, a voice spoke up:

[—"There are! There are dishes so simple even a fool can master them."](# "Speaker: Ink Bird")

Huang Xiao looked down and discovered a tiny ink bird—naturally not the ugly one Cheng Ziyi had written that exploded to muzzle him. This bird was chubby and round, looking quite like his sister's handwriting in Huang Xiao's eyes.

—"I wrote that one,"-Huang Xiaoxiao admitted.

Faithfully fulfilling its duty as a guide, the little ink bird led the Huang siblings to another counter.

This time, there were no stoves upon the counter. Instead, several bizarre contraptions sat atop it, while the menu tags beside them read:

> Roast Chicken Wings — Air Fryer
> Sausage Rice Casserole — Rice Cooker
> Instant Noodles — Microwave Oven

—"Air... Fryer? Rice... Cooker? What strange names for master chefs,"-Huang Xiao muttered.

He took down a tag and quickly realized his misunderstanding.

These turned out to be the names of appliances!

A translucent phantom figure, who could hardly be called a chef, simply tossed the prepared ingredients into these strange vessels, set a timer, and pulled them open; the dish inside was already freshly cooked!

And the taste was actually quite passable.

—"This will do!"-Huang Xiao and Huang Xiaoxiao exclaimed in unison.

Even if Seventh Grandpa couldn't manage it, manufacturing these appliances would be enough for their fellow disciples to solve their own hunger issues!

—"I just wonder how such divine artifacts are made?"-Huang Xiao fretted.

How could the omnipotent Library Pavilion (cough), or rather Zhou Hui, not know the operating principles of a mere air fryer?

In truth, aside from local cultivation world dishes, several recipes from the other world in this area had been manifested directly from Zhou Hui's memories.

Particularly from his undergraduate to doctoral years.

During those times when he couldn't eat at the campus dining hall, Zhou Hui had been forced to fend for himself.

While Zhou Hui was perfectly capable of cooking, [the supreme grinder](# "Literal CN: 卷王 (juǎn wáng) — involution champion; someone who obsessively hustles, optimizes, and out-competes everyone else") that he was viewed spending time on elaborate cooking as an utter waste of life. And since takeout was unhealthy, he was exceptionally fond of one-pot meals and lightning-fast dishes.

However, when Zhou Hui extracted these memories, his direct disciples watched with heartbroken faces.

—"Master actually endured such times?!"-they cried, looking as though Zhou Hui had suffered some kind of inhumane torture.

Yuwen Xuan in particular had cooked elaborate imperial banquets of hundreds of dishes for several days straight. Zhou Hui remained entirely unfazed, but the sect's spirit beasts were fattened up by several whole sizes—until sect disciples filed an outraged petition:

> "We are treated worse than beasts!"
> "If Master won't eat it, give it to us! We don't mind eating leftovers!"

Only then did Yuwen Xuan stop.

In any case, Yuwen Xuan adamantly refused to cook for any humanoid creature other than Master (collateral bonuses didn't count); even if they starved to death, they wouldn't get a single bite!

In short, setting aside the Eldest Senior Brother's absolute lack of virtue, for ordinary disciples who had no choice but to consume dark culinary horrors, an option acceptable to Zhou Hui was naturally a stellar choice.

Huang Xiao and Huang Xiaoxiao studied the schematics of several cooking appliances and recorded them onto bamboo slips.

They couldn't forge them themselves, but they intended to go to the Artifact Refinement Hall to ask.

`*`

—"Sure thing, it's very simple,"-Zhao Yuegui, a newly admitted disciple in the Artifact Refinement Hall, replied briskly.

It was unclear whether he was simply too idle or purely because the question was related to food—Huang Xiao guessed the latter, because the moment it mentioned cooking appliances and played the culinary phantom recordings from the Library Pavilion, a whole crowd gathered around. In short, the Artifact Refinement Hall was exceptionally enthusiastic about inquiries from their beginner class juniors.

Zhao Yuegui even waved a greeting to Jing Chenjun:

—"Aren't you the... little brother who was standing next to me during the mountain gate recruitment?"

—"I am Jing Chenjun,"-Jing Chenjun introduced himself, looking at Zhao Yuegui with admiration:—"I never expected Fellow Daoist Zhao to test into the intermediate class in one fell swoop. Truly impressive!"

—"Oh, it's nothing."

In any case, Zhao Yuegui had been a rogue cultivator in the Eastern Domain capable of hunting demon beasts solo on the open seas; there was a fundamental difference between him and these youngsters who hadn't even practiced basic mental cultivation techniques.

Unable to contain himself, Huang Xiao asked from the side:

—"May I ask, Senior Zhao, is this magical artifact easy to craft?"

Looking at the internal structure, it clearly seemed so complex; he couldn't comprehend how merely running electricity through a few iron wires and metal plates could produce food.

—"Don't call me senior, just call me Lao Zhao,"-Zhao Yuegui waved his hand.—"I'm not saying recreating this exact appliance is simple, but according to its principles, that... microwave oven might be slightly trickier, but the rice cooker and air fryer are genuinely just a matter of a single array formation and two spirit stones. You don't even need complicated electrical currents; even you folks could do it."

—"Eh?"-Huang Xiao and Huang Xiaoxiao exclaimed in confusion.

Zhao Yuegui then proceeded to build an air fryer right before their eyes to demonstrate.

He casually hammered together a sealed pot, slapped a Wind Talisman and a Fire Talisman onto it, mounted a spirit stone, and added an activation switch.

—"And that's it! Heat it up and let the wind blast the food cooked—done deal,"-Zhao Yuegui turned and asked:—"Alright, who can grab some ingredients?"

A crowd of Artifact Refinement Hall disciples watched from the sidelines. Someone was quick to fetch a few chicken wings and tossed them into the pot according to the recipe Huang Xiao had brought.

Since they hadn't crafted a timer yet, the crowd counted the passage of time on a sundial. Before the lid was even lifted, a rich, mouthwatering aroma of roasted chicken wings wafted out.

—"It's cooked!"

—"It's edible!"

—"It tastes way better than Old Huang's cooking!"

The crowd cheered as though witnessing some historic moment.

Huang Xiao and Huang Xiaoxiao felt their cheeks burning hot.

Meanwhile, Jing Chenjun, having "taste-tested" in the Library Pavilion for half the day and long since starving, tasted a chicken wing and furrowed his brows:

—"It's not as good as the original version."

—"But it's [good enough to make do with](# "Literal CN: 凑合 (còuhe) — to make do, settle for something half-assed, cut corners"),"-Zhao Yuegui said.

—"We do not cut corners!"-a voice suddenly declared.

Jing Chenjun and the others watched in surprise as the new disciples of the Artifact Refinement Hall shuddered as if struck by a whip, instantly straightening their legs and standing at attention.

Immediately following, an aloof, gloomy young man descended from the sky, seated atop a multi-treasure chest.

Looking closely, he was tall and quite handsome, but thick black bangs curtained most of his eyes, and his slightly hunched back gave him a distinctly unapproachable aura.

[—"That's Senior Brother Guan Ye, one of the direct disciples,"—](# "Speaker: Artifact Refinement Disciple")someone whispered to the beginner class freshmen who were still oblivious to the situation.

Guan Ye didn't look at the outsiders in the hall, fixing his gaze squarely on Zhao Yuegui:

—"Since you think cutting corners is fine, why don't you make do and eat at the Delicacy Pavilion?"

Zhao Yuegui broke out in a cold sweat.

A few chuckles escaped the watching crowd, but under Guan Ye's chilling gaze, they quickly clamped their mouths shut.

—"This cooking appliance was seen by Master when he traveled to another world,"-Guan Ye said, looking at the air fryer projection.—"For such a simple appliance, why do you think no one in our world ever forged it?"

—"W-well, it's not necessarily true that no one ever forged it, right?"-a disciple ventured boldly.

—"Then why did it never become popular?"-Guan Ye demanded.—"Clearly, by simply setting the temperature and time, food can be roasted on schedule with guaranteed quality. It ought to be exceptionally practical for the Qi Condensation stage, shouldn't it? Why does no one use it? Why did no one promote it? Why have you people never even heard of it?!"

—"..."

This time, no one could answer.

If Zhou Hui were here, he would have told Guan Ye: it was because of demand, and because of commercialization.

Because many people needed such a commodity, and because such a commodity could generate profit, merchants painstakingly researched how much time and what temperature could successfully prepare food, leading to the creation of such appliances.

In the cultivation world, demand existed, but it was minimal.

Cultivators were few in number, resources were monopolized by the upper echelons, and commerce was underdeveloped. Naturally, no merchant would expend time and energy to develop an item for a tiny minority that would earn them barely any spirit stones. Nor would the grand powerhouses waste their time on something so utterly inconsequential.

This was a common reality of medieval worlds: only the demands of the upper class counted as demands; the lower class simply did not exist.

Yet such macroeconomic reasoning was irrelevant to artifact refiners.

Guan Ye approached the matter strictly from a craftsman's perspective:

—"The reason no one uses it is precisely because it was too half-assed!"

Guan Ye picked up the pot Zhao Yuegui had cobbled together with visible disdain:

—"A pot like this has no sundial clock. How can you be certain how many minutes it takes to cook without burning? How many times can the talismans inside be used? Three times? Four times? Spending half an hour drawing a talisman only for it to expire after roasting chicken wings three times? Wouldn't it be easier to just hold it directly over a fire?! Not to mention, the temperature inside is a complete mystery! Can you guarantee that after using it twice, the same amount of time will still cook the chicken wings through?!"

Guan Ye tossed the pot aside and glared sternly at the new disciples:

—"As artifact refiners, you think this is acceptable?!"

Many of the new disciples hung their heads.

—"Since your fellow disciples brought the demand right before your eyes and you understand the principles, reproduce its functionality completely and thoroughly for me!"-Guan Ye commanded.—"This is your first assignment!"

—"No cutting corners!"

Under Guan Ye's orders, the new disciples of the Artifact Refinement Hall scrambled into motion, leaving the little freshmen standing dumbfounded in place, looking thoroughly terrified.

Only then did Guan Ye notice them. He attempted to mimic Master's manner and smile at them, but the youngsters seemed even more frightened.

—"You did well,"-Guan Ye said after a moment's thought.—"Those cooking appliances have been sitting in the Library Pavilion for nearly three months since it was built. Everyone complained about the food being unpalatable, yet no one bothered to seek them out. You found them—very good."

—"Which class are you from? I'll have Second Senior Brother award class points to your class later,"-Guan Ye added.

Only then did the freshmen beam with delight.

Class points were a new system at the Heavenly Spirit Sect, tied to each class's academic scores and daily performance. The youngsters present had never imagined their inadvertent actions could earn them points.

—"Additionally, what kind of pot do you need? You can specify your requirements in greater detail,"-Guan Ye said, looking at Huang Xiao and Huang Xiaoxiao.—"I'll have them forge it for you."

—"C-can you? We don't have enough spirit stones to buy it,"-Huang Xiao said nervously.

—"It's fine. It was originally meant for that critter in Delicacy Pavilion anyway,"-Guan Ye sneered with a cold chuckle:—"Consider it wiping Eldest Senior Brother's ass! If he hadn't constantly gambled with the little demons on the back mountain, lost terribly, and owed a mountain of debt, how could that [old yellow weasel](# "Literal CN: 老黄鼠狼 (lǎo huángshǔláng) — Chef Old Huang's true demon beast form") have taken over the Delicacy Pavilion? Although... I actually rather look forward to seeing Master beat them to a pulp when he finds out. *Jie jie.*"

Huang Xiao and Huang Xiaoxiao instantly curled into a ball of sheer terror.
