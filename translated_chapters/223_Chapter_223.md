# Chapter 223: The Courtyard

—"Is this the entrance to the Immortal Realm?"-Sikong Yao asked skeptically.

Small wonder he harbored such doubts; the scenery before their eyes could hardly be called identical to the Immortal Realm—in truth, [it bore zero relation whatsoever](# "Literal CN: 不能说一模一样，只能说是毫不相干 — popular Chinese meme: 'can't say it's identical, can only say it's completely unrelated'").

They had entered the concealed "secret realm," traversed the ceaseless ancient God-Demon Battlefield, scaled the ten-thousand-year-old Ascension Stairway, and pushed open the "Immortal Gate" atop the stairs, only to behold a garden.

A shattered garden.

This shattering did not refer to physical fracture—the mundane sort where flowers blossomed in one patch and barren dirt lay in another, nor even like those fractured minor worlds outside where entire continents were smashed to smithereens to drift suspended in the void. Rather, the very space of this garden was in utter disarray: patches of void alternated with patches of garden, and the positions of void and garden were shifting continuously.

Such spatial chaos was exceptionally difficult to describe; if one were pressed for an analogy, it resembled a jagged reef at sea level.

This reef (the garden) lay almost level with the surface of the sea (the void). Consequently, as the ocean waters rose and fell, the surface of the reef was sometimes exposed above water, and sometimes submerged below. And because the ebb and flow of the tide possessed no apparent rhythm—or perhaps did possess a rhythm, but one whose cyclical periodicity was currently impossible to observe—to the ants dwelling upon that reef, they could never discern where sea ended and reef began. What was more, because ants could only navigate in two dimensions across a flat surface, they could never truly comprehend the spatial relationship between the reef beneath their feet (the garden) and the surrounding ocean waters (the void).

And the cultivators were those very pitiful ants.

Naturally, if that were *merely* all, it would hardly have drawn such a lament from Sikong Yao.

What truly shook Sikong Yao was the garden itself.

Just as a reef standing in the sea was bound to suffer the erosion of the crashing waves, for the garden before them to endure under the relentless encroachment of the void, it could be no ordinary garden either.

At first glance, this garden appeared breathtakingly sublime. Flora, trees, and ornamental stones, pavilions, towers, terraces, and kiosks—all were magnificent beyond compare. In particular, the Cloud Pool at the center: billowing clouds and mist flowed murmuring like river water, with expansive lotus leaves standing upon the vapor like crystal chalices, beneath which swarmed countless translucent spirit fish refracting kaleidoscopic radiance as they swam past. The shoals coalesced into a shimmering glazed bridge that cast prismatic hues as it stretched into the distance, where another colossal gate that propped up heaven and earth loomed indistinctly, seemingly another Immortal Gate.

Such a scene could rightfully be called an immortal wonderland without objection. Yet upon closer scrutiny, were those clouds truly clouds? Were those leaves truly leaves? Were those fish truly fish? Was that bridge truly a bridge?

They seemed to be objects within Sikong Yao's realm of cognition, yet exhibited completely disparate properties. Setting aside whether recklessly treading across them would inflict indescribable harm due to those anomalous discrepancies, merely gazing upon them made one feel as though their very cognitive faculties were unraveling.

Thus, after a single glance, Sikong Yao dared not look any longer.

He even tore a strip of cloth from his hem and blindfolded his eyes.

—"If this place is the Immortal Realm, then there is no more perilous fairyland under heaven,"-Sikong Yao grumbled as he tied the blindfold over his eyes:—"Even the deepest abyss of the Abyssal Ocean isn't as chaotic as this... Ah, I don't mean to doubt Untiring, of course!"-he hastily clarified at the end.

In truth, even upon witnessing the sight before them, no one questioned the veracity of Zhou Hui's words.

Everyone trusted Zhou Hui's academic rigor and character; without absolute certainty, he would never speak recklessly. Thus, even if Untiring were to point at a demon and claim it was a True Immortal, anyone acquainted with Untiring—even his bitterest adversaries—might voice disbelief or dispute it with their mouths, yet in their hearts, they would most likely believe him eighty percent.

—"Could it be due to the Ascension Stairway having plummeted in antiquity?"-Peilan attempted to analyze:—"Perhaps the garden that once led to the celestial realm suffered erosion from the void, transforming into this state?"

—"Regardless of its origin, this place is exceptionally perilous,"-Ruwang murmured, wearing a rare expression of vigilance.

He stepped slightly forward to shield Untiring's flank.

Witnessing this, Jiuzhai rolled his eyes.

He felt the person least in need of protection in the entire universe was Untiring.

Still, Jiuzhai remarked:
—"It is indeed exceptionally dangerous here. Time and space are both scrambled. Small wonder we cannot maintain real-time communication with Ran Zhen and his group. Stepping into this place means being completely severed from the outside world."

Hearing this, Sikong Yao promptly planted a tracking mark onto Zhou Hui's person.

—"Heh-heh, I'm just afraid of getting lost,"-Sikong Yao explained sheepishly.

In fact, from the very beginning, he had already locked his divine sense perception firmly onto Zhou Hui.

After all, if what the eyes perceived could inflict cognitive confusion, what of what the divine sense perceived? If one's divine sense suffered corruption, that would be leagues more terrifying than a mere visual illusion.

Thus, Sikong Yao determined that he required a calibration reference.

And Untiring's spiritual energy was so colossal, stable, and self-contained that even amidst space this chaotic, it remained completely unaffected. What better reference point could possibly exist?

Besides, he had been dragged here by Untiring in the first place; Untiring was obligated to ensure his personal safety!

—"Splendid idea!"-Tao Zhu said, noticing Sikong Yao's maneuver.

He patted his round belly and promptly followed suit.

Peilan and Jiuzhai exchanged a glance, and without hesitation, did the exact same thing.

Ruwang's expression darkened instantly, and the theatrical mask upon his brow barked in outrage:
—"How dare you insolent—"

—"No matter,"-Zhou Hui said, resting a hand on Ruwang's shoulder:—"In a place like this, as a precaution, mutual tracking markers are the optimal approach. I was also somewhat concerned that once battle commences, I might overlook someone in the fray."

—"Still, for them to... Wait, battle?"-Ruwang had intended to protest, but his attention was caught by a particular word in Zhou Hui's sentence:—"There are enemies?"

—"You could say that,"-Zhou Hui replied.

As he spoke, he raised a hand. Black radiance coalesced within his palm, looking as though he had casually scooped up his own shadow from the ground. Yet as the black luminescence gathered, that shadow grew deeper, darker, and more slender, as if compressing all darkness into the contours of a longsword. And the instant it solidified into tangible form, it shifted from absolute pitch-black to luminous, jade-like white—pure as moonlight.

Seeing this, astonished expressions spread across the others' faces.

Although Zhou Hui had trained a disciple like Mo Bei, and sword cultivation was among the core academic majors of the Heavenly Spirit Sect, everyone knew that after stepping into the Soul Formation stage, the Untiring Immortal Venerable seldom drew a blade. Some presumed the Immortal Venerable had abandoned swordplay to transition into a pure spell cultivator. In truth, however, those acquainted with Untiring understood that shifting priorities did not mean ceasing sword practice. For an [involution champion](# "Literal CN: 卷王 (juǎnwáng) — extreme grinder / involution champion") to abandon a skill he had already mastered without continuously honing it to perfection? Impossible.

The reason Zhou Hui favored spells was simply that spellcasting dispensed with the physical motion of drawing a sword; it was merely more convenient. Furthermore, half the time Zhou Hui's spells were not even meant as attacks, but rather to achieve auxiliary outcomes—such as capturing heavenly tribulation lightning.

Therefore, when Zhou Hui drew a sword with such deliberate solemnity, it typically meant he had entered an offensive stance.

The thorough kind.

The sort where he didn't intend to leave [even a speck of slag](# "Literal CN: 连渣渣都不剩下 — not even leaving dregs/crumbs") behind for the opponent.

Otherwise, from whom did one think Mo Bei's merciless, resolute swordsmanship had been learned?

[Even Untiring has drawn his sword... Is this adversary truly that formidable?]

The others pondered uneasily.

Fortunately, even a pure physician like Peilan, as a Mahayana cultivator, possessed his own life-preserving arts. Though none knew where the "enemy" lurked, each secretly gripped their defensive treasures, bracing for whatever peril lay concealed within this fractured, eerie garden.

Ruwang took another step closer to Zhou Hui, his body dissolving into shadow to fuse with Zhou Hui's silhouette, ready to provide support at a moment's notice—a combat habit forged during his ancient days as Zhou Hui's personal attendant.

—"Good. Keep yourselves safe,"-Zhou Hui said, perceiving his companions' precautions and nodding in satisfaction.

Then, he initiated an elegant sword stance. A magnificent, lunar halo-like radiance billowed outward, cascading toward the shattered garden.

Instantly, the garden and the void let out a mournful wail.

Or rather, the entirety of space cried out in agonizing lament.

That sound was impossible to describe—the sort of sonic frequency where, had an ordinary mortal heard it, their physical body would have been cleaved into a scatter of severed limbs on the spot. While the powerhouses present were far beyond such a fate, they still suffered a substantial spiritual jolt.

What was more, they could hardly tell whether this shockwave stemmed from the lamentation of fractured space or from Zhou Hui's sword technique itself.

When they looked again, cracks resembling shattered glaze had splintered across the space before them—more accurately described as the spiderweb fractures of bulletproof glass, though the titans had never witnessed bulletproof glass and thus knew nothing of the comparison.

Yet this was merely the prelude.

Zhou Hui began to dance his blade.

His sword forms were leisurely and graceful, his pure white hems fluttering gently with each motion. It looked less like a martial assault than a stately ceremonial performance. Even the unfurling sword light was pure and breathtaking—tender, even, akin to soft moonlight enveloping the earth on a sultry summer night, lulling all living things into rapturous intoxication, dissolving into its glow—

Or so it should have been.

At least, that was how the sword techniques of Zhou Hui that Ruwang and Sikong Yao had witnessed had always unfolded: opponents typically failed to feel the technique's might before inexplicably vanishing from existence altogether.

Yet this time, whether because Zhou Hui was exerting his full might, or because his adversary was the entirety of space itself—or perhaps both—his swordplay grew unimaginably terrifying.

If one were to draw a comparison, it was as if gentle moonlight melting all things had transformed into the literal moon hurtling headlong toward Earth in an apocalyptic collision—though the moon of this world was far more complex than a mere orbital satellite. It appeared... capable of annihilating reality itself. The only "good news" was that before true physical obliteration occurred, everything upon the planetary crust would first be liquefied by apocalyptic heat.

Case in point: the garden and void before them. Their fractures not only widened exponentially, but space itself began to warp and melt, until the entire continuum twisted into a bizarre, grotesque topology.

Amidst this distortion, the distant gate warped alongside it. By all appearances, long before they could walk up to it, the gate would be completely liquefied.

Yet before that could happen, the other powerhouses present felt as though they were about to melt first.

—"As expected of Master... even more formidable than imagined,"-Ruwang murmured.

He was far from idle. Where he had previously intended to assist Zhou Hui in attacking, upon witnessing this spectacle, he decisively pivoted from offense to defense, spreading a colossal shadowy shroud to shield the others.

Yet even though Zhou Hui deliberately steered clear of his companions, they were still caught in the collateral shockwaves. At the very least, Ruwang's protective shroud resembled asphalt in the sweltering summer heat, oozing droplets of black tar.

Witnessing this, Jiuzhai felt like he was losing his mind:

—"Stop praising him at a time like this!"-Jiuzhai roared at Ruwang, before bellowing at Zhou Hui:—"What in the world are you doing?! This path wasn't meant to be traversed like this!"

—"Then how is it supposed to be traversed?"-Peilan asked curiously.

As a renowned physician, his greatest value was realized outside secret realms; thus, when it came to exploring bizarre, high-tier secret realms, Peilan possessed the least experience among the entire party.

Tao Zhu, boss of the Abyssal Ocean Society—the most veteran exploration syndicate in the Abyssal Ocean—clarified:
—"Generally speaking, a garden this uncanny is obviously governed by multiple hidden 'rules.' We ought to tread carefully, deduce those rules, solve the puzzles, eliminate the threats, and only then locate the genuine path leading to the 'Gate.'"

—"Then Untiring...?"-Peilan asked.

—"He's simply carrying out [violent demolition](# "Literal CN: 暴力拆迁 (bàolì chāiqiān) — modern Chinese term for forced, ruthless demolition")"-Tao Zhu said.

He looked toward Zhou Hui, clutching his chest in theatrical grief:—"Untiring, if you keep this up, not a scrap of anything will be left!"

—"No time,"-Zhou Hui answered.

His sword forms continued uninterrupted, yet his breath remained completely unhurried, as though taking a leisurely stroll through a courtyard:
—"Following conventional procedures consumes far too much time. The children outside are still waging the 'God-Demon War.' While I have full faith in their capabilities, if this drags on too long, it wouldn't be ideal if even a single fiend slipped past. We must traverse this area as swiftly as possible to locate the source array reproducing the 'God-Demon War.' Moreover, Ran Zhen is located deeper inside, so we cannot afford to dawdle."

—"B-But doing this... won't it cause the entire space to collapse?!"-Sikong Yao stammered, trembling.

—"Hardly. I've tested it before..."-Zhou Hui paused, seemingly realizing how strange that sounded, and added:—"Previously, I considered violently smashing open a thoroughfare straight to the Heavenly Court to ascend directly. I gave it a try back then—even when I ended up [transmigrating](# "Literal CN: 搞'穿越'了 — accidentally transmigrated to modern Earth") myself, the space didn't shatter. Thus, I deem it feasible; it was simply that my strength last time wasn't sufficient."

[No! How is any part of that 'feasible'?!]

Jiuzhai stared with a bizarre expression:
—"Are you certain your strength is sufficient this time?"

—"Quite certain. At the very least, I found the correct road this time,"-Zhou Hui said.

[Besides, the Heavenly Dao and karmic fortune of this entire world are firmly on my side now. I'd love to see how the Heavenly Court intends to stop me this time!]

Zhou Hui curled his lip in a cynical smirk.

For him, it was a rare moment where his emotions slipped past his restraint. Yet even for Zhou Hui, speaking of these multiple cycles of reincarnation, a persistent sinister fire had smoldered in the depths of his heart.

Thus, during the "ascension" he attempted at the very beginning of this current lifetime—that is, "the previous attempt"—Zhou Hui had indeed carried a vicious urge to smash everything to pieces, intending to punch a direct tunnel from this world straight into the Celestial Realm.

In retrospect, what a foolish and reckless notion that had been.

Although this minor world had once formed part of the Heavenly Court, plummeting from the Heavenly Court onto the Demon Eye was an authentic [dimensional strike](# "Literal CN: 降维打击 (jiàngwéi dǎjī) — dimensional reduction attack / strike from a higher dimension"). In spatial dimensionality alone, it was utterly disparate from the Immortal Realm. Even if Zhou Hui had deliberately sought out spatial rifts back then, attempting to tear them open through brute force to forge a path to the Upper Realm, the requisite power would have required nothing short of a cosmic Big Bang.

In truth, Zhou Hui had suffered a miserable failure back then. Not only had he failed, but his divine soul had been cast into another world entirely, where he laid eyes upon that atrocious, eye-searing Heavenly Dao script concerning this world:

[—Wait. Was that truly a "transmigration"?]

A divine soul traversing across dimensions was plausible enough; perhaps back then, due to the excessive deployment of force, he had punched open a corridor leading into an unknown universe devoid of immortal spellcraft. (Though reflecting upon it now, such a universe ought not to have existed at all, for it lacked even spiritual energy, operating under entirely divergent cosmic laws; breaching that planar boundary should have been far harder than breaching the Heavenly Court directly. For now, he could only categorize it as an idiosyncratic minor world.) Yet no matter what, a "Heavenly Dao script" had no business appearing within that minor world.

At any rate, severely traumatized by that overwhelmingly degenerate script, Zhou Hui had briefly abandoned the notion of "ascension."

Truth be told, Zhou Hui had completely lost interest in ascending to the Heavenly Court by now. Yet if the opportunity arose, he wouldn't mind—while locating Ran Zhen and constructing an even sturdier defensive array for his minor world—"incidentally" smashing open the gates of the Heavenly Court to hunt down the fellow(s) who authored the script, settling the score for these seven lifetimes once and for all.

Besides, this "backdoor" had to be demolished anyway.

This world had already broken free from the Heavenly Court's grip, and even the Ascension Stairway had recognized Zhou Hui as its master. To leave such a backdoor intact was rather absurd.

At that thought, Zhou Hui's sword forms grew even more ruthless.

Finally, as the entire space was hacked into utter smithereens and Zhou Hui's sword wind slammed mercilessly into that colossal gate, an anguished howl echoed from behind the portal:

[—"How dare you insolent—!"](# "Speaker: Gate Guardian Entity")

Along with that roar, the shattered fragments of the garden and the void sticky-tacked themselves together into the shape of a gargantuan titan, lunging a colossal paw down toward Zhou Hui.

Whereupon Zhou Hui cleaved it straight across the face with another sword strike.

***

—"AWOOOO—!"

In the deepest sanctum of the Heavenly Court, at a critical location heavily guarded by elite troops, a deafening shriek suddenly erupted.

Hearing that wail, the celestial soldiers patrolling along the palace ramparts revealed startled expressions, though none dared let their eyes wander. Only after ordering his subordinates to hold their positions on standby did their patrol captain cautiously approach the source of the commotion, asking the guards posted at the outermost gate:

—"Did something happen just now?"-the patrol captain asked.

—"This is the Heavenly Emperor's private vault! What could possibly happen?!"-Guard A replied haughtily.

—"It's merely that Lord Tianlu has an upset stomach..."-Guard B whispered, lowering his voice.

—"Like hell it's 'merely'!"-a furious roar erupted from within the gate, the shrieking creature clearly dissatisfied with this diagnosis.

A violent blast of air blew the doors wide open as a winged beast resembling a colossal tiger burst into view:

—"What are you bastards standing around for?! [Someone's tearing an asshole right through my belly!](# "Literal CN: 老子的肚子要被人开出P眼来了 — mythological reference: Tianlu/Pixiu has no anus, eating treasure and never expelling it; here, Zhou Hui is violently hacking open a hole in its stomach")"-the beast roared furiously at the celestial soldiers outside.

[Wait... whose asshole grows on their stomach?]

[Though then again, a Pixiu doesn't have an asshole to begin with.]

The celestial soldiers exchanged baffled glances, though naturally none dared utter that thought aloud.

Only the patrol captain who had come to inquire asked nervously:
—"Then, Lord Tianlu, should we head to the Department of Celestial Medicine to summon a physician for you?!"

—"Cut the crap and go already!"-the Pixiu glared.

The captain was just about to dart off when the Pixiu barked again:
—"Wait! No! Summon a physician for what, damn it?!"-the Pixiu barked before the captain could leave:—"For my situation, go to the Ministry of Celestial Destiny! Go drag that pack of fortune-tellers from the Ministry of Celestial Destiny over here right now!"

—"Fucking hell! The heavenly mandate is veering off course again!"-the beast howled in fury.

[So... what does that have to do with your asshole?]

The captain desperately wanted to ask, but clamped his mouth shut.
