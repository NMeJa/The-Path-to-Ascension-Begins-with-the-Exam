# Chapter 157: God (第157章 神)

[Who?]

This time, Zhou Hui did not ask.

[A thing happens no more than thrice](# "Literal CN: 事不过三 (shì bù guò sān) — proverb meaning matters should not be repeated beyond three times; after three occurrences, one must act or understand"). For someone of the Untiring Immortal Venerable's terrifying cultivation, taking until the third time to confirm the other party's identity was already exceptionally rare.

Yet once he confirmed where the voice originated, Zhou Hui found himself even more astonished.

—"So it's you?"-he said, sweeping his sleeve to summon a storage pouch.

Zhou Hui did not retrieve anything from within; instead, he dissolved into a wisp of azure smoke and slipped directly inside...

No—to refer to this artifact as a mere "storage pouch" was actually somewhat of an insult.

The interior of this pouch was, in truth, a miniature Sumeru pocket dimension. Yet compared to the most sought-after pocket dimensions in the cultivation realm—overflowing with rich spiritual qi, lush mountains, flowing waters, and fertile spiritual fields—this space contained nothing but a boundless void. It lacked even the faintest wisp of atmospheric gas, desolately empty to a terrifying degree. Its sole redeeming feature was that time flowed with agonizing slowness: approximately one day in the outside world (the cultivation realm) equated to an entire century within this pocket dimension.

Such an abnormal dimension was not formed naturally, but was rather an early experimental trial piece crafted by Zhou Hui.

Back then, Zhou Hui had wanted to test just how far he could stretch the scale of time, so he refined this space. Yet even though the temporal flow was stretched immensely, because the void was entirely barren, it could not be used for cultivation, nor did it serve any other practical purpose. Consequently, Zhou Hui could only casually tuck this prototype away in his collection.

Now, however, within this vacant expanse, a miniature world was revolving in orderly motion.

This world was merely the size of a soccer ball, yet within its bounds the sun rose and the moon set; mountains, waters, oceans, and marshes sprawled across its terrain; mortals, beasts, demons, and spirits dwelled within; it even maintained its own internal cycle of spiritual qi, existing as a completely self-contained cosmos.

Yet precisely because of this perfection, the miniature world had been regarded by the True Immortals as an exotic curiosity. It had been forcibly torn from its native coordinates, hauled entirely into the True Immortal Realm, and nearly "purified" into oblivion.

The timing had been immaculate: back when Zhou Hui had just finished slaying the god and was preparing to deal with those True Immortals, he had rescued this miniature world in passing.

Yet though he had saved it, he had found no suitable place to settle it.

A miniature world was a self-contained ecosystem. Having suffered the violent extraction by the True Immortals, its foundational laws had shattered catastrophically, leaving it incomparably fragile. Wherever it was placed—even if returned to its original spatial coordinates—it was liable to be destroyed, for any external environmental variable could destabilize the delicate operation of this tiny cosmos.

After turning it over in his mind, Zhou Hui had realized that his "experimental prototype" pocket dimension was, ironically, the most ideal sanctuary.

This pocket dimension was completely sealed, eliminating any worry of external interference; its interior was utterly devoid of matter, ensuring nothing could perturb the miniature world's condition. Furthermore, the massive disparity in temporal flow was actually a boon. After all, they could not remain kept in a storage pouch forever, and being severed from the outside world for too long would extinguish all their karmic ties. Yet the miniature world desperately required time to rest and recuperate. This time dilation gave the tiny world ample time to heal before returning to the regular cosmos, while also allowing Zhou Hui to better observe its convalescence and make timely interventions.

And so, he had placed the miniature world inside the pouch.

The little world had proved remarkably resilient.

Forcibly ripped from its original position, its temporal, spatial, and physical laws had plunged into utter chaos and collapse. Yet once placed in a stable, undisturbed environment, it had settled down miraculously, showing faint signs of gradual restoration.

Seeing this, Zhou Hui had breathed a sigh of relief.

Once he confirmed that this miniature world retained the capacity to self-repair, he stopped disturbing it frequently, merely checking in on it a few times a day on schedule.

Zhou Hui planned to wait until the miniature world had fully recovered before finding a suitable location to settle it—naturally, finding its original spatial coordinates would be ideal.

What he had not anticipated was that this miniature world would proactively seek him out.

Entering the pocket dimension, Zhou Hui hovered in the void. He did not rashly approach the world, lest he incite panic among its inhabitants. Besides, if the other party could peer through the boundary of the pocket dimension to observe him and speak to him, they would certainly perceive his arrival.

And indeed they did.

The moment Zhou Hui entered the space, he saw the miniature world begin to surge and ripple.

The spiritual energy of the entire world coalesced into a humanoid silhouette, faintly resembling a woman. The sun and moon were her eyes; the stars formed the shimmering fabric of her gown. Even so, she was only the size of a doll, small enough to rest in the palm of a hand.

Catching sight of Zhou Hui, the woman immediately dropped gracefully to her knees, offering a deep, formal bow:

—"Lord Immortal Venerable, thank you for your benevolence!"-she said.-"Were it not for you, this world would have been utterly annihilated."

—"Are you the god of this world?"-Zhou Hui asked.

Indeed, he genuinely sensed a godhead emanating from her—and an extraordinarily vast one at that.

Far more vast than even that so-called "god" residing in the True Immortal Realm.

—"If by that you mean whether I created the living beings of this world, then yes, I am,"-the goddess said.-"Yet as you can see, I too am merely a part of this world. My power is limited; I could not even protect my own realm. It was entirely thanks to your assistance that this world was preserved."

Here, a faint blush rose upon the goddess's cheeks:

—"I am deeply ashamed,"-she murmured.-"I ought to have offered my gratitude to you back then, but at the time, my power had been thoroughly depleted and I was on the verge of dispersing entirely. I only managed to recover recently."

—"No need to take it to heart. I merely did so in passing,"-Zhou Hui replied.

He had never saved them to seek their gratitude in the first place. If anything, the fact that this miniature world actually housed a deity surprised him somewhat.

He looked at her with curiosity:

—"Your recovery—does that mean this world has recovered as well?"-he asked.

—"It is not that simple,"-the goddess said, a sorrowful expression clouding her face as she looked down at her miniature world and shook her head.-"Though the exterior appears flourishing, the civilizations upon its surface have already turned over three whole generations."

["..."]

As expected, a cosmic catastrophe of that magnitude wasn't something that could be recovered from so easily!

Zhou Hui let out a silent sigh in tandem.

—"Still, even though they are creatures I brought into being, the fact that they have endured to this extent has already amazed me,"-the goddess said. She cast a gentle, motherly glance down at her world, then lowered her gaze.-"By contrast, I, this so-called 'god,' am utterly powerless to answer their hopes. I cannot even repay my debt of gratitude to my savior."

—"There is really no need to—"-Zhou Hui started to say, intending to decline.

—"It is just that, when I was probing the state of this space—forgive me, I was not intentionally spying on you; having recovered a sliver of power, I merely wished to confirm where my world was situated. But my power was evidently so faint that you could not even perceive it,"-the goddess explained in haste.

She quickly added:

—"It was also then that I realized... you seem to have very little experience in being a god? It might be rather presumptuous of me to say, but my only advantage is that I have been a deity for an exceedingly long time. Perhaps I might offer you some guidance in this regard?"

[How to be a god?]

Zhou Hui raised an eyebrow slightly.

Truth be told, he harbored no great enthusiasm for the prospect of "attaining godhood." After all, Zhou Hui had no intention of dominating or controlling anything, nor did he desire people to revere or worship him. Even so, he did not decline the goddess's offer of "repayment."

For while he had no interest in "how to act as a god," he was deeply intrigued by the underlying mechanics and theoretical principles governing deities and godheads. Zhou Hui could never refuse the acquisition of new knowledge.

What was more, Zhou Hui happened to be in urgent need of knowledge in this field right now.

If nothing else, take the most recent occurrence: Qu Qianxing had attained power, and his own godhead had inexplicably advanced in tandem. Zhou Hui hadn't simply celebrated and moved on; on the contrary, he worried whether this chain reaction might inflict unintended side effects upon Qu Qianxing.

—"Of course not,"-the goddess replied to Zhou Hui's inquiry.-"If anything, it is precisely because your disciple places his faith in you rather than the Heavenly Court that his power feeds back into your being."

—"Faith, is it?"-Zhou Hui murmured, furrowing his brow.

He preferred that his disciples possess independent critical thinking, rather than placing blind "faith" in some specific, anthropomorphic entity—even if that entity happened to be himself.

Then again, on second thought... did Qu Qianxing and the rest of his disciples actually lack "independent critical thinking"?

Taking Qu Qianxing alone: this was an immortal—no, an astral constellation!—who, the moment Zhou Hui looked away for a brief instant, went right ahead and conquered the entire Demon Realm!

[...Whatever. As long as they're happy.]

Zhou Hui decided that when dealing with his disciples, [feigning ignorance was bliss](# "Literal CN: 难得糊涂 (nán dé hú tu) — famously coined by Qing scholar Zheng Banqiao: 'rare is the wisdom to be muddle-headed'; choosing blissful ignorance or not sweating the details"), and promptly abandoned that line of thought.

Turning his attention back, he asked:

—"Are you saying that so long as one possesses the 'faith of believers,' one's godhead can be elevated?"

—"That is the defining trait of an 'Anthropic God,'"-the goddess said, a faint, cold sneer touching her lips.

It was a ruthless, chilling smile.

Until this very moment, the goddess had displayed thoroughly human warmth. She had seemed akin to an exquisite, sensitive maiden, politely and earnestly striving to thank her benefactor with heartfelt deference.

Yet this smile she wore now, while dazzlingly beautiful, was utterly devoid of emotion.

In a flat, dispassionate voice, the goddess suddenly asked:

—"Lord Immortal Venerable, what do you believe a 'god' truly is?"

—"Well..."-Zhou Hui paused, organizing his thoughts.

In truth, he had been pondering this very question all along.

As a cultivator, Zhou Hui himself sought to become an immortal, to become a god. People like him were the furthest thing from devout believers. If anything, arrogant cultivators of their caliber were the world's most ardent atheists.

Yet the existence of "gods" in this world was incontrovertible.

The goddess standing before him was living proof.

After a moment of contemplation, Zhou Hui ventured:

—"In my view... a deity is probably a type of spirit-monster, isn't it?"

—"A spirit-monster?!"-the goddess echoed. She clearly had not anticipated such an answer; her eyes widened in pure astonishment.

—"Or rather, should I say a manifestation of cosmic rules?"-Zhou Hui offered equivocally.

He deliberately used the word "universe" to differentiate between his current world and that other universe he had once visited in his dreams.

In that other universe, Zhou Hui had been a thoroughgoing atheist.

He believed that universe possessed no "gods"—or rather, it was a purely materialistic world. Therefore, their stones did not gain sentience, their animals did not transform into demons, and even humans... philosophically speaking, could humans in that world truly be certain that their consciousness was anything more than a chaotic stack of hormones, bioelectric pulses, and physiological-physical states? Much like the Artificial Intelligence they created: possessing databases vast enough that it merely appeared to have developed self-awareness?

And in that universe, Earth and all other celestial bodies—though he had not been able to confirm every single one—seemed to operate strictly upon that same principle.

By contrast, this cultivation universe was an idealist cosmos.

Stones could attain sentience, animals could cultivate into demons, and even the toxic miasma lurking in deep mountain forests could coalesce into wandering specters.

If so, on what grounds could anyone claim that "rules" could not gain sentience?

—"Humans are social animals; for their collective long-term development, they abide by certain rules when conducting their affairs,"-Zhou Hui said.-"Therefore, I suspect so-called deities are simply certain rules gaining sentience. For instance, as long as people adhere to certain customs to accumulate wealth, once that shared convention attains sapience, it gives birth to a God of Wealth. By extension, the same applies to Gods of Fire, Gods of Water, and the like."

Pausing for a beat, Zhou Hui added:

—"Naturally, this is mere speculation on my part. After all, it fails to explain how a 'Creator Deity' like yourself came into being."

—"...No, you have practically touched upon the fundamental essence,"-the goddess said, utterly dumbfounded.-"As for Creator Deities, that belongs to a separate system entirely. But spirit-monsters... gods are just a bunch of spirit-monsters! Haha... Hahahahahaha! Truly, utterly brilliant!"

The goddess threw back her head and laughed uproariously, completely abandoning all divine poise.
