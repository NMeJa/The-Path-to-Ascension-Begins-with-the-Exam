# Chapter 171: The Demon Race's Calculations

—"Saved, you say..."-a Wood Spirit breathed, releasing a sigh that went unheard by anyone else.

To invoke the word "saved" clearly indicated that the demon race was confronting a catastrophe of immense proportions.

Yet this was hardly any secret.

In truth, anyone with even a passing knowledge of history would notice that the population and diversity of the demon race had been experiencing a steep, perpetual decline.

Tracing back to the ancient era, long before the human race flourished, it was the demon race that reigned as the absolute masters of the Great Chiliocosm.

In those primordial days, high-ranking demons governed the majority of worlds. They erected a magnificent Demon Court that rivaled—if not surpassed—the grandeur of the present-day Heavenly Court. Lesser demons roamed freely across the realms of heaven and earth, and even terrifying ferocious beasts could gallop across the cosmos unchecked.

Until a certain war broke out.

A devastating war between the Demon Imperial Court and the Ancient Gods.

Regrettably, the demon race possessed no tradition of chronicling history. Consequently, posterity remained in the dark as to why the Demon Court and the Ancient Gods had gone to war in the first place, or even what the Ancient Gods truly were and how they differed from modern deities.

Except for beings like Zhou Hui, practically no one cared anyway. Even the demons themselves were utterly indifferent toward their own past.

The sole undeniable truth was that during that apocalyptic war, the Demon Court fell, and countless demons perished. Yet the Ancient Gods fared little better, suffering catastrophic casualties that wiped out more than half their number. Seizing this window, the human race rose to dominance, founding their own Heavenly Court.

From that moment on, the demon race plunged into decline.

Yet this decline harbored a peculiar eeriness.

By all logic, even in defeat, the sheer reproductive fertility of numerous demon species should have prevented them from being [completely crippled](# "Chinese Idiom: 一蹶不振 (yī jué bù zhèn) — to collapse after a single setback; never to recover") for all eternity. What was more, demons were born with innate demon cores; unlike humans who had to study and cultivate from scratch to embark upon the Dao, demons possessed natural physical gifts and divine talents. Even if they suffered a temporary collapse, opportunities for revival should have arisen over the millennia. Instead, the demon race's fortunes grew progressively bleaker.

They tumbled from cosmic sovereigns to an endangered minority, and from an endangered minority to designated "enemies" inextricably tied to the demon-fiend faction. Some scholars even argued that the primordial devils originated from the demon race, coining the joint term [demon-fiends](# "Cultural/Linguistic: 妖魔 (yāomó) — compound of demon (妖) and devil/fiend (魔), traditionally lumped together as villains across worlds") as they were cast as the archetypal villains of nearly every realm.

Now, however, the demon race was on the verge of losing even that status.

In recent epochs, the number of humans falling into demonic depravity had skyrocketed. Fiend monarchs and fiend sovereigns were increasingly replaced by human fiend cultivators, leaving genuine demons relegated to pathetic underlings and cannon fodder while their total population dwindled even further.

If this trajectory continued, the demon race might one day face complete extinction.

As the Saintess of the demon race, Shui Huang'er had naturally observed this existential crisis and fretted over it ceaselessly.

Unfortunately, she could find no solution within the demon realm. Her gaze naturally shifted with keen curiosity toward the flourishing human race, prompting her to sneak away to the Central Domain—though she could hardly deny that her own desire to play and see the world played a considerable part.

This was precisely why Shui Huang'er had been so captivated the moment she laid eyes on Zhou Hui's educational syllabus, resolving to propagate it across the demon race without the slightest hesitation.

Now that she had witnessed the Untiring Immortal Venerable's terrifying might with her own eyes, Shui Huang'er felt all the more confident in her demon educational reform.

The Wood Spirits, however, did not share her optimism.

—"He is of the human race,"-the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree's Wood Spirit said.-"No matter what, humans would never want the demon race to prosper."

—"Naturally I know that, and I'm hardly foolish enough to plead with him directly!"-Shui Huang'er replied in the depths of her mind.

She tilted her head back, gazing up at that silver-haired Immortal Venerable.

The Immortal Venerable appeared entirely oblivious to the mental communion taking place between Shui Huang'er and the Wood Spirits. His gaze was fixed intently upon his disciple in the midst of breaking through, appearing even more focused and solemn than if he were undergoing a personal ascension tribulation himself.

[So this is the 'legacy' of humanity,] Shui Huang'er mused.

While high-ranking demons like the phoenix clan possessed their own heritage—inscribing the mastery of power directly into their bloodline and marrow—the human transmission of legacy encompassed profound spiritual dimensions far beyond mere brute strength.

At the very least, having such a master by one's side would grant someone the fortitude to gaze upon the future with unshakable conviction—just like Mo Bei, who swung her sword without a shred of hesitation.

—Ah, not that she meant any disrespect to the Wood Spirit Elders who had guided her.

It was simply that when trees cultivated into spirits, their thinking was inevitably stiff and inflexible.

Having considered this, Shui Huang'er continued her telepathic dialogue with the Wood Spirits:

—"A human Immortal Venerable certainly won't help the demon race replenish our population out of the goodness of his heart. However, humans always harbor obsessions that transcend mere survival instincts. So long as that holds true, the foundation for cooperation exists."

—"Do you believe this person would betray the human race's stance for the sake of his personal obsessions?"-a Wood Spirit asked.

—"How could that be possible? This Immortal Venerable is practically a [sage without flaw](# "Chinese Idiom: 完人 (wánrén) — a person of perfect virtue and flawless moral character; an untarnished paragon"),"-Shui Huang'er denied almost instantly.-"Speaking from pure phoenix instinct, from the moment I laid eyes on him, I felt an overpowering urge to fulfill his every wish and carry out his every instruction."

—"So because of an instinctive attraction, you speak on his behalf with all your might, going so far as to introduce his teaching pedagogy regardless of the cost?"-A trace of malice and vigilance laced the Wood Spirit's voice.

There was no helping it; for the demon race, "instinct" was a grave and perilous matter.

Demons were a race governed by primordial nature. Low-tier demons wallowed in visceral sensory desires, while high-tier demons appeared somewhat refined, displaying outward self-restraint. In reality, however, the instinctual appetites of high-tier demons merely took on different forms.

For instance, ancient lore held that the phoenix was a [spiritual bird of benevolent auspices](# "Chinese Tradition: 仁瑞 (rénruì) — auspicious omen of benevolence; ancient mythology holds that phoenixes only appear in reigns of moral virtue"), naturally drawn to individuals of peerless virtue.

Of course, that was merely the human race's wishful romanticization.

The reality was that the formidable power of the phoenix clan stemmed from the "Heavenly Mandate." They possessed the innate ability to draw essence from providence and refine it into cultivation. Consequently, phoenixes instinctively gravitated toward individuals of great destiny to satisfy their innate hunger for power, indifferent even to serving as mounts or pets in the process.

Yet unlike humans, who assumed a "person of destiny" was simply someone blessed with absurd karmic fortune, what the phoenix perceived was not an isolated individual, but the broader "Heavenly Mandate." In an era where the human race reigned supreme, human morality was the true "Heavenly Dao."

To the phoenix clan, a "sage" who walked the true Great Dao and upheld the moral principles established by humanity—even if he was not favored by personal fortune—could furnish them with far greater cultivation nourishment than any lucky "child of destiny."

Without a shadow of a doubt, Zhou Hui met every single criterion.

It was precisely for this reason that several Wood Spirit Elders harbored deep suspicions regarding Shui Huang'er's introduction of Zhou Hui's "educational reform."

They suspected their young Saintess had been thoroughly enchanted by her demonic instincts.

In truth, the Wood Spirit Elders who held such suspicions were in the minority; knowing Shui Huang'er's sharp nature, they merely grumbled rather than genuinely doubting her loyalty.

The real skepticism erupted from the ordinary demons.

Particularly the faction that despised going to school.

Indeed, quite apart from the looming threat of extinction, Shui Huang'er's "educational reform" had run headfirst into massive resistance.

This was only to be expected.

Given the fierce, unruly temperament of most demons, forcing them to sit quietly and listen to lectures was more agonizing than flaying them alive. Even if they had been temporarily dazzled by the livestreams of the human Immortal Arts Assembly and signed up for remedial classes in a burst of reckless excitement, they barely lasted ten minutes into the first lesson before thrashing in unbearable restlessness.

At that critical juncture, Heavenly Spirit Sect's signature method of disciplinary punishment—or rather, "pedagogy"—proved utterly decisive.

For rebellious demons, [combating violence with violence](# "Chinese Idiom: 以暴制暴 (yǐ bào zhì bào) — meeting force with force") was by far the most effective remedy.

It was the only language they truly understood.

Attempting to reason with them politely yielded next to nothing. But string them from the tree branches, slap on restrictive silencing arrays, and establish [the law of the jungle](# "Chinese Idiom: 弱肉强食 (ruò ròu qiáng shí) — the weak are meat for the strong to eat")—and the demons instantly proved far more obedient than humans, at least ensuring their physical bodies sat quietly in the classroom.

When Shui Huang'er had proudly paraded her "academic achievements" before Zhou Hui, she certainly had not meant to mock or disrespect him. She genuinely, wholeheartedly believed that the Lord Immortal Venerable's pedagogical methods were absolute perfection—little wonder he had nurtured an immortal fairy-like sword cultivator like Mo Bei!

Still, while those disciplined young demons submitted on the surface, resentment simmered fiercely within.

A full-blown conspiracy theory was currently sweeping through the demon realm, alleging that their Saintess had been seduced by a human and was preparing to sell out the entire demon race.

This rhetoric rippled through the foliage and boughs of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree until it reached the ears of the Wood Spirit Elders.

Although the elders trusted Shui Huang'er, watching their Saintess fawn over a human male compelled them to drop sarcastic reminders, warning her not to overstep the line.

Evidently, proposing that "a human Immortal Venerable could save the demon race" crossed that line decisively.

Shui Huang'er scoffed at their suspicions, finding them utterly baseless:

—"That I introduced the human curriculum because I was blinded by instinct? What an idiotic joke!"-Shui Huang'er sneered.-"Leaving aside the fact that as the Saintess of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, it is 'utterly impossible' for innate or acquired passions to exert complete control over me! Even if it were ordinary demons—no, even the fiend race most easily enslaved by base desires! If they were to lay eyes upon this Lord Immortal Venerable, even if he stripped bare and offered himself to be devoured, would any of them dare harbor a single blasphemous thought?!

—"The Untiring Immortal Venerable's sheer majesty rivals that of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree itself. A single glance from him turns [monsters and demons of every stripe](# "Chinese Idiom: 牛鬼蛇神 / 妖鬼蛇神 — ox-ghosts and serpent-spirits; all kinds of sinister and freakish beings") into quivering jelly, completely stripping any creature of any desire to feel 'lust' toward him. In fact, I am intensely curious what living creature could possibly dare harbor improper designs on him!"

The Wood Spirit Elders offered no counterargument.

Evidently, they too conceded that the Untiring Immortal Venerable commanded an aura of supreme dignity; even compared directly to their Sacred Tree, he did not lose an ounce of stature.

From the perspective of unorthodox fiends and monsters, attempting to assassinate someone like Untiring was infinitely easier than attempting to seduce him.

The Wood Spirits suddenly seized upon the contradiction in Shui Huang'er's stance:

—"Since you consider him a sage and deem him immune to seduction, how do you plan to exploit him?"-a Wood Spirit asked.

—"'Exploit' is far too ugly a word; 'mutual benefit' is much more fitting,"-Shui Huang'er corrected.-"Didn't I explain it to you all before? This is a rare, golden opportunity. The Untiring Immortal Venerable is clearly accumulating karmic merit for some goal of his own, which is why he is willing to propagate his teaching pedagogy and doesn't even shun the demon race."

This was the exact reasoning Shui Huang'er had used previously to convince the Wood Spirits and demon leaders.

The Wood Spirit Elders, however, felt that importing human educational methods was already pushing the boundaries, let alone allowing an outsider to meddle deeper into internal demon affairs.

—"Even so, there are limits,"-a Wood Spirit Elder urged persistently.-"Even if he truly wishes to amass karmic merit, what merit could he possibly gain by aiding the demon race? He'd be fortunate if it didn't erode his merit! If he discovers your scheme, what if he turns around and 'accords with the Heavenly Dao' by slaying us to amass merit for ascension?!"

[—"What if he has no intention of ascending?"](# "Speaker: Shui Huang'er")

[—"No intention of ascending? How could any human not intend to ascend?"](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit Elder")

—"That might not be true,"-another Wood Spirit muttered before Shui Huang'er could even speak.-"Given the level of power he commands, he could have stepped into immortality long ago."

[—"More than that. A 'sage' recognized by a phoenix—and a phoenix of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree at that—has long qualified to the point where those celestial charlatans in the Heavenly Court would ignore cultivation stage and lifespan, hauling him up by any means necessary."](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit Elder")

[—"Tee-hee, is the Heavenly Court that short on hands?"](# "Speaker: Young Wood Spirit")

—"This is not a matter of whether they lack manpower; they simply cannot allow a sage to remain in the mortal realms,"-an extraordinarily ancient voice spoke up.

The Wood Spirits fell instantly silent.

The ancient voice continued:

—"You ought to know that the sage of any race possesses the power to elevate their people's providential fate and cause their kind to flourish. Now that the human race has already risen to dominance, a human sage wields power far surpassing the sages of other races. If steered in the right direction, they could even elevate their entire race—just as the First Sage of the demon race did in ancient times by unlocking the spiritual intellect of all living creatures. At such a juncture, establishing an independent Heavenly Court is by no means impossible."

—"...Then, will the Heavenly Court allow it?"-a Wood Spirit asked tremblingly, catching the foreboding implications.

—"Of course not,"-the ancient voice sneered.-"Which is why they opened an express ascension corridor for 'sages.' Regardless of how far the individual might reach, bestowing a celestial post first is never a mistake. As for whether those 'sages' will ever have the means to uplift their homeworld afterward, that remains entirely unknown."

[—"As expected of the human race's Heavenly Court."](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit Elder")

A chorus of Wood Spirits broke into a chuckle, [gloating in Schadenfreude](# "Chinese Idiom: 幸灾乐祸 (xìng zāi lè huò) — rejoicing in other people's misfortune").

They did not believe humans would ever aid the demon race, largely because as demons, they harbored little goodwill toward humanity in turn.

Ignoring their chatter, Shui Huang'er remarked:

—"Yet right here stands an exception."

—"Indeed. Why hasn't the Heavenly Court brought him up?"-the Wood Spirits murmured in bewilderment.

—"That matters not. I cannot know everything regarding the humans' affairs, nor do I have any interest in knowing,"-Shui Huang'er said, the usually passionate demon phoenix revealing a cold, calculating edge.-"All I know right now is that this human Immortal Venerable has chosen to stay. You... do you understand what that signifies?"

In that instant, the Wood Spirit Elders fell dead silent.

They understood all too well.

As Wood Spirits, their intellectual cunning might not rival that of humanity. Yet as symbiotic entities of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree, they had inhabited this soil for ten million years, witnessing the endless cycle of life and death across countless civilizations. In a situation like this, they required no complex deductions; raw millennia of experience told them precisely how events would unfold:

If Zhou Hui was firmly resolved not to leave this minor world, humanity would reach an epochal turning point and perhaps surge to even greater heights. Before that could happen, however, the wrathful lightning of the Heavenly Court would inevitably descend. He... *They* would move heaven and earth to halt this divergence.

For this minor world, an apocalyptic catastrophe loomed.

Yet just as Shui Huang'er said, as demons, they did not care in the slightest.

When tempests raged and lightning struck, the first to be blasted was always the tallest tree.

If that tree was insufficiently sturdy or decayed with age, it would ignite, sparking a colossal conflagration. Yet even the fiercest inferno would eventually burn out, and the resulting ashes would become the richest fertilizer for newborn saplings.

If that tree did not perish, weathering every single strike of celestial lightning—that was better still.

The tree would continue to burgeon within this world. Because it was already so gargantuan, it could never be transplanted elsewhere. To survive within this fractured, shattered realm, it would be forced to drive its roots deeper and deeper into the bedrock of the earth veins. Throughout that process, while "it" would inevitably drink immense quantities of spiritual energy from the subterranean depths, so long as it never departed, those vast energies would inevitably cycle back to nourish the entire world.

When that time came, this Immortal Venerable would become an entity akin to the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree—a permanent, foundational pillar of the world itself.

To the Wood Spirits, this was far from a bad outcome.

In fact, it was an immense boon.

After all, so long as a sovereign had not fallen to demonic depravity, none wished to see their home realm reduced to ruin. Zhou Hui, in particular, looked every inch the upright scholar; one could reasonably anticipate that he would construct an orderly, stable world. And in a flourishing, righteous world, whatever else might be lacking, lush flora would never be absent. The Wood Spirits would never need to worry about their survival. With luck, they might even establish a thriving new ecosystem just like the one here at the Sacred Tree—word had it that the Untiring Immortal Venerable had a massive peach tree planted right outside his mountain gate specifically to hang failing students, a disciplinary practice that suited the Wood Spirit Elders' tastes splendidly.

Even so, the Wood Spirit Elders had no desire to take proactive measures.

Such was the nature of plants: they never took the initiative. Time would solve all problems, and time was the cheapest, most worthless commodity to flora.

The Wood Spirits turned their attention back to Shui Huang'er:

—"You wish to claim a seat for us before he 'fully matures'?"

[—"It is not a bad idea. If he truly can vanquish the Heavenly Court and seize control over this realm's Heavenly Dao, he might genuinely carve out new living space for our demon race to multiply—at the very least, he should be able to unearth clues regarding our decline from the ancient Heavenly Dao."](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit Elder")

[—"Still, there is no need to get involved too early, is there?"](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit")

[—"Exactly, exactly! It would be terrible if we were dragged into it."](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit")

[—"While we don't fear the Heavenly Court, there's no reason to provoke that pack of lunatics and place an unnecessary burden upon our Sacred Tree."](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit Elder")

The Wood Spirits were [chattering all at once](# "Chinese Idiom: 七嘴八舌 (qī zuǐ bā shé) — seven mouths and eight tongues; everyone talking at the same time").

As a phoenix bird, Shui Huang'er refused to indulge in [slacking off](# "Modern slang: 摆烂 (bǎilàn) — letting things rot; giving up and doing nothing") like the plant spirits.

—"By then, it will be far too late,"-Shui Huang'er said coldly.-"You said it yourselves: humans will never help us without cause. If we wait until the victor is decided before choosing our stance, the Immortal Venerable will have no need for us. Only while he is still gathering karmic merit can we secure a place within his future world."

[—"But humans cannot be trusted..."](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit")

[—"And the Heavenly Court is not to be provoked lightly..."](# "Speaker: Wood Spirit")

The Wood Spirits were not so easily swayed, stubborn in their objections.

Rather than continuing to debate them, Shui Huang'er quietly waited for the one entity whose word truly mattered.

At last, that ancient voice resonated once more:

—"Since you consider this an opportunity, then you may give it a try."

Hearing this, the corners of Shui Huang'er's mouth curved slightly.

Yet before a smile could bloom across her face, the ancient voice continued:

—"We can follow your plan and present our 'Supreme Treasure' as a gesture of goodwill, but that is all. Beyond that, not a single demon may provide assistance or guidance. Whether he can take it depends entirely upon his own ability."

—"What?!"-Shui Huang'er froze.-"Is that forging a bond or forging a grudge?! What I mean is, without proper guidance, our 'Supreme Treasure' is virtually indistinguishable from a catastrophe! It might cause him to mistake it for a test—"

—"It is a test,"-the ancient voice answered coldly.

[—"If he lacks even the ability to employ our 'Supreme Treasure,' how will he ever stand against the Heavenly Court in the days to come?"](# "Speaker: Ancient Wood Spirit")

[—"And if, upon being gifted the 'Supreme Treasure,' he harbors resentment simply because he cannot use it or handles it improperly, how can we expect him to grant us a sanctuary after he triumphs?"](# "Speaker: Ancient Wood Spirit")

[—"I imagine the Immortal Venerable himself would approve of such a method, would he not? Does he not take immense delight in testing his students?"](# "Speaker: Ancient Wood Spirit")

[—"Then taking this as the beginning of our cooperation is rather fitting."](# "Speaker: Ancient Wood Spirit")

[...Easy enough for you to say, but if the Lord Immortal Venerable fails, it's not like we could hang him from a tree branch, could we?!]

Shui Huang'er thought, a trace of existential despair creeping in.

* * *

While Shui Huang'er and the Wood Spirits reached their conclusion on one side, Mo Xi's breakthrough drew to its final moments on the other.

[He's truly acting reckless,] Mo Bei thought.

Even someone of her nonchalant disposition understood that for a cultivator, breaking through a major cultivation realm was a matter of paramount importance.

Ordinary cultivators, to say nothing of an actual breakthrough, would seek out the safest, most harmonious cultivation grounds and lay down layers of protective warding arrays the second they felt the faintest stirring of an impending bottleneck, dreading any external interference.

Only her idiot younger brother would dare initiate a major breakthrough right in the dead center of the demon race's ancestral domain.

And Master spoiled him rotten, actually serving as his personal Dharma Protector!

Mo Bei grumbled helplessly in her heart, yet from the very beginning, her hand had never strayed an inch from the sword hilt at her waist, nor had her divine perception drifted from her "idiot brother" for a single breath.

In truth, Mo Bei understood Mo Xi's condition far better than anyone else present.

After all, having been bred as [paired dual-cultivation vessels](# "Xianxia trope: 双修道具 (shuāngxiū dàojù) — dehumanizing classification of captive cultivators bred to be used and discarded for breakthrough power") destined for market, the Mo twins shared an innate, mystical resonance that extended beyond simple telepathy into their cultivation.

To be precise, Mo Bei and Mo Xi shared an identical origin. According to their [factory settings](# "Modern tech metaphor: 出厂配置 (chūchǎng pèizhì) — original stock configuration"), the spiritual energy of both siblings originally manifested as a viscous black liquid. When they kissed and embraced—disgusting violations that Fallen Immortal Palace had forced upon them in their youth, though fortunately they were too young at the time to be subjected to further horrors—they could actively share each other's spiritual energy, cast spells in tandem, or even cultivate on the other's behalf, achieving an intertwined synchronization of cultivation progress. Thus, even though Mo Xi was a male ill-suited for the Extreme Yin Constitution, in the late stages he could have kept pace with his elder twin sister's cultivation at the cost of excruciating agony and bodily mutation, before being sold off together by Fallen Immortal Palace as a premium breakthrough commodity.

Naturally, that dark future no longer existed.

Especially after Mo Xi altered his cultivation technique.

Under Master's meticulous guidance, the spiritual energy coursing through Mo Xi's body had sublimated into golden-crimson flames, achieving a delicate, harmonious equilibrium between his Extreme Yin Constitution and his masculine frame.

Meanwhile, Mo Bei was also progressing—in the exact opposite direction.

The spiritual energy within Mo Bei was steadily freezing, layer upon layer, cycle upon cycle. According to Master's requirements, her spiritual energy had to be tempered to the resilience of ten-thousand-year glacial ice before she could forge the next stage of her "sword embryo," at which point she would claim her natal spiritual blade.

Consequently, the shared resonance between Mo Bei and Mo Xi had been severed completely.

Fire and ice could never coexist.

Neither Mo Bei nor Mo Xi felt the slightest regret. Barring coercion, neither had any desire to cultivate on the other's behalf or permit the other to squander their hard-won spiritual energy. In fact, both siblings privately regarded their twin as a complete idiot; sharing spiritual energy would only get them dragged down by the other's blunders, to say nothing of the prerequisite physical intimacy required to activate it.

Both twins considered themselves distant and detached from one another. Their current dynamic—sensing the other's cultivation state, perceiving when the other was in danger, and reminding them not to court death—was just right.

At this very moment, for instance, Mo Bei could clearly perceive that Mo Xi was smoothly condensing his golden core.

It was a process of [discarding the dross and preserving the essence](# "Chinese Idiom: 去芜存菁 (qù wú cún jīng) — weeding out the chaff to extract the purest core").

During core condensation, spiritual flames incinerated the impure residues accumulated within the foundational base, leaving behind refined essence to coalesce into a golden core. Depending on the flame utilized and the innate luster of the core, golden cores differed wildly in quality. The further one advanced into later realms, the more pronounced this disparity became—in extreme cases, the gap between two cultivators of the same nominal realm was wider than the gap between a human and a dog.

The golden core Mo Xi was currently forming undoubtedly belonged to the supreme tier.

Even stripped of the technique tailored to his Extreme Yin Constitution, Mo Xi's spiritual roots and aptitude remained at the pinnacle of talent. Backed by the foundation of a supreme spirit stone vein, his spiritual energy radiated a dazzling golden-crimson brilliance, appearing as though it were perpetually ablaze even at rest. In any other sect, spiritual energy of such refinement would have qualified him to condense a core long ago. Yet compared to his senior martial brothers... no, even compared to his elder twin sister, Mo Xi's fundamentals had appeared relatively unpolished. Everyone had assumed Mo Xi would form his core well after Mo Bei, and that if he slacked off, lagging seven or eight years behind would be entirely normal.

Yet in the cultivation world, karmic opportunity was often far more potent than sheer diligence.

This expedition to the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree had miraculously brought Mo Xi into contact with the purest demon fire at the summit of the canopy. Most exquisitely of all, demons were Yin entities while fire was Pure Yang; this demon fire harmonized flawlessly with Mo Xi's masculine Extreme Yin Constitution, refining and elevating his spiritual energy to its absolute zenith.

[I suppose fortune favors fools,] Mo Bei mocked dryly in her heart as she felt Mo Xi's golden core solidify.

—"Are you not jealous?"-an abrupt voice asked out of nowhere.

Mo Bei's expression did not flicker. Turning her head, she surveyed her surroundings before looking toward her master.

—"What is the matter?"-Zhou Hui asked.

Though he kept a watchful eye on Mo Xi's progress, he never neglected his other disciples.

—"I heard a malicious voice attempting to drive a wedge between Mo Xi and me,"-Mo Bei said.

—"A voice? I didn't hear any voice?"-Tao Zhu piped up before Zhou Hui could speak. He certainly lacked the iron-plated nerves of the Heavenly Spirit Sect master and disciples—to think they would stage a breakthrough right in the heart of demon territory! Consequently, he had remained in a state of extreme, fidgety vigilance the entire time.

Tao Zhu glanced around, finding it improbable that anything could bypass the divine senses of a Mahayana powerhouse to whisper directly to a Foundation Establishment junior—especially with Untiring standing right there! Tao Zhu knew this fiercely protective teacher would never permit an outsider to slander his disciples or sow discord among them.

Tao Zhu deduced a plausible explanation:

—"You might have been affected by demonic qi. That wouldn't be surprising—demonic qi is similar to fiendish qi, amplifying the dark corners of the soul—"

—"The dark corners?"-Mo Bei repeated. She gave an expressionless nod.-"That makes a lot of sense. I've always thought that fellow was an idiot, yet by some stroke of dumb luck, this idiot formed his core before me. By all standard logic, I ought to be jealous."

—"You..."-Tao Zhu's potbelly quivered.

While he had offered the possibility, he had never expected the young girl to admit it so nonchalantly, as though being jealous of her younger brother were the most natural thing in the world.

Tao Zhu hardly considered this a healthy mindset and shot Zhou Hui an uneasy glance.

Zhou Hui had raised an eyebrow as well, appearing on the verge of offering parental guidance to his disciple. Yet before he could speak, the young sword cultivator had already shifted her gaze toward the brother she claimed to "envy":

—"Ah, he finished forming his core. A truly flawless golden core,"-Mo Bei described to her master with a deadpan face, yet even Tao Zhu, who barely knew her, could tell that beneath this flat, matter-of-fact tone lay an unmistakable undercurrent of pride and joy.

In short, it had nothing whatsoever to do with "jealousy."

* * *

Unfortunately, there was no time to dissect Mo Bei's "peculiarity."

Mo Xi's successful core condensation meant that the grand gates of the Sun Wheel Palace at the summit of the Fire Abyss Sacred Tree had "finally" swung open.

Under Mo Xi's thorough refinement, the raging demon fire that had shrouded the palace vanished without leaving behind a single ember. The majestic palace revealed its true splendor, towering proudly above the canopy boughs. Its colossal bronze gates stood wide open, wafting forth exotic fragrances alongside glimpses of glittering golden decor, alluring demon maidens, and banquet tables laden with sumptuous delicacies.

Evidently, Shui Huang'er's original itinerary had included a lavish grand banquet to welcome the Untiring Immortal Venerable, only for Mo Xi's unexpected breakthrough to delay the festivities.

The demons gathered inside and outside the banquet hall had clearly not anticipated this turn of events. After [staring blankly at each other](# "Chinese Idiom: 大眼瞪小眼 (dà yǎn dèng xiǎo yǎn) — big eyes staring at small eyes; gazing in mutual dumbfounded silence") with Mo Xi for two seconds, they finally snapped back to reality.

—"Respectfully welcoming the Lord Immortal Venerable!"-some demon hurried to cry out.

Taking this as the signal, the demon maidens inside the banquet hall scrambled into motion, while intermittent avian calls echoed from the surrounding foliage, taking several minutes before coalescing into melodious music.

Naturally, this commotion also jarred Shui Huang'er out of her prolonged mental conference with the Wood Spirits.

She gave Zhou Hui an awkward smile:

—"Forgive our poor manners. We never anticipated your disciple would break through to a new realm here... Or rather, should I say, as expected of your disciple?"

Shui Huang'er cast a glance of genuine admiration toward Mo Xi.

Mo Xi's expression, however, remained somewhat dazed, devoid of the exuberant delight typical of a breakthrough.

Yet this was standard; cultivators typically required a period of seclusion to consolidate their newfound realm, all the more so since Mo Xi had initiated his breakthrough completely unprepared.

Zhou Hui, however, was thoroughly dissatisfied with this "standard reaction." In his eyes, nothing took precedence over his disciples' cultivation.

—"You are far too courteous,"-the Untiring Immortal Venerable replied, utilizing [social pleasantries](# "Literal CN: 社交辞令 (shèjiāo cílìng) — polite diplomatic formulas / corporate pleasantries") he was thoroughly unaccustomed to, while his mind raced to figure out where to find a suitable cultivation spot on the Sacred Tree for Mo Xi, not to mention keeping an eye on Mo Bei's situation. Weighing his words, he continued,-"However..."

—"However, such a cliché and meaningless banquet is utterly unworthy of an Immortal Venerable like yourself, not to mention that your disciple requires a superior environment to stabilize his cultivation base,"-Shui Huang'er cut in.

As a qualified host, Shui Huang'er had clearly noticed her guest's most pressing need—or rather, a sudden new intention had sparked in her mind. Lowering her eyelashes, she inquired with a dignified poise befitting a true Saintess:

—"Lord Immortal Venerable, would you be interested in appraising our demon race's 'Supreme Treasure'?"
