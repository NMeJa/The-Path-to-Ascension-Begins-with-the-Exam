# Chapter 224: Place of Origin

Internal grumbling aside, the patrol captain dared not defy the Pixiu's command.

Divine beasts held no official bureaucratic rank, yet only high-ranking deities were entitled to keep them; within the Heavenly Court, they constituted a privileged caste of their own—particularly a Pixiu assigned to oversee the Heavenly Emperor's private vault.

This Pixiu possessed a head and tail resembling a dragon, a body akin to a winged tiger marked with black-and-white stripes, and a towering, ferocious frame that struck terror into all who beheld it. Even when it merely crouched idly by the vault's entryway without stirring, ordinary folk dared not draw near.

—Naturally, as the supreme sanctuary of virtue and benevolence yearned for by countless worlds, the Heavenly Court was supposed to harbor neither scoundrels nor thievery. Within the Heavenly Realm, misplacing so much as a sewing needle in broad daylight was an unheard-of anomaly, let alone within the sanctum of the Heavenly Emperor's private vault. If this place were ever breached by an intruder, it would mean the entire Heavenly Court had already been overturned. Hence, the celestial soldiers patrolled day and night with meticulous care not to fend off external thieves, but rather out of terror that the "treasures" sealed within the vault might trigger some catastrophic disturbance.

As for the Pixiu crouching daily within the innermost recess of the vault, calling it a guardian was less accurate than calling it a living component of the vault itself.

In fact, the most indispensable part.

Given the Pixiu's renowned nature of "taking in everything, expelling nothing," even mortal humans placed ornamental figurines in their homes to hoard wealth. As for this authentic, living specimen inside the Heavenly Emperor's private vault, it certainly wasn't kept merely for aesthetic charm. It had quite literally swallowed the most critical and hardest-to-contain "treasures" directly into its gullet, ensuring they could never break free.

Now that this Pixiu was suddenly complaining of an upset stomach, there was a very real possibility that the "treasures" inside were staging a rebellion.

At least two-thirds of the "treasures" crammed into its belly possessed the power to sway the rise and fall of realms.

Viewed from that angle, it truly might alter celestial destiny.

Having pieced together the underlying gravity of the situation, the patrol captain dared not tarry for an instant, hurtling at top speed toward the Ministry of Celestial Destiny.

***

Inside the Ministry of Celestial Destiny, however, pandemonium reigned.

This was a remarkably rare spectacle.

Given their boundless lifespans, the immortals and deities of the Heavenly Court operated at a glacially slow pace. Although there existed a theoretical morning roll call each day, aside from the few fixed annual imperial audiences—which even the Heavenly Emperor himself didn't always bother to convene on schedule, let alone the immortals who took leave as easily as breathing—most deities only touched administrative paperwork when driven to the utter brink of boredom. If in a foul mood, fully aware of the decades or centuries of temporal dilation separating them from the lower realms, they would shamelessly go into seclusion or invent excuses for paid field assignments and sabbaticals—even vanguard commanders on the anti-demon frontlines like Lin Qi behaved this way.

The Ministry of Celestial Destiny, which held sway over the Heavenly Dao governing three thousand myriad worlds, was naturally no exception.

The immortal scribes of this department were akin to [a flock of homing pigeons that had attained human form](# "Literal CN: 一群鸽子成精 — internet slang: 'releasing pigeons' (放鸽子) means flaking, ghosting, slacking off, or missing deadlines; thus 'pigeons attained spirits' = habitual procrastinators and deadbeats"): slacking off whenever they could slack off, recycling boilerplate whenever they could recycle, plagiarizing whenever they could plagiarize. Generally speaking, so long as their outputs technically conformed to celestial statutes and ordinances, they couldn't care less whether the Heavenly Dao they drafted suited the realities of that minor world, whether their scripted cosmic laws spawned bugs, or whether such a Heavenly Dao possessed an ounce of human warmth. After all, life in any world was tenacious as weeds; given the narrowest fissure, it would stubbornly claw its way toward the light. And if it failed to sprout, whatever—the Heavenly Court's civil service roster was already filled to bursting; they hardly lacked staff.

The sole task capable of eliciting genuine effort from the scribes of Celestial Destiny was scripting mortal tribulations for celestial colleagues descending to the lower realms for karmic trials.

Not due to any sordid backroom transactions.

Having attained official posts in the Ministry of Celestial Destiny, these scribes were largely devoid of mundane desires (on the surface, at least); precious few treasures could tempt them. The only real predicament during the endless, sun-drenched aeons of the Heavenly Realm was figuring out how to pass the time. Thus, amidst stifling boredom, obliging a colleague's vanity by authoring a third-rate web-novel-tier power fantasy script and watching them act out an absurd reality show was prime entertainment.

In fact, even without requests from colleagues, officials occasionally plucked a few "lucky winners" from the lower realms to stage their third-rate melodramas. This granted even broader creative latitude—tragedy or farce, dictated purely by the author's whims. Yet unrelated lower-realm mortals ultimately lacked the delicious spite of puppeteering familiar acquaintances.

Consequently, what the scribes savored most was authoring the lives of "sinners"—not the pampered sort who committed trivial infractions and returned after a single cozy cycle of mortal reincarnation, but authentic "sinners" irrevocably stripped of their celestial citizenship over grave transgressions.

They were both "familiar former peers" and "powerless outsiders whose destinies could be manipulated with impunity." What could possibly offer greater amusement? If anything could top it, it was tweaking the fundamental Heavenly Dao of that world to ensure a flawless script and dramatic climax—a complicated undertaking that risked spawning more glitches and gratuitous headaches.

But so what?

If one couldn't indulge in even this meager degree of willful caprice, what was the damn point of ascending in the first place?

Of course, the Ministry of Celestial Destiny was not entirely without restraint.

After all, it was merely one administrative branch of the Heavenly Court. Although gifted with the authority to dictate the fates of men and entire worlds, if they behaved with such reckless abandon that it provoked catastrophic fallout intolerable even to the Heavenly Court, or worse, destabilized the Heavenly Dao of the Immortal Realm itself—

Yes. As a world in its own right, the Immortal Realm naturally possessed its own Heavenly Dao.

Rather, the Immortal Realm's Heavenly Dao served as the universal yardstick for all minor realms, its ordinances forming the baseline for every world. The Ministry's original duty was precisely to calibrate the Celestial Realm's Heavenly Dao across all minor worlds, thereby governing and binding them.

The more minor worlds subordinated to the Heavenly Court and executing its decrees, the more unyielding and absolute this [Celestial Yardstick](# "Literal CN: 天尺 (tiānchǐ) — the heavenly ruler/standard against which all worlds are measured") became, imposing ironclad constraints across all domains. Conversely, should too many worlds—or worlds of disproportionate might—deviate from their ordained trajectories, the backlash would inevitably warp the Heavenly Court's own cosmic foundation.

Two standard methods existed to read the Heavenly Court's Heavenly Dao.

The first was the Wordless Heavenly Scripture.

This Wordless Heavenly Scripture recorded the history of all minor worlds, all civilizations, and all living beings.

Formerly housed in the Langhuan library archives, after Langhuan "vanished," the scripture was entrusted to successive incarnations of the Wenqu Star. Under normal circumstances, however, even the Heavenly Emperor could not peruse it at will without the custodian's authorization, for the volume of data was so colossal that an unprepared mind would be utterly paralyzed.

Naturally, the scribes of Celestial Destiny possessed virtually zero clearance to view it. Yet as the architects of each minor world's Heavenly Dao, tasked with the duty of ensuring their scripted destinies never skewed off course, they were required to observe the Heavenly Dao.

For this, they relied upon another instrument:

The [Astral Ink-Marker](# "Literal CN: 墨斗 (mòdǒu) — carpenter's chalk-line / ink-line reel; here, a cosmic apparatus in the Star Pool").

Suspended within the Star Pool high above the Heavenly Court, operating ceaselessly, this ink-marker snapped taut, dim threads of ink across the deep celestial firmament, weaving a titanic cosmic chessboard. By observing this board and the shifting coordinates of the stars, one could calculate and extrapolate the destiny of any desired world or living soul.

This was also the conceptual origin of the Ministry's standard-issue artifact: the Star Array Disc.

However, whenever the Heavenly Dao fell out of equilibrium, the lines snapped by the ink-marker began to warp, reducing the "chessboard" to tangled chaos. In severe cases, it could even trigger the plummet of stellar constellations.

—Whenever such a crisis arose, the scribes of Celestial Destiny were forced to shoulder accountability and patch their blunders. If branded directly culpable, a scribe would plummet from a puppetmaster of fate into a disgraced "sinner" toyed with by their own colleagues!

This was precisely the supreme dread shared by every official in the Ministry.

Being intimately familiar with one another—and likely harboring petty personal grudges—they understood all too well that falling into a peer's sadistic hands was an ordeal that made "worse than death" sound like high praise.

Hence, the pandemonium currently engulfing the Ministry.

A few minutes prior—the exact minutes were impossible to pinpoint, for the Heavenly Court possessed no temporal units as granular as minutes, rarely even bothering with two-hour intervals; across their indolent existence, the shortest recognized units were counted in suns and moons—the ink-marker had abruptly drifted, snapping a razor-straight... diagonal line.

This diagonal line pointed straight at the Emperor Star, as though poised to bisect it clean down the middle!

Fortunately (?), an official happened to be calibrating an array disc by the Star Pool and caught the anomaly immediately. Otherwise, given the administrative efficiency of the Heavenly Court, the Emperor Star might have crashed into the dirt before anyone noticed a thing.

Yet the scribes had no leisure for relief.

The moment they discovered the ink-marker's drift, they scrambled to cross-reference the authoring logs of every world's Heavenly Dao, desperate to pinpoint which script had blown up so they could patch it before catastrophic fallout ensued.

Or, once catastrophic fallout ensued, find someone to dump the blame onto.

Thus, when the celestial patrol captain reached the Ministry of Celestial Destiny, what greeted his eyes was this exact scene of panicked chaos:

[—"Which world number is glitched out?!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Number 2678, perhaps? That minor world seems to have vanished."](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Worlds vanish every single day! Besides, that was a microscopic world; vanishing is entirely expected. I say it's Minor World 798—it's on the verge of being overrun by demons!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"That's completely routine too! In my opinion—"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Stop offering opinions! Can't you see? The Heavenly Dao of Number 67 has completely spiraled out of control! That's far worse than being invaded!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"67? What a familiar number..."](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Nonsense, it'd be bizarre if you didn't recognize it! That was a massive script! Because multiple high-profile sinners were cast into it, and it involved the Wordless Heavenly Scripture itself, almost the entire Ministry took part in drafting it!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"That's the Langhuan world, isn't it? Wasn't that world slated for decommissioning? Wouldn't Heavenly Dao loss of control be par for the course then?"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Losing control *before* it's officially scrapped is a catastrophic disaster!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"I knew it—that script had too many cooks in the kitchen! Riddled with bugs! I recall running the simulation several times, and it failed to clear every single time!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Wait, didn't someone actually run it through to completion? They even got promoted for it!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Then has that world been successfully decommissioned?"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Not really. It seemed to automatically reboot. There must still be lingering glitches. But since it cleared once, and whatever needed to be harvested was partially recycled, I figured an automatic reboot wouldn't hurt, right?"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"I agree—better leave it well alone! If that thing blows up, the entire department shares joint liability!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Is now the time to shirk responsibility?! If we ignore it and the Emperor Star takes damage, when an investigation is launched, every single one of us is finished!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Then what do you propose? March up and plead guilty right now?"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Enough, enough, stop bickering! How about we consult Lord Tianyan and let him decide?"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Precisely! Speaking of that script, he was the chief director!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

...

The scribes squawked like ducks in a wet market, totally abandoning decorum, until they finally settled on their chosen scapegoat.

Yet "Lord Tianyan" was the supreme head of the Ministry of Celestial Destiny, commanding immense authority. While everyone burned to pass the buck to him, none possessed the courage to bell the cat. They traded nervous, shifty glances as the atmosphere thickened with awkward silence.

Sensing that awkward tension, the patrol captain—whose reaction was several beats too slow—finally realized he had arrived at the worst possible moment. Though he couldn't grasp the full picture of what had transpired, he had unmistakably overheard things he had no business hearing.

The captain wanted nothing more than to slip away right then and there. Yet at such moments, as if obeying an unwritten law of the cosmos, someone noticed him:

—"Who's there?!"-someone barked.

[Finished.]

The captain lamented inwardly, but forced himself to step forward looking as though he had just arrived, seen nothing, and heard nothing:

—"Disaster! Esteemed lords of Celestial Destiny, something has happened at the Heavenly Emperor's private vault!"-the captain shouted.

—"..."

To this announcement, the scribes displayed not a shred of surprise.

Instead, they appraised the celestial captain with razor-sharp gazes, seemingly calculating how best to destroy the corpse and eliminate all evidence, leaving the captain feeling as though their stares were dismembering him on the spot.

The captain could only bite the bullet:

—"The treasures in Lord Tianlu's belly seem to have gone awry! It appears to be linked to celestial destiny!"-the captain stammered.

[—"Linked to celestial destiny?!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

A scribe finally shrieked.

Yet to the captain's ears, why did that shriek sound so profoundly... delighted?

Paying no further heed to the captain, the ecstatic scribe crowed:

[—"Did you hear that?! Perhaps nothing is wrong with the Heavenly Dao we authored at all—perhaps a treasure in the Heavenly Emperor's private vault malfunctioned, throwing celestial destiny into turmoil!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Treasures in the private vault destabilizing the Emperor Star? Entirely plausible! Utterly reasonable!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Unforeseen accidents happen, after all!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[Is this buck-passing seminar never going to end?]

The captain groaned inwardly, yet had no choice but to ask:

—"...May I inquire, esteemed lords, how such a situation ought to be addressed?"-the captain asked.

—"Under normal circumstances, one simply investigates the 'treasure,'"-the scribes' mood had brightened considerably; they no longer cared about this celestial soldier's eavesdropping. One even inquired amiably:—"Do you happen to know which treasure went wrong?"

—"This..."-the captain choked.

He was merely a celestial soldier; how could he possibly know what treasures were churning inside the Pixiu's stomach?

The scribe caught on:

—"Tell you what, I'll go have a look in person to see what transpired,"-the scribe proposed.

[—"A rare opportunity—I might as well take a look too!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Leave such matters to me!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Count me in!"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

...

In an instant, the remaining scribes clamored in unison.

None wished to remain stranded within the Ministry before the array disc recording the runaway Astral Ink-Marker; staying another second felt like volunteering to shoulder the crime of tilting the cosmic ink-marker.

Someone whispered quietly:

[—"By the way, has this been reported to Lord Tianyan?"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"Shouldn't we check on Lord Tianlu first?"](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

[—"No, I mean the deviation of the Astral Ink-Marker..."](# "Speaker: Scribe of Celestial Destiny")

—"...Fine, report it now, then!"-a scribe conceded grudgingly. He fished a jade slip from his robes, transmuting it into a fluttery white butterfly messenger. Yet the butterfly flew in erratic, drunken zigzags, leaving one to wonder if it would ever reach its destination before the next kalpa.

Yet the scribes exhaled a synchronized sigh of relief as if a monumental duty had been discharged.

[—"Let's go!"](# "Speaker: Scribes of Celestial Destiny")

With that, the grand assembly of scribes marched in an imposing, bustling procession toward the private vault.

***

On the other end, in that shattered garden, the titanic giant was once again smashed to smithereens like rotten wood collapsing under a tempest.

Its existence had not exceeded three seconds.

[—"H-How can this be?!"](# "Speaker: Gate Guardian Entity")

[—"Even against the Heavenly Emperor, I had confidence I could hold out!"](# "Speaker: Gate Guardian Entity")

[—"Just who on earth are you?!"](# "Speaker: Gate Guardian Entity")

A buzzing voice echoed in sheer disbelief.

It did not issue from the giant's mouth, but reverberated from every direction of the surrounding space. Yet ask though it did, it had no genuine desire for an answer; Zhou Hui's sword strike had utterly pulverized its courage, leaving it only desperate to flee.

Visually, the entire shattered space contracted and warped, rapidly folding in like a sheet of plastic sheeting whipped away by the wind on a mountain peak, whooshing into the massive gate at the far end, leaving behind only the fragmented corpse of the titan.

—"Shall we pursue?"-Ruwang inquired.

As he asked, shadowy black tendrils probed toward the threshold behind the gate.

—"No, not that direction,"-Zhou Hui checked his array disc. Rather than stepping straight through the second gate, he rummaged through the giant's dismembered remains, carving a bloody cavity right into its abdomen:—"This way."

With that, he plunged straight in without hesitation.

The others traded glances.

Still, they were all veteran cultivators; bizarre, grotesque escapades of this caliber were hardly unprecedented, so they hopped in after him without complaint.

—"Is it not behind that door?"-Tao Zhu asked curiously as he leaped.

—"I originally assumed it was, but that seems to lead merely to the Immortal-Demon Realm,"-Zhou Hui replied nonchalantly.

—"'Merely' the Immortal-Demon Realm?!"-Tao Zhu squawked.

To cultivators of their realm, reaching even the Immortal-Demon Realm would already qualify as a triumphant ascension!

Alas, regret came too late; he had already tumbled into the bloody breach behind Zhou Hui.

When their senses recovered, they had landed in a boundless domain of pure, dazzling brilliance, devoid of anything else.

No, not entirely devoid—there was also a bizarrely contoured metallic... contraption?

Even the worldly and well-traveled Boss Tao couldn't fathom what in the world it was.

Not that he had the bandwidth to care, for he instantly perceived that this sea of radiance was saturated with astronomical spiritual energy—so unimaginably dense that inhaling a single breath would suffice to detonate his physical vessel!

Seeing this, the assembled grandmasters hurriedly sealed their spiritual roots.

To perish from over-inhaling spiritual energy would be a farce no cultivator could laugh at.

In a realm overflowing with such cataclysmic spiritual force, even Ruwang's protective ward collapsed. Fortunately, Zhou Hui promptly raised a fresh barrier in time, sheltering everyone beneath his aegis.

—"Where on earth is this place now?"-Tao Zhu asked once more.

—"The collision point of several universes, and the place of origin of our world,"-Zhou Hui said.

Zhou Hui stared almost obsessively at that machine, and the surrounding source of luminescence.

His expression made onlookers dread that, in the very next instant, he might dissolve himself wholly into the primordial cradle of this world.
