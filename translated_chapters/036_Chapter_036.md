# Chapter 36: Entering the Dao (第36章 入道)

Guan Ye considered himself [utter trash](# "Literal CN: 渣渣 (zhāzhā) — dregs / worthless scrap / utter trash").

Though people might hold him in higher regard merely because he was a direct disciple of the Untiring Immortal Venerable, he knew all too well that he was nothing more than a fish eye mingling among luminous pearls.

This was an objective reality.

His Eldest Senior Brother, Yuwen Xuan, possessed peerless aptitude—the sort of freak who gained cultivation just by drawing breath.

His Second Senior Brother, Qu Qianxing, had a brilliant intellect; whether it was core cultivation manuals or esoteric miscellany, he grasped concepts the moment they were pointed out, deducing three new insights from a single lesson when learning spells. Blessed with the divine amplification of a Gold Heavenly Spirit Root, his cultivation velocity was practically the theoretical limit of mortal cultivators.

His Eldest Senior Sister, Ran Deng, appeared lazy and languid on the surface, but in truth [slept on classics and feasted on literature](# "Literal CN: 枕典席文 (zhěn diǎn xí wén) — resting one's head on canon and sitting upon prose; immersing oneself entirely in reading"), never letting scrolls leave her grasp. She was the sole direct disciple to have read through every volume in the library pavilion (albeit strictly limited to miscellaneous lore outside of cultivation manuals and spells), and could seamlessly integrate those eclectic studies into her formation and architectural designs—truly living up to her heritage from an ancient array clan.

His Fifth Junior Brother, Lu Shulong, was even more terrifying. Not only did he possess Eldest Senior Brother's cultivation speed and Second Senior Brother's sharp mind, he also had an uncanny gift for identifying hundreds of spirit herbs by scent alone, paired with an alchemical luck stat that boasted a literally zero percent failure rate. Were it not for his slightly bizarre thought process, Guan Ye felt this junior brother would likely be the most formidable existence between Heaven and Earth.

As for his youngest junior sister and youngest junior brother, there was no need to even mention them. While their Extreme Yin Constitutions had been the root of their past suffering, it also served as their guaranteed baseline—so long as they persevered, their potential would never be lacking.

Only he, Guan Ye, was a thorough, unadulterated mortal.

And the only reason he had been taken in as a disciple by the Untiring Immortal Venerable was likely because he had simply been too pitiful.

When Guan Ye first met his Master, his dantian was shattered, the meridians in his hands and feet severed, his spine ripped out, and he was lying in a mass gravesite waiting to die... No, at that point, he was virtually indistinguishable from a corpse. Had Zhou Hui not possessed the transcendent senses of a Tribulation Transcendence powerhouse, catching that faint, lingering ember of life below while soaring through the skies, Guan Ye would have been dead without question.

And the reason Guan Ye had ended up in such a wretched state was because he was too stupid.

He had been clumsy enough to lose control of the forge fire, ruining a Dragon Tendon Whip that the foreman was crafting for the sect master's personal disciple. An exorbitant treasure of that caliber was something Guan Ye could never repay even in multiple lifetimes, so they severed his tendons, extracted his spine, and tossed him into the mass grave.

Yes—before joining the Heavenly Spirit Sect, Guan Ye had actually belonged to a sect.

He was born into an ordinary mortal blacksmith's household. During the local cultivation sect's grand recruitment drive, he was found to possess a trash Five-Element Spirit Root. Aptitude of that caliber didn't even meet the baseline criteria for an outer-sect handyman, and he was originally slated to be sent back. However, because his blacksmith upbringing had taught him a bit of metalworking, he was assigned to the artifact-refining hall of a cultivation clan subordinate to the sect, serving as a [fire-tending boy](# "Literal CN: 看火童子 (kān huǒ tóngzǐ) — an apprentice or menial laborer whose sole job is monitoring and maintaining furnace temperatures").

Only Guan Ye was hopeless at cultivation and possessed zero talent for artifact refinement.

He could never wrap his head around the foreman's descriptions—"raise the heat a hair," "drop it a notch," "the flame should be yellow leaning toward red," "the core of the fire should be azure blue." Because of this, he botched his assignments time and time again.

Looking back, ruining that Dragon Tendon Whip had been all but inevitable...

—"Wait, that doesn't make any sense!"-Mo Xi had exclaimed upon hearing this tale, his translucent, cat-like eyes widening with a bizarre expression.-"If it was a material as precious as dragon tendon, why wasn't it forged by the finest master smiths in the sect's main artifact hall? Why farm it out to a subordinate cultivation clan?!"

—"Perhaps our foreman was particularly skilled?"-Guan Ye had ventured uncertainly.

In truth, aside from the day his spirit roots were tested, Guan Ye had never once set foot inside the sect's inner grounds, so he had no way of comparing the quality of the sect's hall against the clan's workshop. That said, the foreman had constantly bragged that his master was so-and-so in the sect's artifact hall... Huh?

—"Even taking ten thousand steps back, even if that foreman was supposed to be hot stuff, why on earth would he leave you to mind the fire, Fourth Senior Brother?!"-Mo Xi had pressed relentlessly.-"For an item that critical, wouldn't it be far better for him to watch it himself?"

—"He injured his leg that night in a fall, and it just so happened that only I was free..."-Guan Ye replied.

Mo Xi was visibly dissatisfied with that answer.

Wrinkling his nose, he stared at Guan Ye for a long time before finally shaking his head:

[—"Whatever, let's leave it at that! Either way, that miserable clan doesn't exist anymore..."](# "Speaker: Mo Xi")

—"Why doesn't it exist?"-Guan Ye asked, puzzled.

Mo Xi looked at him like he was looking at an alien:

[—"After treating you like that, would Master ever permit them to continue existing?"](# "Speaker: Mo Xi")

One had to remember that the Fallen Immortal Palace had been plowed three feet deep; had they not possessed the towering shade of Carefree Palace backing them, they would have vanished without a trace long ago.

—"...Back then, my condition was too precarious. Dealing with that clan was secondary,"-Guan Ye said slowly.

In the state Guan Ye was in at the time, a single moment's delay would have put him beyond saving.

Especially under the catastrophic trauma of having his spine extracted and his dantian shattered.

Ordinary spiritual medicines were entirely useless. Guan Ye's natural spine had been tossed who-knows-where after being ripped out, and was nowhere to be found in the mass grave. Zhou Hui had been forced to improvise with whatever materials he had on hand in an emergency—refining a replacement spine from a composite of blood coral, blood scorpion tails, and jade marrow. But because Guan Ye had been mangled beyond anything resembling a human form, making it impossible to measure his proportions accurately, the crafted spine had turned out slightly too large. Even now, whenever Guan Ye stripped off his upper garments, half of that blood-red spine protruded outside his back.

Yet grafting a spine alone was far from enough. Zhou Hui had subsequently carried Guan Ye to seek out the Divine Physician, Master Lan Pei, hoping to mend his shattered dantian. Alas, too much time had been lost; even Master Lan Pei was powerless to reverse the damage.

—"Aaaagh, doesn't that make it even more infuriating?!"-Mo Xi held grudges with relentless fury.-"After butchering you like this, how could they allow that kind of scum to keep living in this world?!"

—"Master did ask me where I had worked... but the fact remains, I destroyed a priceless dragon tendon!"-Guan Ye replied.

He still remembered that shimmering tendon—so gorgeous, so brimming with spiritual aura. Thinking about that unfortunate dragon having its tendons pulled and bones stripped was pitiful enough, yet the dragon tendon left behind had been incinerated into a clump of black ash because of him.

Guan Ye genuinely believed receiving punishment had been only natural.

Mo Xi only thought his fourth senior brother was completely unhinged:

[—"Haha! You deserved punishment, but what about the bastards who ripped out your bones and severed your tendons?! Do they not deserve to be punished?!"](# "Speaker: Mo Xi")

—"I will do that myself,"-Guan Ye said in a gloomy tone.-"I know full well those people are scum, which is all the more reason their filthy blood cannot soil Master's hands. One's own grudges must be avenged by one's own hands."

Letting it sit for a while wouldn't hurt anything anyway. Unlike the Fallen Immortal Palace, Guan Ye's personal impression was that so long as it didn't involve a calamity like the dragon tendon, while that clan was harsh and demanding, they didn't ordinarily commit murder. His ordeal... truth be told, had been rather bizarre.

—"A steamed bun! A doormat! Fourth Senior Brother, what breed of [spineless steamed bun](# "Literal CN: 包子 (bāozi) — soft steamed bun; slang for a helpless, timid doormat who never fights back and lets everyone squeeze or walk all over them") are you?! Aaaagh, you're infuriating me to death! I hate stories like this the most!"-Mo Xi looked as though he wanted nothing more than to give Guan Ye a sound thrashing.

Stomping his feet in sheer exasperation over [iron that refused to become steel](# "Literal CN: 恨铁不成钢 (hèn tiě bù chéng gāng) — feeling frustrated that someone fails to live up to expectations or stand up for themselves"), he bolted out the door.

Yet after a short while, he came sprinting right back.

—"No! Leaving the story hanging here feels completely unresolved! Just thinking about it will keep me awake all night!"-Mo Xi huffed angrily at Guan Ye.-"When the day comes that you go for revenge, Fourth Senior Brother, make sure to take me with you! I have to witness those people's end with my own eyes, or I'll never find peace!"

[—"...I will strive for that day to come."](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

Guan Ye blinked in surprise, then offered his youngest junior brother a faint smile.

Deep down, however, Guan Ye harbored severe doubts as to whether that day would ever arrive. Judging by his cultivation progress, he seemed destined to remain stalled in the Foundation Establishment Middle Stage forever. The patriarch of that cultivation clan alone was at the Foundation Establishment Great Perfection, making talk of revenge little more than a pipe dream.

What was more, after so many years, even his memories of his enemies' faces had blurred.

Perhaps, just as his junior brother said, he truly was a spineless steamed bun...

—"Ah!"-Snapping out of his daze, Guan Ye discovered he had hammered a section of purple gold conduit crooked. Fortunately, this was not a dragon tendon; it wouldn't ruin anything beyond costing a little extra time.

He tossed the warped conduit back into the refining furnace to remelt.

Just then, Guan Ye spotted his assistant, Qin Xian, striding over with a face brimming with excitement.

—"Senior Brother Guan, I heard the Immortal Venerable bestowed five spirit springs upon you?!"

—"They weren't bestowed upon me,"-Guan Ye corrected, knitting his brows.

Everything about this sect was wonderful, except that rumors were invariably exaggerated out of all proportion.

Take some time ago, for instance: when Master instructed him and Eldest Senior Sister to construct the library pavilion, granting them free access to materials from the Ascension Platform and mineral veins, rumors spread that Master had gifted the entire Ascension Platform and mineral veins to him and Eldest Senior Sister.

Then a while back, when Master switched Youngest Junior Brother's cultivation technique and provided a mineral vein to nourish his body, people claimed Master had gifted another vein to Youngest Junior Brother—well, Youngest Junior Brother had only himself to blame for that one; the fellow was like a cat with a bell around its neck, desperate for everyone in the world to hear the jingle.

And now, to help Master recuperate more comfortably, he had laid conduits to divert the spirit springs straight to the exterior of Master's cave dwelling. Master felt the other disciples needed them as well, so he instructed Guan Ye to continue construction and authorized him to tap into the springs. Somehow, sect gossip had morphed into Master being overjoyed and bestowing the five spirit springs upon him as a reward!

Master was not that sort of person at all!

Nor did Guan Ye do things for Master for the sake of rewards!

Nursing a headache, Guan Ye explained:

[—"Those spirit springs are meant for all disciples across the sect. Priority goes to public facilities first, and then cave dwellings in need may submit applications."](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

Hearing this, Qin Xian grew even more thrilled than before, beaming from ear to ear:

[—"Those are spirit springs! How could anyone possibly not want them?! Senior Brother Guan! Our Artifact Refinement Hall is definitely the first to get pipes installed, right?!"](# "Speaker: Qin Xian")

—"Naturally,"-Guan Ye nodded.-"Though we have to finish constructing the bathhouse first."

On matters like this, he had no fear of anyone accusing him of using public office for private gain.

The Artifact Refinement Hall was responsible for all everyday utilities across the entire sect, making it the department with the greatest practical need for spirit springs in the first place.

Furthermore, as department head (sort of), Guan Ye felt that even if he was a spineless steamed bun, he would never shortchange his own subordinates over such matters. After all, only adequate compensation could incentivize them to work harder!

Taking ten thousand steps back, this was also a necessary trial run.

Running plumbing conduits into personal cave dwellings was unprecedented work. Installing them in their own department first allowed them to troubleshoot any unforeseen issues, accumulating ample experience before rolling the system out elsewhere.

Calculating the logistics in his head, Guan Ye told Qin Xian:

[—"You all worked hard earlier. The dwellings of our Artifact Refinement Hall disciples will also be among the first batch to receive spirit spring conduits. Turn around and have everyone write up their applications, then submit them to Second Senior Brother."](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

—"Oh yeah!"-Qin Xian cheered.

In his eyes, the boss was already extraordinarily generous.

In other sects, an ordinary disciple being permitted to cultivate near a spirit spring once or twice was already an immense reward, to say nothing of laying pipes to deliver spirit spring water directly to one's living quarters!

Not to mention that once the public bathhouse was completed, they would get to use it as well.

On this merit alone, Qin Xian felt that never mind laying pipes for the Immortal Venerable—if his cultivation and artifact-refining mastery reached higher levels in the future, even if he had to construct a celestial aqueduct to divert the Heavenly River down from the firmament for the Immortal Venerable's personal use, he would do so with wholehearted devotion!

Besides, the prior work hadn't even been grueling!

When the head of the department rolled up his sleeves to hammer pipes alongside them, what ground did anyone have to complain?

Speaking of which, recalling how he had just snuck off to the secret realm for a round of recreation, Qin Xian felt an inexplicable twinge of guilt. Swapping into his work robe, he moved to take over Guan Ye's task:

—"Senior Brother Guan, let me handle the rest!"

—"No need,"-Guan Ye replied without releasing his grip on the hammer. He simply told Qin Xian:-"Since we must design the public bathhouse, I requested architectural sketches from Eldest Senior Sister and drafted a preliminary piping schematic. Take a look at them first, and then draw up a proposal of your own."

—"Understood!"-Qin Xian answered happily.

This total lack of gatekeeping was what he adored most about Senior Brother Guan.

Watching Qin Xian run off in high spirits, Guan Ye swung his hammer once more.

Though everyone seemed to regard such labor as exhausting, Guan Ye actually enjoyed this sort of mindless, repetitive work. For one, making a mistake carried no stakes; for another, it allowed his thoughts to wander wherever they pleased.

Come to think of it, where had his thoughts drifted earlier?

Ah, right: what if he remained stuck in the Foundation Establishment Middle Stage for the rest of his life?

Not avenging his grudge was no great loss, but possessing cultivation this dismal truly brought shame to Master.

Frowning in gloom, Guan Ye let his mind wander through melancholic thoughts.

His arm came crashing down with heavy momentum. *CLANG!* The hammer slammed squarely onto the purple gold.

`*`

—"Leaving a gift that my disciple put so much filial devotion into crafting completely unused truly feels improper,"-Zhou Hui muttered to himself.

In truth, sitting inside his cave dwelling, he had been staring across at that spirit spring for nearly an entire day.

Why hadn't he told Guan Ye to pick a different location when the boy first unfurled those paper cutouts? This coordinate was simply too awkward.

Zhou Hui felt a rare prickle of chagrin.

Outward appearances aside, he still retained the dignity and reserve expected of a master. Stripping naked to soak in an open-air bath suspended in the sky directly above the entire sect—even if no one could see him—somewhat exceeded his bottom line of modesty.

And yet... it did sound rather intriguing.

Hadn't the other world featured novelties like infinity sky pools? Though back then, Zhou Hui had felt that traveling to outer space for a zero-gravity bath was the true height of romance.

While this aerial spirit spring couldn't compare to a space bath, it was the painstaking fruit of his disciple Guan Ye's heart and soul.

Without any direct instruction or guidance from him, a native of the cultivation world had inspected the industrial concepts of another realm and independently manufactured this creation—its intrinsic value surpassed almost anything else.

After hesitating back and forth, late the following night during the dead silence of the [Chou hour, third ke](# "Literal CN: 丑时三刻 (chǒu shí sān kè) — approx. 2:45 AM, the deep dead of night when all ten thousand sounds fall silent"), when all plants and living things slept, Zhou Hui finally walked out of curiosity toward the floating spirit spring island nearest his dwelling.

This particular spirit spring had likely been drawn from a geothermal spring near the volcanoes northwest of Mount Tianling. Heated by subterranean thermal energy, the waters were steaming hot and carried the faint scent of sulfur. Although at Zhou Hui's cultivation realm, physical temperature was entirely inconsequential, Guan Ye had meticulously engineered a temperature-regulation mechanism—cooling the water to a soothing, comfortable level before it flowed into the basin, and draining it away once the temperature dropped, maintaining a flawless thermodynamic water cycle.

Steam could be seen billowing upward, misting the dim yellow paper lanterns and wafting past blooming red plum branches, coaxing droplets of thawed water from perennial icicles.

One had to admit: lazy though the girl Ran Deng was, her aesthetic sensibilities remained impeccably refined.

The sole drawback was that the Heavenly Spirit Sect had virtually no ambient lighting at night; though the pavilion was open to the sky above, gazing downward revealed only pitch-black darkness—though naturally, Zhou Hui would never have come during daylight hours anyway.

Yet no sooner had Zhou Hui swiftly changed into a light bath robe and pushed open the decorative brushwood door leading to the spring than he froze: an old man with an eight-foot-long white beard was already seated inside the waters, gazing up at him with imposing majesty:

[—"Rebellious son, you've finally arrived?"](# "Speaker: Master Lan Pei")

"..."

Zhou Hui wordlessly pulled the brushwood door shut, then pushed it open again.

The elderly man in the spring had vanished. In his place sat an alluring, sultry woman:

[—"Come on over, let's make merry~~"](# "Speaker: Master Lan Pei")

"..."

Zhou Hui turned on his heel to leave. The "woman" giggled, and a ribbon of spring water surged up like a liquid chain, catching Zhou Hui's sleeve:

[—"Aiyaya, little Zhou Hui, don't run off the second you lay eyes on me!"](# "Speaker: Master Lan Pei")

—"Don't touch me, you wanton fox!"-Zhou Hui snapped.

Zhou Hui raised his hand, making a crisp flicking motion toward the air.

*SMACK!* A sharp, resonant crack echoed as an invisible force flicked the "woman's" forehead, snapping her head violently backward. The transformation shattered instantly into a puff of white smoke; once the mist cleared, a scruffy, yellowish ball of fur was left floating on the surface of the spring.

—"Wuwu, is this how you treat a physician who traveled all this way to examine your illness?"-the furball accused tearfully.

—"Master Lan Pei, had you chosen a more dignified fashion to greet me, I would have received you with proper courtesy,"-Zhou Hui said with undisguised distaste.

Indeed—this fox was none other than the illustrious Divine Physician, Lan Pei.

Or rather, none of the mortals or cultivators under heaven had any inkling that the legendary Divine Physician Lan Pei was actually a wild fox.

He was not even a spiritual fox; he possessed no exquisite, shimmering pelt, only scruffy yellow fur that looked rather bald and scraggly while soaking in the water.

Seeing his rather balding appearance, Zhou Hui's mood actually brightened considerably.

Walking over to the edge of the pool, he dipped his legs into the water and sat down.

The pleasantly warm temperature caused Zhou Hui to narrow his eyes in comfort.

Noticing Zhou Hui's contented expression, the yellow fox ceased his fake sobbing and said with sheer envy:

[—"I heard this spirit spring was a filial gift from your children? How wonderful! Unlike my own little brats—not a single one of their shadows is ever to be seen."](# "Speaker: Master Lan Pei")

Zhou Hui felt quite pleased hearing this, but still maintained polite modesty:

[—"What do I need their filial devotion for? If only they were half as accomplished as your children, I would be content—setting aside the universally acclaimed Medicine Immortals and Divine Healers, didn't one of them ascend just a dozen years ago?"](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"Tch, forget it, what kind of ascension was that? Even you wouldn't look twice at it,"-Lan Pei snorted, rolling his beady eyes toward Zhou Hui.-"Though according to your eldest disciple, you took a tumble yourself? Got fried by heavenly lightning last time? Want me to take a look?"

As he spoke, he stretched out a dripping wet, furry paw toward Zhou Hui's wrist.

Zhou Hui swatted it away in distaste once more:

[—"Change into another form. You're dirtying the spirit spring looking like that."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"I changed into forms at first, but you weren't satisfied. Humans are so impossible to please,"-Lan Pei grumbled.

This time, however, he refrained from teasing Zhou Hui further, obediently shifting into the appearance of an elegant, distinguished, gaunt middle-aged scholar in his thirties—the guise the yellow fox most commonly presented to the world.

Only then did Zhou Hui nod with satisfaction, sliding gently into the spring waters and extending his wrist for the other to take his pulse.

Even so, Zhou Hui remarked:

[—"It's not nearly as dire as they made it out to be; those children overreacted. Rather than my health, there is another matter I wish to trouble you with."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"As long as you're not using me to curry favors,"-Lan Pei said.

Zhou Hui thought of Su Xin, who was currently bouncing off the walls in the Hall of Calming the Heart and noisily demanding to enter the secret realm, and felt an inexplicable pang of guilt.

—"*Cough*, not that,"-Zhou Hui said.-"My fourth disciple—you should still remember him, yes?"

Lan Pei wanted to claim he didn't, but among Zhou Hui's disciples—setting aside that terrifying eldest disciple—the one with the deepest connection to him was obvious:

[—"You mean the one whose dantian was shattered?"](# "Speaker: Master Lan Pei")

—"Precisely,"-Zhou Hui nodded.

—"Hopeless, incurable, give it up!"-Lan Pei rattled off the three-part denial without a moment's hesitation.

At the mention of this case, Lan Pei found Zhou Hui terribly stubborn.

Digging a ruined child like that out of a mass grave and reviving him was one thing, but with a dantian pulverized to that extent, it would have been kinder not to save him in the first place!

—This was not to say someone with a shattered dantian was unworthy of salvation. Lan Pei's criteria for patients rested purely on karmic affinity; when the timing was right, he had cured countless mortals. But the absolute worst kind of patient was someone who had once glimpsed everything, only to be stripped of all future prospects—because "hope" was something no physician could prescribe.

A child whose dantian was destroyed and could no longer cultivate was like someone who had tasted a higher realm only to have all doors slammed shut; clinging to life without letting go was merely a prolonged cruelty.

Yet Zhou Hui had refused to abandon that child back then, and clearly had no intention of abandoning him now.

—"You told me as much years ago, and I have no intention of asking you to treat him medically,"-Zhou Hui mused.-"However, there ought to be cultivation paths independent of spirit roots... I heard that you entered the Dao through Medicine?"

—"Ha! Brat, you know full well that is not a question you should be asking!"-Lan Pei sneered, flashing sharp canine teeth.-"Unless you intend to become my Dao companion, prying into someone's primordial origins like this would make dying of poison no great injustice."

—"My apologies, Senior Lan Pei,"-Zhou Hui said solemnly, gazing earnestly into Lan Pei's eyes.-"Yet I truly wish to know."

This was Guan Ye's final chance.

Legends spoke of two primary paths of cultivation in the cultivation realm. The first was orthodox cultivation: spirit roots absorbed spiritual qi, the flesh nurtured that energy, meridians were reshaped, and one diligently advanced through the realms. The other path relied upon "Enlightenment"—a far more metaphysical domain. In theory, sitting in a daze could work, as could honing an expertise to its absolute zenith. One school of thought held that elevating one's state of mind alone could trigger ascension, much like the ancient sages; another maintained that reaching the pinnacle of an earthly craft allowed one to ascend through sheer technical mastery—just as Lan Pei was rumored to have "entered the Dao through Medicine."

Zhou Hui suspected Guan Ye could walk this very path.

No—he definitely could walk this path.

The Heavenly Dao script had stated that after the Heavenly Spirit Sect's destruction, Guan Ye survived the catastrophe. Driven by vengeance, he surrendered himself to a puppet master of an evil sect, undergoing transformation into a living automaton. Counterintuitively, his power skyrocketed, turning him into a prominent mid-game boss who pursued the protagonist relentlessly until the latter's ascension, even leaving behind an intricate manual of mechanism arts for the protagonist to claim.

Zhou Hui had initially assumed that the process of "refining into an automaton" had forcibly boosted Guan Ye's power. Yet upon closer reflection, he detected a glaring plot hole: the process of elevating an automaton's combat strength irreversibly obliterated their human sanity. The fact that Guan Ye had been able to compose and leave behind a comprehensive manual of mechanism arts proved that his intellect had remained lucid.

That left only a single explanation: Guan Ye had "entered the Dao through Craftsmanship."

Reflecting on how this disciple's innate genius had remained unexcavated under his own tutelage, yet reached consummate heights in the hands of an evil faction...

Zhou Hui felt deeply ashamed. Having no prior experience with such paths himself, he could only consult Lan Pei.

Lan Pei locked eyes with him for a long moment before letting out a soft sigh:

—"Sigh, you... you have been frighteningly stubborn since the old days."

He rubbed his furry paws together:

[—"Still, there is nothing that must be hidden. A wild fox like me is scarcely different from your human Five-Element Spirit Roots. I lacked your obsession, and orthodox methods failed me, so I did indeed enter the Dao through medicine... What, did that little brat of yours discover some extraordinary latent talent?"](# "Speaker: Master Lan Pei")

—"You could say that,"-Zhou Hui nodded, about to explain the origins of the aerial spirit springs.

Who would have thought this fox spirit wouldn't grant him the opportunity to show off his disciple? Narrowing his eyes, he chuckled:

[—"Then that really... has zero relevance!"](# "Speaker: Master Lan Pei")

[—"What?"](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"Entering the Dao has almost nothing to do with innate talent,"-Lan Pei said.-"Otherwise, every renowned chef, divine physician, and master craftsman under heaven would have ascended—ah, excluding those who were whisked away to the upper realms because they were too useful, which is its own bizarre variety of ascension!—Entering the Dao is governed solely by **[practice count](# "Literal CN: 练习度 (liànxí dù) — degree/volume of practice; repetition count and mastery through grinding")**."

[—"Practice count?"](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

—"Indeed. Whether one possesses talent or not, whether your mind wanders into wild daydreams or focuses on medicine, as long as you are diligent... no, diligence isn't even the crucial part. Your head can be swimming with rambling daydreams while you work, but your hands must never stop,"-Lan Pei said.-"No matter how boring or mundane the skill, repeat it a thousand times, ten thousand times—speaking of which, aren't you the one most intimately familiar with this, Mr. Hard Worker?"

—"No, my path was entirely ordinary..."-Zhou Hui demurred.

—"Yours is anything but ordinary! A normal person could never pull that off!"-Lan Pei's fox ears and bushy tail suddenly burst forth, his fur standing on end.-"You think an ordinary Four-Element Spirit Root can just grind their way to the top? Countless cultivators work themselves to the bone, only to perish as Golden Cores! For you to attain Mahayana at such a young age—beyond sheer effort, I wager you have an element of **'Entering the Dao through the Dao Itself'**, which granted you an immense amplification while making it appear as though you climbed through orthodox means."

—"Is that so?"-Zhou Hui mused.-"So the method of 'Entering the Dao through a Craft' simply requires practicing more?"

—"Precisely. Keep doing it without ceasing, but you cannot simply repeat identical motions blindly—you must push past your comfort zone, grinding and iterating continuously,"-Lan Pei said.-"When you accomplish a feat unprecedented in history and unmatched by posterity, no matter how minuscule that feat may seem, it will be sufficient to draw the gaze of a certain presence, and you will succeed."

—"A certain presence? The Heavenly Dao?"-Zhou Hui frowned.

—"No. Do you think someone like me could draw the gaze of the Heavenly Dao?"-The wild fox swished his scraggly tail.-"It is an even more profound, unknowable presence. Ah, obtuse humans would never understand."

"..." Indeed, after this wild fox's explanation, Zhou Hui felt even more bewildered.

No matter:

—"Practicing is enough for now,"-Zhou Hui said, unfazed.-"As it happens, I instructed that child to continue laying conduits; by the time he finishes piping the entire sect, he should reap some reward."

—"Ah? Laying pipes?"-Lan Pei cocked his head in utter incomprehension.

So what was that wretched child's latent genius anyway? A talent for plumbing?!

Zhou Hui offered no clarification:

[—"Speaking of which, I have soaked long enough; it is time to rest."](# "Speaker: Zhou Hui")

With that, he rose from the spirit spring, drying his bath robe with a flick of his sleeve while letting his damp silver hair trail over his shoulders as he walked outward.

Letting this spirit spring water linger in his hair for a while longer could passively replenish a bit of spiritual energy.

—"Wait a moment, I haven't finished my diagnosis!"-Lan Pei cried.

Lan Pei chased helplessly behind his uncooperative patient.

Yet no sooner had he followed Zhou Hui a few paces than he saw Yuwen Xuan striding toward them, escorted by dozens of hovering, steaming platters of food. The dishes drifted in midair, showcasing their culinary contents—most notably a glistening, golden-brown roast chicken... roast chicken, roast chicken!

It smelled divine!

Lan Pei's drool instantly cascaded into a river. Yuwen Xuan, however, showed not the slightest reverence toward the Divine Physician he had traveled to fetch, smiling warmly as he presented fine wine to Zhou Hui:

[—"Master, seeing you soaking in the hot spring with Master Lan Pei, how could there be no wine and dishes to accompany the occasion? I prepared some delicacies; shall we partake together?"](# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan")

"..."

—"Wuwu, I still say filial disciples are the best!"-Lan Pei proclaimed in sheer envy.

What [fucking use](# "Literal CN: 有个P用 (yǒu gè pì yòng) — what fart of a use / what damn use") was having accomplished disciples?!

`*`

[—"Huh? Your Artifact Refinement Hall hasn't retired for the night yet?"](# "Speaker: Lu Shulong")

While Zhou Hui was entertaining (?) the divine physician from afar, Lu Shulong emerged from the alchemy chambers, rubbing his tired eyes.

The pill concoction he had refined this time had been rather intricate, dragging on until this ungodly hour. He hadn't expected that upon stepping outside, the neighboring Artifact Refinement Hall—which ordinarily shut down early—would still be ablaze with activity, echoing with the rhythmic clatter of hammer striking metal.

[That rhythm... is it Fourth Senior Brother?]

This was a perfect chance to call him to head back together.

Yawning, Lu Shulong thought as he stepped comfortably into the hall, only to discover to his surprise that not only was Guan Ye present, but other hall disciples were there as well—most of them huddled around desks in intense discussion.

Qin Xian was seated directly facing the doorway; spotting Lu Shulong enter, he promptly hurried over to greet him.

—"Senior Brother Lu, you've arrived?"-Qin Xian greeted warmly.-"I'll go fetch Senior Brother Guan for you."

Due to the alchemy chambers being situated right next door, the Artifact Refinement Hall disciples were thoroughly familiar with Lu Shulong. Moreover, Guan Ye and Lu Shulong shared an exceptionally close bond; while the other direct disciples were also fond of one another, none quite matched the dynamic between Guan Ye and Lu Shulong. Rumors even claimed that Lu Shulong had been raised by Guan Ye—a notion that left ordinary disciples utterly baffled, since Guan Ye and Lu Shulong appeared to be of identical age. How could Guan Ye have raised him?

Lu Shulong offered a gentle smile:

[—"I can head over myself."](# "Speaker: Lu Shulong")

—"Very well, very well,"-Qin Xian agreed. Still, with the hall currently engaged in a 'grand project,' he worried Lu Shulong might accidentally disturb something delicate, so he accompanied him closely.

Yet Lu Shulong was evidently well-versed in protocol.

—In truth, the taboos governing alchemy far outnumbered those of artifact forging.

Approaching Guan Ye's station, Lu Shulong noticed his senior brother was in the middle of a critical juncture, so he refrained from stepping closer or making a sound, watching quietly from a distance.

Qin Xian breathed a sigh of relief.

Watching alongside Lu Shulong, Qin Xian gazed with envy as a blazing lump of purple gold yielded like soft clay beneath Guan Ye's hammer, taking whatever shape the smith desired.

And yet outsiders still claimed Senior Brother Guan's cultivation was lacking.

If Qin Xian could possess even half of that skill—no, forty to fifty percent!—he would be thoroughly satisfied!

—"Truly remarkable,"-Lu Shulong murmured at that moment in admiration.

Senior Brother Lu truly had an eye for excellence; geniuses truly understood one another.

Moved by the sentiment, Qin Xian turned to Lu Shulong:

[—"Our Senior Brother Guan truly is a genius, isn't he!"](# "Speaker: Qin Xian")

—"Indeed. So breathtakingly beautiful,"-Lu Shulong said, his voice entranced.

[Beautiful?]

[Did he mean his smithing technique?]

Qin Xian wondered in confusion, glancing back at Guan Ye to check.

Guan Ye possessed decent facial features, but merely decent; compared to his other martial brothers—especially Lu Shulong, who ranked among the absolute fairest—he was only ordinarily handsome. And even if one were truly stunning, sweating in front of a scalding forge left little room for beauty.

At least at this moment, Guan Ye was anything but beautiful.

He had stripped off half his robe sleeve, tying his short black hair into a stubby ponytail. Raising his arm again and again, his lean musculature glistened with beads of sweat, making that half-protruding, vivid scarlet spine look exceptionally eerie.

If an uninitiated outsider walked in, they might very well be frightened half to death by Senior Brother Guan.

So Qin Xian thought.

Yet because his attention was fixed entirely on Guan Ye, he naturally failed to notice that beneath Lu Shulong's long, silver-blue bangs, those crystal-like eyes had morphed into vertical slit pupils rimmed in gold, gazing with rapt fascination at the space surrounding Guan Ye.

There hovered an ethereal layer of entities invisible to mortal eyes—unknown creatures bearing wings of golden gauze. They were alighting with joyous delight upon Guan Ye's arms, his shoulders, and his hammer, multiplying in numbers and glowing brighter by the second.

The moment these tiny entities reached a critical mass—

*CLANG!*

Guan Ye brought his hammer down in its final strike.

Confronted by the flawless creation before him, he stood dazed as if unable to process the reality. Only after a long moment did he finally lift his head, his gaze meeting Lu Shulong's.

Without even asking when Lu Shulong had arrived, Guan Ye spoke in a hoarse, trembling voice:

[—"Shu... Shulong! I... I seem to have broken through my bottleneck!"](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")

[—"I've reached the Foundation Establishment Late Stage!"](# "Speaker: Guan Ye")
