# Chapter 124: The Motivation to Learn (第124章 学习动力)

[What on earth is that creature?!]

Ran Deng stared in stark terror at the "little black lamb."

She was thoroughly spooked—in every conceivable sense of the word.

Although Zhou Hui had given her a forewarning before bringing her here, cautioning that this sector was profoundly abnormal and that some sinister conspiracy was undoubtedly afoot, breeding mutated aberrations, demonic fiends, ghosts, and monsters, Ran Deng had never envisioned it reaching such a horrific extreme.

When that volley of heavenly thunder descended, it was as if someone had [poked a hornet's nest](# "Literal CN: 捅了马蜂窝 (tǒng le mǎfēngwō) — stirring up a hornet's nest; provoking a massive, dangerous swarm")—swarms upon swarms surged into the air with a deafening, chaotic clamor.

In the past, Ran Deng had witnessed Zhou Hui summoning lightning tribulations several times. Wherever the celestial lightning struck, everything was instantly annihilated into pulverized ash. Never before had she seen a scenario where, the moment one layer was exterminated, another dense layer erupted from beneath! Even knowing they could never evade Master's divine thunder, merely looking upon that thick, suffocating carpet of grayish-white ash made one's throat constrict with dread—to say nothing of how the fiends below actively used the pulverized ashes of their brethren as shields, frantically clawing to breach the lightning's retribution.

[Even in the Demon Realm, it couldn't possibly be this bad!] Ran Deng thought.

Yet what truly made Ran Deng's hair stand on end was the black lamb her Master had brought along.

Whether because Zhou Hui did not wish to squander too much of his newly harvested heavenly lightning, he did not personally execute those aberrations that had managed to evade the first wave of thunder. Instead, he casually patted the black lamb on the back.

In truth, the black lamb needed no explicit instructions whatsoever. The instant it saw demonic fiends boiling upward like foul sludge churning to the surface of raw sewage, suspicious liquid was already drooling from the corners of its mouth. Having received Zhou Hui's authorization, the black lamb opened its jaws wide and exhaled forcefully downward:

A howling whirlwind whipped up above the crackling lightning.

Ashes, severed carcasses, and demonic fiends alike were violently swept into the vortex, churned into a single swirling mass, and miraculously sorted by color—looking bizarrely like a rainbow-colored glass of fruit juice.

Letting out a contented "Ying~", the little black lamb stretched its mouth to the absolute widest limit it could manage. Like a yawning black hole, it began vacuuming downward.

The swirling vortex of that demonic rainbow roll was dragged upward. Like the tapering cone of an ice cream (though Ran Deng had not actually eaten such a delicacy yet), the tail grew smaller and smaller until every last drop was sucked clean into the black lamb's bottomless maw.

Not a single crumb was spared.

This manner of consumption had completely transcended the definition of "eating."

Watching the little black lamb's [hurricane inhalation](# "Literal CN: 暴风吸入 (bàofēng xīrù) — hurricane inhalation / vacuuming down food at gale-force speed; internet slang for ravenously inhaling food/drink"), Ran Deng felt an icy chill prickle the back of her neck. She instinctively took several steps away from the black lamb and clutched Zhou Hui's sleeve.

The little black lamb seemed to notice Ran Deng's dread.

Having thoroughly sucked the demonic rainbow roll clean, it deliberately kept its mouth stretched in that black-hole yawn, turning its face straight toward Ran Deng.

Seeing this, Zhou Hui brought a swift knife-hand strike chopping down onto the back of the lamb's neck:

—"Stop fooling around!"-Zhou Hui snapped.

—"Ying, ying!"-the little black lamb whimpered aggrievedly as the chop forced its mouth shut.

Ran Deng suddenly felt that this black lamb's [shameless, cheeky expression](# "Literal CN: 贝戋 (bèi-jiān) — split glyph for 贱 (jiàn), meaning shameless, cheeky, or rascal") was hauntingly familiar.

The little black lamb paid no mind to his junior sister staring at him.

Having successfully pulled off this mischievous stunt, he was thoroughly delighted, not forgetting to push his luck with Master via divine sense:

—"Recently all the big feasts have been cooked by electricity, and they're all a bit charred,"-the lamb whined via divine sense, pushing its luck.-"It'd be nice if Master had a few more cooking techniques in his repertoire."

Zhou Hui glanced at Ran Deng, continuing to indulge his eldest disciple's habit of [plugging his ears while stealing a bell](# "Idiom: 掩耳盗铃 (yǎn'ěr-dàolíng) — plugging one's ears to steal a bell; burying one's head in the sand / self-deception").

He replied via divine sense as well:

—"Rest assured, there will be no more after this,"-Zhou Hui answered via divine sense.-"I only employed heavenly thunder this time to avoid accidentally harming the innocent. I have little heavenly thunder remaining; I must reserve some for my own research, your Second Junior Brother needs to refine it for his cultivation, and I have plans for another portion. We must ration it carefully."

[—"Ying?!"](# "Speaker: Yuwen Xuan (Black Lamb)")

—"Furthermore, although it was purified once with heavenly thunder, this strike was not as thorough as the volume during the last heavenly tribulation,"-Zhou Hui said, staring down at the black lamb.-"If you gorge yourself on demonic matter, you will suffer indigestion, will you not? An occasional snack is permissible, but you cannot eat any more."

The little black lamb widened its eyes at Zhou Hui, looking as though it were being subjected to unspeakable cruelty.

Yet because the lamb's facial expressions were so overly animated, it completely forgot that Ran Deng was still staring intently at it.

Ran Deng weighed the silent staredown between Master and the lamb, and suddenly asked:

—"Master, you wouldn't happen to be having a secret conversation via divine sense with this black lamb behind my back, would you?"

—"?!" The black lamb whipped its head around, staring nervously at Ran Deng.

Zhou Hui offered an enigmatic smile.

—"...The demonic aberrations below have mostly been cleared away,"-Zhou Hui answered evasively, retrieving a storage pouch and handing it to Ran Deng.-"A-Ran, why don't you lead the way?"

—"Understood,"-Ran Deng replied without pressing further.

For Master to switch topics was, in itself, an answer.

Ran Deng paid no further heed to the little black lamb glaring at her with wary suspicion. Opening the storage pouch, she peered inside, but the moment she saw the materials, her eyebrows knitted together:

—"Master, this is..."-she muttered, pulling a stalk of straw from the pouch.

If she was not mistaken, this was the exact material Jiuzhai had used to weave the scarecrow substitute for Lord Siwu.

It was thanks to this that Si Wu had managed to deceive the upper realm and the Heavenly Dao, utilizing a straw substitute to help Master reveal the map across the Abyssal Ocean.

—"Indeed. I requested it from Jiuzhai,"-Zhou Hui said.-"The situation below is deeply suspicious. It is best that neither the upper realm nor the Heavenly Dao detects us—or at the very least, we must conceal our presence from Carefree Palace."

—"I understand, Master,"-Ran Deng nodded, her brow furrowing in concentration.

She grabbed a handful of straw and began weaving it into a palace lantern.

Yet Ran Deng—who could effortlessly snip a miniature replica of the Thousand Stars Hall from a single sheet of paper—found her hands behaving as though they belonged to someone else when weaving this lantern. The woven palace lantern came out crooked and lopsided, so misshapen one could hardly tell it was supposed to be a lantern at all.

[So hideous!] The only reason the little black lamb didn't blurt the insult aloud was fear of blowing his cover, but mockery was plastered across his face as he egged Zhou Hui on via divine sense:

—"Master, make her reweave it,"-the black lamb whispered via divine sense.

Ran Deng cast an anxious, self-conscious glance toward Zhou Hui.

—"Can it be used?"-Zhou Hui merely asked.

—"I'll give it a try,"-Ran Deng said.

She gingerly lifted the unsightly palace lantern, opened her mouth, and exhaled a droplet of amber-yellow light from the tip of her tongue. It drifted gently into the heart of the lantern, igniting into a steady flame.

Bathed in that gentle, warm amber glow, the hideous lantern suddenly did not look quite so hideous after all.

—"Splendidly done,"-Zhou Hui praised.-"Continue to maintain this lantern. Do not allow it to extinguish until we depart the Abyssal Ocean."

—"Eh?!"-Ran Deng gasped, her eyes flying wide.

She immediately sought to back out:

—"Master, that's far too difficult!"-she protested, desperate to back out.-"Because it is meant to conceal us from the Heavenly Dao and the upper realm, I must maintain an unbroken, perfectly uniform output of spiritual energy! In truth, never mind igniting it—merely weaving this material is extraordinarily difficult. Even Patriarch Jiuzhai must have expended tremendous mental effort, right? And he merely wove a scarecrow; he never ignited it! I don't think I can do this..."

—"This is an exam,"-Zhou Hui said.

Ran Deng froze dead in her tracks.

—"If you cannot accomplish it, then upon our return, you shall undergo a diagnostic baseline exam all over again, just like Shulong,"-Zhou Hui said mercilessly, paying no heed to Ran Deng's rapidly blanching face.

This time, Ran Deng stiffened from head to toe.

She was well aware of how Fifth Junior Brother had suddenly been subjected to an isolated diagnostic exam a while back, and had heard that because he performed poorly, he was subsequently forced to attend the Immortal Arts Assembly in shackles!

Ran Deng had secretly wondered what atrocious blasphemy Lu Shulong had committed—who would have thought the axe was about to fall on her neck next!

[Master isn't preparing to administer a 'final exam' to everyone, is he?!]

Just as Ran Deng was spiraling into frantic dread, Zhou Hui patted her on the shoulder:

—"Very well, A-Ran, lead the way!"

—"Yes, Master,"-Ran Deng replied, sounding as though she were about to burst into tears.

Cupping the lantern far more gingerly than before, she allowed it to hover before her, drifting forward at a tortoise's crawl.

Zhou Hui did not rush her in the slightest; he always possessed boundless patience for his disciples.

He strolled leisurely behind his third disciple, watching his eldest disciple trot circles around his feet.

—"Well played this time! Master, you've always been far too gentle with Little Junior Sister,"-the little black lamb gloated via divine sense. His use of "Little Junior Sister" referred to Ran Deng; after all, she had been his junior sister for nearly two centuries, and the habit was ingrained. Although Yuwen Xuan had corrected his form of address after Mo Bei arrived, in private settings like this, he still habitually called her that.-"You treat her far more gently than you ever treated me or Old Second, which is why she's grown so spoiled and lazy! Giving her regular exams is the only way! That's the only way she'll ever take things seriously!"

[...What utter nonsense is the fellow who comes in dead last every single exam spewing?!] Zhou Hui thought, casting a sidelong glance at the black lamb.

Yet in truth, only an exam could compel Ran Deng to take this lantern even slightly seriously.

Zhou Hui gazed at the palace lantern drifting before Ran Deng.

This child Ran Deng truly never spared a single glance toward anything that failed to interest her. Before coming here, Zhou Hui had brought up the Heavenly Dao with Patriarch Jiuzhai, but Ran Deng had clearly dismissed it as 'Master and another Tribulation titan discussing complicated, difficult matters' and hadn't inquired a single word.

This was the fundamental difference between Ran Deng and Qu Qianxing. In terms of emotional intelligence and sheer observational power, Ran Deng was actually slightly superior; yet she never pried into matters outside her interest. Qu Qianxing, on the other hand, possessed a control desire that was far too intense; he would pursue anything he did not comprehend to its very roots. That was why Qu Qianxing had noticed the little black lamb constantly clinging to Master's lap early on, whereas Ran Deng only took note of the sheep when brought into intimate proximity.

With an attitude like hers, Zhou Hui hadn't even known how to broach the subject.

Indeed, this lantern had been prepared specifically for Ran Deng.

Although Zhou Hui's descent into the Abyssal Ocean carried the intention of concealing their presence from Carefree Palace, 'merely' Carefree Palace was hardly worthy of Zhou Hui being so meticulous. And just as Ran Deng pointed out, Jiuzhai had never ignited his scarecrow; lighting it was by no means a prerequisite to hide from Carefree Palace.

This lantern was intended less to deceive Carefree Palace than to reverse-refine Ran Deng's spiritual energy.

Even Zhou Hui's request to Patriarch Jiuzhai for straw that could deceive the Heavenly Dao was entirely for this purpose.

In the Heavenly Dao's script... no, in the previous life, Ran Deng had perished because she was not fast enough when fleeing. Although according to Lu Shulong, Ran Deng might have [slacked off and let things rot](# "Literal CN: 摆烂 (bǎilàn) — letting things rot; modern slang for completely giving up on effort") to some extent, her lack of raw combat prowess was an undeniable reality.

Yet expecting a formation master to duel others in brute combat power was inherently absurd.

Therefore, Zhou Hui resolved to refine Ran Deng's spiritual energy using Jiuzhai's divining straw—material capable of deceiving the Heavenly Dao and the upper realm.

That way, even if she could not flee swiftly enough, her refined spiritual energy could still blind the Heavenly Dao and the upper realm. Should an unforeseen calamity arise, she could mask her coordinates and wage battle through the most suitable methodologies—imagine a formation master who could deceive the Heavenly Dao itself! In the absolute worst-case scenario, even if Ran Deng were still slain, her refined spiritual energy would ensure she could [fake her death to escape](# "Literal CN: 死遁 (sǐdùn) — death escape; literary/trope term for faking one's death to escape a plot or danger").

This was the single most tailored ability for Ran Deng, bar none.

Yet given Ran Deng's temperament, she had likely never even considered utilizing formations to fight others. At present, the only instance where she utilized formations in battle was playing Immortal Chess against Qu Qianxing. By contrast, despite her verbal grumbling, she executed architectural cave-dwelling engineering projects flawlessly; her preferences were glaringly obvious at a glance.

Naturally, most crucially of all: even if Ran Deng wished to learn, she might not necessarily possess the capacity to master it.

According to Jiuzhai, divination cultivation was an art intensely reliant on innate talent and serendipitous destiny; the heavens virtually dictated whether one was suited for the craft—and ironically, only those chosen by the heavens were capable of deceiving the heavens.

Thus, Jiuzhai had turned a blind eye, tacitly agreeing to Zhou Hui's request.

Whether Ran Deng could achieve it depended entirely upon herself.

Zhou Hui understood all too well the anguish of striving desperately yet remaining fundamentally unable to learn. Consequently, he deemed it best not to inform Ran Deng beforehand, letting her test first whether she possessed this 'karmic affinity.'

[If she cannot manage it, force-feeding education will be the only option left,] Zhou Hui thought, gazing at Ran Deng's back. [Starting with remedial tutoring in combat formations that she despises—]

Ran Deng shivered instinctively, as if sensing the impending peril.

She felt her Master was deadly serious—quite possibly already drafting an exhaustive remedial syllabus for her.

—"...Master, are you truly preparing to go to war against the Heavenly Dao?"-Ran Deng asked suddenly.

—"Why do you ask that?"-Zhou Hui countered.

—"Your conversation with Patriarch Jiuzhai sounded rather strange,"-Ran Deng said.-"Although there were parts I couldn't comprehend, ever since the heavenly tribulation descended—no, even before that—you seemed to grow tense, as though you were eager to settle our cultivation paths for us... Even though you've always told us there was no rush."

Just as observed earlier: this child was exceptionally perceptive.

—"I have no intention of going to war with the Heavenly Dao,"-Zhou Hui replied.-"Primarily because even if I wished to, I could not. After all, a video game character cannot modify the backend code."

—"What does that sentence mean... Aahh!?"-Ran Deng let out a brief shriek as the lantern flame nearly sputtered out while she was speaking.

She hurriedly stabilized her spiritual energy output. Once the palace lantern's flame was secured, she babbled as though reassuring herself:

—"It's fine! The spiritual energy flow around here was just slightly turbulent, which caused the flame to flicker for a second. But I've already stabilized the flame; it didn't go out! Yes, it didn't go out!"

—"...Aren't you going to examine what caused the spiritual energy flow to become turbulent in the first place?"-Zhou Hui asked.

—"Right away!"-Ran Deng answered in a fluster.

She channeled more spiritual energy and surveyed the surroundings, her eyes widening in disbelief:

Naturally, it was not that some demonic aberrations had appeared. Even if there had been any, they had all been obliterated by heavenly thunder earlier, and whatever survived the thunder had been devoured by the black lamb.

What astonished Ran Deng was that beneath the fissure ahead, a sprawling, dense cluster of buildings emerged.

They paid almost no heed to exterior aesthetics, maximizing spatial utilization to such an extreme that the subterranean complex resembled a squirming, suffocating insect hive. Merely looking at it made one's scalp tingle with dread.

Fortunately, having just witnessed those grotesque demonic abominations, Ran Deng was somewhat desensitized to such sights.

She turned delightfully to Zhou Hui:

—"Master, just as you foresaw, it truly isn't an ordinary demonic nest! Someone has indeed been orchestrating foul play down here!"

[And then, Master, just like before, please blast the buildings below with lightning, charge straight to the mastermind's lair, wrap this up, and let us leave as quickly as possible!]

Ran Deng clutched the palace lantern, her eyes sparkling with anticipation.

The lantern's flame brightened in tandem with her soaring spirits, nearly scorching the surrounding straw. Ran Deng scrambled to rein in her spiritual energy once more, dimming the radiance.

Truly, she did not wish to endure this lantern for a single second longer.

Unfortunately, Zhou Hui did not immediately summon heavenly thunder. Looking upon those structures, he too wore a look of genuine surprise:

—"...I never expected this,"-Zhou Hui marveled.-"To think *this* would be located here."

[—"'This'?"](# "Speaker: Ran Deng")

Zhou Hui looked at Ran Deng in excellent spirits:

—"A-Ran, let me test you,"-Zhou Hui said in excellent spirits.-"Looking at the structures below, which component do you consider the most vital?"

[Master, can we please not bring up exams?!]

Ran Deng reluctantly surveyed the vista, pointing toward a certain sector:

—"Over there? It appears to have the tightest defenses, so it must be the most crucial section of this complex."

—"Incorrect. It is this."

Zhou Hui took a step toward the fissure, planting his foot firmly atop what he deemed the most important object.

It was a staircase.

A seemingly ordinary staircase traversing the entire facility.

Yet Zhou Hui declared:

—"This is no ordinary staircase. This is the Ascension Stairway."

—"The Ascension Stairway... The *Ascension Stairway*?!"

Ran Deng blanked for a second before finally recalling what Zhou Hui was referring to.

The flame of the palace lantern wavered alongside her shock. Fortunately, Ran Deng was not an archaeology fanatic like her father, otherwise she would have set the entire lantern ablaze.

Ran Deng steadied her emotions and complained:

—"Master, could you please stop scaring me out of my wits?! It is truly, extraordinarily difficult for me to keep this lantern steady!"

—"Alright, alright,"-Zhou Hui humored her.

Yet Zhou Hui's thoughts were clearly ensnared by the Ascension Stairway. Descending the steps one by one, he sighed with profound sentiment:

—"Truly... whether it is a suitable heavenly tribulation, the White Jade Capital, or the Ascension Stairway—whenever one actively seeks them, they are nowhere to be found. Yet the moment one stops caring, they deliver themselves to one's doorstep one after another."

[Your expression doesn't look like you don't care in the slightest, Master!]

Ran Deng gave a wry smile, straining to control the palace lantern as she followed behind him.

She knew that even if Master claimed he was no longer ascending, he could never resist researching such phenomena—especially the Ascension Stairway, a legendary existence said to connect directly to the upper realm.

Ascension was by no means as simple as flying above the cloud layer.

Whether the True Immortal Realm, the Immortal Demon Realm, or even the Demon Realm and Beast Realm, all were fundamentally distinct dimensions. Not only did their spatial dimensions differ, but the very flow of time varied drastically. The folkloric adage that "a day in heaven is a year on earth" was merely a crude conversion; the true divergences ran far deeper. Consequently, a stairway bridging the two planes had to possess extraordinary spatio-temporal properties.

This fell squarely within Zhou Hui's domain of cultivation. From that perspective, the value of the Ascension Stairway to him was even greater than the White Jade Capital!

—"Still, how extravagant!"-Ran Deng examined the Ascension Stairway with growing curiosity.

Unlike the pristine, well-preserved White Jade Capital that looked almost identical to its ancient glory, this stairway appeared dilapidated and weathered, virtually indistinguishable from other broken ruins scattered throughout the Abyssal Ocean.

To be frank, had Zhou Hui not pointed it out, Ran Deng would never have recognized it as the Ascension Stairway.

Judging others by her own standards, Ran Deng remarked:

—"Did they even know this was the Ascension Stairway? To actually treat it as an ordinary staircase..."

—"Of course they knew,"-Zhou Hui said, a hint of genuine displeasure creeping into his voice at the topic.-"Didn't you notice? Every single building was constructed tightly hugging this segment of the Ascension Stairway. To put it bluntly, their testing facility is simply siphoning the Ascension Stairway's power."

—"So that's how it is,"-Ran Deng sighed.

Observing the subterranean structures once more through the discerning eye of an architect, she realized that this hive truly was crammed suffocatingly around the staircase. Many sectors defied architectural logic, looking so haphazard it was difficult to discern where one might even begin repairs.

Ran Deng could not resist critiquing:

—"These buildings are completely irrational; they barely took structural integrity into account."

—"Indeed. Carefree Palace certainly has some 'talent' among them—who would have thought I'd witness a style reminiscent of [cyberpunk](# "Literal CN: 赛博朋克 (sàibópéngkè) — cyberpunk; high-tech / low-life aesthetic with densely crammed, chaotic modular megastructures") in the cultivation world..."-Zhou Hui remarked.

—"What kind of style?!"-Ran Deng called out.

She thought she heard Master utter a very peculiar word, but by the time she asked, she realized Master's voice was already echoing from far below.

Clearly, her Master had become so engrossed in the Ascension Stairway that he had momentarily forgotten his disciple.

Because Ran Deng walked slowly to maintain the lantern's flame, and because she was curious about the surrounding architecture, she had been left behind.

Yet being left behind hardly mattered.

Ran Deng trusted her Master's might implicitly. Following the earlier heavenly thunder purge, this place harbored zero danger, and she possessed the guidance of this lantern.

—This lantern was by no means casually refined; it was a beacon capable of guiding a cultivator to the 'truth' they sought, even if they themselves were unaware of what 'truth' they desired.

Phased that way, it sounded somewhat [idealistic / mind-over-matter](# "Literal CN: 唯心 (wéixīn) — philosophical idealism; mind over matter"), yet cultivation itself was inherently idealistic.

Thus, with this lantern in a place stripped of danger, Ran Deng needed only to descend at her own pace to reunite with her Master. She wasn't afraid in the slightest—

—As if!

—"This accursed place... I could come ten thousand times and still never get used to it,"-Ran Deng muttered under her breath.

Before admitting to herself that she was a slacker, she had always believed that the reason she refused to inherit the family clan business was because her grandmother and father used to drag her running around beneath the Abyssal Ocean during her childhood, scaring the wits out of her. Although it served as an excuse, it was genuinely part of the reason.

—"M-Master, wait for me!"-Ran Deng called out, pulling a peach wood tally from her storage pouch to contact him, lest Zhou Hui remain absorbed and forget about her for hours.

Yet before she could transmit her voice, a sharp *clack-clack-clack* echoed, freezing her mid-motion.

—"What is that..."-Ran Deng breathed, her hands shaking so violently from the sudden noise that her peach wood tally slipped from her fingers and tumbled down the stone steps.

Ran Deng had no chance to retrieve it, for within the shadow cast by an adjacent structure, a pair of gleaming, luminous eyes was watching her intently.

That figure, that silhouette...

—"Little black lamb?"-Ran Deng called out tentatively.

The "black lamb" cast a fleeting glance at Ran Deng, then abruptly wheeled around and bolted into an alley branching off the other side of the staircase.

—"Wait! Don't run—!"-Ran Deng shouted.

Good heavens, had this black lamb seized the opportunity while Master was engrossed in the Ascension Stairway to make a break for it?!

Although she already harbored several incredible suspicions regarding the black lamb's true identity, witnessing the terrifying manner in which it had devoured demonic abominations earlier made her reluctant to confirm her deduction. She preferred to believe it was not an ordinary "sheep," but rather some kind of bizarre beast.

If it escaped, Master would have quite a headache on his hands, wouldn't he?

Yet could she catch it by herself?

Perhaps calling Master over directly would be much faster?

Ran Deng deliberated in anxious distress. She took a few steps forward attempting to pursue the "black lamb," glanced down toward the depths of the staircase, and finally looked up at the dimly glowing palace lantern hovering above her head.

—"Do you think I can catch that fellow's stubby tail?"-Ran Deng asked the palace lantern.

The palace lantern immediately pivoted and began floating unhurriedly toward the direction where the "black lamb" had fled.

—"...Fine!"

Ran Deng let out a defeated sigh.

Reluctantly trailing behind the palace lantern:

—"I'm telling you right now, I'm completely useless,"-she grumbled.-"I still feel going to find Master would be much more reliable."

Unfortunately, the palace lantern could not comprehend her complaints. Resolute and steady, it pursued the "black lamb," drifting deep into the heart of the subterranean architecture.
