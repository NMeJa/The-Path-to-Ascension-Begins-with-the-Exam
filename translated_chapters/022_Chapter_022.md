# Chapter 22: As a Master

Qu Qianxing stood before the "chessboard" with a furrowed brow.

He couldn't fathom why a perfectly good open lecture had devolved into something like this. Yet as long as it was Master's command, there was bound to be profound meaning behind it. Qu Qianxing had no objections to playing this [instructional match](# "Literal CN: 指导棋 (zhǐdǎo qí) — game where a master or stronger player plays to instruct and demonstrate concepts to students"); although he didn't relish it, as long as Master required it, assisting with anything was of no consequence.

What truly displeased Qu Qianxing was Yuwen Xuan's conduct.

Although Qu Qianxing had never encountered this kind of "chess" before, he could discern that the initial conditions of this match were far from fair—the strength of the "pieces" clearly dictated how difficult it would be to secure victory in the end.

Small wonder Yuwen Xuan had acted like a shameless rascal before Master.

Yet after wheedling those pieces from Master, that bastard didn't even use them himself, shoving Qu Qianxing forward to play against Ran Deng. What did he mean by "he wouldn't bully junior sister"? Did he think Qu Qianxing liked bullying junior sister?!

Qu Qianxing thought this with deep annoyance, and said to Ran Deng:
—"A-Ran, shall we swap pieces? Otherwise it isn't fair,"-Qu Qianxing said.

—"Second Senior Brother, you underestimate me too much,"-Ran Deng refused.-"Perhaps my practical combat is not as strong as yours, but this is chess, and it involves formations. I won't necessarily lose to you."

—"But..."-Qu Qianxing began.

—"Besides, this is Second Senior Brother's first time playing this kind of chess, isn't it?"-Ran Deng laughed.-"I, on the other hand, helped Master calibrate the board. I've already played against Master several times, so I'm your senior in this regard."

Saying this, Ran Deng's smile deepened:
—"Or could it be that Second Senior Brother is already making excuses for your defeat?"

[...This junior sister of mine is not the least bit cute.]

The corner of Qu Qianxing's mouth twitched, forcing a fake smile:
—"Then please instruct me."

*

Zhou Hui smiled as he waited for his two disciples to take their positions at opposite ends of the "chessboard."

—"Now that the players are in place, let us select a board! Since it is everyone's first time playing this game, we shall select a small board—namely, the 'Secret Realm' mode,"-Zhou Hui announced, raising a hand slightly.

His wide sleeve swept over the chessboard. Exquisite flowers, plants, and trees sprouted upon it, eliciting another wave of admiration from the disciples.

Zhou Hui then asked an unexpected question:
—"Qianxing, do you know how 'secret realms' are formed?"

As an honor student, Qu Qianxing was naturally unfazed by such sudden pop-quizzes. Lengthy theoretical explanations capable of numbing ordinary listeners flowed from his lips in an instant:
—"The formation of secret realms is divided into natural and artificial origins. Yet regardless of which kind, whether due to innate or acquired causes, a spatial structure akin to a formation array forms, rendering the space here sturdier than its surroundings. However, the flow of spiritual energy is inconstant. The space of the mortal realm constantly undergoes destruction and rebirth under the erosion of spiritual energy. A relatively sturdy pocket of space will not be destroyed, yet when covered by newly generated space, fragments are formed within the seams of space-time—which are what are commonly termed 'fragments' [of ancient realms]."-Qu Qianxing recited.

Zhou Hui asked again:
—"Then what is the crux of exploring a secret realm?"

—"Since a secret realm is born of a formation array, the crux is naturally to determine the category of the array, and either take control of or destroy the array eye,"-Qu Qianxing replied, having zero flaws in his theoretical knowledge.

—"Very good,"-Zhou Hui nodded approvingly.-"Then let us begin with the most ordinary circular array."

Along with Zhou Hui's words, a horde of demonic beasts "sprouted" across the board, encircling various positions. At the center of the board guarded by the beasts, a small hill rose up, upon which a treasure chest glittered and shone.

—"The victory condition is very simple: whoever obtains the treasure chest wins,"-Zhou Hui announced.-"Begin!"

With those words, the "pieces" sprang into motion, eager to fight.

Qu Qianxing felt this transparent "secret realm," visible in a single glance, required little overthinking. He first dispatched his "Eldest Senior Brother" to blaze a trail, while "he himself" led the junior brothers and sisters behind, marching toward the hilltop.

Yu Cong finally got to witness the combat performance of the direct disciples' pieces.

Unlike Yan Fei, who had used his best stance, the piece representing Yuwen Xuan used the most basic [auto-attack](# "Literal CN: 平A (píng A) — gaming slang for basic normal attacks without using special skills"): whatever came his way, he hacked it down with a single sword strike. Even so, Yuwen Xuan's attack power was vastly superior to Yan Fei's; Golden Core stage demon beasts went down in a single blow.

Yan Fei caught Yu Cong's disdainful expression and curled his lip:
—"He's a Soul Formation Peak existence after all. Of course he's that strong."

—"Yeah, your piece probably would have been chewed to death ages ago!"-Yu Cong joked.

He glanced over at Ran Deng's side, only to freeze in place.

Ran Deng had actually deployed only a single piece: Yan Fei.

Of course, Yan Fei's attack power could not even begin to compare to Yuwen Xuan's. But as a sword cultivator, he possessed inherent advantages: high flying speed and agile dodging. To be precise, his speed was still inferior to Yuwen Xuan's, but he didn't need to fight monsters!

As a result, they watched that tiny Yan Fei piece deftly dodge a Foundation Establishment demon beast, snatch the spiritual herb it guarded, and a tiny boot icon popped up above its head: Speed +3.

Repeating this, the piece's speed was swiftly buffed to around 10. Like a bolt of lightning, it streaked toward the center of the board and popped open the treasure chest.

The entire process took less than half a cup of tea—or rather, by the new Sundial Clock's reckoning, less than three minutes. When Ran Deng won, Qu Qianxing hadn't even processed what had just occurred.

—"That's not fair!"-Qu Qianxing protested in dissatisfaction after reviewing the match.-"I had no idea there were spiritual herbs that boost speed."

—"I reminded Second Senior Brother at the very beginning that being familiar with the rules is paramount, didn't I?"-Ran Deng laughed.-"How about another round?"

And so, they began a second match.

In this round, Qu Qianxing went to seize the spiritual herbs first. Ran Deng, on the other hand, fielded a spell cultivator alongside her sword cultivator. Rather than picking herbs, they ran off to provoke the demonic beasts—the spell cultivator attacked from afar while the sword cultivator [pulled aggro](# "Literal CN: 拉仇恨 (lā chóuhèn) — gaming term for drawing enemy enmity/aggression"), quickly turning the entire map into utter chaos and wiping out Qu Qianxing's entire team.

—"Despicable!"-Qu Qianxing's eyes flushed red with fury, looking strikingly gorgeous.

—"Another round, then?"-Ran Deng chuckled.

The third match was quite a spectacle. Right from the start, Qu Qianxing directed his pieces straight at Ran Deng. It seemed his temper had flared up, casting aside any sibling affection to eliminate Ran Deng first. Unexpectedly, Ran Deng [waited at ease for the exhausted enemy](# "Literal CN: 以逸待劳 (yǐ yì dài láo) — 36 Stratagems: waiting at leisure while the enemy tires themselves out"): she first ambushed Qu Qianxing's pill cultivator and artifact cultivator, entangled the body cultivator with demonic beasts, lured the piece representing Qu Qianxing into a trap, and finished him off with her sword cultivator.

Yan Fei's face flushed red with excitement:
—"I killed Second Senior Brother!"

—"Yes, yes, you killed Second Senior Brother,"-Yu Cong said, his eyes burning with envy.

In reality, in an actual battle, ten Yan Feis wouldn't be enough for Qu Qianxing to slaughter. But these were pieces with fixed attack power—there was no cultivation realm suppression, and the board featured all kinds of spiritual herbs, beasts, and traps as buffs.

Even knowing this, Qu Qianxing's personal mood was anything but pleasant.

He paid no attention to two mere ordinary disciples, keeping his gaze locked onto Ran Deng. This time, without waiting for her to say a word, he took the initiative:
—"One more round!"

—"Ah..."-Ran Deng let out a helpless laugh, glancing toward Zhou Hui.-"Master—"

—"Qianxing, enough. Let us conclude this lesson's instructional match here,"-Zhou Hui said with a smile.

Qu Qianxing let out an "Ah," only now remembering why he had played this game in the first place. He looked around and discovered the other disciples gazing at him while chattering in hushed discussion.

From the corners of his eyes to the tips of his ears, Qu Qianxing flushed beet red. Yet this only made him all the more unwilling to accept his defeat:

[It was all because I didn't know the rules!]

[Just one more time! One more time and I can win!]

Unfortunately, Zhou Hui gave him no further chance:
—"If you like, you may invite your junior sister to play in private. For now, let the other disciples give it a try."

—"...Yes, Master."

Qu Qianxing was obedient after all. Unwilling though he was, he took a step back, shooting a glare at Ran Deng that made it clear he intended to settle this score with her later.

Before Ran Deng could even react, Yuwen Xuan made a provocative gesture behind her back, firmly pulling all of Qu Qianxing's [aggro](# "Literal CN: 仇恨 (chóuhèn) — gaming slang for monster/player enmity and hate") onto himself.

[Ah, that bastard Yuwen Xuan definitely did this on purpose!]-Qu Qianxing remembered who the true culprit was.

Zhou Hui paid no mind to the "intimate bonding" among his direct disciples, for the other disciples in the sect were already itching to have a go.

Rather, Qu Qianxing's defeat had only spurred the others further:

[Even with such overpowered pieces, he played like that! Second Senior Brother is a [terrible chess player](# "Literal CN: 臭棋篓子 (chòuqílǒuzi) — someone who plays chess terribly but loves playing anyway"), but if I went up, it would definitely be different!]

*(Qu Qianxing: ...)*

Of course, not everyone wanted the direct disciples' set of pieces. Ran Deng's performance had also served as an inspiration, making many eager to experience what it felt like to defeat Heavenly Spirit Sect direct disciples on the board.

Finally, there was a small group with particularly ambitious ideas—such as Yu Cong.

Yu Cong plucked up his courage and asked loudly:
—"Lord Immortal Venerable, may I use my own chess piece?"

—"Of course you may,"-Zhou Hui replied, giving him a meaningful look.

Before Yu Cong could ponder the deeper meaning of that glance from the Immortal Venerable, he saw the Untiring Immortal Venerable wave his sleeve. A strange jade platform appeared in the corner of the classroom.

Zhou Hui announced:
—"You may [gashapon-twist](# "Literal CN: 扭 (niǔ) — gashapon/capsule toy twisting mechanic") your own pieces there. However, be advised: generating a chess piece not only requires a certain cultivation realm, but also places considerable demands on your technical skills."

[The Immortal Venerable answered me!]

Yu Cong thought in delight, entirely ignoring Zhou Hui's warning.

He and a crowd rushed over to the jade platform, attempting to craft their own pieces.

Yet no matter how many times he followed the instructions, he failed.

—"What's going on? My cultivation is clearly high enough!"-Yu Cong said in disbelief. Furthermore, from a sword cultivator's standpoint, Yu Cong felt he was even a bit stronger than Yan Fei.

Could there be a malfunction with the jade platform? No, this was a magical artifact crafted by the Immortal Venerable; it couldn't possibly have problems.

In the end, Yan Fei patted his shoulder and pointed him to the instructions on the jade platform:

> Piece Generation Rules:
> 
> 1. Must possess a cultivation realm of Foundation Establishment or above;
> 2. Must have mastered at least one specialized skill;
> 3. Must possess corresponding foundational knowledge (Reference subjects: Cultivation Arts 60 points, Artifact Forging 40 points, Wood-Attribute Spells...)

[The third... possess corresponding foundational knowledge? Even the scores are listed?!]

Yu Cong's vision turned black.

He recalled his tragic Grand Unified Examination scores.

[Sob... I'll study!]

[I'll study, okay?!]

*

Indeed, no matter the world, practical classes always brought students the most joy, Zhou Hui thought.

Seeing the disciples swarming around the "gaming table," Zhou Hui left the task of maintaining order to Qu Qianxing and the other direct disciples. Erasing his presence, he stood to the side, watching.

In truth, "practical classes" were not rare in the cultivation world. Yet once anything turned into studying, it ceased to be pleasant—especially learning without immediate feedback; even the fiercest [Involution Kings](# "Literal CN: 卷王 (juǎnwáng) — champion of grinding/studying") would lose their passion.

Thus, for ordinary cultivators, the most popular "practical class" was probably only exploring secret realms.

Unfortunately, secret realm resources were scarce—[too many monks and too little congee](# "Literal CN: 僧多粥少 (sēng duō zhōu shǎo) — idiom meaning supply falls far short of demand"). Even great sects holding secret realm resources only opened them once every thirty to fifty years, and only elite disciples qualified.

On the other hand, combat within a genuine secret realm was no joke. It was a true life-and-death struggle, hardly a place suitable for "teaching."

There were also cultivators skilled in illusion arts who created "illusory realms." But such techniques consumed vast spiritual energy and required meticulous effort. Thus, most were used for trials or guarding cave abodes—or at worst, like the undersea palace from the exam hall a few days ago, used for grand banquets to entertain honored guests. (Of course, using such a palace for examinations was likely unique to the Heavenly Spirit Sect.) Seldom did anyone use them for amusement; even if some cultivators did so for fun, it was strictly done in private.

At least, prior to this, Zhou Hui had never imagined illusion arts could reach this level.

He had not expected that in another world where illusion arts did not exist at all, a virtual world could be constructed purely of numbers and signals—and the "game" before him was merely the tip of the iceberg.

Ever since waking from that "grand dream," Zhou Hui occasionally found himself pondering:

"Humanity" in that other world was like a singular, unified organism. A thought generated by one neuron (an individual human) would be amplified, broadcast, and become nourishment for the entirety of "humanity." Even if parts turned necrotic or rigid, on the whole, it was evolving and growing.

Looking at the cultivation world by contrast, the difference between mortals and cultivators was like two separate species. Even among cultivators, Mahayana and Foundation Establishment practitioners seemed like two distinct species as well. Most were dangling by two carrots—"lifespan" and "power"—plodding in circles like donkeys turning a millstone. Even if exceptionally visionary individuals occasionally arose, their minor reforms were not worth mentioning before absolute power. The entire world was like rigid, unyielding mountain rock, remaining unchanged for thousands of years. The only way to transcend this plane was to wring massive amounts of energy from this world to achieve "Ascension"—

—"Master? Master!"

—"...Ah?"-Zhou Hui snapped out of his reverie in bewilderment, only to find his third disciple, Ran Deng, looking at him with concern.

—"My apologies, I was lost in thought just now,"-Zhou Hui smiled.-"Is something the matter?"

—"Nothing much, just that... Master's expression looked a bit strange a moment ago,"-Ran Deng said worriedly.

Ran Deng had actually wanted to ask if Master was feeling unwell anywhere. When she had glanced in this direction earlier, she had felt her Master was turning translucent, like a ghost that was about to vanish in the very next instant, giving her quite a fright.

Now that she calmed down, she felt she must have seen wrong. After all, theoretically, a Tribulation Transcendence existence would suffer no illness or physical agony, and his lifespan was bound to be vastly longer than a mere early Golden Core like herself.

Sure enough, once Zhou Hui snapped back to reality, the previous anomaly was nowhere to be seen.

He replied casually:
—"I was likely just thinking about things too deeply."

—"I see,"-Ran Deng said, though she still wasn't completely reassured.

Even if she chalked it up to overthinking, ever since the last heavenly tribulation, when Zhou Hui claimed he would cultivate no more and would simply enjoy life—yet proceeded to pour all his energy into sect affairs—she couldn't shake the feeling that Master was settling his final affairs.

Thinking of this, Ran Deng pressed on with concern:
—"Are you worried about Second Senior Brother?"

[Qianxing?...] Zhou Hui glanced toward the chessboard and smiled:
—"You noticed?"

—"I didn't realize before, but after playing two games of chess, how could I not?"-Ran Deng glanced at Qu Qianxing, who was patrolling the hall, and giggled.-"Second Senior Brother is great at theoretical [armchair strategizing](# "Literal CN: 纸上谈兵 (zhǐshàng-tánbīng) — fighting on paper / armchair strategist, based on Zhao Kuo of the Warring States period"), but his practical experience is still too lacking. He doesn't understand what situations arise in a secret realm under the influence of formations. It's truly brilliant of Master to conceive this method for Second Senior Brother to practice."

—"This method was not conceived by me,"-Zhou Hui said.

—"Then whoever conceived it must be a master of illusion arts!"-Ran Deng said nonchalantly.

Zhou Hui had intended to explain in detail, but Ran Deng didn't actually care where this "chessboard" came from. She took the initiative to shoulder the task, a rare occurrence for her:
—"Master, you can rest easy leaving this place to me! I'll definitely make Second Senior Brother get familiar with all kinds of secret realm configurations as quickly as possible!"

Hearing this, Zhou Hui's smile deepened:
—"Good, then I leave it in your hands."

[For A-Ran's chronically lazy temperament to take such initiative is rare indeed. As expected, games truly do ignite enthusiasm!]

Unaware of Zhou Hui's misunderstanding, Ran Deng looked at her Master with anticipation:
[...Go rest in peace!]

Zhou Hui smiled:
—"Since Qianxing is in your hands, I shall go check on your youngest junior brother."

—"Eh? But Youngest Junior Sister has already gone..."

—"As expected, Bei'er is worried too,"-Zhou Hui nodded, and vanished on the spot.-"Since the last exam, Xi'er exhausted all his spiritual energy and has yet to recover. It is time I went to see him."

With these words, his figure vanished completely, leaving Ran Deng standing there in dazed bewilderment:
—"Wait, that's not..."

[I wanted you to go rest!]

[This brother of mine is probably an idiot,]-Mo Bei thought with concern.

As she peeled the skin of an immortal peach, she glanced toward the jade bed beside her.

Her twin brother lay there, his large eyes tightly shut. That little face so resembling her own was pale with a sickly greenish tint, yet crimson talisman runes crawled from beneath his collarbones all the way to the corners of his eyes, making that usually endearing, lovely face appear bizarre... and bewitching.

Mo Bei knew, however, that these runes were the "fractures" that appeared when a [cultivation furnace](# "Literal CN: 炉鼎 (lúdǐng) — human vessel exploited for dual-cultivation or spiritual energy extraction") overextended spiritual power.

Yes, Mo Bei and Mo Xi were a pair of cultivation furnaces.

Or rather, they had once been treated as a pair of cultivation furnaces by the Palace of Fallen Immortals.

Mo Bei wasn't entirely clear how she had fallen into that situation. Was she sold? Or abducted? Who were her parents? Where was her hometown? Having lost her memories before the age of seven, Mo Bei had no idea where she and Mo Xi came from—she wasn't even sure if "Mo Bei" and "Mo Xi" were their real names.

When her memories began, she was already in the Palace of Fallen Immortals.

It sounded tragic, yet Mo Bei didn't feel she was particularly pitiful.

Because her aptitude as a cultivation furnace was exceptionally good—the top-tier kind—she hadn't suffered much abuse. Instead, she had been pampered and enshrined like high-priced merchandise.

Although Mo Bei vaguely sensed her future likely wouldn't be very bright, before they were sold, they wouldn't do anything to depreciate their "merchandise." Besides, who could say for sure about the future? What if the buyer turned out to be a handsome young hero who cherished beauty? At least, that was how the storybooks she read portrayed it.

Yet Mo Xi didn't seem to think that way.

From their time in the Palace of Fallen Immortals, Mo Xi had always preferred showing off.

He wanted more spirit stones, more dharma treasures, more wealth; he proactively took interest in the world outside Fallen Immortal Palace; he actively communicated with outsiders, sometimes even using Mo Bei as a foil to make himself stand out.

Mo Bei couldn't understand what meaning there was in her twin brother doing this. As "merchandise," she understood her and Mo Xi's value all too well—they were a pair, bundled together for sale. Precisely because they were twins, their price doubled, making them prized and precious. Losing either one would slash their value in half. So what was this fellow hoping to accomplish by prancing around so much? Did he think someone would take such a special liking to him that they'd keep him alone and not sell him?

In truth, all of Mo Xi's "efforts" in the Palace of Fallen Immortals came to naught.

Suddenly, one day, the Palace of Fallen Immortals was overturned by a cultivator. He rescued the abducted girls, opened the pitch-black warehouse of Fallen Immortal Palace, and turned the "merchandise" within back into human beings.

Regrettably, that cultivator was nothing like the young, handsome swordsman described in storybooks—ah, he was handsome, certainly, but his seniority and age were a bit too grand for Mo Bei—and naturally, he didn't embark on a romance with Mo Bei. He even sent all the "merchandise" back to their hometowns. Because Mo Bei and Mo Xi truly had nowhere to go, he took them in as disciples.

Because of this, Mo Bei became all the more certain—

Certain that her destined young swordsman was waiting for her in the distant future.

And having experienced all this, Mo Xi seemed to have become certain of something as well. Once untethered from his bundle with Mo Bei, he pranced around even more vigorously in the Heavenly Spirit Sect.

In the past, Mo Xi disdained currying favor with others in Fallen Immortal Palace. Yet in the Heavenly Spirit Sect, he bustled about behind Eldest Senior Brother and Second Senior Brother without pause. It was only because Master spent most of his time in secluded cultivation; otherwise, he would have shamelessly camped out in Master's cave abode as a servant boy doing cleaning and sweeping chores.

At the same time, Mo Xi's desire to amass his private stash grew even stronger.

He gathered several lackeys, stirring up trouble from the inner sect to the outer sect, dipping his hands into dharma treasures, spiritual tools, and medicinal pills alike. No one knew what he wanted so many spirit stones for. In terms of cultivation, the resources direct disciples received were more than ample; even Mo Xi himself had admitted their resources surpassed those of direct disciples in prestigious orthodox sects.

Since that was the case, why was Mo Xi still unsatisfied?

Mo Bei couldn't figure it out.

The fuse for this latest incident had been ignited in just this manner.

Mo Bei wasn't clear on the exact details. She heard that in order to please Eldest Senior Brother and earn some spirit stones on the side, Mo Xi had his lackeys resell the exam papers from the previous assessment, only to be caught red-handed by Master and Second Senior Brother. To make amends, Mo Xi took on the mission of hosting the Grand Unified Examination. Yet Mo Xi didn't merely want to "make amends"—he also wanted to prove himself before Master. He took on far too many tasks, and in the end had no choice but to bring out his "pet."

That "pet" was a Shen Demon, whose exhaled mist could transform into an undersea palace.

It had originally been "merchandise" of Fallen Immortal Palace as well, used to host lavish banquets with aquatic characteristics for distinguished guests.

Naturally, such a Shen Demon was also "high-grade merchandise." Like all high-priced items, it was difficult to maintain, requiring vast amounts of yin-attribute spiritual energy to nourish. If not naturally yin-aligned aquatic creatures, it required consuming large numbers of cultivators practicing yin-attribute arts—and "cultivation furnaces" became the most suitable consumables.

Even with Mo Bei and Mo Xi, while preserving their commercial value, Fallen Immortal Palace still exploited them by having them supply spiritual energy to nourish the Shen Demon, keeping the beast in a "warehouse" near them. Thus, when Fallen Immortal Palace was flattened by Zhou Hui, Mo Xi brought it along with him.

This time, unable to renovate the Palace of Silence in time, Mo Xi planned to use the Shen Demon's palace as a substitute.

However, Mo Xi seemed to have overlooked one thing: in the past, even Fallen Immortal Palace had Mo Bei and Mo Xi cooperate to nourish the Shen Demon—their cultivation arts were specifically selected complementary arts that, when paired together, produced a tenfold amplification effect. Relying solely on Mo Xi alone, no matter how supreme his [Extreme Yin Constitution](# "Literal CN: 极阴之体 (jíyīn zhī tǐ) — rare constitution of extreme yin energy, making the possessor the most prized and sought-after cultivation furnace") was, he could not fully supply the spiritual energy.

Yet Mo Xi did not ask Mo Bei for help. He silently bore all the Shen Demon's spiritual energy demands by himself. As a result, when the exam finally concluded, he could hold on no longer, fainting from total exhaustion, and had remained bedridden ever since.

Mo Bei had no idea what meaning there was in Mo Xi's stubborn persistence. His brain was probably broken...

—"Your brain... is the one that's broken,"-a raspy voice suddenly croaked from the bed beside her.

Mo Bei paid him no mind, focusing intently on peeling the very last ribbon of peach skin. Only then did she raise her eyes, seeing those eyes that shared the exact same shape as her own—yet were always praised for being clever and sharp—glaring at her... or rather, at the peach in her hand.

—"Ah, you're awake!"-Mo Bei said.

—"You're being so noisy next to me, how could I not wake up?"-Mo Xi said in a dry, hoarse voice.

After fainting, he had been placed by Master in the Meditation Hall—a dedicated meditation room for Heavenly Spirit Sect disciples situated above a thick spiritual stone vein, providing sufficient ambient spiritual energy to nourish Mo Xi's depleted body and speed up his recovery. However, Mo Xi's cultivation was clearly still too low; although he could barely practice inedia, days of unconsciousness without a single drop of water had left him in a state of extreme weakness.

Mo Bei cast a pained look at Mo Xi, leisurely sliced off a piece of peach flesh, and remarked:
—"How was I noisy? I didn't make a sound."

—"You know full well I can hear what you're thinking!"-Mo Xi said, his eyes still glued to the peach.

—"Ah, you can hear it?"-Mo Bei asked, popping that piece of peach into her own mouth.

—"..."-Mo Xi glared at her in disbelief.-"I thought you came here to look after me?"

—"I did. Second Senior Brother ordered me to,"-Mo Bei nodded.-"I couldn't get out of it. Today's open lecture was so interesting, yet after dropping in for a bit I had to come back and look after you."

—"I don't need you looking after me!"-Mo Xi snapped irritably.

—"Don't say that. In the eyes of outsiders, we were sold as a package deal,"-Mo Bei leisurely sliced off another piece of peach.-"Though I really don't understand you. It was one thing back in Fallen Immortal Palace, but here no one wants to sell you. Why push yourself into such a pathetic state?"

Mo Xi frowned:
—"Someone like you, who constantly daydreams that others will come save her, naturally wouldn't understand the feeling of being unable to grasp your own future."

—"What's wrong with our future?"-Mo Bei gave her twin brother an odd look, taking a bite of the second piece of peach.

—"..."-Mo Xi glared at her, refusing to speak.

—"Why aren't you answering? We aren't in Fallen Immortal Palace anymore, no one wants to sell us, and we don't lack food or clothing. What are you tossing and turning for?"-Mo Bei asked. Growing impatient with slowly slicing the peach, she simply took a bite directly out of the remaining fruit, recommending:-"Oh right, the kitchen got a new basket of spirit peaches today. When I came to see you earlier, I picked this one out on the way. It's so juicy and sweet, absolutely delicious."

—"............"

As Mo Bei spoke, she finished the entire peach.

Seeing that Mo Xi still had no intention of speaking, she spat out the peach pit and said:
—"If you don't want to talk, forget it. Since you're awake, I'll go inform Second Senior Brother."

Licking the peach juice from her fingers, she rose and walked outside without the slightest lingering attachment.

Mo Xi watched with disgust until her back completely vanished down the path outside the door, then grumbled:
—"You don't understand at all..."

Saying this, he struggled to prop his body up.

He hadn't realized before moving, but not only was his throat parched, his body was limp and devoid of strength. Having starved past the point of hunger, he hadn't felt starved, yet the lingering fragrance of the peach that wretched Mo Bei had eaten drifted in the air, making his stomach spasm with hunger.

Why did Second Senior Brother have to send Mo Bei to "look after" him?!

Mo Xi felt so aggrieved he nearly wanted to cry. Just as he prepared to fend for himself, a plate of neatly sliced immortal peaches was placed before him:
—"Don't understand what?"

—"M-Master?!"-Mo Xi exclaimed in shock.

He tumbled back onto the bed, only to see Zhou Hui sitting in the very chair Mo Bei had occupied moments ago, smiling at him.

—"Eat something to line your stomach first before speaking."

—"Master..."-Mo Xi said, deeply moved.

Looking at the shimmering white halo radiating around Master, he suddenly remembered the first day he had met him.

That day was supposed to be an ordinary day. As usual, he had been locked inside that resplendent, gilded cage like a doll locked in a heavy gold box. But on that day, that exquisite coffin-like lid was suddenly ripped open and crushed into powder. An immortal enveloped in moonlight descended from the sky.

It was also on that day that Mo Xi understood what a true "immortal" should look like.

Though he always ridiculed his twin sister Mo Bei's fairy-tale daydream of a "savior descending from heaven" as unrealistic, every time he gazed upon Master's radiant aura, he felt that indulging slightly in this fleeting serenity wouldn't hurt, would it?

Thinking this, Mo Xi gingerly picked up a slice of immortal peach and placed it in his mouth.

Sure enough, just as Mo Bei had said, it was succulent and sweet.

No, the peach Master brought was definitely better than the one that wretched Mo Bei got! Mo Xi ate slowly, feeling the physical discomfort from days of unconsciousness completely vanish, replaced by a warm, sated fullness.

Yet Mo Xi was reluctant to finish it all at once, slowing his pace even further.

Zhou Hui did not rush him, waiting with gentle patience.

Unfortunately, no matter how many peach slices there were, they would eventually run out. Mo Xi slowly finished the last slice, staring blankly at the juice on his hands. Mindful of his dignity, he couldn't bring himself to lick his fingers before Master.

Zhou Hui didn't give him the chance to, either.

Seeing that Mo Xi had finished, Zhou Hui casually conjured a sphere of water for Mo Xi to wash his hands, then asked in a measured tone:
—"To continue our topic from earlier: why do you believe Bei'er doesn't understand?"

—"Ah, Master, you heard what she and I said?"-Mo Xi asked. Recalling what Mo Bei had said, he complained:-"You don't need to pay it any mind. That Mo Bei has no sense of [dwelling in danger while in safety](# "Literal CN: 居危思安 (jū wēi sī ān) — Mo Xi's inverted formulation of the classic idiom 'dwelling in safety while mindful of danger' (居安思危)") at all, nor does she consider the future. Instead, she thinks I do too much."

—"Then, is there anything about the Heavenly Spirit Sect that requires you to dwell in danger?"-Zhou Hui asked.

—"O-Of course not!"-Mo Xi bristled with anxiety in an instant, dreadfully afraid of displeasing Master.-"With Master here, the Heavenly Spirit Sect is the safest place under heaven."

—"Your actions do not say so,"-Zhou Hui shook his head, speaking with earnest gravity.-"Your usual craving for spirit stones aside, you are far too obsessed with completing missions. In truth, from the moment I learned you had taken on so many tasks, I was waiting for you to reflect and honestly admit that you could not handle it."

Who would have thought Mo Xi would persist until the very end, refusing to speak even as his spiritual power depleted to the point of collapsing into a coma.

—"Master..."

Zhou Hui sighed:
—"Frankly speaking, when I saw you collapse, I couldn't help questioning myself. Was I so harsh in the past that my disciples don't even dare rely on their Master—"

—"Of course not! This was my own choice!"-Mo Xi vehemently denied.

He tried to grasp Zhou Hui's hand, but his shoulders ultimately drooped:
—"It's my own inner knot... But I have always felt that one cannot rely on Master forever. Master will ascend sooner or later."

Rather, like Mo Bei, always waiting for someone to come save her was what Mo Xi could never understand.

Mo Xi had always tried to seize control of his own future. Whether in the Palace of Fallen Immortals or in the Heavenly Spirit Sect, he knew that as an [Extreme Yin Constitution](# "Literal CN: 极阴之体 (jíyīn zhī tǐ) — rare constitution of extreme yin energy, making the possessor the most prized and sought-after cultivation furnace")—the supreme furnace most coveted by cultivators—once he lost the protection of the Untiring Immortal Venerable, what kind of abyss he would fall into.

Mo Xi didn't want that nightmare to become reality:
—"It sounds terribly traitorous, but in the past, every time Master faced a heavenly tribulation, I secretly hoped Master would fail. If only I were stronger—strong enough to hold my own future—I wouldn't have such thoughts of hindering Master."

—"There is no need..."-Zhou Hui began.

Zhou Hui wanted to say "you still have your senior brothers, it's not as if Heavenly Spirit Sect would fall just because I ascend." But thinking of the Heavenly Dao's script, he really had no right to say that.

Facts proved that he hadn't even ascended yet! He had merely gone into secluded cultivation, and the Heavenly Spirit Sect had met that tragic end. Though Mo Xi himself had played a role in fueling those flames, this fear was not entirely groundless.

Zhou Hui gave a helpless sigh:
—"I have abandoned ascension now. You should be at ease, shouldn't you?"

—"..."-Mo Xi hung his head even lower, like an innocent kitten that had shattered a glass vase.

He didn't believe a word about "abandoning ascension."

Even if his Master truly gave up, Mo Xi felt the Immortal Realm should pave an immortal path down to welcome Master's ascension. After all, if even his Master wasn't qualified to ascend, who else under heaven was!

Looking at Mo Xi, Zhou Hui knew he didn't believe him.

Zhou Hui felt deeply helpless. Why was it that no matter how many times he said it, nobody believed him?

Zhou Hui rubbed his temples:
—"At the very least, if you encounter something in the future where you might die of exhaustion, you must ask me for help in time."

—"Yes, Master,"-Mo Xi obediently looked up with round, feline eyes.-"I won't dare do it again."

Lies.

He would definitely dare do it again next time.

If he didn't exhaust himself near death, how would Master feel heartache for him?!

Doing poorly on exams lost points; not finishing homework properly lost points; not doing homework and reselling test papers lost even more points; and biting off more than he could chew, failing to complete missions, and running to Master for help would be the biggest point-deduction of all!

He, Mo Xi, was never Master's most cherished disciple to begin with. If he did that, he'd be completely finished!

However, exhausting himself to the limit to complete a mission, persevering until the very last moment to put on a good performance—that earned bonus points! Not to mention that while sustaining that entire palace, he had still taken the exam with utter seriousness!

In this way, wouldn't the persona of a remarkably useful, stubborn, yet pitiable and endearing little disciple be established?

Master peeling the peach for him with his own hands was proof!

Back in Fallen Immortal Palace, Mo Xi had known that while people spoke of feeling heartache, what they truly could not tolerate was "useless" trash. Usually, when Mo Xi put in a bit of extra effort while pretending to be injured, they were willing to give several or dozens of times the rewards. (Of course, those scumbags could never compare to Master. But precisely because Master was a truly gentle person, he would feel even more guilt over letting his beloved disciple fall into a coma.)

Thinking this in his heart, Mo Xi didn't forget to consolidate his persona:
—"By the way, Master, what was my exam score?"

—"We can discuss such matters later. Come with me first,"-Zhou Hui sighed.

He enveloped Mo Xi in a white halo of light.

—"Master?"-Mo Xi acted surprised on the surface, but rejoiced internally:

[See? It worked! I actually got *that* Master to say 'exam scores don't matter'!]

As if answering Mo Xi's secret glee, Zhou Hui performed a spatial transfer with his youngest disciple. When the white light dissipated, they were no longer in the Meditation Hall, but in a cavern somewhere.

This cavern seemed to be deep underground, sweltering and cramped.

Yet strangely, it was not dark at all, but blindingly bright and brimming with surging spiritual energy.

The moment Mo Xi arrived, he realized what this was. Looking toward the cavern opening, he discovered that beneath the cavern ran a magnificent "river"—only this "river" was not composed of water, but of spirit stones.

Massive spirit stones formed a subterranean vein like a flowing river, shimmering and laid bare across the earth. One didn't even need to mine them; bending down, one could pick them up.

—"Is this the spirit stone vein of our Mount Tianling?"-Mo Xi asked in an excited tone.

Yet his eyes weren't quite so excited.

Though he loved spirit stones, he had seen plenty of treasures in Fallen Immortal Palace. No matter how precious a treasure was, if it didn't belong to him, it couldn't stir a ripple in his heart—let alone a spirit vein belonging to his Master. (He would steal Fallen Immortal Palace's treasures without a shred of guilt, but how could he ever touch Master's treasures?)

As for Zhou Hui bringing him here at this moment, Mo Xi thought he knew what "reward" Master intended to give him.

Still, he should probably refuse, right?

Though turning down spirit stones was agonizing, getting spirit stones was nowhere near as good as keeping Master's heartache forever.

Mo Xi calculated meticulously as always.

Zhou Hui seemed oblivious to Mo Xi's petty calculations, smiling faintly:
—"Do you like it? Then I shall give it all to you."

—"Eh?"-Mo Xi's cat-like eyes widened in sheer shock.

Then he panicked:
—"Master, you must be joking! A spirit vein like this is enough for a major sect to squander for hundreds of years!"

How could it possibly be given to him alone?

—"In the Heavenly Spirit Sect, this is hardly rare,"-Zhou Hui replied nonchalantly.-"Besides, with this spirit vein, you can rest assured and switch out your cultivation art, can't you?"

—"Cultivation art?"

—"Yes,"-Zhou Hui nodded, looking sternly at his youngest disciple.-"The art you practice is a pure Yin technique, extremely ill-suited for a man. When you first entered my tutelage, I asked if you wished to change it, but you refused back then. Originally, I thought your art and your sister's could complement each other, so it wasn't too terrible. But looking at things now, given how you use it, it is better to discard it and switch to an art you can practice on your own."

—"B-But if I do that, I'll be of no use to Master!"-Mo Xi said dryly.

Abolishing his pure Yin art meant completely severing ties with everything from his past.

If he did that, even with the Shen Demon, he would never again be able to conjure that undersea palace.

To this, Zhou Hui did not hesitate in the slightest:
—"I never needed you to be useful."

As a Tribulation Transcendence cultivator, what could he want that he could not obtain?

—"As your Master, what I desire is for you to have an honorable, dignified future."
